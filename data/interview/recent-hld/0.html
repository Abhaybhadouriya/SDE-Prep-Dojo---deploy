The story: asking the kids who already took the test

Explain like I'm 5

Imagine a school where every year the big test has a "draw a city" question. Nobody tells you which city. But lots of older kids wrote notes after their test: "I had to draw a train station", "mine was a hospital", "I got the train station too!". If you collect all those notes and count them, you can see that the train station comes up again and again. So you practise the train station first, then the hospital, then the rest. That is all this page does. The notes are interview posts. The cities are system designs.

A high-level design (HLD) round gives you a one-line prompt such as "Design a ticket booking system" and 45 to 60 minutes. You draw the big boxes (clients, services, databases, queues), explain how a request flows, and defend your choices when the interviewer pushes. Most prompts come from a fairly small pool. Knowing which ones are common this year lets you spend your practice time where it pays.

From your world

This is like reading a bug tracker before a release: you sort issues by how many customers hit them and fix the top ones first. Here the "issues" are design prompts and the "customers" are candidates who wrote about their loop. Where the analogy breaks: a bug count is complete, but these posts are a sample of people who chose to write. Treat the counts as a strong hint, not a guarantee.
