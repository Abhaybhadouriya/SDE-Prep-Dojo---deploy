How to use this page

Explain like I'm 5

When you read a story and meet a word you do not know, you ask a grown-up. This page is that grown-up. Find the word, read the short "Like I'm 5" line to get the picture, read the "Precisely" line to get the real meaning, then jump back to your story. If you want the whole story of the word, click the page in the "Learn it" column.

You told us something honest: you are fast at depth and slow at vocabulary. Words like sidecar, ingress, idempotent, eventual consistency and p99 stop you more than the ideas behind them. So every row here has four parts:

  1. The word with a coloured track chip (Docker, Kubernetes, Kafka, Pipeline, Cloud, IaC, CI/CD, SRE, HLD, LLD or Interview). A green must know chip marks the 113 words you will hear in almost every interview.
  2. Like I'm 5 - one picture sentence with no jargon at all.
  3. Precisely - the real definition, with the real names of the processes and settings, and, in italics, the idea from Linux or networking that it maps to.
  4. Learn it - the page on this site that teaches the word properly, with diagrams and commands.

Use the A-Z bar below, your browser's find (Ctrl+F), or the site search at the top. Every row has its own link target, so any page can point straight at a word, for example glossary.html#idempotent.

From your world

Most of this vocabulary is old ideas with new names. A container is a process with namespaces and a cgroup. A ClusterIP is a VIP made of NAT rules. A Kafka partition key works like ECMP flow hashing. Consumer lag is queue depth. Backpressure is flow control. Where the analogy breaks, the row says so in the precise column, so keep reading past the italics.

Same word, different meaning. Watch for these pairs. High watermark (Kafka replication: how far all in-sync copies have caught up) is not the event-time watermark (a stream processor's guess of how far time has moved). A Linux namespace is not a Kubernetes namespace. A Kafka partition is not a network partition in the CAP theorem. A Kafka controller is not a Kubernetes controller. There is a full table of these near the end: you might confuse these.
