Facade (pattern)
LLD		One big 'start movie' button that turns on the TV, speakers and lights for you.A structural pattern that gives a simple front interface to a complex subsystem without hiding the subsystem from those who need it. From your world: A single ioctl or library call that sets up a whole multi-step device config.Facade
Factory Method
LLD		Each bakery decides which bread to bake when you just say 'bread, please'.A creational pattern where a method (often overridden in subclasses) decides which concrete class to create, so callers depend only on the interface. From your world: A probe() that returns the right device object for the hardware it found.Factory Method
Failover
HLD		When the goalkeeper gets hurt, the reserve goalkeeper steps in.Switching work from a failed component to a standby or replica. Needs failure detection (heartbeats, timeouts), a safe election (avoid split brain) and clients that find the new leader. From your world: VRRP/HSRP switchover, with the same timer trade-offs.Reliability at Scale: SLOs, Multi-Region & DR
Fan-out (on write / on read)
HLD must know		Telling the news by visiting every friend's house, or letting friends come to read it on your door.Delivering one item to many readers. Fan-out on write (push) copies a post into every follower's feed at write time: fast reads, expensive for celebrities. Fan-out on read (pull) assembles the feed when it is read. Real feeds mix both. From your world: Multicast replication at ingress vs at egress.Pattern: Fan-out (Push vs Pull)
AWS Fargate
Cloud		You bring the lunch box; someone else owns and runs the kitchen.Serverless compute for containers on ECS (and EKS): you size each task by vCPU and memory, and AWS runs it in its own isolated micro-VM with no EC2 instances for you to patch or scale. Each task gets its own ENI in your subnet.ECS, Fargate & Karpenter
Feature flag
HLD		A light switch inside the app that turns a new feature on or off without a new app.A runtime setting that enables code paths per user, percentage or region without a redeploy. Served by a configuration service with staged rollout and a fast kill switch; flags need owners and clean-up dates or they pile up.Distributed Configuration Service
Fencing
Kafka		Taking the old captain's whistle away so they cannot shout orders after a new captain is chosen.Stopping an old, out-of-date instance (a zombie) from writing. Done with numbers that only go up: Kafka producer epochs and group generations, leader epochs, or fencing tokens checked by a storage system. From your world: Stonith in HA clusters.Delivery Semantics & Exactly-Once
FinOps
SRE		Reading the electricity bill every month and switching off the lights nobody uses.The practice of making cloud cost visible and owned by engineering teams: tagging and showback, right-sizing, Spot, commitments (Savings Plans, Reserved Instances), storage lifecycle and cutting data-transfer charges.Cloud Cost / FinOps
Flannel
Kubernetes		A simple tunnel that carries messages between rooms.A simple CNI plug-in that gives each node a pod subnet and connects nodes with a VXLAN overlay by default. Easy to run, but it does not enforce NetworkPolicy by itself. From your world: VXLAN with VTEPs on every node.Services & Cluster Networking
Follower replica
Kafka		A helper copying the leader's diary line by line.A replica of a partition that fetches records from the leader and stays in sync. If it falls behind for more than replica.lag.time.max.ms (30 s default) it leaves the ISR. It can become leader if the leader fails. From your world: The standby in a redundancy pair, kept in sync by state replication.Replication & ISR
FR / NFR (functional / non-functional requirements)
Interview		What the toy must do (it rolls) versus how well (it never breaks, it is fast).Functional requirements list what the system does ("users can book a seat"). Non-functional requirements say how well, with numbers: scale, latency (for example p99 under 200 ms), availability, consistency, cost. Write both in the first five minutes of every HLD round.The 45-Minute Interview Framework
fsync and the page cache
Kafka		Writing in pencil on the board (fast) vs carving it in stone (slow but safe).A write normally lands in the OS page cache and reaches disk later; fsync forces it to disk. Kafka does not fsync per message by default; durability comes from having copies on several brokers. From your world: The same dirty-page writeback you know from the kernel.Storage Internals & Performance Secrets
