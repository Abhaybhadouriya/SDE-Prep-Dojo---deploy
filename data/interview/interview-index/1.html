The shape of a typical loop

Almost every large company uses the same five stages. The names change, the shape does not. The dot moving below is you.

  1. Online assessment (OA). A timed test on a site like HackerRank. Usually one or two coding questions. Amazon adds a work-style survey that checks fit with its Leadership Principles.
  2. Phone or video screen. One engineer, one or two data-structure and algorithm (DSA) problems, sometimes a short design chat.
  3. The loop. Four or five one-hour rounds on one day (or spread over a week when virtual). Mix of DSA, low-level design (LLD: classes and interfaces), high-level design (HLD: boxes and arrows at web scale) and behavioural.
  4. Debrief. The interviewers compare written notes. At Google, a hiring committee reads the packet instead of the interviewers deciding alone.
  5. Level and offer. The level (SDE-1 or SDE-2) is often decided here. A weak design round can lead to an offer at a lower level instead of a rejection.

Two new words. LLD (low-level design, also called OOD, object-oriented design) means designing classes, interfaces and their relationships for one program, like a parking lot or Splitwise. HLD (high-level design, or system design) means designing many machines and services working together, like WhatsApp. See HLD vs LLD.
