When you do not know the answer

You will hit a question you cannot answer. Everyone does. What is scored is how you handle it.

  1. Say it honestly, briefly. "I have not worked with that directly."
  2. Reason from first principles out loud. "But if it is a replicated log, a write must reach a majority before it is safe, so I would expect..."
  3. State your assumptions. "I am assuming the producer retries on timeout; if not, we could lose data here."
  4. Offer how you would find out. "In practice I would check the docs for the default and test it with a broker kill in a lab."
  5. Move on. Do not spend five minutes on one gap. Time is part of the rubric.

Never bluff a number or a feature. An experienced interviewer knows the default retention or the name of the API. A confident wrong answer costs more than "I am not sure, but here is how I would reason about it."
