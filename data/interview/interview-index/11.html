60-second answers

Say these out loud until they feel like your own words.

"Tell me about yourself." (This is the one canonical script on the site; the question bank and the study plan link here.) "I am a software engineer with [N] years in systems and networking: I worked on [driver / control-plane / data-path] software for [type of product]. The parts I enjoyed most were debugging performance under load and making failover reliable. Over the last few months I have been moving towards backend and distributed systems: I learned Docker, Kubernetes and Kafka properly, and built an end-to-end order pipeline with change data capture, stream processing and a dead-letter queue, running on Kubernetes. I am looking for an SDE role where that mix of low-level depth and distributed systems is useful, which is why this team interests me."

"Why are you moving away from networking?" "I am not running away from it; I am building on it. The problems I liked most, like flow control, failure detection and consistent state, are central to large backend systems. I want to work closer to products and users, and at a larger scale. I have already invested in the move by building real projects in this space."

"How do you approach a system design question?" "I clarify functional and non-functional requirements first, including scale. Then I do a quick estimate of traffic and storage, define the main APIs and the data model, and draw a simple high-level design. After that I pick one or two hard parts to go deep on, like consistency or hot keys, and finish with failure modes, monitoring and what I would change at ten times the load. I keep checking with the interviewer about where they want depth."
