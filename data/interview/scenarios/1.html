The six-move debugging loop

Use the same six moves for every drill. Saying them in order is half the score, because it shows the interviewer you have a method and will not panic. The dot below walks the loop; real incidents often go round more than once.

  1. Detect. How did we find out? An alert on an SLO (service level objective, a promise like "99.9% of requests succeed"), a dashboard, or an angry user. Ask the interviewer which one, because it tells you how big the problem is.
  2. Scope. Ask clarifying questions. All users or some? One region, one service, one partition, one node? Since when? What changed around that time (a deploy, a config push, a traffic spike, an OS update)? Most incidents are caused by a change.
  3. Mitigate. Protect users before you understand everything. Roll back the last deploy, shift traffic to a healthy region, scale out, turn off a feature flag, shed low-priority load. Say clearly that this is a temporary step.
  4. Diagnose. Gather evidence layer by layer: metrics (what and how much), logs (what exactly failed), traces (where in the call chain), events (what the platform did). Form a hypothesis, then test it with one command.
  5. Fix. Make the real change (code, config, capacity), roll it out carefully (canary), and verify with the same metric that alerted you.
  6. Prevent. Add the missing alert or test, write a blameless postmortem (a write-up that finds causes, not culprits), and list action items with owners.

Interviewers love this. Saying "first I would check what changed in the last hour" and "before root-causing I would roll back to stop user impact" are two of the strongest signals you can give in any troubleshooting round. They show production maturity in one sentence each.
