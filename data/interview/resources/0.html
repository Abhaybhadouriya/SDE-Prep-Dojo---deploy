How to use this page

Explain like I'm 5

A library has millions of books. If you try to read them all, you never finish one. A good librarian says: "Start with this picture book. When you can read it, try this one. When you love the subject, here is the big book." This page is that librarian. Pick one thing from your current level, finish it, then climb.

You asked for "links that are worth my time: official docs, one good video, one good blog. Not 20 links." So every topic below has the same three shelves, and the first link on each topic carries a green if you open only one chip. Open that one first. The other links on the shelf are there for when you want more, not a reading list you must finish.

  1. Start (Level 1-2). One short video and one official tutorial. Enough to know the words and run your first command.
  2. Build (Level 3). A longer course or book chapters plus a hands-on lab, so you can explain how it works inside and why the settings exist.
  3. Go deep (Level 4-5). Internals talks, design documents, papers and real company post-mortems - the material that makes interview answers sound like experience.

Each link has a coloured tag: doc official documentation, video, course (courses and labs), book, blog (blogs and newsletters), repo (GitHub), paper. Videos you can play right here were checked to exist; links were checked to load (a few company blogs block scripts but open in a normal browser, and say so).

From your world

You already learned networking the right way: an RFC or vendor config guide for the facts, a lab (GNS3, a test rack) for the feel, and post-mortems for judgement. Use the same three sources here. The official docs are the RFCs, the browser labs are your test rack, and the engineering blogs are the outage reports you used to read on the NANOG list. Where this breaks: web docs change much faster than RFCs, so always check the date on a blog post (see the gotchas).
