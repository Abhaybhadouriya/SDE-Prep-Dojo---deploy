What one day looks like

Every day has the same five parts. Click Play to see a day unfold. In weeks 1-2 a day takes about two hours. The reading grows as the plan climbs: in weeks 6-7 a day on the 8-week pace is 3 to 4 hours. If you have a full-time job, use the 12-week pace, which keeps most days near two hours.

  1. Warm up with DSA. Two coding problems first, while your head is fresh. This site covers design; your loop still has coding rounds.
  2. Read. The pages listed for the day, in level order. Weeks 1-2: 25-70 minutes. Weeks 6-7: often 2 hours or more on the 8-week pace. Stop at Level 3 on a tired day, but come back for Levels 4 and 5.
  3. Lab. Type the commands yourself. Then break something on purpose and read the error. Errors are where real understanding comes from.
  4. Design of the day. From week 3: read the prompt, set a 20-minute timer, sketch your own answer on paper, then read the page.
  5. Say it out loud. Answer the day's drill in 60 seconds, as if to an interviewer. Record it once a week.
  6. Tick the boxes. Your progress is saved. Also press "Mark as done" at the bottom of each page you finished.
