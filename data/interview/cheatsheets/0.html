The story: the recipe card on the fridge

Explain like I'm 5

When grandma bakes her famous cake, she does not read the whole cookbook again. She looks at one small card stuck on the fridge. It says: two cups of flour, three eggs, oven at the medium mark, 40 minutes. She already knows why each step matters, because she learned it long ago. The card just reminds her of the exact numbers so she does not have to remember them. This page is that card. You learn the "why" on the other pages. Here you keep the exact numbers and words.

A cheat sheet is a compressed summary: the commands you type most, the default values that cause surprises, and the rules of thumb you quote in design rounds. It is not a place to learn something new. If a line here does not make sense, follow the link to its concept page first.

From your world

You already have one of these in your head for router CLIs: the handful of show commands you run in the first minute of any outage (interfaces, counters, routes, logs). The sheets below are the same idea for containers, clusters and streams. kubectl describe pod is your show interface; kafka-consumer-groups.sh --describe is your queue-depth counter. Where the analogy breaks: these tools talk to an API over the network, so "the box" you are inspecting is often a cluster of many machines, and the answer you get is the control plane's view, which can lag reality by seconds.
