Quick quiz

Which beat most often turns a correct answer into a senior-sounding answer?

  1. Saying the claim twice
  2. Listing every tool you know
  3. Stating the trade-off and when you would switch
  4. Drawing more boxes
Interviewers grade decisions. The cost and the switch condition show you understand that every choice has a price.

A consumer group has 8 partitions and 12 consumers. What happens?

  1. Each partition is shared by 1.5 consumers
  2. 8 consumers get one partition each; 4 sit idle
  3. Kafka creates 4 more partitions
Within one group, each partition has exactly one consumer, so parallelism is capped at the partition count.

In a production scenario question, what should come before root-cause diagnosis?

  1. Mitigating user impact (roll back, shed load, fail over)
  2. Writing the postmortem
  3. Rewriting the service
Stop the bleeding first; it is often reversible and it buys time to diagnose calmly.

Exit code 137 most often means:

  1. A segmentation fault
  2. A clean SIGTERM shutdown
  3. SIGKILL, often from the OOM killer after hitting the memory limit
137 = 128 + 9. Signal 9 is SIGKILL. 143 (128 + 15) means it was ended by SIGTERM.

What is the honest way to describe "exactly once" for a consumer that writes to a database?

  1. Kafka guarantees it end to end with one setting
  2. At-least-once delivery plus an idempotent write (upsert or dedupe key)
  3. It is impossible, so duplicates must be accepted
Kafka transactions cover Kafka-to-Kafka. Side effects outside Kafka need idempotency to be effectively once.

You get a question about a tool you have never used. Best first move?

  1. Restate the question and anchor on what you do know
  2. Guess a confident answer
  3. Say you cannot answer and wait
Restate, anchor, reason, check. Reasoning from first principles scores far better than a bluffed fact.
