The story: a gated apartment complex

Explain like I'm 5

Imagine a big gated apartment complex. Inside the wall, every flat has its own number, and nobody outside can walk in unless the guard at the main gate lets them. Some buildings face the street: shops live there, and visitors can come in through the gate. Other buildings are deep inside: that is where families sleep, and strangers can never walk in.

But families inside still need things from outside: pizza, parcels, letters. So there is a mail room near the street. A family hands a letter to the mail room, the mail room sends it out with its own return address, and when the reply comes back, the mail room carries it to the right flat. Outsiders only ever see the mail room's address. They can answer letters, but they cannot start a visit.

That complex is a VPC. The flat numbers are IP addresses. The street-facing buildings are public subnets, the inner buildings are private subnets, the gate is the Internet Gateway, the mail room is the NAT gateway, and the little signboards at every corridor that say "street this way, mail room that way" are route tables.

By the end of this page you will be able to draw a production VPC on a whiteboard, choose its address ranges, explain exactly which route makes a subnet "public", and trace a request hop by hop. Interviewers at Amazon, Flipkart and Walmart ask exactly this, usually as "draw the network for this design".
