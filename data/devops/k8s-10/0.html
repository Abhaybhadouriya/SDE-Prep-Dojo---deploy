The story: name tags and a robot babysitter

Explain like I'm 5

In the playground, kids can sit anywhere. If one goes home, any other kid can take the swing. Nobody cares who is who. But in the school band it is different. Maya always plays the drum and keeps the drum music in her own folder. Leo always plays the flute with his own folder. If Maya is sick today, you cannot hand her folder to just anybody - the band waits for Maya (or someone who becomes Maya, with Maya's folder) to come back. They also walk on stage in order: first the drum, then the flute, then the piano.

Looking after a band is tricky, so the school buys a robot babysitter who knows band rules: "tune instruments before the show", "never let two drummers play at once", "if a new kid joins, give them some of the songs". You just tell the robot "I want a band of three", and it does all the careful work.

The playground kids are Deployment pods: identical, interchangeable, replaced by any copy (see Deployments). The band members are StatefulSet pods: each has a stable name (kafka-0, kafka-1), its own disk that follows it, and a fixed order. The robot babysitter is an operator: a program that runs inside the cluster and encodes the expert knowledge of running one specific system - for Kafka, the most popular one is Strimzi. And the new word you teach Kubernetes ("band", or "Kafka") is a CRD, a Custom Resource Definition.

This page connects three tracks. It uses the storage you just learned, it is how the brokers from the Kafka track run on Kubernetes, and it is the base of the pipeline's Kubernetes deployment.
