Static vs dynamic provisioning

Good to know (SDE-2)

There are two ways a PV comes to exist.

Static provisioning still matters for existing data (an NFS export that already holds files) and for local disks on bare-metal nodes, where the local static provisioner turns each NVMe drive it finds into a PV.

A production StorageClass

apiVersion: storage.k8s.io/v1
kind: StorageClass
metadata:
  name: fast-ssd
  annotations:
    storageclass.kubernetes.io/is-default-class: "false"
provisioner: ebs.csi.aws.com          # which CSI driver
parameters:                           # passed to the driver as-is
  type: gp3
  encrypted: "true"
reclaimPolicy: Retain                 # keep the disk if the PVC is deleted
volumeBindingMode: WaitForFirstConsumer
allowVolumeExpansion: true

The parameters are opaque to Kubernetes - each driver documents its own. The three fields under it are the ones interviewers ask about:

  • reclaimPolicy - Delete (default for dynamic volumes: deleting the PVC deletes the real disk) or Retain (the PV goes to Released and the disk stays until a human cleans it up). For a production database, Retain is the safety net against a kubectl delete typo.
  • volumeBindingMode - Immediate (create the disk as soon as the PVC appears) or WaitForFirstConsumer (wait until the scheduler has picked a node for the pod).
  • allowVolumeExpansion - lets you grow a volume by editing spec.resources.requests.storage on the PVC. Growing works online for most drivers; shrinking is never allowed.
