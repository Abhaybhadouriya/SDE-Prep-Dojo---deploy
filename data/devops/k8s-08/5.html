The zone trap and WaitForFirstConsumer

Good to know (SDE-2)

Cloud block disks are zonal: an EBS volume in zone us-east-1a can only attach to a VM in us-east-1a. Now imagine Immediate binding: the PVC appears, the driver creates the disk in some zone (say 1a), and only later does the scheduler look at the pod. If the only node with free CPU is in 1b, the pod can never run there. It sits Pending with a message like "node(s) had volume node affinity conflict".

That is why every sensible cloud StorageClass says WaitForFirstConsumer. It has a second, quieter effect you must remember: once a pod's disk exists in zone 1b, that pod is pinned to zone 1b forever. The PV carries a nodeAffinity for that zone. If zone 1b has no capacity, or goes down, the pod cannot move to 1a. Stateful apps survive a zone failure by replicating data at the application level (Kafka replicas, Postgres standbys in other zones), not by moving one disk around.

From your world

Think of a line card with locally attached optics: you cannot move the fibre to a card in another chassis. Redundancy comes from a second path through another chassis (like a protected circuit), not from moving the cable. A zonal disk is the same: the second copy must already be living in another zone. Where this breaks: regional disks (GCP regional PD, Azure ZRS) do replicate across zones in the storage layer, at the price of extra write latency.
