The story: lockers at school

Explain like I'm 5

At school you sit at a desk. But desks change: one day you sit by the window, the next day near the door. If you leave your drawings inside the desk, they are gone when you move. So the school gives you a locker. The locker stays in the hallway no matter which desk you sit at. Your drawings are safe.

To get a locker, you do not walk around opening lockers. You fill in a little form: "I need a big locker, just for me." The school office reads the form and either hands you a free locker, or, if none is free, a helper builds a brand-new one. The office has different kinds of lockers too - cheap metal ones and fancy fast ones - and the form says which kind you want.

In Kubernetes, the desk is a pod. Pods are cheap and get thrown away and replaced all the time (see Pods and Deployments). Anything written inside a container's own filesystem disappears with it. The locker is a PersistentVolume (PV) - a real piece of storage that lives longer than any pod. The form is a PersistentVolumeClaim (PVC) - a request that says "I need 10 GiB that one node can write". The kinds of lockers are StorageClasses - recipes like "fast SSD" or "cheap HDD". The helper who builds new lockers is a CSI driver (Container Storage Interface), a plug-in that knows how to talk to a real storage system such as AWS EBS, Azure Disk, Google Persistent Disk, Ceph or a local NVMe drive.

Why should you care? Because every interesting system you will design has state: databases, Kafka brokers, search indexes, caches that take an hour to warm up. Running stateless web pods is the easy 80% of Kubernetes. Storage is where the hard 20% - and many interview follow-ups - live.

From your world

You already know this split from SAN storage: a storage array exposes a LUN, a host asks for it, the array maps it, the host's driver sees a block device, and the host mounts it. Kubernetes follows the same shape. The PV is the LUN, the PVC is the host's request, the CSI driver is the vendor driver plus the array's management API, and the kubelet does the final mount. Where this breaks: in Kubernetes the "host" changes every time the pod is rescheduled, so the disk must be detached from one node and attached to another automatically.
