The story: one booklet, many LEGO towns

Explain like I'm 5

Riya builds LEGO towns. The first town she built by looking at a picture and guessing. The second town came out a bit different. So she wrote a booklet: "a house = 4 walls, 1 door, 1 roof". Now any friend can build the same house just by reading the booklet and choosing a colour. Riya keeps three towns: a practice town on the floor, a show town for her parents, and the real town on the shelf that everyone looks at. She keeps a separate notebook for each town, listing every brick in it. One night her little brother sneaks in and swaps a red roof for a blue one on the shelf town. The notebook still says "red". The next morning Riya reads the notebook, compares it with the town, and spots the blue roof before she builds anything new on top of it. Before she touches the shelf town she always writes down her plan, shows it to her mom, and only then follows exactly that plan. Some bricks, like the big base plate, are glued with a note: "never take this off".

That story is this whole page. The booklet is a module: a reusable recipe with inputs (the colour) and outputs. The three towns are environments (dev, staging, prod). The three notebooks are separate state files. The swapped roof is drift: reality changed but the record did not. Comparing the notebook to the town is a refresh-only plan. Writing the plan down and following it exactly is a saved plan reviewed in CI. The glued base plate is prevent_destroy.

From your world

You have lived this on routers. A Terraform module is a golden config template (a "VPC with three subnets and a route table" is just a router config template with variables for the prefix). Separate state per environment is like keeping lab, pre-prod and production devices on separate management planes so a script pointed at the lab cannot touch prod. Drift is the classic running-config vs startup-config mismatch: someone typed a change on the CLI at 2 a.m. and never saved it to the config repo. terraform plan -refresh-only is show archive config differences. A saved, reviewed plan is a change ticket with the exact command list approved in a maintenance-window review, and prevent_destroy is the reload confirmation prompt you cannot skip. Where the analogy breaks: a router stores its own running config, so the diff is local; Terraform has to ask a cloud API about every object, which is slow and rate-limited, so state size matters.

This page builds on Terraform Basics (providers, resources, the plan/apply loop) and Terraform State (tfstate, S3 backends, locking). New words: config drift, GitOps, rollback.
