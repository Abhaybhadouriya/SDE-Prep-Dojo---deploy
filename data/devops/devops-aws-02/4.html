Auto Scaling Groups: a control loop for machines

Must know (SDE-1)

An Auto Scaling Group (ASG) holds three numbers - min, max and desired - plus a launch template and a list of subnets (one per AZ). Its whole job is to make the number of healthy InService instances equal desired. If an instance dies, it launches a replacement. Scaling policies change desired; the ASG does the rest.

  1. Traffic doubles. Two instances each serve 2,000 requests a minute against a target of 1,000.
  2. CloudWatch sees the per-target request count breach the target; the high alarm fires.
  3. The target tracking policy computes the new desired capacity: 4 (clamped between min and max).
  4. The ASG launches two instances from the launch template, spreading them across AZs.
  5. After boot and health checks they join the target group and load per instance falls back to target.

Health checks: who decides a machine is sick?

  • EC2 status checks (always on): the hypervisor's view. Catches dead hardware or a kernel that will not boot. Misses an app that is up but returning 500s.
  • ELB health checks (turn these on): the load balancer's view, for example GET /healthz every 10 seconds. If the target group marks an instance unhealthy, the ASG terminates and replaces it.
  • Also available: EBS impaired-volume checks, VPC Lattice and custom health checks (aws autoscaling set-instance-health).
  • Health check grace period: how long a new instance may fail checks before the ASG acts. Defaults to 300 s when created in the console but 0 s from the CLI/SDK - a famous trap where freshly booting instances are killed in a loop.
  • Lifecycle hooks pause an instance in Pending:Wait or Terminating:Wait so you can warm caches, register with service discovery or drain connections before it serves or dies.

From your world

An ASG is a reconciliation loop, the same pattern as a Kubernetes controller or a watchdog daemon that respawns crashed processes: observe actual state, compare with desired, act, repeat. Health checks are your keepalives and BFD: the EC2 check is "link is up", the ELB check is "the routing protocol is actually exchanging hellos". Only the second one tells you the service works.
