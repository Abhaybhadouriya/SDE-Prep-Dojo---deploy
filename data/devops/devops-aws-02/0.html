The story: a restaurant chain that never closes

Explain like I'm 5

A restaurant needs cooks. Each cook is a machine. On a quiet Tuesday, three cooks are enough. On Saturday night, the queue goes round the block. A smart manager watches the queue: when it gets long, she calls more cooks in; when it gets short, she sends some home. If a cook falls ill, she replaces him without anyone asking. The manager always follows one recipe card for new cooks, so every new cook knows the same dishes. That manager is an Auto Scaling Group, and the recipe card is a launch template.

At the kitchen door stands a bouncer with a guest list. He remembers everyone he let in, so when they walk back out he does not stop them. That is a security group. Down at the street there is a gate guard with a printed rule sheet who checks every single person in both directions and remembers nobody. That is a network ACL.

And the clever owner runs the restaurant in three buildings across town. If one building loses power, the other two keep serving. Those buildings are Availability Zones.
