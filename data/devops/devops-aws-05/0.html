The story: the night-shift letter sorter

Explain like I'm 5

A small post office gets letters at strange times. Some nights there are none. Some nights a truck drops a thousand.

The old way: pay one sorter to sit at the desk all night, every night, even when no letter comes. The new way: a bell rings when a bag of letters arrives. A helper appears, sorts that bag, writes "3 letters for the police, 40 for the bank" on a board, and goes home. If ten bags arrive at once, ten helpers appear. You only pay for the minutes they were sorting.

If a helper finds a letter they cannot read, they do not throw it away. They put it in a special "problem letters" box so a grown-up can look at it in the morning.

That is AWS Lambda used as glue in an observability pipeline. The bags of letters are batches of log lines (from CloudWatch Logs, S3 files, a Kinesis stream or an SQS queue). The helper is your function. The board is a set of metrics and alerts. The "problem letters" box is a dead-letter queue (DLQ).

We build that picture step by step, ending with the cases where Lambda is the wrong choice.
