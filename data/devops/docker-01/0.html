The story: lunchboxes and flats

Explain like I'm 5

Imagine you make the best sandwich in your class. You make it at home and it tastes perfect. But when your friend tries to make it at their house, it tastes wrong. Their bread is different, they have no pickles, and their butter is salty.

So you try something new. You pack the sandwich in a lunchbox, together with your own bread, your pickles and your butter. Now the sandwich tastes the same at school, at the park, or at grandma's house. Nobody needs to have the right kitchen. They just open the lunchbox.

A container is a lunchbox for a computer program. It carries the program and everything the program needs, so it runs the same on every computer.

Here is a second picture that helps later. Think of a tall building of flats (apartments). Every family has its own locked front door, its own rooms and its own name on the bell. But all the flats share the same foundation, the same water pipes and the same electricity line coming into the building.

Containers are like those flats. Each container has its own "rooms" (its own files, its own list of running programs, its own network address). But all containers on one computer share the same foundation: the computer's kernel. The kernel is the core part of the operating system that talks to the hardware, decides which program runs on the CPU, and hands out memory.

A virtual machine (VM) is different. A VM is like a separate house with its own foundation. Each VM boots its own whole operating system, with its own kernel, on top of pretend hardware. That is safer but heavier, just like building a new house is slower than giving someone a flat.
