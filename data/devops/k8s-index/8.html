Quick quiz

Why do people write Kubernetes as "K8s"?

  1. It was version 8 of a Google tool
  2. It runs 8 containers per pod
  3. There are 8 letters between the K and the s
It is a numeronym, like i18n for internationalization: K + 8 letters (ubernete) + s.

In the school story, what is a "node"?

  1. A kid
  2. A classroom (a server)
  3. The principal's note
Nodes are the machines (classrooms). Pods are desks and containers are the kids sitting at them.

What does kind do?

  1. Runs each Kubernetes node as a Docker container on your laptop
  2. Builds Docker images faster
  3. Creates a managed cluster in the cloud
kind = Kubernetes IN Docker. Great for learning and CI, not for production.

If the Kubernetes control plane crashes, what happens to containers that are already running?

  1. They all stop immediately
  2. They keep running, but nothing new gets scheduled or healed
  3. They move to another cluster
Like non-stop forwarding on a router: the data plane carries on. You lose the ability to change or repair things until the control plane is back. Page 02 explains why.

In a typical SDE-1/2 loop, where does Kubernetes most often come up?

  1. In the DSA coding round
  2. Only in behavioural rounds
  3. Resume deep-dives, HLD rounds, and heavily in platform/infra team loops
Generalist loops touch it through your resume and design rounds; platform teams go deep.
