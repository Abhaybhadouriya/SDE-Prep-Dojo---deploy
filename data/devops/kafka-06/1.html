The three promises, with real names

must know

A delivery semantic (or "delivery guarantee") is a promise about how many times a message affects the receiver when things fail. There are three:

SemanticWhat can go wrongHow you get it in KafkaGood for
At most onceMessages lost on failure, never duplicatedProducer: acks=0 or no retries. Consumer: commit the offset before processingMetrics where a gap is fine, high-volume telemetry, "latest value wins" sensors
At least onceDuplicates on failure, never lostProducer: acks=all + retries. Consumer: process then commitAlmost everything, if the handler is idempotent
Exactly onceNothing lost, nothing doubled - inside the scope it coversIdempotent producer + transactions + read_committed consumers (Kafka to Kafka), or idempotent sinksStream processing, money, counts, anything where a double is a bug

Two new words, defined now because the rest of the page leans on them:

  • Idempotent (eye-dem-POH-tent): doing it twice has the same effect as doing it once. "Set balance to 500" is idempotent. "Add 100 to balance" is not. Pressing a lift button five times is idempotent - the lift still comes once.
  • Side effect: anything your code changes outside itself - a row in a database, an email sent, a card charged. Side effects are what duplicates actually hurt.

Interviewers love this: say "exactly-once processing" or "effectively-once", not "exactly-once delivery". Over an unreliable network you cannot guarantee a message is delivered exactly once; you can guarantee its effect is applied exactly once by deduplicating. Saying this sentence early separates you from candidates who "overclaim" Kafka's guarantee.
