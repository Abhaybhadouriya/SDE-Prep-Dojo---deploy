Quick quiz

A consumer commits the offset, then processes the batch, and crashes halfway. What semantic is this?

  1. At least once
  2. At most once
  3. Exactly once
The bookmark moved before the work finished, so the unfinished records are skipped on restart: they may be lost, never repeated.

What does the broker use to drop a retried duplicate batch from an idempotent producer?

  1. The record key
  2. A hash of the value
  3. The producer ID, epoch and per-partition sequence number
  4. The timestamp
Each batch is numbered per partition; a sequence the leader has already written for that producer ID is acknowledged without being appended again.

Your app crashes and restarts, then re-sends its last record. Does idempotence alone stop the duplicate?

  1. No - the restarted producer has a new producer ID
  2. Yes - the broker remembers the key
  3. Yes - sequence numbers are stored on the client disk
Idempotence works within a producer session. Continuity across restarts needs a stable transactional.id.

A producer uses transactions, but consumers keep the default isolation level. What do consumers see?

  1. Only committed records
  2. Committed, open and aborted records
  3. Nothing until they restart
The default is read_uncommitted, which reads up to the high watermark including aborted data.

What does sendOffsetsToTransaction() achieve?

  1. It speeds up the commit
  2. It sends offsets to ZooKeeper
  3. It makes the consumer's offset commit part of the same atomic transaction as the output records
Output and "I have consumed up to here" commit together, so a crash cannot leave one without the other.

A read_committed consumer is stuck at offset 700 while the high watermark is 9,000. Most likely cause?

  1. The partition has no leader
  2. The consumer's max.poll.records is too small
  3. Retention deleted the data
  4. A transaction starting at offset 700 is still open
read_committed stops at the last stable offset, the first record of the oldest open transaction.

Your consumer charges cards through a payment API. Which gives "charged exactly once"?

  1. At-least-once consumption plus an idempotency key on the API call and a unique payment ID in the ledger
  2. Kafka transactions around the API call
  3. acks=all with min.insync.replicas=2
Kafka transactions cannot roll back an external charge. Deduplication must happen where the side effect happens.

How is a zombie instance stopped from writing?

  1. The consumer group kicks it out
  2. The new instance's initTransactions() bumps the epoch, and writes with the old epoch are rejected
  3. Kubernetes kills it
Fencing by epoch: the highest epoch for a transactional.id wins; the zombie gets ProducerFencedException.
