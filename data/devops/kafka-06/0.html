The story: did my letter arrive?

Explain like I'm 5

You post a birthday card to Grandma and wait for her to phone you and say "got it". The phone never rings. What do you do?

Option 1: shrug and do nothing. If the card was lost, Grandma gets no card. That is at most once: zero or one card.

Option 2: post another card. Maybe the first one arrived and only the phone call was lost - now Grandma has two cards. That is at least once: one or more cards.

Option 3: write a number on every card: "card #7". You post a new copy, and Grandma keeps a little stamp book. When a second "card #7" arrives she says "already got #7" and throws it away. Grandma sees each card exactly once, even though the postman carried it twice.

That last trick is the whole page. Networks and computers fail in the middle of things, so the sender can never be sure. The only real choice is between "maybe lose it" and "maybe send it twice". Exactly-once is not magic delivery; it is "at least once" plus a way to throw away repeats. Kafka does this with numbers on every batch (the idempotent producer) and with all-or-nothing groups of writes (transactions). Outside Kafka - in your database or a payment gateway - you have to build the stamp book yourself.

From your world

You already live with this in TCP. A lost ACK makes the sender retransmit, so the receiver sees the same bytes twice, and drops them because their sequence numbers are already acknowledged. Kafka's idempotent producer is exactly that idea, applied per partition: the broker tracks a sequence number per producer and drops repeats. Where the analogy breaks: TCP's state dies with the connection. Kafka keeps the producer's sequence state on the broker (and replicates it), so it survives a reconnect - but not a restart of your application, which gets a new identity unless you use transactions.
