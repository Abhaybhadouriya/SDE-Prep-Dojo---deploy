Real numbers and real teams

One of LinkedIn's busiest clusters is described in the Kafka docs (Java version page) as a reference point. Keep it as a mental picture of "big but normal":

60brokers
50kpartitions (RF 2)
800kmsgs/sec in
300 MB/sinbound
1 GB/s+outbound
~21 msp90 GC pause
  • Outbound is over 3x inbound - exactly the replication-plus-readers effect from the sizing section.
  • Slack wrote about "self-driving" Kafka clusters built from open-source parts such as Cruise Control, so humans stop doing partition moves by hand.
  • PhonePe (about 100 billion events a day in 2023) separates Kafka operations from development teams, segregates clusters, and mirrors each data center with MirrorMaker 2 for disaster recovery.
  • Pinterest ran 50+ clusters and 2,500+ brokers in 2020, then moved some large pipelines to MemQ (micro-batches on S3) to save cost, accepting higher latency. The ops cost of brokers with local disks is a real business number.
  • Walmart (25,000+ consumers) found rebalances caused lag and that more partitions cost file handles, memory and threads, and built a messaging proxy in front of consumers. See Kafka vs others for how share groups now cover part of that need.
