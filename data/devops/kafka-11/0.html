The story: running the supermarket

Explain like I'm 5

Imagine a big supermarket. Building it was the easy part. Now you have to run it every single day.

How many checkout lanes do you open? If you open too few, the lines get long. If you open too many, you pay lots of cashiers who stand around doing nothing. So you count: "On a busy Saturday 600 people check out per hour, and one cashier can do 40 per hour, so I need 15 lanes. Let me open 20 so there is room to grow."

You also watch a few signs on the wall: How long is the longest line? Is any lane closed by accident? Is any cashier missing? You do not stare at everything - just the few signs that tell you trouble is coming.

And sometimes you fix things while the shop stays open: you swap one cashier at a time, so customers never see the shop close.

Running Kafka is the same job. The checkout lanes are partitions (the ordered logs a topic is split into, see Core concepts). The signs on the wall are metrics such as consumer lag and under-replicated partitions. Swapping one cashier at a time is a rolling restart: you restart one broker (one Kafka server) at a time so the cluster never goes down.

People call this work "day-2 operations". Day 1 is installing the system. Day 2 is every day after that: sizing, tuning, watching, upgrading, fixing. Interviewers at SDE-2 level love day-2 questions because they show you have thought about a system after the demo works.

From your world

You already do capacity planning: "this line card does N Mpps, the uplink is 100G, the buffer is X MB, so at this load the queue fills in Y ms." Kafka sizing is the same arithmetic with new units: MB/s per partition, consumers per group, days of retention. Metrics are your interface counters and SNMP traps. A rolling restart is an in-service software upgrade (ISSU) done card by card. Where this breaks: in a router the hardware is fixed and you size buffers once; in Kafka you can add brokers on demand, but moving data onto them is slow and costs network, so mistakes in partition count are expensive to undo.

What to read first: this page assumes you know partitions and offsets (kafka-02), producers and batching (kafka-03), consumer groups and lag (kafka-04), and replication with ISR (kafka-05). For Kubernetes basics see StatefulSets, CRDs & Operators.
