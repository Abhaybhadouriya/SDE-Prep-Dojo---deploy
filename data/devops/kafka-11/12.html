Quick quiz

Target 60 MB/s. One partition takes 20 MB/s from producers, but one consumer only processes 4 MB/s. What is the minimum partition count?

  1. 3
  2. 4
  3. 15
  4. 60
max(60/20, 60/4) = max(3, 15) = 15. The slower side - the consumer - decides. You would then add growth headroom.

You increase a keyed topic from 6 to 8 partitions. What is the main risk?

  1. Existing data is deleted
  2. Keys map to new partitions, so per-key ordering can break for a while
  3. The topic becomes read-only
Old data stays where it is, but hash(key) % 8 differs from % 6, so new events for a key may land on a different partition than its older events.

Which metric means "some partitions have no leader right now"?

  1. OfflinePartitionsCount > 0
  2. UnderReplicatedPartitions > 0
  3. BytesInPerSec dropping
Offline partitions have no leader, so nobody can read or write them - an outage. Under-replicated means a follower is behind but the partition is still served.

Lag is 120,000 messages and the group consumes 4,000 msg/s. The SLA is "within 1 minute". Is this an incident?

  1. No, lag under a million is always fine
  2. Yes, any lag above zero is bad
  3. Yes: 120,000 / 4,000 = 30 s now, but only if it is growing will it break the SLA soon - check the trend
Turn lag into time (30 s). It is inside the 1-minute SLA today; what matters is whether it is growing. A steady 30 s is fine, a steady climb is an incident in the making.

You add 3 brokers to a 6-broker cluster. What happens to existing partitions?

  1. Kafka spreads them evenly at once
  2. Nothing, until you run a reassignment (or Cruise Control)
  3. They are copied to all 9 brokers
New brokers only get new partitions or explicitly reassigned ones. Use kafka-reassign-partitions.sh with a throttle, or Cruise Control.

A client outside Kubernetes connects to a bootstrap load balancer, then every produce times out. Most likely cause?

  1. Brokers advertise internal addresses the client cannot reach
  2. The topic has too many partitions
  3. Compression is off
Clients connect to the partition leader's advertised address. Strimzi's external listeners (loadbalancer, nodeport, ingress) give each broker an address reachable from outside.

Ingress is 10 MB/s, RF is 3 and there are 3 consumer groups. Roughly how much data do leaders send out per second?

  1. 10 MB/s
  2. 30 MB/s
  3. 50 MB/s
2 followers x 10 = 20 MB/s of replication plus 3 groups x 10 = 30 MB/s of reads: about 50 MB/s out for 10 MB/s in.
