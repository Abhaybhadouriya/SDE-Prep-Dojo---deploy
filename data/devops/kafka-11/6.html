Day-2 operations: changing a live cluster

Kafka clusters are upgraded, patched and resized while traffic flows. The core skill is the rolling restart: one broker at a time, and never start the next one until the cluster is healthy again. Click through.

  1. Check health first. Every partition is fully replicated (URP = 0) and the controller quorum is healthy. If not, stop: restarting now could take a partition below its minimum ISR.
  2. Controlled shutdown. On SIGTERM, broker 2 tells the controller it is leaving. The controller moves leadership of P1 to an in-sync follower before the process exits (controlled.shutdown.enable=true is the default).
  3. Restart. Broker 2 is down for a short time (new config, new version, OS patch). Its partitions are under-replicated but still served. Wait.
  4. Catch up. Broker 2 comes back and fetches from the leaders everything it missed, then rejoins the ISR.
  5. Back to normal. URP returns to 0. With auto.leader.rebalance.enable=true (the default), leadership returns to the "preferred" replica. Only now move on to broker 3.

# 1) Under-replicated partitions right now (should print nothing on a healthy cluster)
kafka-topics.sh --bootstrap-server localhost:9092 --describe --under-replicated-partitions

# 2) Health of the KRaft controller quorum
kafka-metadata-quorum.sh --bootstrap-server localhost:9092 describe --status

# 3) Throttle a noisy producer to 10 MB/s
kafka-configs.sh --bootstrap-server localhost:9092 --alter \
  --entity-type clients --entity-name clickstream-ingest \
  --add-config 'producer_byte_rate=10485760'

# 4) Shorten retention on a topic that is eating the disk (24 hours)
kafka-configs.sh --bootstrap-server localhost:9092 --alter \
  --entity-type topics --entity-name clickstream \
  --add-config retention.ms=86400000

# 5) Replay: move a stopped group back to a point in time
kafka-consumer-groups.sh --bootstrap-server localhost:9092 --group payment-service \
  --topic orders --reset-offsets --to-datetime 2026-11-22T00:00:00.000 --execute

CurrentVoters are the controllers (the Raft quorum); CurrentObservers are brokers that follow the metadata log. A growing MaxFollowerLag means a controller or broker is falling behind on metadata.

Host basics still matter. The Kafka ops docs suggest allowing at least 100,000 open file descriptors for the broker process as a starting point, because every segment file and connection uses one. Other common advice (not hard rules): avoid swapping (a very low vm.swappiness), put the log directories on their own disks, and use a modern filesystem such as XFS or ext4.
