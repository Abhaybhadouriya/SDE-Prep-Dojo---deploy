Why not just a LoadBalancer Service per app?

Must know (SDE-1)

You can expose every Service with type: LoadBalancer. It works, but:

  • Each one creates a separate cloud load balancer with its own public IP and bill. Thirty microservices means thirty load balancers.
  • It is L4 only: it cannot route by path or host name, cannot add headers, cannot redirect HTTP to HTTPS.
  • Every Service must handle its own TLS certificates.

So teams put one L7 proxy at the edge (itself exposed by one LoadBalancer Service) and describe routing rules as Kubernetes objects. The proxy reads the rules and routes.

From your world

The cloud LB here is an L4 load balancer: 5-tuple hashing and NAT, like an ECMP + NAT box. The L7 proxy is a full TCP terminator: the client's TCP session ends at the proxy, which opens a fresh session to the backend - think of a TCP split proxy or a WAN optimiser in the path. That is why it can read and rewrite HTTP, and also why it adds a hop and must be scaled and made highly available like any other data-plane box. Where it breaks: the routing table is not built by a protocol, it is compiled from Kubernetes objects by a controller.
