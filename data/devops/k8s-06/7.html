Service mesh in one picture

Good to know (SDE-2)

"Sidecar" and "service mesh" come up in almost every platform interview. You met sidecars on the Pods page: a helper container that lives in the same pod. A service mesh (Istio, Linkerd) puts a proxy sidecar, usually Envoy, into every pod and quietly steers all of the pod's traffic through it. The app still thinks it calls http://pay:8080 directly.

Explain like I'm 5

Every kid in class gets a personal helper who carries their notes. The kid hands a note to their helper, the helper locks it in a box, walks it to the other kid's helper, who unlocks it and hands it over. The kids never change how they write notes, but now every note is locked, counted, and re-sent if it gets lost. The head teacher (the mesh control plane) gives all helpers the same rule book.

What you get without changing app code: mutual TLS between every pair of services (encryption plus identity), per-call metrics and traces (success rate and latency for every service-to-service edge), and traffic rules such as retries, timeouts, circuit breaking and canary splits for internal calls.

From your world

A sidecar proxy is a bump-in-the-wire middlebox per host: like putting an inline IPsec or firewall appliance in front of every server, with policy-based routing steering traffic into it. The mesh control plane is the central policy manager that pushes the same rules and keys to every box. Where it breaks: you are running one extra proxy per pod, not per rack, so the CPU and memory cost multiplies with pod count, and every call takes two extra proxy hops. Newer designs (Istio ambient mode, Cilium's mesh) move the proxy to a per-node agent and remove the sidecar for most traffic.

When a mesh pays off
  • Compliance asks for encryption and identity on every internal hop
  • Dozens of services, many languages: one place for retries and timeouts
  • You need per-edge golden metrics without touching each app
  • Fine-grained canaries for internal services
When to skip it
  • A handful of services: the operational load is bigger than the benefit
  • Tight latency budgets: each call adds two proxy hops (typically around a millisecond or less, but it adds up across a chain)
  • Memory-tight clusters: a sidecar per pod costs real RAM and CPU
  • The team cannot yet debug the plain Service path; a mesh adds another layer to debug

Interviewers love this: "Gateway = one front door at the edge for north-south traffic; mesh = a proxy beside every pod for east-west traffic. A mesh gives mTLS, retries and per-call telemetry without app changes, at the cost of a proxy per pod and two extra hops per call. I would start with a Gateway and NetworkPolicies, and add a mesh, or a sidecar-less one, when mTLS everywhere or per-edge telemetry is a real requirement."
