"A pod is the smallest unit Kubernetes schedules: one or more containers that always run together on one node. In Linux terms they share a network namespace, which a tiny pause container holds open, so they have one IP and talk over localhost; they can also share volumes, while each keeps its own filesystem and cgroup limits. Pods are disposable: if one is lost, a controller creates a new one with a new IP, which is why clients use Services instead of pod IPs. The kubelet manages the lifecycle: it restarts crashed containers with exponential back-off, which is what CrashLoopBackOff means, and it runs three probes: startup for slow boots, liveness to restart a wedged process, and readiness to take a pod out of load balancing without restarting it. On deletion the pod is removed from endpoints while the kubelet runs preStop, sends SIGTERM, and after the grace period, 30 seconds by default, SIGKILL. In production I never create bare pods; I use Deployments or StatefulSets."
