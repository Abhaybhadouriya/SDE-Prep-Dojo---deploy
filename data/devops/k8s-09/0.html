The story: seats in the classroom, counters in the shop

Explain like I'm 5

A new student arrives at school. The teacher has to choose a classroom. First she crosses out the rooms that do not work: "this room is full", "this room is the chemistry lab and you have no lab coat", "you asked to sit near your best friend, so not this one". Then, from the rooms that are left, she picks the best one - maybe the one with the most empty chairs. Then she writes the student's name in the register. That is all she does. Walking to the room is the student's job.

Now think of a supermarket. When the queue gets long, the manager opens more checkout counters. When the shop itself is too small for more counters, the owner builds a new aisle. When the shop is quiet at night, counters close and the owner even shuts an aisle to save electricity.

The teacher is kube-scheduler: it picks a node (a machine) for each new pod and writes the choice down in the API server. It never starts anything - the kubelet on the chosen node does that. Opening more counters is the Horizontal Pod Autoscaler (HPA): more copies of a pod. Building a new aisle is the Cluster Autoscaler or Karpenter: more nodes. Giving one counter a faster till is the Vertical Pod Autoscaler (VPA): a bigger pod.

This page is where Kubernetes meets the kernel you already know. "How much CPU may this pod use?" ends up as numbers in cgroup files. "Why was my pod killed?" ends in the kernel OOM killer. Good news: you will find this page easier than most web developers do.
