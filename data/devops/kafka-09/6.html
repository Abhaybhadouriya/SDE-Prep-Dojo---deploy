Batch vs streaming. A nightly job is fine when nobody acts on the result before tomorrow: monthly invoices, finance reports, training an ML model, back-filling a warehouse. Streaming is worth its complexity when value drops quickly with time: fraud checks during payment, live dashboards for a sale, inventory counts, alerts, feeds. A useful interview line: "Streaming is a choice about latency, not about size. If a 24-hour delay is acceptable, batch is simpler and cheaper." Many real systems do both: the stream for fresh numbers, a batch job to correct them later from the lake.

Kafka Streams strengths
  • No extra cluster; scales with your pods
  • Exactly-once inside Kafka with one config
  • Interactive queries: serve state from the app itself
Kafka Streams limits
  • Java/Kotlin/Scala only
  • Kafka in, Kafka out
  • Very large state means slow restores and many internal topics

Real systems that use these

  • Razorpay (AWS Big Data Blog): transactional databases, then Debezium CDC, then a Kafka Streams "Harvester", then Amazon MSK, feeding real-time anomaly detection.
  • Shopify: Debezium on Kafka Connect capturing changes from more than 100 MySQL shards of its monolith.
  • Netflix Keystone: a Kafka-fronted pipeline with Flink as a service; the InfoQ summary (2018) traces its growth from tens of billions to trillions of events per day.
