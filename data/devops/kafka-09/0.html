The story: a toy factory with a conveyor belt

Explain like I'm 5

Picture a toy factory. In the middle there is a long conveyor belt. Boxes ride on it all day. That belt is Kafka.

At one end there is a loading dock. Trucks from other places (a warehouse, a shop) arrive, and dock workers lift their boxes onto the belt. At the other end there is another dock where workers lift boxes off the belt and load them into trucks going out. Those dock workers are Kafka Connect. They do not open the boxes. They just move boxes on and off.

Along the belt sit craft workers. Each one picks up a box, does something to it (paints it, counts it, puts two boxes together) and puts the result back on the belt. Some of them keep a little notebook, like "I have seen 17 red cars today". Those craft workers are stream processors. A small team that lives inside your own workshop is Kafka Streams. A big separate workshop next door, with its own manager, is Flink.

And there is one very clever dock worker who watches the shop's diary. Every time the shopkeeper writes "sold one teddy" in the diary, this worker copies that line onto the belt. Nobody has to remember to tell him. That clever worker is Debezium.

Let us turn the story into real names. Kafka itself (the brokers) only stores and serves streams of records. To be useful, data has to get into Kafka, be changed while it flows, and get out to databases, search indexes, caches and data lakes. Three kinds of tools do this:

  • Kafka Connect moves data between Kafka and other systems (in and out), using ready-made plug-ins called connectors.
  • Debezium is a family of Connect source connectors that turn every database change into a Kafka record. This is called CDC (change data capture).
  • Stream processors read records, compute something, and write new records. Kafka Streams is a Java library you put inside your own service. ksqlDB lets you write that logic in SQL. Apache Flink is a separate processing cluster that is strong at big stateful jobs.

Your question answered: "Are Connect, Streams and Flink part of Kafka or separate things?" Kafka Connect and Kafka Streams are part of the Apache Kafka project: they ship in the same download and have the same Apache 2.0 licence. But neither runs inside the brokers. Connect runs as its own worker processes; Streams runs inside your application. Debezium is a separate open-source project (Apache 2.0) that plugs into Connect. Flink is a completely separate Apache project with its own cluster. ksqlDB is made by Confluent under the Confluent Community License, which is not Apache 2.0.

From your world

Think of a router. The forwarding fabric (Kafka brokers) moves packets and does not care what is inside them. Line-card interfaces (Connect connectors) adapt the fabric to each outside medium: Ethernet, SONET, serial. Service cards that do NAT, DPI or firewalling on the traffic as it passes (stream processors) are where the actual per-flow logic and per-flow state tables live.

Where the analogy breaks: a service card works on packets in microseconds and throws them away. A stream processor writes its results back to Kafka as new durable records, so the "output traffic" can be replayed later.
