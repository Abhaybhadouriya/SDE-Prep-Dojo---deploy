The story: the toy-shop sorting table

Explain like I'm 5

Picture a toy shop with a long moving belt of order cards. A helper sits at the belt. For every card she looks up the price of each toy, writes the total on the card, and puts it in the "ready" basket.

Sometimes the price book is with someone else for a minute. She does not sit and wait while all the other cards pile up behind her. She puts that card on a "try again later" shelf with a sticky note: "try at 10:05". A second helper watches the shelf and puts the card back on the belt when the time comes.

Sometimes a card is just broken: scribbles, or a toy that does not exist. Trying again will never fix it. She puts it in a red "needs a grown-up" box with a note saying what was wrong, and keeps going.

At the end of the belt, a third helper copies ready cards into the big shop book. If he is not sure he copied a card, he copies it again. That is fine, because the book has one line per order number, so copying twice just writes the same line twice. And a fourth helper counts how much money came in each minute, by the time written on the card, not by when the card reached him.

That is this whole page. The belt is a Kafka topic. The first helper is the enricher, a stream processor (a program that reads events forever, one batch after another, and writes new events). The "try again later" shelf is a retry topic. The red box is a dead-letter queue or DLQ (a topic where records that cannot be processed are parked for a human). The book copier is an idempotent sink (a writer where doing the same write twice gives the same result as doing it once). The money counter is a windowed aggregation.

From your world

This is a packet pipeline with exception paths. The fast path forwards good packets. Packets the hardware cannot handle right now get punted to a slow path with a rate limit and a retry; packets that are malformed go to a drop queue with a reason counter. You never let one weird packet stall the whole RX ring. Where it breaks: a dropped packet is gone, but a DLQ record is kept (14 days in ShopStream) so a person can fix the cause and replay it.
