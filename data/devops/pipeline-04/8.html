Disruptions: PDBs, drains and rolling restarts

A PodDisruptionBudget (PDB) limits how many pods of an app a voluntary disruption may take at once. Voluntary means someone asked: kubectl drain before a node upgrade, a cluster autoscaler removing a node. It does not stop crashes or a node losing power.

# k8s/31-enricher.yaml (PDB part)
apiVersion: policy/v1
kind: PodDisruptionBudget
metadata:
  name: enricher
  namespace: shopstream
spec:
  # maxUnavailable (not minAvailable): with 1 replica, minAvailable: 1 would block node drains forever.
  # This still stops a drain from evicting several consumers at once (= one rebalance storm).
  maxUnavailable: 1
  selector:
    matchLabels: {app: enricher}

That comment is a real production lesson. The order-api and postgres-sink have at least 2 replicas, so minAvailable: 1 is safe for them. The enricher can scale down to 1, and a PDB that says "keep 1 available" for a 1-replica app means "never evict", so the node drain hangs forever and the platform team's upgrade is stuck.

Kafka brokers get their own PDB from Strimzi, and Strimzi's rolling-restart logic waits for each broker's partitions to be back in sync before moving on. Together with min.insync.replicas: 2 and three zones, a drain of one node at a time never stops writes.

Rebalances during deploys: every pod replacement makes the consumer group rebalance. The shared config supports static membership: set GROUP_INSTANCE_ID to a stable name and a quick restart of the same member does not trigger a rebalance until session.timeout.ms (45 s) passes. That needs stable pod names, which a StatefulSet gives you and a Deployment does not; the project leaves it off for Deployments.
