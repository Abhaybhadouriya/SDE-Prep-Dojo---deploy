Animated packet walk: pod A to pod B on another node

Must know (SDE-1)

Now put everything together. Pod A (10.244.1.20, on node-1, Flannel VXLAN, kube-proxy in iptables mode) runs curl http://backend. Click Play and watch the headers change at each hop.

  1. DNS. curl asks the resolver in /etc/resolv.conf (nameserver 10.96.0.10, the kube-dns Service in front of CoreDNS pods). CoreDNS watches Services through the API and answers with the ClusterIP.
  2. Leave the pod. The SYN goes out of pod A's eth0, through its veth pair, and into the node's root network namespace. The destination is the virtual IP.
  3. DNAT. In the node's netfilter nat table, the rules kube-proxy installed match 10.96.45.10:80 and rewrite the destination to one ready endpoint (here 10.244.2.9:8080). conntrack records the translation.
  4. Route and encapsulate. The node's routing table sends 10.244.2.0/24 to flannel.1. The kernel's VXLAN driver wraps the packet in UDP 8472 addressed node-1 to node-2.
  5. Cross the underlay and decapsulate. The physical or VPC network routes a normal UDP packet between node IPs. node-2's VXLAN device strips the outer headers.
  6. Deliver. node-2 routes the inner packet to its cni0 bridge, through pod B's veth, into pod B. Pod B sees pod A's real IP.
  7. Reply. The reply travels back through the tunnel. On node-1, conntrack un-does the DNAT, so the source becomes 10.96.45.10:80 again. Later packets of this connection skip the rule walk entirely.

Interviewers love this: being able to say which component wrote which state. CoreDNS answered from Service objects; the API server allocated the ClusterIP; the EndpointSlice controller listed the ready pod IPs; kube-proxy turned those into netfilter rules; the CNI plugin created the veth and routes; the kernel did the DNAT, the routing and the VXLAN. Nothing in the data path is a "Kubernetes process" - it is all your kernel.
