The story: the school with badges

Explain like I'm 5

Think of a big school. At the front door, a guard asks "who are you?" and looks at your badge. That is checking your name.

Next, the guard looks at a list: "Teachers can open classrooms. Cooks can open the kitchen. Kids can open only the playground." Your badge says you are a cook, so you may enter the kitchen and nothing else. That is checking what you are allowed to do.

Then a second guard checks your bag: "No scissors, no fireworks." Even a teacher cannot bring fireworks in. That is checking what you are carrying.

Inside, the classrooms have walls and doors, so a noisy class cannot disturb another one. And the secret exam papers are locked in a safe, not left on a desk.

Kubernetes security is exactly this: badges (authentication), the list of who may do what (RBAC), the bag check (admission control and Pod Security), walls between rooms (namespaces and NetworkPolicy) and the safe (Secrets done properly).

Three words to learn now:

  • Authentication (authn): proving who you are. "I am alice, here is my certificate."
  • Authorization (authz): deciding what you may do. "alice may read pods in namespace shop."
  • RBAC (Role-Based Access Control): the authorization system Kubernetes uses. You write roles (lists of allowed actions) and bind them to people or programs.

From your world

You already know AAA from network devices: TACACS+ or RADIUS for authentication, command authorization ("privilege level 15 may run configure terminal, level 1 may only run show") for authorization, and accounting logs for audit. Kubernetes has the same three, built into the API server. Where this breaks: on a router the subjects are mostly humans on a CLI. In Kubernetes most callers are programs (controllers, your pods, CI pipelines), and each of them needs an identity and a minimal set of rights.
