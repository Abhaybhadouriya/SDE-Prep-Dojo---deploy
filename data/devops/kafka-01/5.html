Fan-out: one event, many readers

The third superpower is fan-out: one message, many independent readers. There are two classic ways to deliver messages, and Kafka does both:

  • Point-to-point (a work queue): each message goes to one worker out of a pool. Ten workers share the work, like ten chefs taking tickets off one rail.
  • Publish-subscribe (pub/sub): each message goes to every subscriber. The email team, the analytics team and the fraud team all get every order.

Kafka combines them with one idea, the consumer group: a named team of consumers. Inside a group, the work is shared (point-to-point). Between groups, every group gets every message (pub/sub). You will meet groups properly on page 04; for now, just look at the shape.

From your world

Pub/sub is IP multicast: one sender, a group address, many receivers, and the sender does not know who joined. Where this breaks, and why it matters: multicast is fire-and-forget, so a slow or rebooting receiver just misses packets. In Kafka the "packet" is stored, and each group keeps its own read position, so a slow group falls behind instead of losing data, and a new group can even start from the beginning. It is multicast with a replay buffer that lasts for days.
