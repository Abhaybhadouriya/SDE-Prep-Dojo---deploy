How real companies use this

  • LinkedIn built Kafka to replace a tangle of point-to-point pipelines for activity tracking, metrics and logs. By 2019 it reported over 100 clusters and about 7 trillion messages a day (LinkedIn Engineering).
  • Slack put Kafka in front of its Redis job queue as a durable shock absorber after a memory-exhaustion outage (Slack Engineering).
  • Uber uses retry topics and dead-letter topics so that one bad message does not block the rest, and later built a consumer proxy so services in many languages can consume without each team mastering Kafka (Uber Engineering: reliable reprocessing).
  • Walmart described processing trillions of Kafka messages a day with more than 25,000 consumers, and building a messaging proxy to stop consumer rebalances from causing lag (ByteByteGo summary of the Walmart post).
  • PhonePe reported handling about 100 billion events a day on Kafka in 2023, with one cluster per data centre mirrored for disaster recovery (PhonePe Tech).

You will see these patterns inside full designs on this site: the notification system, e-commerce checkout, flash sale and log aggregation designs all put a log in the middle for exactly the reasons on this page.
