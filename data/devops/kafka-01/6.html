Queue vs log: is Kafka a queue?

This is the question that confuses almost everyone at first, so let us answer it directly. Kafka is not a classic queue. It is a log. The difference is what happens after a message is read.

  1. Both start with A, B, C. In the queue, messages wait for a worker. In the log, messages have numbered positions called offsets, and each reader has its own pointer.
  2. One message is processed. The queue deletes A once a worker acknowledges it. In the log, email just moves its pointer to 1; A is still there, and analytics has not even started.
  3. Everything is processed. The queue is empty and the history is gone. The log still holds A, B, C; each reader is simply at a different position.
  4. A new reader arrives. With a queue, a new team only sees future messages. With a log, it can start at offset 0 and replay the past. You can also rewind an old reader to fix a bug and reprocess.

Then is Kafka just a database?

Also no, though it shares one property with databases: it stores data durably on disk. The difference is how you ask for data. A database lets you ask "give me order o-981" or "all orders over 500 rupees" - it has indexes for random lookups and it lets you update rows in place. Kafka only lets you say "give me records from offset N onwards in partition P". You cannot update a record, and you cannot search by content. Kafka is the stream of changes; a database is the current state. They work together: consumers read the stream and build databases, caches and search indexes from it. (Kafka's own docs call this the stream-table duality, and page 09 shows it.)
