The story: food trucks, a caterer and a smart parking lot

Explain like I'm 5

You want to sell sandwiches at a big fair. You write a recipe card: what bread, what filling, how big the box is. Then you tell a helper: "always keep three sandwich stalls open; if one closes, open another one".

Where do the stalls stand? Option one: you rent your own trucks, park them, fill them with fuel, fix their tyres, and put stalls inside them. Option two: you hire a caterer who brings a tiny stall exactly the size of your recipe whenever you ask, and takes it away when you are done. You never see a truck.

Now imagine a smart parking lot for the trucks. When people queue and no stall has room, the lot manager orders exactly the right size of truck in a minute - a cheap second-hand one if you said that is fine, a new one for the stall that must never close. At night, when most stalls are empty, he squeezes the remaining stalls into fewer trucks and sends the extra trucks home.

The recipe card is an ECS task definition (or a Kubernetes pod spec). The helper keeping three stalls open is an ECS service with a desired count of 3 (or a Kubernetes Deployment). Renting your own trucks is the EC2 launch type or EKS node groups. The caterer is AWS Fargate. The smart parking lot manager is Karpenter, with its rules written in NodePools; cheap second-hand trucks are Spot instances, and squeezing stalls together at night is consolidation.

This page assumes you know containers (Running Containers) and Kubernetes scheduling (Scheduling & Autoscaling), and a little AWS networking (VPC, subnets, route tables) and EC2 (EC2, Auto Scaling Groups, Security Groups).
