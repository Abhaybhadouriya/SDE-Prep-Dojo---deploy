Why "just send JSON" eventually breaks

Many teams start with JSON. It is readable and needs no tools. Then one day the orders team renames amount to total and ships on a Friday evening. The payment service, the analytics job and the search indexer all read amount, get nothing, and either crash or quietly write zeros. Kafka is happy the whole time, because to Kafka it is all just bytes.

A schema fixes this by writing the contract down: field names, types, which fields are optional, and default values. A registry makes the contract shared and versioned. A compatibility rule makes the contract enforceable: a producer that tries to register an incompatible change is refused before any bad message reaches the topic.

How small is binary, really? One record, counted by hand

Take the record {"id":42,"name":"Asha","amount":499}. As compact JSON it is 36 bytes, because every message repeats the field names and writes numbers as text. From your world

Protobuf is basically TLV (type-length-value) encoding, the same idea as BGP path attributes, IS-IS TLVs or LLDP. A receiver that sees an unknown tag can skip it using the length, which is exactly why "add a new field with a new tag" is safe. Avro is more like a fixed C struct sent with #pragma pack: tiny on the wire, but the reader must know the exact layout the writer used. That is why Avro needs a registry and Protobuf merely benefits from one.
