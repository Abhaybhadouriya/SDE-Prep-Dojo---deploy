Paging alerts vs ticket alerts

Must know (SDE-1)

Every alert costs attention. A page interrupts sleep; too many pages and people stop reading them (alert fatigue), which is how the one real page gets missed. The SRE book's rule: every page must be urgent, actionable and user-visible (or about to be). If a human cannot do something useful right now, it belongs in a ticket or on a dashboard.

SignalPage or ticket?Why
Checkout error budget burning 14x over 1 h and 5 min windowsPageSymptom users feel; fast budget loss
p99 latency of login above SLO for 30 minPageUser-visible, actionable (roll back, scale)
Disk 80% full, filling 1%/dayTicketWeeks of runway; fix in working hours
Disk 95% full, full in 2 hoursPageWill become user-visible very soon
One of 30 pods restartedDashboardKubernetes already healed it; no user impact

TLS certificate expires in 14 daysTicketNeeds action, but not tonight (and should be automated)

Notice the theme: page on symptoms, not causes. "Users get errors" is a symptom; "CPU is high" is a possible cause. Symptom alerts built on SLOs and burn rates (covered in Reliability at Scale) catch every cause, including ones you never imagined, and stay quiet when a cause has no user impact.

# prometheus rule: page on fast error-budget burn (99.9% SLO), ticket on slow burn
groups:
  - name: checkout-slo
    rules:
      - alert: CheckoutErrorBudgetFastBurn
        expr: |
          (sum(rate(http_requests_total{service="checkout",code=~"5.."}[1h]))
             / sum(rate(http_requests_total{service="checkout"}[1h]))) > (14.4 * 0.001)
          and
          (sum(rate(http_requests_total{service="checkout",code=~"5.."}[5m]))
             / sum(rate(http_requests_total{service="checkout"}[5m]))) > (14.4 * 0.001)
        labels:
          severity: page
        annotations:
          summary: "Checkout is burning its 30-day error budget 14x too fast"
          runbook_url: "https://runbooks.example.internal/checkout/high-error-rate"
      - alert: CheckoutErrorBudgetSlowBurn
        expr: |
          (sum(rate(http_requests_total{service="checkout",code=~"5.."}[3d]))
             / sum(rate(http_requests_total{service="checkout"}[3d]))) > (1 * 0.001)
        labels:
          severity: ticket

From your world

Think of syslog levels. You page on 0 emerg to 2 crit (link down on a core uplink with traffic loss), you ticket 3 err to 4 warning (a fan running hot), and you only log 5 notice and below. An interface flap that BFD and ECMP already healed is a dashboard item, exactly like a Kubernetes pod restart. And alert inhibition (do not page for every service when the region is down) is SNMP trap correlation: one "chassis down" trap suppresses the 48 "port down" traps behind it.
