Blameless postmortems: learning without blame

Must know (SDE-1)

A postmortem is a written record of an incident: what happened, the impact, how it was mitigated, why it happened, and what will change so it does not happen again (or hurts less). Blameless means it looks for systemic contributing causes and assumes everyone acted in good faith with the information they had. It does not mean "nobody is accountable"; it means the system, not a person, is what we fix.

Why blameless? Because blame makes people hide mistakes. If the engineer who ran the bad command fears punishment, they will not say "I ran this, and here is why it looked safe", and that sentence is exactly the data you need. "Human error" is never a root cause; it is a starting question: why did the system let a normal human action cause an outage?

When to write one

Agree triggers before incidents happen. The SRE book's list is a good default:

  • User-visible downtime or degradation beyond a threshold
  • Any data loss
  • On-call had to intervene (a rollback, rerouting traffic)
  • Resolution time above a threshold
  • A monitoring failure: humans found it before alerts did
  • And any stakeholder may ask for one

The template

SectionWhat goes in it
SummaryTwo or three sentences a VP can read
ImpactWho was affected, how much, how long: "8% of checkouts failed for 41 min; ~3,100 orders; error budget spent: 60% of the month"
TimelineTimestamped facts (UTC): change, first error, alert, ack, decisions, mitigation, all-clear
Root cause and contributing factorsThe technical trigger plus every condition that let it become an outage
DetectionHow we found out; how we could have found out sooner
ResolutionWhat stopped the pain, and what fixed it properly
What went well / went wrong / where we got lucky"Lucky" is the most honest and most useful heading
Action itemsEach with an owner, a priority, a ticket and a due date

From timeline to causes: 5 whys and contributing factors

The 5 whys technique keeps asking "why?" until you reach something the organisation can change. Worked example for the checkout outage:

  1. Why did checkouts fail? checkout-api v412 returned 503 for requests with saved cards.
  2. Why? A new config key for the card vault client was missing in ap-south-1, so the client fell back to a 50 ms timeout.
  3. Why was it missing? Region config is maintained by hand in three files; the PR updated two.
  4. Why did the canary not catch it? The canary ran in us-east-1 only, and synthetic checks do not use saved cards.
  5. Why did detection take 9 minutes? The alert used a 10-minute window instead of a multi-window burn rate.

Notice that "why" branched: real outages rarely have one root cause. The Swiss cheese model describes it well: each defence layer has holes, and an outage happens when the holes line up.

  • Every action item has one owner and a ticket. "The team will be more careful" is not an action item. "Be more careful" fixes people; good items fix systems.
  • Mix the types: prevent (stop the trigger), detect (find it faster), mitigate (hurt less when it happens again).
  • Track completion. Many organisations review open postmortem items weekly; an unfinished P1 action item is a known, unfixed outage waiting to recur.
  • Review and share. The SRE book recommends senior review of every postmortem and sharing it as widely as is useful, in a searchable repository, without any information that identifies end users.

Blameless is not toothless. The postmortem can say "the deploy script let one engineer push to all regions at once without a second approval". That is a sharp finding about the system. What it does not say is "Ravi should have known better". If you catch yourself writing a name next to a mistake, rewrite the sentence around the missing guard rail.

From your world

A good kernel crash RCA already reads like this: "The NULL dereference in the driver's RX path happened because the ring was torn down while NAPI polling was still scheduled; the teardown function lacked a napi_disable(); the race was not covered by the stress test; the watchdog took 60 s to reboot". The fix list has a patch, a new test, and a faster watchdog: prevent, detect, mitigate. Nobody writes "the developer forgot". Postmortems just apply that discipline to the whole socio-technical system: deploy tools, alerts, runbooks and communication included.
