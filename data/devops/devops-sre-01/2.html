Escalation policies: what happens when nobody answers

Must know (SDE-1)

An escalation policy is the ordered list of who gets paged, and after how long without an acknowledgement ("ack"). An ack means "I have it, stop paging others". Without escalation, one sleeping engineer with a dead phone can turn a five-minute blip into an hour-long outage.

  1. A symptom-based alert fires, for example the payments error budget is burning 14 times faster than allowed.
  2. Tier 1: the primary on-call is notified through the app, then SMS, then a phone call.
  3. No acknowledgement within 5 minutes: tier 2, the secondary, is paged as well.
  4. Still nothing after another window: tier 3, the engineering manager or service owner.
  5. The first person to ack owns the response. They fix it, or declare an incident and pull in more people.

Tools such as PagerDuty, Opsgenie or Grafana OnCall hold the schedules and escalation tiers. The monitoring side decides which alerts go to the pager at all. Here is a real Prometheus Alertmanager routing config that sends only severity="page" alerts to PagerDuty and everything else to a ticket/chat channel:

# alertmanager.yml
route:
  receiver: team-tickets              # default: not urgent
  group_by: [alertname, service]
  group_wait: 30s                     # batch related alerts into one notification
  group_interval: 5m
  repeat_interval: 4h
  routes:
    - matchers:
        - severity="page"
      receiver: payments-pager
      repeat_interval: 30m
receivers:
  - name: payments-pager
    pagerduty_configs:
      - routing_key_file: /etc/alertmanager/secrets/pd-payments   # never inline secrets
        severity: critical
  - name: team-tickets
    slack_configs:
      - api_url_file: /etc/alertmanager/secrets/slack-payments
        channel: "#payments-alerts"
inhibit_rules:
  - source_matchers: [alertname="RegionDown"]       # if the whole region is down,
    target_matchers: [severity="page"]              # do not also page for every service in it
    equal: [region]

Escalate on purpose, too. Escalation is not only a timer. The on-call should feel free to escalate manually the moment they are out of their depth: "I need a database expert" is a strength, not a failure. Good teams write "when to escalate" into each runbook.
