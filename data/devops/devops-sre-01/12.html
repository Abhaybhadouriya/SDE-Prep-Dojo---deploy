Quiz

Which alert should page a human at 3 a.m.?

  1. One node's CPU is at 90%
  2. A TLS certificate expires in 14 days
  3. Checkout is burning its error budget 14 times faster than allowed
  4. One pod restarted and is healthy again
Pages are for urgent, actionable, user-visible symptoms. The others are dashboards or tickets.

What is the main job of the incident commander?

  1. Coordinate: hold the overall picture, assign roles, decide, keep comms flowing
  2. Type the fix into production
  3. Write the postmortem alone
  4. Find who caused the incident
The IC coordinates and decides. Hands-on changes belong to the ops lead's group.

A deploy went out 10 minutes before errors spiked. You are not sure it is the cause. What first?

  1. Read the diff line by line until you are sure
  2. Roll it back now, then investigate
  3. Wait for more data
  4. Open a ticket for next sprint
Mitigate first. A rollback is cheap and reversible; debugging while customers fail is not.

Which is a good postmortem action item?

  1. "Engineers will be more careful with config"
  2. "Ravi to attend training"
  3. "Improve monitoring"
  4. "CI fails if a config key is missing in any region - owner Priya, P1, due Dec 5"
Good items change the system, are specific, and have an owner, a priority and a date.

According to Google's SRE book, which is NOT a reason to declare an incident?

  1. A second team is needed to fix it
  2. The outage is visible to customers
  3. A single pod restarted once
  4. It is still unsolved after an hour of focused analysis
The three criteria are: another team needed, customer-visible, or unsolved after an hour. A healed pod restart is none of these.

Why are postmortems blameless?

  1. So nobody is ever accountable
  2. Because blame makes people hide mistakes, and their honest account is the key data
  3. Because incidents are always caused by machines
  4. To shorten the document
Blamelessness keeps information flowing. Accountability moves to systems and owned action items.
