Runbooks: the cards on the fire-station wall

Good to know (SDE-2)

A runbook (or playbook) is a short document for one alert or one failure type: what it means, how to check, how to mitigate, when to escalate. Its job is to make the response not depend on one specific engineer's memory, especially at 3 a.m. Link it from the alert itself (the runbook_url annotation above), so it is one tap away from the page.

# Runbook: CheckoutErrorBudgetFastBurn
Owner: payments-team   Last tested: 2026-10-30 (game day)   Severity hint: SEV-2 if > 5% errors

## What this means
Checkout 5xx rate is high enough to burn 2% of the monthly error budget per hour.
Users are failing to pay right now.

## Check (2 minutes)
1. Dashboard "Checkout / Golden signals": which region, which endpoint, since when?
2. Recent changes:  kubectl -n payments rollout history deploy/checkout-api
                    and the #deploys channel (feature flags, config pushes).
3. Dependencies: payment-gateway latency panel, DB connections panel.

## Mitigate
- Deploy in the last 60 min?  kubectl -n payments rollout undo deploy/checkout-api
- Gateway timeouts?           enable flag checkout.fallback_gateway (see flag console)
- One region only?            shift traffic: runbook "regional-failover" (needs IC approval)

## Escalate
- No improvement in 15 min, or data looks wrong (double charges): declare SEV-1,
  page payments-db on-call and the payments EM.

  • Keep them short and tested. A runbook nobody has run in a year is fiction. Exercise them in game days.
  • Prefer links and copy-pasteable commands over prose.
  • Automate the boring steps. If the runbook says "restart X" every time, the next step is to make the system do it, and the alert goes away. Runbooks are a path toward automation, not the end state.
