Severity levels: how big is this fire?

Must know (SDE-1)

A severity level is a shared label for impact, decided early, that sets how many people join, how often you communicate and who is told. Agreeing levels in advance stops arguments at 3 a.m. A common scheme (this one follows PagerDuty's public incident response guide):

LevelTypical meaningExampleResponse
SEV-1Critical: many customers affected, SLA breached, or customer data exposedCheckout down in all regions; data leakMajor incident: IC, comms, exec liaison, public status page
SEV-2Critical system issue severely impairing many customersPayments failing for one card network; one region downMajor incident: IC, comms, status page
SEV-3Stability or minor customer-impacting issue needing the owner nowSearch results slow for some usersOwning team handles; escalate if it spreads
SEV-4Minor: no effect on customers' ability to use the productA single node failed; a batch job is delayedTicket, working hours
SEV-5CosmeticTypo on a settings pageBacklog

When unsure, pick the higher severity. It is cheap to downgrade a SEV-2 to a SEV-3 after ten minutes. It is expensive to discover after an hour that a "SEV-3" was really losing orders, with no commander and no customer communication.

Google's SRE book gives a simple test for when to formally declare an incident: if any of these is true, declare it. Do you need a second team to fix it? Is the outage visible to customers? Is it still unsolved after an hour of focused analysis? Declaring early costs little; declaring late costs a lot.
