The story: the football coach

Explain like I'm 5

A football team must always have 11 players on the field. The coach does not play. The coach just keeps looking at the field and counting. If a player gets hurt and walks off, the coach sends a new player on. Nobody has to ask; the coach just keeps counting.

Now the team gets new shirts. The coach does not pull all 11 players off at once (the other team would score!). The coach swaps them one at a time: a new player in a new shirt runs on, and only then does one old player walk off. At every moment there are at least 11 players on the field. If the new shirts are itchy and players start falling over, the coach says "stop, put the old shirts back on".

That coach is a Deployment. You tell Kubernetes "I want 3 copies of my web app, running version 2". You never start or stop the copies yourself. A program inside Kubernetes keeps counting and fixing until the real world matches what you asked for. When you change the version, it swaps copies one by one so users never see an outage. When the new version is bad, one command puts the old one back.

On the Pods page you learned that a pod is one running copy of your app (one or more containers sharing an IP). Pods are like paper cups: cheap, and thrown away when broken, never repaired. So something must make new cups. That something is the Deployment and its helper, the ReplicaSet.
