Rolling updates: maxSurge and maxUnavailable

Must know (SDE-1)

A rolling update replaces old pods with new pods a few at a time. Two knobs control the speed and safety:

KnobPlain meaningDefaultRounding
maxSurgeHow many extra pods may exist above replicas during the update (costs capacity).25%rounded up
maxUnavailableHow many pods may be missing (not available) below replicas (costs safety).25%rounded down

Worked example with the defaults and replicas: 4: surge = 25% of 4 = 1, unavailable = 1. So during the update there can be at most 5 pods in total and at least 3 available. With replicas: 3: surge = ceil(0.75) = 1, unavailable = floor(0.75) = 0. Both cannot be 0 at the same time (it could never make progress), and the API rejects that.

The safest common setting for user-facing services is maxSurge: 1, maxUnavailable: 0: never drop below full capacity; add one new pod, wait until it is Ready, then remove one old pod. Watch it happen:

  1. Start. 3 pods of v1. You change the image. The Deployment controller creates a new ReplicaSet for v2 with 0 replicas.
  2. Surge one. New RS is scaled to 1. A v2 pod starts. Only after its readiness probe passes (plus minReadySeconds) does it count as available. Then the old RS is scaled from 3 to 2, so one v1 pod gets SIGTERM.
  3. Again. New RS 2, old RS 1. At no moment were fewer than 3 pods serving.
  4. Last swap. New RS 3, old RS 0.
  5. Done. kubectl rollout status prints "successfully rolled out". The old ReplicaSet still exists with 0 replicas; that is your rollback button.

From your world

A rolling update is ISSU (in-service software upgrade) done card by card: bring a new card into the chassis, check it is forwarding, then retire an old one. The readiness probe plays the role of "the card is up and its forwarding entries are programmed" before you shift traffic onto it. Where it breaks: here the "cards" are cheap and identical, so we create brand-new ones instead of upgrading in place.

The live view, while traffic keeps flowing to both versions for a short time. Note: during a rolling update old and new versions serve users at the same time. Your app and database schema must handle that (this is why database migrations use the "expand, then contract" pattern).

$ kubectl set image deployment/web web=nginx:1.28
deployment.apps/web image updated

$ kubectl rollout status deployment/web

Waiting for deployment "web" rollout to finish: 2 out of 3 new replicas have been updated...
Waiting for deployment "web" rollout to finish: 1 old replicas are pending termination...

Tip: CHANGE-CAUSE shows <none> unless you set the annotation kubernetes.io/change-cause on the Deployment (for example "bump nginx to 1.28"). In real teams the change history lives in git (GitOps), and that is the better source of truth.
