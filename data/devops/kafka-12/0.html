The story: three ways to pass notes in school

Explain like I'm 5

Your school has three ways to pass messages.

The class diary (Kafka). The teacher writes every announcement on the next empty line of a big diary. Nothing is ever torn out. Every kid has a bookmark. Ravi reads up to line 40, Meera only up to line 12, and a new kid can start reading from line 1. If you missed something yesterday, you just flip back.

The post room (RabbitMQ). A clever post-room helper looks at the label on each letter and drops it into the right pigeonhole: "Grade 1", "Sports club", "Everyone". When a kid takes a letter and says "got it", the letter is thrown away. The helper is great at sorting, but there is no diary to flip back through.

The magic drop box (SQS). You do not even know who runs it. You drop a note in, and later someone pulls one out. If they do not say "done" within 30 seconds, the note quietly pops back in for someone else. You never clean it or fix it. It just works, and you pay a tiny coin for every note.

That story is the whole page in miniature. Every messaging system is one of two shapes, or a mix of both:

  • A log (like the diary): messages are appended and kept for a time. Readers move their own bookmark, called an offset. Many independent readers can read the same data. You can replay (re-read old messages). Kafka, Pulsar, Kinesis, Azure Event Hubs, Redpanda and RabbitMQ Streams are logs.
  • A queue (like the post room and the drop box): messages wait until one worker takes and acknowledges ("acks") them, then they are deleted. It is about handing out work. RabbitMQ classic and quorum queues, Amazon SQS and Google Pub/Sub subscriptions behave like this.

Interviewers rarely want "Kafka is better". They want to see that you know which shape a problem needs and what each system costs you to run. If you keep the diary and the post room in your head, you will not get lost.

From your world

A queue is like a NIC receive ring where the driver consumes a descriptor and the slot is freed for reuse: one reader, and once read, it is gone. A log is like dmesg or a filesystem journal that many tools can read at their own index, except it lives on disk and old data is dropped in whole chunks (segments) by time or size, not overwritten in a circle. RabbitMQ's exchange is like a forwarding table lookup: it reads a label (the routing key) and picks the egress queue.

Where this breaks: a real ring buffer overwrites old slots when full. Kafka never overwrites and never blocks the writer for a slow reader; it simply deletes old segments when retention says so, even if a slow reader has not read them yet.
