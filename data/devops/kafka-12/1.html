The big split: a log vs a queue

Watch the two pictures below. On the left, a queue: each message goes to one worker, and after the worker acks it, it is gone. On the right, a log: messages stay in order, and two different teams (consumer groups) read the same messages at their own pace.

QuestionQueue (post room)Log (diary)
What happens after a message is read?Acked, then deletedKept until retention (time or size) removes old segments
Can a new team read old messages?No - they are goneYes - start from offset 0 or a timestamp
Who remembers progress?The broker, per message (acked or not)The consumer group, as one number per partition (the offset)
How do you add parallelism?Add workers to the same queue, as many as you likeAdd partitions; one partition goes to one consumer in a group
OrderingRoughly FIFO, broken by retries and many workersStrict within a partition
Natural useJobs, tasks, commands ("resize this image")Events, facts, history ("order 42 was placed")

You might confuse this with: "pub/sub". Pub/sub just means one message can reach many subscribers. A log does pub/sub by giving each consumer group its own offset. RabbitMQ does pub/sub by copying the message into several queues (a fanout exchange). SNS does it by pushing copies to many SQS queues. Same goal, different plumbing.

Commands vs events. A command says "please do X" and usually has one handler (a queue fits). An event says "X happened" and anyone may care, now or next year (a log fits). Saying this out loud in an interview is a strong signal. See Why Messaging? for the sync vs async basics.
