Kafka wins when
  • Many independent consumers need the same events
  • You need replay (bug fix, new service, backfill)
  • Very high, sustained throughput
  • You want Connect / Streams / Flink / Debezium around it
Kafka loses when
  • Volume is small and the team is small (ops cost dominates)
  • You need per-message delays, priorities or rich routing
  • Each job is slow and independent (until share groups fit)
  • You need request/response with an immediate answer

About benchmarks: you will find posts showing one system crushing another. Confluent published a Kafka vs Pulsar vs RabbitMQ benchmark; it is vendor-run, so say so if you cite it. In interviews, talk about shape and trade-offs, not who won a benchmark.
