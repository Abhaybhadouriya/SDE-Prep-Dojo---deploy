Apache Pulsar: a log with the storage pulled out

In Kafka, a broker both serves clients and stores the partition on its own disk. Pulsar splits those jobs. Brokers are stateless: they handle producers and consumers and cache recent data. The data itself lives in Apache BookKeeper, a separate cluster of storage nodes called bookies. A topic's log is cut into segments (ledgers) that are spread across many bookies. Metadata lives in a metadata store (ZooKeeper or an alternative).

Why people pick Pulsar:

  • Multi-tenancy built in: tenants → namespaces → topics, with quotas and auth per tenant. Useful when one platform team serves hundreds of product teams.
  • Geo-replication built in between clusters, configured per namespace.
  • Both queue and stream styles through subscription types: Exclusive (one consumer), Failover (one active, others standby), Shared (round-robin across many consumers, a work queue, no ordering), and Key_Shared (many consumers, but each key sticks to one consumer, so per-key order holds).
  • Elastic brokers: moving a topic to another broker is a metadata change, not a data copy.

Why people do not: you operate three systems (brokers, bookies, metadata store) instead of one; the ecosystem (connectors, stream processing, hiring pool, managed offerings) is smaller than Kafka's; and Kafka has closed some gaps with tiered storage (KIP-405) and share groups (KIP-932).

An Indian e-commerce data point: Flipkart has spoken publicly (Pulsar Summit Europe 2024, "Flipkart's Async Bus Migration from Kafka to Pulsar") about moving parts of its async messaging from Kafka to Pulsar. Use it as a discussion point about multi-tenancy and storage-compute separation, not as "Pulsar beats Kafka". We found no official throughput numbers, so do not quote any.
