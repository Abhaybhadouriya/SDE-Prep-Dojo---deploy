Common mistakes and gotchas

  • "Kafka is a queue." Not by default. Reading does not delete. Retention deletes old segments whether or not anyone read them.
  • "SQS gives exactly-once." Standard SQS is at-least-once and may duplicate. FIFO de-duplicates only within a 5-minute window. Your consumer still needs idempotency.
  • "RabbitMQ can't scale." It scales well for many queues and moderate per-queue throughput; one queue still lives on one leader node, so a single giant queue is the bottleneck.
  • Using Kafka for delayed jobs. Kafka has no per-message delay. Use SQS delay (up to 15 minutes), RabbitMQ TTL tricks, a scheduler service, or delay topics.
  • Forgetting the ops bill. "Kafka" in a design for a 3-person startup sending 10 messages per second is a red flag unless you say "managed".
  • Comparing on benchmarks alone. Shape (log vs queue), ordering scope, replay and who operates it decide almost every real choice.
  • Mixing up SNS and SQS. SNS pushes copies to subscribers and stores nothing long-term; SQS stores messages until a consumer deletes them.
  • Thinking Kinesis has no hot-key problem. One key goes to one shard, and a shard caps at 1 MB/s writes.
