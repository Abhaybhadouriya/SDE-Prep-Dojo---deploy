Kinesis numbers are from the AWS key concepts page as recorded in our research notes. Quick math so the limit feels real: a stream that must accept 50 MB/s of writes needs at least 50 shards, and at the same time no single key can write more than 1 MB/s, because one key always lands in one shard. That is exactly the "hot partition" problem from Kafka, with a hard number on it.

Real migration story: Atlassian moved its StreamHub event platform from Kinesis to Kafka at about 145 billion events per day. Reading their reasons (cost and control at very high volume) is great prep for "managed vs self-run" questions.

Microsoft interviews: Event Hubs' Kafka endpoint means "we use Kafka" and "we use Azure" are not in conflict. Standard Kafka producers and consumers connect by changing bootstrap.servers and the SASL settings.
