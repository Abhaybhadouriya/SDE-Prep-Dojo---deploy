Follow-up traps and the right reply

Interviewer saysTrapRight reply
"So Kafka guarantees ordering?""Yes"Only within a partition. Choose the key so the things that need order share it.
"acks=all means no data loss, right?"AgreeingOnly with min ISR 2 (and RF 3, unclean election off, and checked send callbacks).
"Kafka gives exactly-once, so the payment is safe?"AgreeingEOS covers Kafka-to-Kafka. The card charge needs an idempotency key.
"Just add consumers to fix lag?"AgreeingOnly up to the partition count, and only if the consumer, not the database, is the bottleneck.
"Add partitions later when needed?"AgreeingIt remaps keys and breaks per-key order for a while; size for growth up front.
"Reading a message deletes it?"AgreeingNo; retention deletes segments. Offsets just move.
"Kafka still needs ZooKeeper?"AgreeingNot since 4.0; KRaft controllers hold metadata in a Raft log.
"Kafka fsyncs every message for durability?"AgreeingNo; durability comes from replication. Flush is left to the OS by default.
"Put all brokers behind one load balancer?"AgreeingClients must reach the specific leader; use per-broker advertised listeners.
"Set session timeout higher to stop rebalances?"AgreeingCheck max.poll.interval.ms first; heartbeats run on a separate thread.

You might confuse these: partition vs replica (a partition is a slice of the topic; a replica is a copy of a slice); consumer group vs share group (one owner per partition vs many with per-record acks); offset vs high watermark (a position vs the highest committed position); idempotent producer vs transactions (dedup of retries in one session vs atomic multi-partition writes with fencing across restarts).
