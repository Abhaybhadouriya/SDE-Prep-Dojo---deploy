Drill 2: stall a consumer

The script runs docker compose pause postgres-sink (Docker freezes every process in the container through the cgroup freezer, much like SIGSTOP), sends load for 20 seconds (the default), prints the group three times, then unpauses and prints how long the sink was really frozen. The freeze is longer than the load time, because each of the three describe calls starts a JVM inside the broker (often 3 to 5 s each).

==> Pausing postgres-sink (docker freezes its processes, like SIGSTOP)
==> Sending load for 20s in the background
GROUP          TOPIC           PARTITION  CURRENT-OFFSET  LOG-END-OFFSET  LAG  ...
postgres-sink  orders.enriched 0          804             837             33   ...
postgres-sink  orders.enriched 1          790             826             36   ...
...
postgres-sink  orders.enriched 0          804             871             67   ...
...
postgres-sink  orders.enriched 0          804             904             100  ...
==> Unpausing - watch lag drain
sink was frozen for 31 s (rebalance if over 45 s)
postgres-sink  orders.enriched 0          904             904             0    ...

What you see: CURRENT-OFFSET is frozen, LOG-END-OFFSET climbs, LAG grows in steps. The Total sink lag panel rises, while Total enricher lag and the order-api stay normal. After unpause, the lag drains within seconds.

Why: Kafka decouples producers from consumers. The log keeps the records; the slow consumer only hurts itself. This is the core promise of a log-based pipeline: a slow database does not slow down checkout.

Keep the whole freeze under about 35 seconds (load time plus the describe calls), well below 45. A frozen process sends no heartbeats either. Past session.timeout.ms (45 s in ShopStream's config) the group coordinator removes the member, and on unpause it has to rejoin, so you will also see a rebalance. Try 60 seconds once on purpose and look for the "assigned" and "revoked" lines in the sink's logs.

A related drill to try yourself: docker compose stop postgres for 30 seconds. The sink logs db write failed ... reconnecting every 2 seconds, keeps its batch, and does not commit offsets, so lag grows exactly like this drill, and nothing is lost when Postgres returns. That is backpressure done right.
