The story: the dashboard and the fire drill

Explain like I'm 5

A car has a dashboard: speed, fuel, engine temperature. You do not open the bonnet every minute. You glance at a few needles, and if one goes into the red, then you look closer.

A school has fire drills. Nobody waits for a real fire to find out whether the doors open and everyone knows where to go. You ring the bell on a calm Tuesday, watch what happens, and fix what went wrong.

For a pipeline, the dashboard is observability: metrics (numbers over time), logs (what each program said) and traces (one request's path across services). The fire drill is chaos engineering: breaking things on purpose, in a controlled way, to check that the system behaves the way you think. ShopStream ships both: a Prometheus + Grafana stack and a folder of drill scripts, scripts/chaos/.

From your world

This is SNMP polling and streaming telemetry for software. Prometheus pulls (scrapes) /metrics from each service every 10 seconds, like an NMS polling interface counters. Consumer lag is queue depth on an egress queue: a growing depth means the drain is slower than the fill. A chaos drill is the lab test where you pull a line card and check that traffic reconverges. Where it breaks: interface counters are exact and local, while lag is computed by comparing two numbers from different places (the broker's end offset and the group's committed offset), so it is only as fresh as the last commit.
