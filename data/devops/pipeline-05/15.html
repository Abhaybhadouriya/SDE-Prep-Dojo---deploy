60-second answers

Practise these out loud until they sound like you talking, not reading.

"Tell me about a pipeline you built."
"I built ShopStream, an order pipeline on Kafka. An API publishes order events keyed by customer, with an idempotent producer and acks=all on a 3-broker KRaft cluster with min ISR 2. An enricher validates and prices orders inside Kafka transactions, sends temporary failures to a retry topic with backoff and bad records to a DLQ. A sink upserts into Postgres and commits offsets after the DB, so it is effectively-once, and an analytics service computes per-minute revenue by event time. I ran it on Kubernetes with Strimzi, scaled the consumer on lag with KEDA, and tested it with chaos drills: broker kills, stalled consumers, poison messages, rebalances and replays, all watched on a Grafana lag dashboard."

"What happens when a broker dies?"
"The controller sees missed heartbeats, removes the broker from the ISR of its partitions and elects new leaders from the in-sync replicas. Clients refresh metadata and continue. With RF 3, min ISR 2 and acks=all, nothing acknowledged is lost and writes keep working. If a second broker dies, writes are refused rather than risk loss. When the broker returns it catches up and rejoins, and a preferred leader election spreads leadership again. I have run this drill and watched under-replicated partitions go up and back to zero."

"How do you monitor a Kafka consumer?"
"Lag per group and per partition, converted to time and compared with retention, with an alert when it grows for a few minutes. Throughput in versus out, to tell more traffic from a slower consumer. Error outcomes: retry and DLQ rates. Rebalance frequency. And the user's view, end-to-end latency from event time to the sink. When lag grows I check, in order: more input, consumer errors, rebalances, a hot partition, a slow dependency."

Turning a drill into a STAR story

You asked how a pipeline story becomes a behavioural answer. Here is one built from drill 4, in the Situation, Task, Action, Result shape (use your own real version once you have run it):

  1. Situation. "Our order consumer showed lag spikes of about 45 seconds on two partitions every time a pod was replaced."
  2. Task. "I needed to find out why replacements were not smooth, because those spikes delayed order confirmations."
  3. Action. "I reproduced it in a local cluster: a clean stop caused no spike, a hard kill did. That pointed at the session timeout: killed pods never sent a leave-group. I added SIGTERM handling that finishes the batch, commits and closes the consumer, set the termination grace period above our batch time, and switched to the cooperative-sticky assignor."
  4. Result. "Rolling deploys stopped producing lag spikes on the dashboard, and the drill became part of our release checklist."
