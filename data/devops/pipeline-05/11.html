Drill 6: the hot partition

Not a script, just the load generator with a flag: half of all orders come from one customer, C-HOT.

docker compose up -d --scale enricher=3
python loadgen/loadgen.py --rps 20 --seconds 60 --hot-customer 0.5

What you see: in Enricher lag by partition, one line is far above the others (which partition depends on the hash of C-HOT). Total lag may look acceptable while one customer's orders are delayed. Scaling the group does nothing for that partition.

Why: the order-api keys by customer_id, and Kafka sends every record with the same key to the same partition (hash of the key modulo the partition count). That is what keeps one customer's orders in order. The price is that a single huge key cannot be spread.

Fixes, with their costs: a more fine-grained key (for example order_id, losing per-customer order); key salting for known hot keys (C-HOT#0..7, losing order across salts and needing a merge step); making each record cheaper; or isolating very large customers on their own topic. There is no free option; say the trade-off out loud. See Top-K Heavy Hitters for hot keys in a counting system.

From your world

This is the elephant flow on an ECMP bundle: the 5-tuple hash keeps one flow on one link to preserve packet order, so one big flow saturates one member link while the others sit idle. The fixes match too: finer hashing (losing order), flowlet switching (salting), or special handling for known elephants. Where it breaks: ECMP can rehash a flowlet in microseconds and the packets re-merge at the destination for free; salting a Kafka key is a design change, and something downstream must merge the salted streams back together (and give up order across salts).
