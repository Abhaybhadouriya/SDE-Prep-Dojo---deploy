Drill 4: rebalance

The script scales the enricher 1, then 3, hard-kills one member, then goes back to 1, printing group membership each time. Run python loadgen/loadgen.py --rps 20 --seconds 180 in a second terminal to see it under traffic.

Which partitions go where will differ; the 2/2/2 split is the point.

What you see: on scale-up, the six partitions split 2/2/2. With the cooperative-sticky assignor, the first member keeps two of its partitions and only gives away four, so it never stops processing completely. On the hard kill, that member's two partitions get no progress for about 45 seconds: the Enricher lag by partition panel shows exactly two lines climbing, then dropping once the coordinator gives up on the dead member and reassigns.

Why: a clean shutdown (SIGTERM, which docker compose stop and Kubernetes both send) runs consumer.close(), which sends a leave-group request: rebalance at once. A SIGKILL sends nothing, so the coordinator can only notice missing heartbeats after session.timeout.ms. That is why ShopStream's GracefulExit class exists, and why the Kubernetes grace period matters.

Deep dive (good to know): if the killed enricher had an open transaction, its uncommitted output in orders.enriched holds back read_committed readers of that partition (they stop at the last stable offset) until the transaction coordinator aborts it, at most transaction.timeout.ms (60 s in the enricher). So a hard kill can briefly stall the sink too. One more reason graceful shutdown matters.

From your world

A rebalance is re-hashing ECMP buckets when a member joins or leaves; cooperative-sticky is resilient hashing, which moves only the buckets that must move. session.timeout.ms is a BFD or hello dead interval: a member that vanishes without saying goodbye is only declared dead when the timer expires. A clean leave-group is like a graceful shutdown notification (a goodbye hello) that lets neighbours reconverge at once.
