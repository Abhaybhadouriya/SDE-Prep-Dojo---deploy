Why ZooKeeper had to go

must know

Before KRaft, metadata lived in ZooKeeper. One broker was elected as "the controller" (by racing to create an ephemeral znode). That controller watched ZooKeeper and pushed changes to all other brokers with RPCs (LeaderAndIsr, UpdateMetadata).

The problems, in plain words:

  1. Two distributed systems. ZooKeeper had its own config, its own security model, its own monitoring, its own upgrades and its own failure modes. Teams had to be experts in both.
  2. Slow controller failover. When the controller broker died, the new one had to read the entire metadata state from ZooKeeper before it could act. With hundreds of thousands of partitions this could take a long time, and during that time no leader elections happened.
  3. Push-based updates could diverge. The controller pushed metadata RPCs to every broker. If one was lost or late, brokers could briefly disagree about who leads a partition.
  4. A ceiling on partitions. All of the above grew with partition count, which practically limited how many partitions one cluster could hold.

KIP-500 (2019) proposed replacing ZooKeeper with a self-managed metadata quorum. The timeline: early access in 2.8 (2021), production ready in 3.3 (2022), migration tooling in 3.4 to 3.6, 3.9 is the last release that supports ZooKeeper (the "bridge" release), and 4.0 (March 2025) removed ZooKeeper entirely.

What KRaft gives you
  • One system to deploy, secure and monitor
  • Standby controllers hold the full metadata already, so failover is fast
  • Brokers replicate a log with offsets, so they can tell exactly how up to date they are
  • Room for far more partitions per cluster
  • Faster broker restarts (they load a local metadata snapshot plus the newest records)
What to watch
  • A quorum needs a majority: lose 2 of 3 controllers and metadata changes stop
  • Combined mode is tempting but risky in production
  • Migration from ZooKeeper must go through 3.9 first
  • New tools to learn (kafka-metadata-quorum.sh, metadata shell)

You might confuse this with: the group coordinator and transaction coordinator. Those are ordinary brokers that manage consumer groups (__consumer_offsets) and transactions (__transaction_state). The controller manages cluster metadata only. Three different "coordinators", three different jobs.
