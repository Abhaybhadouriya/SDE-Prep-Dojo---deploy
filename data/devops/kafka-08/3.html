Raft in five minutes (the part Kafka uses)

good to know

Raft is a consensus algorithm: a way for a small group of servers to agree on one ordered log even when some of them crash. Kafka's version is a pull-based variant (followers fetch, like partition replication), but the ideas are classic Raft: terms called epochs, a leader elected by majority vote, and an entry counted as committed once a majority has it. Walk through an election and a commit.

  1. Leader gone. Controller 1 (the active controller) crashes. The followers notice because their fetches to it stop succeeding.
  2. Become a candidate. After the timeout (plus randomness, so two do not start together) Controller 2 bumps the epoch to 5 and asks the others for votes, sending where its log ends.
  3. Majority vote. Controller 3 votes yes because Controller 2's log is at least as complete as its own. Two of three is a majority.
  4. New leader. Controller 2 is now the active controller for epoch 5. Anything still claiming to be leader with epoch 4 is ignored (this is fencing).
  5. Append. An admin creates a topic. The active controller appends records describing it to the metadata log.
  6. Commit. Controller 3 fetches the new records. Once a majority (leader + 1) has them, they are committed and the high watermark advances. Brokers then fetch and apply them.

From your world

Compare with VRRP: VRRP elects a master by priority, and with a network partition you can get two masters (split brain). Raft prevents that with a majority rule: a leader needs votes from more than half of the voters, and two different majorities of the same group always overlap in at least one member, who will not vote twice in the same epoch. The epoch is like a generation number on a control message: anything stamped with an older epoch is dropped.

Where the analogy breaks: VRRP fails over in under a second with one survivor. Raft with 3 voters needs 2 alive; one lonely controller cannot elect itself.

How many controllers?

Controllers (voters)Majority neededFailures toleratedUse
110Laptop / CI only
321Most production clusters
532Large or critical clusters; can lose one during maintenance and still survive a crash

The rule from the docs: to survive N concurrent failures you need 2N + 1 controllers. Even numbers do not help (4 controllers still tolerate only 1 failure). Put controllers in different racks or availability zones. The KRaft ops docs suggest that for a typical cluster about 5 GB of memory and 5 GB of disk for the metadata log directory is enough per controller; treat that as their guideline, not a hard rule.
