The story: tracing paper and school lockers

Explain like I'm 5

Your teacher gives you a beautiful picture book (the image). You are not allowed to draw in it. So you lay a sheet of tracing paper on top and draw on that. It looks like you drew in the book. At the end of the day the tracing paper goes in the bin, and the book is clean for the next child. That tracing paper is the container's writable layer.

But what about your homework? You do not want it thrown away. So you put it in your locker. The locker stays in school even after you go home, and tomorrow you open the same locker again. That locker is a volume.

Sometimes you bring something from home, like your own pencil box, and use it at school. That is a bind mount: a folder from the host computer used inside the container. And a note you write on a whiteboard that gets wiped when the bell rings is tmpfs: memory only.

Containers are meant to be thrown away and re-created all the time: on every deploy, every crash, every scale-up. So anything they write inside themselves is temporary by design. When data must outlive a container (database files, uploads, Kafka logs), you put it somewhere that is not inside the container. This page shows the three places Docker offers and how to choose.
