Your 60-second answer

"Kubernetes is a system for running containers across many machines. Instead of telling it step by step what to do, I declare the desired state, for example three replicas of my API image, and Kubernetes stores that in its API. Controllers then run continuous loops that compare desired and actual state and fix any difference. That gives me scheduling onto machines with free capacity, self-healing when containers or nodes fail, a stable service name and virtual IP with load balancing, rolling updates with rollback, and autoscaling. Coming from networking, I think of it like a router: the control plane holds intent and computes decisions, the nodes are the data plane that does the work, and running workloads keep going even if the control plane is briefly down. The cost is operational complexity, so for one or two small services I would use a simpler managed container platform."
