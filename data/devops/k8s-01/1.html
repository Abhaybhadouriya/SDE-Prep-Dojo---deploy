The real problem: one box is easy, a fleet is hard

Must know (SDE-1)

Docker makes it easy to run one container on one machine: docker run -d -p 80:80 myshop:1.0 and you are done. Now imagine your shop app becomes popular. You need:

  • Many copies of the app, because one copy cannot handle all the users.
  • Many machines, because one machine cannot hold all the copies (and if it dies, everything is gone).
  • New versions every day, without the website going down.
  • Other services (payments, search, cart) that need to find each other, even though their copies keep moving.

Here is the picture. On the left, life is simple. On the right, someone has to make dozens of decisions every hour.

The hard questions on the right are the whole reason Kubernetes exists:

  1. Placement. Server-5 is empty and server-6 is full. Where should the next copy of shop go? Which server has enough free CPU and memory?
  2. Failure detection. Server-3 just died. Who notices? How fast? What was running on it?
  3. Repair. The copies of shop and cart that lived on server-3 must be started somewhere else, automatically.
  4. Finding each other. cart needs to call pay. But pay might be on a different server tomorrow, with a different IP address. How does cart find it?
  5. Safe upgrades. Version 2.0 of shop is ready. How do you replace 30 running copies without the site going down, and go back quickly if 2.0 is broken?
  6. Scaling. It is the start of a big sale. Traffic is 10 times normal. Who adds copies, and who removes them after the sale?
