Errors, timeouts and backpressure

Every record ends in exactly one of two ways: the callback gets an offset, or the callback gets an error. Your job is to always look at the error. The producer sorts errors into two kinds:

KindExamplesWhat the producer doesWhat you do
Retriable (temporary)NOT_LEADER_OR_FOLLOWER (leader moved), NOT_ENOUGH_REPLICAS, request timeout, network disconnectRefreshes metadata if needed, backs off (retry.backoff.ms, 100 ms growing to retry.backoff.max.ms, 1 s), and retries until delivery.timeout.ms runs outNothing, unless the deadline is hit; then you get a timeout error in the callback
Non-retriable (permanent)RecordTooLargeException, serialization errors, authorization failure, invalid topicFails the record immediatelyLog, alert, and park the record somewhere safe (a local file or a dead-letter path); retrying will not help

The two clocks

This is why tuning retries directly is old advice. Modern clients set retries to a huge number (2147483647) and let delivery.timeout.ms decide when to give up. Think in time ("I accept up to 2 minutes of trying"), not in attempt counts.

Backpressure: when brokers are slower than your app

What blocking means for you depends on where the producer is called:

  • In a batch job or log shipper, blocking is perfect: the job simply slows to the speed Kafka can absorb.
  • In a web request handler, blocking for up to 60 s is a disaster: request threads pile up and the whole service stops answering. Set max.block.ms low (for example a few hundred milliseconds), catch the error, and decide: reply "try again", or write to a local fallback. Better still, the outbox pattern (HLD page 10) avoids calling Kafka inside the request at all.
  • In Python confluent-kafka, a full local queue raises BufferError from produce() instead of blocking. The usual fix is to call p.poll(1) to let deliveries drain, then try again.

From your world

A full buffer.memory is a full socket send buffer: a blocking write() sleeps until the peer's window opens, a non-blocking one returns EAGAIN. Python's BufferError is the EAGAIN version. It is flow control, just like the TCP receive window closing. Where this breaks: TCP's window is negotiated by the receiver; Kafka brokers do not send "slow down" signals per producer, except for quota throttling. The producer infers it simply from replies coming back slowly.
