acks: how sure is "got it"?

The acks setting tells the leader broker when it may answer the producer. It is the main durability versus latency dial. Step through the three settings and then the extra safety rule.

ISR in one breath: the in-sync replicas are the leader plus every follower that has caught up to the leader within the last 30 seconds (replica.lag.time.max.ms, measured in time, not in messages). A slow or dead follower drops out, so the ISR can shrink all the way down to just the leader (size 1). That is why min.insync.replicas exists. Full story on Replication & ISR.

  1. acks=0. The producer sends and immediately counts the record as done. The broker never replies, so the producer does not even learn the offset (Java reports offset -1). Fastest, and any network blip or broker crash silently loses data. Fine for throwaway metrics, almost nothing else.
  2. acks=1. The leader replies once the batch is in its own log. That means "in the leader's page cache", not fsynced to disk and not yet on any follower. If the leader dies before a follower copies the batch, a new leader takes over without it and the record is gone, even though the producer was told "OK".
  3. acks=all (also written -1; the default since 3.0). The leader replies only after every replica currently in the ISR (in-sync replicas) has the batch. Losing one broker can no longer lose acknowledged data. The cost is waiting for the slowest in-sync follower, usually a few extra milliseconds inside one data centre.
  4. Add min.insync.replicas=2 (a topic or broker setting). acks=all means "all of the ISR", and the ISR can shrink to just the leader. Min ISR sets a floor: if fewer than 2 replicas are in sync, the leader refuses acks=all writes instead of quietly accepting them on one copy. Availability is traded for durability, and you see it as an error rather than as silent loss.

Say it as one line: "Replication factor 3, min.insync.replicas=2, acks=all, idempotence on." That survives one broker failure with no data loss and still accepts writes. Then add the honest caveat: if two of the three replicas are down, writes stop - on purpose. The replication side (ISR, high watermark, leader election) is on page 05.

From your world

acks=all with min ISR 2 is "do not ACK the write until a quorum of line cards (or routers) has programmed it". Kafka's durability comes from copies on other machines, not from fsync: by default the broker does not fsync each message, it relies on replication plus the OS page cache. It is RAID-1 across hosts rather than a battery-backed write cache. Where this breaks: a whole-rack power loss that takes out all replicas at once can still lose page-cache data, which is why replicas are spread across racks or availability zones (broker.rack).
