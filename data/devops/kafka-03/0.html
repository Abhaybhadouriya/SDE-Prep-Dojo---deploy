The story: the school bus

Explain like I'm 5

Every morning, kids wait at bus stops to go to school. The school could send a car for each kid, one at a time. That would be a lot of cars and a lot of traffic. So instead the school sends a bus.

The bus driver has two simple rules. Rule one: "When the bus is full, go." Rule two: "If the bus is not full, wait a tiny bit for more kids, then go anyway." That way no kid waits for long, and the road is not full of cars.

There is a different bus for each classroom, so kids for Room 1 never get mixed with kids for Room 2. And when the bus reaches school, the teacher at the door can do one of three things: not check at all, check that the kids walked into the building, or wait until every kid's name is written in the class book and in two copy books. Only then does the teacher wave "all good" to the driver.

If the driver does not see the wave, the driver drives the same kids back again. To stop the teacher from writing the same kid twice, every kid wears a numbered badge. "Badge 7 again? I already have you." The kid is still counted only once.

That story is the whole producer. A producer is the piece of your program that writes records into Kafka. It does not send each record the moment you hand it over. It quietly groups records going to the same partition into batches, sends a batch when it is full or when a short timer runs out, waits for the broker's acknowledgement ("ack"), and retries if something goes wrong - without creating duplicates.
