Offsets: positions, and who remembers them

An offset is a 64-bit number that says where a record sits inside one partition. The full address of any record in Kafka is the triple (topic, partition, offset), for example (orders, 1, 42).

Who stores the consumer's offset - the broker or the consumer? Both play a part, which is why people get confused:

  • The consumer decides which offset it has finished, and it commits that offset (either automatically every 5 seconds by default, or explicitly in code).
  • The broker stores it. Committed offsets are written into an internal Kafka topic named __consumer_offsets (50 partitions by default), managed by a broker acting as that group's group coordinator. Old Kafka versions kept offsets in ZooKeeper; that is long gone.
  • The broker does not track per-message delivery. It remembers one number per (group, partition). That is a big part of why Kafka is cheap and fast compared with brokers that track every message.

Committed offset means "next to read". If a consumer finished offset 103, it commits 104. That off-by-one trips up people writing their own commit code. Committing too early (before processing) can lose messages on a crash; committing too late causes re-processing. Page 04 and page 06 go deep on this.

From your world

The committed offset is a read index into a producer-consumer ring, stored durably so a restarted driver can resume exactly where it stopped. And because the broker only keeps one number per reader per partition (not per message state), it is closer to a TCP cumulative ACK than to per-packet selective ACKs: "I have everything up to here."
