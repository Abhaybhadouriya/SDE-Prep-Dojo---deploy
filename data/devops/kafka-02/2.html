What is inside one record

Each record (Kafka's word for a message or event) is a small bundle. The producer fills in most of it; the broker adds the offset when the record is written.

  • Kafka does not care what the bytes mean. Turning your object into bytes is the client's job (a serializer); turning bytes back is the reader's job (a deserializer). Agreeing on the format between teams is what a schema registry is for (page 10).
  • Size matters. The broker setting message.max.bytes defaults to about 1 MiB. Large things like images or PDFs go to object storage (such as S3) and the record carries only a link. That trick is called the claim-check pattern.
  • Timestamp defaults to the time the producer created the record (CreateTime). A topic can be set to use the broker's append time instead.
