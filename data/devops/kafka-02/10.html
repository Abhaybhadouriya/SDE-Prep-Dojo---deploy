Fine print: partitions in production

How many partitions should a topic have?

There is no magic number, but there is a method you can say out loud in an interview:

  1. Throughput. Measure what one partition can do for your record size, on the producer side and on the consumer side (your consumer's processing is usually the slow part). Then partitions ≥ target throughput ÷ per-partition throughput, for each side. Take the larger.
  2. Consumer parallelism. The maximum number of consumers in one group that can do useful work equals the partition count. If you will ever need 24 consumers at peak, you need at least 24 partitions.
  3. Growth. Adding partitions later remaps keys (you just saw it). So pick a count with headroom for a year or two of growth if ordering by key matters. A number with many divisors (12, 24, 48) spreads evenly over different consumer counts.
  4. Cost. Every partition (times its replication factor) is a set of open files, memory buffers and metadata, and more partitions mean more work when a broker fails or restarts. KRaft raised the cluster-wide ceiling a lot, but partitions are still not free. Thousands of tiny topics, or "one topic per customer", is a classic anti-pattern.

Rule of thumb, not a law: many teams start a busy topic at 6 to 12 partitions on a 3-broker cluster and measure. Always say "I would measure with kafka-producer-perf-test.sh and kafka-consumer-perf-test.sh" rather than quoting a universal per-partition MB/s figure.

The ordering guarantee, precisely

"Kafka keeps order within a partition" is true for what is stored. Two more conditions make it true end to end:

  • On the producer side, retries could reorder batches if several are in flight to the same partition. Modern defaults handle this: the idempotent producer (on by default since Kafka 3.0) attaches sequence numbers, so the broker keeps order with up to 5 in-flight requests per connection. That is on the next page.
  • On the consumer side, records arrive in order, but if your code hands them to a thread pool, you can reorder them yourself. Keep one worker per partition, or route by key to a fixed worker.

Partition vs replica - do not mix them up

Partitions split the data (each holds different records). Replicas copy the data (each holds the same records). A topic with 6 partitions and replication factor 3 has 18 partition replicas spread across the brokers. Partitions are for scale; replicas are for safety.
