The log: the one idea under everything

A log here does not mean a debug log file with error messages. It means a very simple data structure: a sequence of records that you can only add to at the end. Three rules make it a Kafka log:

  1. Append-only. New records go at the end (the "tail" or "head", people use both words). Nothing is inserted in the middle.
  2. Immutable. Once written, a record is never changed. To "change" something, you append a new record that says the new value.
  3. Ordered and numbered. Every record gets the next offset: 0, 1, 2, 3, ... Offsets only go up and are never reused, even after old records are deleted.

Watch the log below. New records fly in from the producer and always land at the end. Two readers move along at their own speed; reading never removes anything.

From your world

A partition is very close to a journal (like the ext4 or a database write-ahead log) or the dmesg ring: writes are sequential appends, and readers keep their own read index. Multiple consumer groups reading one partition are like several consumers each holding their own index into one shared ring. Where this breaks: a ring buffer has a fixed size and silently overwrites the oldest slot when it wraps, so a slow reader loses data without knowing. A Kafka log is not overwritten in place. It grows on disk and the oldest data is removed in big chunks (whole segment files) only when they pass the retention time or size. Offsets keep increasing forever; they never wrap like a ring index.

Interviewers like this framing: "Kafka is a distributed, partitioned, replicated commit log." Distributed = many brokers. Partitioned = each topic split into logs. Replicated = each partition copied to several brokers. Commit log = append-only, ordered, durable.
