The story: the toy-robot doctor

Explain like I'm 5

Imagine a big room full of toy robots. Each robot has a job: one sings, one draws, one carries blocks. One morning a robot stops working. A good robot doctor does not hit it with a hammer. She does four simple things.

She looks at it (is it standing, sitting, or lying on the floor?). She reads its diary (the robot writes down what it did, line by line). She checks the notice board (the room's helpers pin notes like "Robot 7 could not find a chair"). And she checks the thermometer (how hot is it, how fast is its heart beating?).

Kubernetes gives you the same four tools. Looking = kubectl get. The diary = logs. The notice board = events. The thermometer = metrics. Learn to use those four in the right order and almost every problem becomes easy.

Two big words on this page:

  • Observability means "can I understand what is happening inside the system just by looking at what it reports?" A system is observable when it gives you good metrics (numbers over time), logs (text lines the program writes), traces (the path one request takes through many services) and, in Kubernetes, events (short notes the cluster writes about objects).
  • Troubleshooting means using those signals to go from "something is broken" to "this exact thing is broken, and here is the fix".

You already do this at work. When a line card drops packets, you do not guess. You check counters, read syslog, look at the interface state, and follow the packet. Kubernetes debugging is the same habit with new tool names.

From your world

Metrics are your SNMP counters and streaming telemetry. Logs are syslog. Events are like the "show logging" lines the control plane writes when an interface flaps or a neighbour goes down. Traces are a packet trace across hops, but for one HTTP request moving through many services. Where this breaks: in Kubernetes the things you debug (pods) are short-lived and get replaced, so the evidence can disappear with them. You must learn where the evidence is kept and for how long.
