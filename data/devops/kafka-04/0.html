The story: a homework team

Explain like I'm 5

Your teacher has a big pile of worksheets, sorted into 4 folders. Your team has to finish all of them. The rule is simple: each folder goes to exactly one kid on the team. If your team has 2 kids, each kid takes 2 folders. If a new kid joins, the teacher takes one folder from someone and gives it to the new kid. If 6 kids show up for 4 folders, 2 kids have nothing to do and just wait.

Every kid keeps a bookmark in each folder they own: "I finished up to worksheet 37". If a kid goes home sick, another kid picks up the folder and starts from the bookmark, not from the first page.

Now, the art class next door wants the same worksheets to make posters. They are a different team, so they get their own copy of all 4 folders and their own bookmarks. Neither team slows the other down.

That is the whole idea of this page. The folders are partitions. The kids are consumers. The team is a consumer group. The bookmark is the committed offset. The teacher who hands out folders is the group coordinator, and the moment when folders get handed around is called a rebalance. The pile of worksheets nobody has done yet is the lag.

If you have not read Core Concepts yet, the short version is: a Kafka topic is split into partitions; each partition is an append-only log where every record gets a number called an offset (0, 1, 2, ...). Reading a record does not delete it. That one fact explains most of this page.
