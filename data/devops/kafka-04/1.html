What a consumer really is

must know

A consumer is just your program (a Java, Python or Go process) using a Kafka client library. It asks brokers for records. Kafka never pushes records to it. The consumer pulls, in a loop, as fast as it can process. This loop is called the poll loop:

  1. subscribe Tell the client which topics you want and which group you belong to (group.id).
  2. poll Call poll(). The client sends fetch requests to the brokers that lead your partitions and returns a batch of records (up to max.poll.records, 500 by default).
  3. process Do your work with each record: save to a database, call an API, update a cache.
  4. commit Tell Kafka "my group has finished up to offset N in partition P" so a restart resumes from there.
  5. repeat Go back to poll(). Calling poll regularly is also how the consumer proves it is alive.

From your world

The poll loop is NAPI, not interrupts. A NIC driver under load stops taking an interrupt per packet and instead polls a ring with a budget. A Kafka consumer does the same: each fetch is a long poll (fetch.min.bytes, fetch.max.wait.ms = 500 ms) that returns a batch. The partition is the RX ring, the offset is your read index into it, and the broker never overwrites your unread slots.

Where the analogy breaks: a ring buffer is overwritten when it wraps. A Kafka log is not; old data is removed only by retention (whole segment files, after 7 days by default), whether or not anyone read it.

One topic, two groups: queue and pub/sub at the same time

Inside one group, each partition is owned by exactly one consumer. That makes a group behave like a work queue: the work is split. Across groups, every group gets all the records. That makes Kafka also behave like publish/subscribe: the data is copied out to everyone who is interested. The trick is that nothing is copied on disk. Each group simply keeps its own set of offsets into the same log.

Answering your question "Is Kafka a queue? Is a message deleted when read?" No to the second part. Reading moves only your group's bookmark. The record stays until retention removes it, so a new group (or yours, after a bug fix) can re-read history. A classic queue such as RabbitMQ or SQS deletes a message once it is acknowledged. Kafka 4.2 added share groups (KIP-932) for true queue-style work, covered at the end of this page.

More consumers than partitions? The extras sit idle

You asked: "10 partitions and 15 consumers - what do the extra 5 do?" They join the group, they heartbeat, and they get no partitions. They are hot standbys. If one of the 10 busy consumers dies, a standby takes over its partition at the next rebalance. So the maximum parallelism of a group equals the number of partitions. Adding the 11th consumer does not make anything faster.

What if I really need more workers than partitions? Three honest options: (1) create the topic with more partitions from day one (adding later remaps keys, see Producers); (2) have one consumer hand records to a worker pool inside the process, while keeping per-key order yourself (this is what Uber's and Walmart's consumer proxies do); (3) use a share group (Kafka 4.2+), which lets many consumers read the same partition with per-record acks but no ordering.
