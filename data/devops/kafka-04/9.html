Interview questions

Company chips mean "commonly reported at product-company backend rounds", not "asked on a specific date". More questions live on Kafka Interview Questions.

What is a consumer group, and what happens if there are more consumers than partitions?

A consumer group is a set of consumers that share one group.id and split a topic's partitions between them, so each partition is read by exactly one member. That gives load-balancing inside the group, and every other group still gets all the data. If there are more consumers than partitions, the extra ones get no partitions and stay idle as standbys, so a group's maximum parallelism is the partition count.

How to answer

Say the rule ("one partition, one consumer per group"), then the two consequences: queue-like inside a group, pub/sub across groups; parallelism capped by partitions. Finish with the fix if you need more: more partitions up front, a worker pool with per-key ordering, or share groups.

Likely follow-ups
  • Why not let two consumers read one partition? (Ordering and offset tracking would break.)
  • How do you pick the partition count?
  • What are share groups?
What is a rebalance, why is it bad, and how do you reduce its impact?

A rebalance is the group recomputing which consumer owns which partition, triggered when members join, leave, crash, miss max.poll.interval.ms, or when subscriptions change. In the classic eager protocol all members revoke all partitions first, so the whole group stops consuming until the new plan is in place; lag builds up, and records processed but not yet committed get re-delivered. To reduce it: use cooperative sticky assignment or, on Kafka 4.0+, the KIP-848 protocol (group.protocol=consumer), where the broker reconciles members incrementally; use static membership (group.instance.id) so rolling restarts do not trigger rebalances; keep each poll batch fast; close consumers cleanly on shutdown; and stop autoscalers from flapping.

How to answer

Definition, triggers, why it hurts (stop-the-world plus duplicates), then fixes ordered from "config change" to "protocol change". Mentioning that Walmart's engineering blog called rebalancing a main cause of lag at their scale shows real-world awareness.

Likely follow-ups
  • What changes in KIP-848 compared with JoinGroup/SyncGroup?
  • What is the downside of static membership?
  • How would you detect a rebalance storm in metrics?
Where are consumer offsets stored, and when should you commit?

Committed offsets are stored in Kafka itself, in the compacted internal topic __consumer_offsets (50 partitions by default), through the group coordinator, which is the broker leading the partition that the group ID hashes to. The committed value is the next offset to read. For most services I turn off auto-commit and commit after the work is done, which gives at-least-once delivery, and I make the handler idempotent so the duplicates after a crash or rebalance are harmless. If results go to a single database, I can store the offset in the same database transaction and seek to it on startup.

How to answer

Answer "where" in one sentence, then spend your time on "when", because that is what the interviewer is really testing: commit-before-process = at-most-once, commit-after-process = at-least-once.

Likely follow-ups
  • What can go wrong with auto-commit?
  • commitSync vs commitAsync?
  • What happens to offsets of a group that is idle for two weeks?
Consumer lag is growing during a sale. What do you do?

First I find out whether it is a few partitions or all of them. If only one or two partitions lag, it is a hot key or a stuck/poison record, and adding consumers will not help. If all lag, I look for the bottleneck: consumer CPU, GC, or the downstream system (usually the database). Then I act: scale consumers up to the partition count, batch downstream writes, reduce per-record work, and if the topic has too few partitions, accept that adding partitions remaps keys and plan it carefully. In parallel I protect data by checking that time lag stays well under retention (and raise retention temporarily if needed), and I can shed or defer low-priority work.

How to answer

Show a debugging order, not a list of knobs: measure (per-partition lag, consume rate), localise (one partition vs all), find the bottleneck, fix, protect against data loss. Interviewers mark down "just add more consumers" because it is capped by partitions.

Likely follow-ups
  • How do you fix a hot partition?
  • What alert would you set for lag?
  • Would share groups help here?
Your consumers keep getting kicked out of the group every few minutes. Why, and how do you fix it?

The usual cause is that processing one poll batch takes longer than max.poll.interval.ms (5 minutes default), so the client leaves the group and a rebalance starts; when it finally commits it gets a CommitFailedException. Other causes are missed heartbeats from long GC pauses or CPU starvation (session timeout), pods being restarted by a failing liveness probe, or an autoscaler adding and removing pods. I would confirm from the consumer logs and the coordinator's member list, then reduce max.poll.records, speed up or batch the slow downstream call, use pause/resume for long jobs, and add static membership for restarts.

How to answer

Name the two different timers (poll interval = "are you making progress?", session timeout = "are you alive?"), say which symptom maps to which, then give fixes.

Likely follow-ups
  • Which thread sends heartbeats?
  • Why not just set max.poll.interval.ms to one hour?
How does the new consumer rebalance protocol (KIP-848) differ from the classic one?

In the classic protocol, members do JoinGroup and SyncGroup at a barrier, and one consumer, the group leader, runs the assignor on the client side. In KIP-848, which is GA in Kafka 4.0, members only send ConsumerGroupHeartbeat; the group coordinator on the broker computes a target assignment with a server-side assignor (uniform or range) and reconciles each member incrementally, so unaffected members never stop. Session and heartbeat timeouts become broker configs, and the client opts in with group.protocol=consumer. Live groups can be migrated with a rolling restart.

How to answer

Contrast "client brain + barrier" with "broker brain + per-member reconcile". One sentence on why: fewer stop-the-world pauses, simpler clients, central control.

Likely follow-ups
  • What happens to custom client-side assignors?
  • How would you roll it out safely?

Scenario: "We reprocessed a whole day of payments after a deploy"

The payments consumer group was paused for 9 days during a migration. When it restarted, it re-sent a day of "payment succeeded" emails, and on another topic it skipped a week of data. The interviewer asks what happened.

Weak answer: "Kafka lost our offsets, it is unreliable. We should switch to a database queue."

Strong answer: "An empty group's offsets are deleted after offsets.retention.minutes, 7 days by default. On restart there was no committed offset, so auto.offset.reset applied: earliest on one topic (replayed everything still retained) and latest on the other (skipped everything). Fixes: make the email consumer idempotent with a sent-notification table keyed by payment ID; before long pauses, export offsets (--describe) and restore them with --reset-offsets --to-offset or --to-datetime; set auto.offset.reset=none for critical groups so a missing offset is an alert, not a silent guess; and monitor group state."

Likely follow-ups
  • How would you replay exactly from a timestamp?
  • How do you dedupe emails across restarts?

Your 60-second answer: "Explain consumer groups and offsets."

"A consumer group is a team of consumers with the same group ID. Kafka gives each partition of the topic to exactly one member, so the team splits the work, and the partition count is the maximum parallelism. Another group gets its own full copy, because each group just keeps its own offsets into the same log. Offsets are committed to an internal topic, __consumer_offsets, through a broker called the group coordinator. I commit after processing, so a crash gives duplicates rather than loss, and I make the handler idempotent. When members join or leave, the group rebalances; I reduce the pain with the new KIP-848 protocol or cooperative-sticky assignment plus static membership for rolling restarts. And I watch lag - log-end offset minus committed offset - per partition, as a trend and in seconds."
