Rebalancing: why people say it "stops the world"

must know

A rebalance is the group agreeing on a new partition-to-consumer map. It is triggered when:

  • a consumer joins (new pod, scale-up, restart),
  • a consumer leaves cleanly (close()) or dies (no heartbeat within the session timeout),
  • a consumer is kicked out because it did not call poll() within max.poll.interval.ms (5 minutes by default) - the most common surprise,
  • the subscribed topics change (partitions added, or a regex subscription matches a new topic).

Eager vs cooperative, as a timeline

In the original eager protocol, every consumer must give up every partition before anyone gets the new assignment. For the length of the rebalance, the whole group consumes nothing. With 200 pods that can be many seconds, during which lag grows. Cooperative rebalancing (KIP-429, Kafka 2.4) keeps everything that is not moving, and only the partitions that change owner pause briefly.

From your world

A rebalance is routing reconvergence. While OSPF reruns a full SPF, forwarding for affected prefixes is unstable; incremental SPF recomputes only what changed. Eager vs cooperative is exactly full SPF vs incremental SPF. And partition assignment is ECMP bucket assignment: a sticky assignor is "resilient hashing", which moves the fewest flows when a member joins or leaves.

Where it breaks: a router keeps forwarding on the old FIB during reconvergence. An eager consumer group really stops; there is no "old FIB" to keep using.

The classic protocol in 4 messages

  1. JoinGroup Every member sends JoinGroup to the coordinator with the topics it wants and the assignors it supports. The coordinator waits until all known members rejoin (that wait is the barrier).
  2. Leader election The coordinator picks one member (usually the first to join) as the group leader and sends it the full member list. Note: the leader is a consumer, not a broker.
  3. SyncGroup The leader runs the assignor on the client side and sends the result back. Everyone else sends an empty SyncGroup and receives its share.
  4. Heartbeat Members heartbeat (every heartbeat.interval.ms = 3 s). When a rebalance is needed, the heartbeat reply says "rejoin", and the cycle starts again.

Two weak points: the barrier (one slow member delays everyone) and the client-side leader (a bug or an old client version in one pod can break the whole group's assignment).

From your world

heartbeat.interval.ms / session.timeout.ms (3 s / 45 s on the classic protocol) are the hello interval / dead interval of OSPF, or BFD's tx-interval times its multiplier. Failure detection time is roughly the dead interval, not the hello interval. Where it breaks: Kafka uses seconds, not BFD's milliseconds, because a false "dead" verdict is expensive: it triggers a rebalance that moves partitions and replays uncommitted work.

Stop here for SDE-1? If you can explain why rebalances happen and eager vs cooperative, you are fine for most SDE-1 rounds. The KIP-848 protocol details and static membership below are SDE-2 / deep dive material: useful for "how would you avoid rebalance storms on Kubernetes?" follow-ups.

The new protocol: KIP-848 (GA in Kafka 4.0) deep dive

KIP-848 moves the brain to the broker. There is no JoinGroup/SyncGroup barrier any more. Each consumer only sends a ConsumerGroupHeartbeat (every 5 s by default, set by the broker config group.consumer.heartbeat.interval.ms). The coordinator computes a target assignment with a server-side assignor and then moves each member towards it one member at a time: "please release P2", then later "you now own P2". Members that are not affected never stop.

What you need to know as a developer:

  • It is on by default on brokers since 4.0, but the client must opt in with group.protocol=consumer. The client default is still classic. The Kafka docs say the plan (KIP-1274) is for the Java consumer to default to the new protocol in Kafka 5.0 and to support only it in 6.0.
  • With group.protocol=consumer, the client settings heartbeat.interval.ms, session.timeout.ms and partition.assignment.strategy are not usable. Timeouts become broker settings (group.consumer.session.timeout.ms, 45 s default), and the assignor is chosen on the broker (uniform by default, or range).
  • max.poll.interval.ms still applies: a consumer that stops calling poll still loses its partitions.
  • You can migrate a live group with a rolling restart: when the first new-protocol consumer joins, the group is converted, and old classic members keep working through it (as long as the old assignor does not use custom metadata). Custom client-side assignors are not supported in the new protocol yet.

Static membership: no rebalance for a quick restart SDE-2

In Kubernetes, a rolling deploy restarts every consumer pod. With normal (dynamic) members, each restart is a leave plus a join, so two rebalances per pod. Setting group.instance.id (for example to the pod name of a StatefulSet, like billing-0) makes the consumer a static member (KIP-345). If it comes back with the same ID before the session timeout, it simply gets its old partitions back, with no rebalance at all. See Deployments for how rolling updates work on the Kubernetes side.

Careful: static membership trades fast restarts for slow failure detection. If a static member really dies, its partitions sit unread until the session timeout expires. Pick a session timeout a bit longer than your typical restart, not "10 minutes just in case".
