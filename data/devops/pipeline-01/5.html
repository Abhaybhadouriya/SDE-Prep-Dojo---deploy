Partitions and consumer groups: why 6?

A partition is the unit of parallelism. Inside one consumer group, each partition is read by exactly one member. So the partition count is the maximum number of members that can do useful work. Six partitions means: one enricher handles everything; two split 3/3; three split 2/2/2; six get one each; a seventh would sit idle.

How the number 6 was picked, the way you would explain it in an interview:

  1. Throughput need. A laptop demo needs a few hundred orders per second at most. One partition could do that. So throughput is not the reason.
  2. Parallelism need. We want to demonstrate scaling the enricher to 2, 3 and 6 instances and see a clean split. 6 divides evenly by 1, 2, 3 and 6.
  3. Room to grow. Because adding partitions remaps keys, we pick a number slightly larger than today's need. In production people commonly start with a count that covers a year or two of expected growth (a rule of thumb, not a law).
  4. Not too many. Each partition costs open files, memory, replication traffic and recovery time. Hundreds per topic for a small service is waste.

The production formula from the Kafka research notes: partitions should be at least the larger of (target throughput / throughput one partition can take from a producer) and (target throughput / throughput one consumer can process). Measure the per-partition numbers on your own hardware; do not copy someone else's.

Still, you need a sense of size. The Kafka operations docs describe one of LinkedIn's busiest clusters at peak:

60brokers
50kpartitions (replication factor 2)
800kmessages/s in
300 MB/sinbound
1 GB/s+outbound (many consumer groups)

Source: Kafka docs, Java version / LinkedIn reference cluster.

Rule of thumb, measure yourself (an estimate, not a spec): divide those numbers and you get about 5 MB/s inbound per broker and only about 16 messages per second per partition on average. Real clusters have many quiet partitions; one partition can carry far more than that. So for magnitude: a laptop demo at a few hundred orders per second is tiny for Kafka, and the first thing to run out is usually your consumer's per-record work (a database call, an HTTP call), not the partition. Measure both sides with kafka-producer-perf-test.sh and kafka-consumer-perf-test.sh before you size a real topic.
