The story: a post office with sorting rooms

Explain like I'm 5

Picture a post office. At the front desk, a clerk takes your letter, puts a stamp with a number on it, and drops it into one of six chutes. Which chute? The clerk looks at your name, and the same name always goes down the same chute. That way, if you send three letters, they come out at the other end in the order you sent them.

Below the chutes is a sorting room. Three sorters each watch two chutes. They read each letter, write the postage price on it, and put it on the "ready" belt. If a letter has a smudged address that might dry and become readable, it goes on the "try again later" shelf with a note saying when. If the address is plain nonsense, it goes into the "dead letters" bin, with a note saying what was wrong.

At the end of the "ready" belt, two different teams take copies: one team files every letter in the big cabinet (the record), the other team counts stamps per minute for the manager's wall chart. And every chute is copied into three separate buildings, so a fire in one building loses nothing.

Every part of that story is a real design decision in ShopStream. The chutes are partitions. "Same name, same chute" is key-based partitioning. The three buildings are replication factor 3. "Try again later" is a retry topic, the bin is a dead-letter topic (DLQ), and the two teams at the end are two independent consumer groups. On this page we go through each decision, slowly, and then turn it into interview answers.

From your world

Designing a Kafka pipeline feels a lot like designing a forwarding path. You pick a hash key so a flow stays on one path and stays in order (ECMP 5-tuple hashing, here the customer id). You decide how many parallel lanes exist (ECMP members, here partitions). You decide what happens to packets that cannot be forwarded (punt or drop with a counter, here the DLQ with error headers). And you decide where state must be replicated before you acknowledge (like waiting for the standby supervisor to sync before declaring a config committed, here acks=all with min.insync.replicas=2). Where it breaks: a router drops under pressure; Kafka never drops, it stores, and the reader falls behind.
