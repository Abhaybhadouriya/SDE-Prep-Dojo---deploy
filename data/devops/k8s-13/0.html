How Kubernetes shows up in SDE interviews

Explain like I'm 5

Imagine a spelling test where the teacher does not ask you to spell every word in the dictionary. She asks a few common words, then one hard word, then "use it in a sentence". Kubernetes interviews are like that. A few basics, one or two "how does it really work" questions, and then "how would you use it to run this system?"

Be honest with yourself about where these questions come from. In generalist SDE-1 and SDE-2 loops at Microsoft, Amazon, Google, Flipkart or Walmart, Kubernetes usually appears in three places:

  1. The resume deep-dive. "You wrote that you deployed on Kubernetes. Walk me through it." They test whether you really did it.
  2. Inside a system design (HLD) round. "How would you deploy, scale and roll out this service?" Kubernetes is a tool in your design, not the topic.
  3. Platform, infra and cloud teams (Azure AKS, AWS EKS, Google GKE, internal platform teams at Flipkart, Walmart, Uber). Here they go deep: networking, scheduling, control-plane scale, operators. Your kernel and networking background is a real advantage on these teams.

From your world

Your best interview move is translation. "The Kubernetes control plane is like a router's route processor and the nodes are line cards: if the control plane fails, running pods keep forwarding, like non-stop forwarding. Controllers are reconciliation loops, like a routing daemon keeping the FIB in sync with the RIB. A ClusterIP is a virtual IP implemented as DNAT rules on every node." One sentence like that, said early, tells the interviewer you understand the mechanism, not just the vocabulary. Where this breaks: do not stay in your world for long. Translate, then use the Kubernetes words.
