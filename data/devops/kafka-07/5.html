Retention: deleting whole notebooks

must know

Kafka is not a queue that deletes a message once someone reads it. Records stay until a retention rule removes them, whether anyone read them or not. With cleanup.policy=delete (the default), there are two rules, and whichever triggers first wins:

  • Time: retention.ms (default 7 days; broker-wide log.retention.hours=168). A segment is deletable when its newest record is older than this.
  • Size: retention.bytes (default -1, unlimited), measured per partition, not per topic.

A few consequences that bite people:

  1. Retention is "at least", not "exactly". The active segment is never deleted. If a low-traffic topic's active segment holds 6 days of data and rolls only every 7 days, records can stay around for almost two weeks. Lower segment.ms if you need tighter deletion (for example for privacy rules).
  2. Slow consumers can fall off the end. If a consumer group is 8 days behind on a 7-day topic, its next fetch asks for an offset that no longer exists. The consumer gets "offset out of range" and falls back to auto.offset.reset (default latest, which silently skips data). Alert on lag measured in time, not just record count.
  3. Deletion is delayed a little. A deletable segment is first renamed with a .deleted suffix, then removed after file.delete.delay.ms (default 1 minute), so in-flight reads finish cleanly.
