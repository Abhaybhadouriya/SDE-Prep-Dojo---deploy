Data transfer: the bill follows the route table

Good to know (SDE-2)

Data transfer is where your networking background pays off directly. In AWS, which path a byte takes decides what it costs. Inbound from the internet is generally free. Typical charges:

  • Out to the internet: charged per GB after a free 100 GB per month (pooled across services and Regions).
  • Between Availability Zones in the same Region: charged per GB, in each direction. Chatty microservices spread across three AZs pay this on every call.
  • Between Regions: charged per GB, more than cross-AZ.
  • Through a NAT gateway: an hourly charge plus a per-GB processing charge (the AWS pricing example uses $0.045 per hour and $0.045 per GB), on top of any transfer charge.
  • Through a gateway VPC endpoint (S3 and DynamoDB): no hourly and no data processing charge.

From your world

This is plain longest-prefix match. The private subnet's route table has 0.0.0.0/0 -> nat-xxxx. Creating an S3 gateway endpoint inserts a route whose destination is a managed prefix list (pl-xxxx, the S3 address ranges for the Region) pointing at vpce-xxxx. Those prefixes are more specific than /0, so they win, exactly like a static more-specific route steering traffic off a metered transit link onto a free peering link. Cross-AZ charges are the cloud equivalent of paying for traffic that crosses your inter-POP backbone: keep chatty flows inside one AZ (topology-aware routing in Kubernetes, AZ-local read replicas) the way you keep east-west traffic inside a rack.

$ aws ec2 create-vpc-endpoint --vpc-id vpc-0abc1234 \
    --service-name com.amazonaws.ap-south-1.s3 --vpc-endpoint-type Gateway \
    --route-table-ids rtb-0priv1a rtb-0priv1b --query 'VpcEndpoint.State'
"available"
$ aws ec2 describe-route-tables --route-table-ids rtb-0priv1a \
    --query 'RouteTables[0].Routes[].[DestinationCidrBlock||DestinationPrefixListId,GatewayId||NatGatewayId]' --output text
10.0.0.0/16     local
0.0.0.0/0       nat-0de12f34a56b78c90
pl-78a54011     vpce-0f1e2d3c4b5a69788

Other levers: put a CDN in front of heavy egress, let Kafka consumers fetch from a same-AZ replica (client.rack), and compress logs and batches with zstd or gzip.

Interviewers love this. "Our NAT gateway line jumped 5x after we moved logs to S3." The strong answer: the nodes are in private subnets, so S3 traffic hairpins through the NAT and pays per-GB processing; add an S3 gateway endpoint to the private route tables, then confirm in VPC Flow Logs or Cost Explorer (usage type NatGateway-Bytes) that the bytes moved.
