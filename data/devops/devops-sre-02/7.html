Idle and orphaned resources

Must know (SDE-1)

Some of the cheapest savings come from deleting things nobody uses. These are the usual suspects, and how to find them from the CLI:

ResourceWhy it billsHow to find it
Unattached EBS volumesLeft behind when instances are terminated without delete-on-terminationdescribe-volumes with status=available
Old snapshots and AMIsEvery snapshot is billed for its stored blocksSnapshots older than your retention, AMIs no launch template uses
Unused public IPv4 addressesAWS charges $0.005 per hour for every public IPv4 address, in use or idledescribe-addresses with no association
Idle load balancersHourly charge even with zero targetsLoad balancers with no healthy targets or near-zero request count
Forgotten dev environmentsFull stacks running nights and weekendsenv=dev tag + schedule them to stop outside working hours
Idle NAT gatewaysHourly charge per gateway per AZ, plus per-GB processingNAT gateways in VPCs with no active workloads

Do not delete blindly. The safe workflow is tag, notify, wait, snapshot, delete: tag the candidate with a deletion date, message the owner, wait a week, take a final snapshot for anything with data, then delete. Automate the reporting; keep a human on the delete for anything in production.

Turn off what sleeps. A dev cluster used 10 hours a day on weekdays runs about 50 of 168 hours in a week. Scaling its node groups to zero outside working hours removes most of its compute cost without touching code.
