Where cloud money actually goes

Must know (SDE-1)

A cloud bill is not one number. It is millions of tiny line items: instance-seconds, GB-months of storage, GB of data moved, requests, IP-address hours. For most product companies they fall into a handful of buckets. The shares below are illustrative for a typical web backend on AWS, not a statistic; your own Cost Explorer view is the only real number.

Three ideas explain almost every bill:

  1. You pay for what you reserve, not what you use. An m7i.2xlarge costs the same per hour at 5% CPU or 95% CPU. A Kubernetes node is paid for in full even if pods only request half of it.
  2. Price depends on how you buy. The same machine can be bought On-Demand (full price, no promise), with a 1- or 3-year commitment (discount for a promise), or as Spot (deep discount, can be taken back).
  3. Everything that persists keeps billing. A stopped instance stops compute charges, but its EBS volume, its snapshots and its Elastic IP keep billing until you delete them.
