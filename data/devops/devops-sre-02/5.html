Reserved Instances vs Savings Plans

Good to know (SDE-2)

For load that runs 24/7, you can trade a promise for a discount. You commit to 1 or 3 years and pay All upfront, Partial upfront or No upfront (more upfront means a bigger discount).

OptionYou commit toFlexibilityMax discount (AWS docs)
Compute Savings PlanA $/hour spend on computeAny EC2 family, size, OS, tenancy or Region, plus Fargate and LambdaUp to 66%
EC2 Instance Savings PlanA $/hour spend on one family in one Region (for example m7i in Mumbai)Any size, OS or tenancy inside that family and RegionUp to 72%
Database Savings PlanA $/hour spend on managed databasesRDS, Aurora, DynamoDB, ElastiCache and others, across engines and RegionsUp to 35%
Reserved Instances (EC2, RDS, ElastiCache, OpenSearch...)A specific instance type (and for zonal RIs, an AZ, which also reserves capacity)Standard: fixed; Convertible: can be exchangedSimilar to the matching Savings Plan

For new EC2 compute most teams prefer Savings Plans because they follow you across instance families, Regions and even to Fargate. Reserved Instances still matter for services Savings Plans did not cover historically, and a zonal RI or an On-Demand Capacity Reservation is the way to guarantee capacity in an AZ, which a Savings Plan never does. Note that Savings Plans cover the EC2 instances under EKS, ECS and EMR, but not the EKS control-plane fee.

How much to commit: the coverage and utilisation pair

  • Coverage = share of eligible usage paid at the discounted rate. Low coverage means you pay On-Demand for steady load.
  • Utilisation = share of the commitment actually used. Below 100% means you paid for nothing.

A safe recipe: right-size first (otherwise you lock in waste), look at 30-60 days of hourly usage, commit to roughly the steady minimum, then buy more in small slices every month or quarter (laddering) instead of one big purchase. Cost Explorer generates recommendations from your history:

$ aws ce get-savings-plans-purchase-recommendation \
    --savings-plans-type COMPUTE_SP --term-in-years ONE_YEAR \
    --payment-option NO_UPFRONT --lookback-period-in-days THIRTY_DAYS \
    --query 'SavingsPlansPurchaseRecommendation.SavingsPlansPurchaseRecommendationSummary.[HourlyCommitmentToPurchase,EstimatedSavingsPercentage,EstimatedMonthlySavingsAmount]'
[
    "4.21",
    "23.8",
    "731.55"
]

From your world

This is CIR sizing for a WAN link. You buy committed bandwidth at the 95th-percentile floor you are sure of, not at the peak; peaks ride the burst rate. Commit too high and you pay for idle capacity for the whole contract; too low and you pay premium rates every day. A Compute Savings Plan is like a carrier contract priced in dollars rather than in a specific port: it follows you if you move from a 10G port to a 25G port (c5 to m7g) or to another POP (another Region).
