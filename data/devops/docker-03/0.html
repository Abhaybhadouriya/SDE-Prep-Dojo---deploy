The story: a recipe card and a baker with a good memory

Explain like I'm 5

Grandma writes a recipe card: "1. Take a big bowl. 2. Add flour. 3. Add sugar. 4. Add chocolate chips. 5. Bake." Anyone who follows the card gets the same cookies.

Our baker is clever. After each step, she takes a photo of the bowl. Next time she bakes, she checks: "Is step 1 the same as last time? Yes, use the photo. Step 2 the same? Yes, use the photo." The moment a step is different, say you changed the chips to raisins at step 4, she must do step 4 again, and every step after it, because the bowl is now different.

So the smart trick is: put the steps that almost never change (bowl, flour, sugar) at the top, and the step you change all the time (which chips today?) near the bottom. Then most of the baking is just looking at old photos.

A Dockerfile is that recipe card. It is a plain text file, usually named Dockerfile, with one instruction per line. docker build follows it top to bottom and produces an image. Each step that changes files becomes a layer, and the "photo" is the build cache: if a step and everything before it are unchanged, Docker reuses the old layer instantly.

That single idea, order your steps from rarely-changing to often-changing, is the difference between a 5-second rebuild and a 5-minute one. The rest of this page builds up from there.
