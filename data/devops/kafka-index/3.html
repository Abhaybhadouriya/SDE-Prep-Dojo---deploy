What you will be able to do

Each rung is something an interviewer can actually ask you to do on a whiteboard.

1

Explain why anyone needs a message system

Sync vs async, coupling, absorbing traffic spikes, fan-out to many readers. Also when you should not use Kafka.

2

Draw a topic with partitions, offsets and brokers

And explain why ordering is guaranteed only inside one partition, and how a key picks the partition.

3

Reason about safety settings

acks, min.insync.replicas, replication factor, ISR, consumer commits - and say exactly which setting loses data in which failure.

4

Explain the internals

Why Kafka is fast (sequential I/O, page cache, zero-copy sendfile, batching), what is on disk, how KRaft elects a controller, how exactly-once really works.

5

Use Kafka in a system design round

Pick a partition key, size partitions, design retries and a dead-letter topic, fix the dual-write problem with an outbox, and compare Kafka with RabbitMQ, SQS and Kinesis in one sentence each.

Honest note about interviews: Kafka shows up in SDE-1/2 loops in three ways. (1) Inside an HLD round, as the queue between services in designs like a notification system, order pipeline or click analytics. (2) In a resume deep-dive, if your resume says Kafka. (3) As a direct question, often "Kafka vs RabbitMQ" or "how do you avoid duplicates". Level 2-3 is enough for most SDE-1 loops. SDE-2 loops expect Level 4 answers on delivery guarantees and failure modes.
