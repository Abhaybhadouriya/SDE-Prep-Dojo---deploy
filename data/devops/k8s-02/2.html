From your world: Kubernetes is a router

Must know (SDE-1)

If you have built software for a chassis router or a big switch, you already understand 80% of this page. Here is the same picture drawn twice.

Router idea you knowKubernetes equivalentWhere the analogy breaks
Running-config in a config databaseObjects in etcdetcd is replicated across 3 or 5 machines with Raft consensus, not mirrored active/standby.
Management plane (CLI, NETCONF, gNMI)kube-apiserver (REST + watch)Every internal component also uses this same API. There is no private back channel.
Routing protocols compute the RIBScheduler and controllers compute "what should exist where"They do not talk to each other; they communicate only by writing objects.
RIB-to-FIB download to line cardskubelet watches pods bound to its node and programs the runtimeThe kubelet pulls (watches) its work; nobody pushes to it.
NAT / ACL tables on every line cardkube-proxy rules on every nodeRules are rebuilt from Service and EndpointSlice objects, not from a CLI.
Non-stop forwarding during RP switchoverPods keep running if the control plane is downNo new scheduling, scaling or healing happens until it returns.
Hello / dead timers, BFDNode Lease heartbeats, 50 s grace period, 300 s eviction tolerationTimers are tuned for safety, not speed: seconds to minutes, not milliseconds.
Line card inserted into a slotNode joins the clusterNodes are general Linux servers that come and go; there are no fixed slots.

From your world

You might ask: "if the control plane dies, do my running pods die too? I suspect not." Your instinct is right: no. The kubelet keeps the containers it already knows about running, and kube-proxy's NAT rules stay in the kernel, so traffic keeps flowing. That is non-stop forwarding. What you lose is everything that needs a decision: no new pods, no rescheduling after a node failure, no scaling, no rollouts, and kubectl stops working. Like a router with a dead RP: forwarding continues on the last FIB, but no route changes are learned.
