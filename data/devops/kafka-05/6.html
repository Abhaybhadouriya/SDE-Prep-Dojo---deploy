"Can Kafka lose data?" - the honest list

must know

Yes, if you configure it to, or if the application misuses it. Every case below is a real interview favourite:

  1. RF = 1 One disk dies, the data is gone. (The default when you do not set RF.)
  2. acks=0 or acks=1 + leader crash The leader acked, then died before followers copied the record.
  3. acks=all + min.insync.replicas=1 The ISR had shrunk to the leader only, so "all" meant one copy.
  4. Unclean leader election enabled A stale replica became leader and the newer records vanished.
  5. Correlated failure All replicas in one rack or AZ, which lost power together (page cache not yet flushed).
  6. The producer ignored errors send() is asynchronous. If you never check the callback or the future, a failed send is silently dropped.
  7. Consumer-side "loss" Auto-commit before processing, or retention deleting records before a slow consumer read them. The data was in Kafka; your app lost it.

The durable recipe in one line: RF=3 across 3 AZs, min.insync.replicas=2, producer acks=all with idempotence on (the default since 3.0), unclean election off, check every send callback, consumers commit after processing. Delivery guarantees end to end are on Delivery Semantics.
