A broker dies: what happens, step by step

must know

In Kafka 4.x the cluster has a controller quorum (KRaft). Each broker sends a heartbeat to the active controller every broker.heartbeat.interval.ms (2 s). If the controller hears nothing for broker.session.timeout.ms (9 s by default), the broker's lease expires and it is fenced. Two words: the lease is the 9 s window that each heartbeat renews; fenced means the controller records that this broker may not lead or serve partitions until it re-registers. Click through:

  1. Healthy. Each broker leads one partition. The producer writes P0 to broker 1. All brokers heartbeat to the active controller.
  2. Broker 1 dies. Its process is gone. Requests to it time out. Nothing has been decided yet: P0 has no working leader, so P0 is briefly unavailable.
  3. Detection. The controller gets no heartbeat for broker.session.timeout.ms (9 s) and fences broker 1. This detection delay is most of the failover time.
  4. Election. For every partition broker 1 led, the controller chooses a new leader from the ISR (here broker 2 for P0), bumps the leader epoch, removes broker 1 from the ISRs and writes all of this to the __cluster_metadata log.
  5. Clients move. Brokers replay the metadata log and act on it. A producer still talking to broker 1 gets an error such as NOT_LEADER_OR_FOLLOWER, refreshes its metadata and retries to broker 2. With acks=all and min ISR 2, writes continue on the 2 remaining replicas.
  6. Broker 1 returns. It may have records at the end of its log that never got committed. It asks the leader where its last epoch ended, truncates anything after that point, then fetches until it is in sync and rejoins the ISR.
  7. Rebalance leaders. The first broker in a partition's replica list is its preferred leader. With auto.leader.rebalance.enable=true (default) the controller checks every 300 s (leader.imbalance.check.interval.seconds) and moves leadership back when a broker's share of non-preferred leaders exceeds leader.imbalance.per.broker.percentage (10% by default), so one broker does not end up leading everything. You can also run kafka-leader-election.sh --election-type PREFERRED.

From your world

broker.heartbeat.interval.ms / broker.session.timeout.ms (2 s / 9 s) are a routing protocol's hello interval / dead interval, or BFD's transmit interval times its multiplier. Detection time is roughly the dead interval: the controller only acts once 9 s pass with no hello. Consumers have the same pair (heartbeat.interval.ms 3 s / session.timeout.ms 45 s, see rebalancing). Where it breaks: Kafka timeouts are seconds, not BFD's milliseconds, because a false positive is expensive: it moves partition leaders or triggers a consumer rebalance, and a GC pause or a slow disk would cause one constantly at millisecond timers.

Interviewers love this: say who notices (the controller, via missing broker heartbeats), how long it takes (session timeout, about 9 s by default, plus metadata propagation), what it writes (new leader + bumped epoch + new ISR in the metadata log) and how clients react (error, metadata refresh, retry). That is the "never make me guess which process does what" answer.
