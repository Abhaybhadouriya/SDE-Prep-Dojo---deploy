What do acks=0, 1 and all mean, and why is acks=all not enough on its own?

acks=0 means the producer does not wait for any reply. acks=1 means the leader has appended the record to its log. acks=all means every replica currently in the ISR has it. The catch is that the ISR can shrink: if both followers fall behind, the ISR is just the leader, and acks=all gives you one copy. Setting min.insync.replicas=2 makes the leader reject writes when fewer than 2 replicas are in sync, so every acknowledged write exists on at least two brokers. With RF=3 that survives one broker failure with no data loss and no write outage.

How to answer

Give the three meanings in one breath, then go straight to the trap (shrinking ISR) and the fix (min ISR 2), then state the recipe RF=3/minISR=2/acks=all and what it tolerates.

Likely follow-ups
  • What error does the producer see when the ISR is too small?
  • Why not min.insync.replicas=3?
  • Does acks=1 mean the data is on disk?
Explain ISR and the high watermark. Why can't consumers read up to the leader's log end?

The ISR is the leader plus followers that have caught up within replica.lag.time.max.ms (30 s). Each replica has a log end offset; the high watermark is the minimum LEO across the ISR, so everything below it is on every in-sync replica. Consumers only read below the HW because records above it exist only on some replicas: if the leader died now, a new leader might not have them, and a consumer would have seen data that then "disappears". Reading only committed data keeps every consumer's view consistent across failovers.

How to answer

Define, then give the "what would go wrong otherwise" story. A tiny diagram of three logs with the HW line wins this question.

Likely follow-ups
  • How does the leader learn follower positions?
  • Is ISR lag measured in messages or time?
  • What is a leader epoch for?
A broker dies. Walk me through what happens.

The broker stops heartbeating to the active KRaft controller. After broker.session.timeout.ms (9 s default) the controller fences it. For each partition it led, the controller picks a new leader from the ISR (or the eligible leader replicas), bumps the leader epoch, shrinks the ISRs, and appends these changes to the metadata log. Brokers apply the new metadata; producers and consumers get "not leader" errors, refresh metadata and continue against the new leaders. The affected partitions are under-replicated; with RF 3 and min ISR 2, writes continue. When the broker comes back it truncates any uncommitted tail using leader epochs, catches up, rejoins the ISR, and preferred leader election later moves leadership back.

How to answer

Tell it as a timeline with named actors: detect (controller, heartbeat), decide (election from ISR, epoch), publish (metadata log), react (clients), heal (catch-up, preferred leader). Mention the 9 s detection delay unprompted.

Likely follow-ups
  • What if two brokers die?
  • What is unclean leader election?
  • How did this work with ZooKeeper?
Can Kafka lose acknowledged data? Give concrete cases.

Yes. With acks=1 the leader can ack and then die before followers copy the record. With acks=all but min.insync.replicas=1, the ISR can shrink to the leader alone. With RF=1, a disk failure loses data. Unclean leader election lets a stale replica lead and discards newer records. Replicas placed in one AZ can fail together. And on the application side, producers that ignore send errors or consumers that commit before processing lose data without Kafka being at fault. With RF=3 across AZs, min ISR 2, acks=all, idempotence and unclean election off, losing an acknowledged write needs two brokers to fail at almost the same moment.

How to answer

Do not say "Kafka never loses data". Split into broker-side and client-side causes, then give the config that closes each.

Likely follow-ups
  • Why does Kafka not fsync each message?
  • How would you test this (chaos drill)?
Why does Kafka use an ISR model for data instead of a majority quorum like Raft?

With a majority quorum you need 2f+1 replicas to tolerate f failures. Kafka's ISR model waits for all in-sync replicas, so f+1 replicas tolerate f failures for committed data: RF=3 tolerates 2 failures of committed records (as long as one ISR member survives) instead of 1. That saves disk and network, which matters for a high-throughput log. The cost is that a slow follower can hold back commits until it is removed from the ISR, and the ISR membership must be managed by a reliable controller. Kafka does use Raft, but for the metadata log in KRaft, where a small quorum and strong agreement matter more than throughput.

How to answer

Do the f+1 vs 2f+1 arithmetic out loud, then name the cost. Ending with "and Kafka does use Raft for metadata" shows you know both.

Likely follow-ups
  • What happens if a follower is slow but not dead?
  • What is ELR?

Scenario: "Producers fail with NotEnoughReplicas during a rolling upgrade"

During a broker upgrade, the payments service starts throwing NotEnoughReplicasException for a few minutes. Topic: RF 3, min ISR 2. The on-call engineer wants to set min ISR to 1 "so this never happens again".

Weak answer: "Yes, lower min.insync.replicas to 1, or switch the producer to acks=1, so writes never fail."

Strong answer: "The error is the safety net working: fewer than 2 replicas were in sync, probably because the next broker was restarted before the previous one had caught up, so two of three were out of the ISR at once. Lowering min ISR would trade this visible error for silent data loss on payments. The fix is in the process: restart one broker at a time and wait for UnderReplicatedPartitions to reach 0 before the next (Strimzi and Cruise Control automate this); give the producer enough delivery.timeout.ms to ride through a short leader move; and alert on URP and UnderMinIsrPartitionCount."

Likely follow-ups
  • Which metrics would you put on the upgrade dashboard?
  • How long can the producer retry before the user sees an error?

Your 60-second answer: "How does Kafka replication work?"

"Every partition has a replication factor, usually 3, on different brokers in different zones. One replica is the leader and takes all writes; the followers pull from it like consumers. The leader tracks which followers are caught up - the in-sync replica set - and the high watermark, the lowest log end across the ISR. Only records below the high watermark are committed and readable. Producers choose acks: with acks=all and min.insync.replicas=2, every acknowledged write is on at least two brokers, and the cluster still takes writes with one broker down. When a broker dies, the KRaft controller notices missing heartbeats after about 9 seconds, elects new leaders from the ISR with a bumped leader epoch, and clients refresh metadata and retry. Unclean election is off by default, so Kafka prefers being unavailable to losing committed data."
