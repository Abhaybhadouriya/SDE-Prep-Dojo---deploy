Leader epoch: who was leader when this was written?

Every time a partition gets a new leader, its leader epoch goes up by one. Every record batch is stamped with the epoch of the leader that wrote it, and each replica keeps a small file (leader-epoch-checkpoint) mapping "epoch N started at offset X". This was added by KIP-101 because the older method (truncate to the high watermark after a restart) could lose committed data or leave two replicas with different records at the same offset after quick back-to-back failures.

From your world

A leader epoch is a generation number / term, like a Raft term, or the stale-path marking in BGP graceful restart: a node that comes back sees a newer generation and knows its own extra state is stale. Where it breaks: Kafka's data replication is not Raft (no majority vote per record). Only the metadata (KRaft) uses Raft; partition data uses the ISR model.

Why not majority quorum like Raft?

A Raft-style log with 2f+1 replicas tolerates f failures. Kafka's ISR model with f+1 replicas can tolerate f failures for committed data, because the leader waits for all in-sync replicas, not a majority. The cost is that one slow follower can delay commits until it is dropped from the ISR (30 s). The Kafka design doc discusses this trade-off: fewer copies for the same safety, in exchange for depending on the ISR being managed well.

Unclean leader election: availability vs data

What if every ISR member is dead and only an out-of-sync replica is alive? Two choices:

Wait (default: unclean.leader.election.enable=false)
  • No committed data is lost.
  • The partition stays offline until an ISR member returns.
  • Right for orders, payments, ledgers.
Elect the stale replica (true)
  • The partition is back online immediately.
  • Records it never received are gone for good, and offsets may be reused.
  • Sometimes acceptable for logs or metrics.

This is the CAP trade-off made visible in one config flag. The bridge: promoting a stale standby and accepting a route-table rollback.

Eligible Leader Replicas (KIP-966)

Newer Kafka adds a middle ground. When the ISR has dropped below min.insync.replicas, the high watermark cannot advance (no new records become committed), so a replica that leaves the ISR at that point still has every committed record. Kafka remembers such replicas as ELR (eligible leader replicas; the Elr: column in the describe output). On election the controller tries the ISR first, then the ELR, and only then, if unclean election is enabled, the last known leader. ELR is available from 4.0 and on by default for new clusters from 4.1. The Kafka 4.4 docs also say records become visible to consumers only when they are on all in-sync replicas and the min ISR condition is met, whatever the acks setting.
