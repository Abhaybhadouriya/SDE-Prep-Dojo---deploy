Leaders, followers and the replication factor

must know

When you create a topic you choose a replication factor (RF): how many copies of each partition exist. With RF = 3, every partition has 3 replicas on 3 different brokers. For each partition:

  • One replica is the leader. Producers write to it. By default, consumers read from it too.
  • The others are followers. They do not take client writes. They fetch from the leader, exactly like a consumer does, and append what they get to their own copy of the log.
  • Leaders are spread across brokers, so every broker is leader for some partitions and follower for others. The work is shared.

From your world

You asked: "Is leader/follower/ISR like VRRP or a redundancy pair?" Yes, quite closely. The ISR is the set of standbys that are allowed to become active, because their state is in sync. Kafka does this per partition, so one broker is "active" for some partitions and "standby" for others at the same time, like running many VRRP groups with different masters on the same pair of routers to share load.

Where it breaks: VRRP elects the master among the routers themselves with priorities and adverts. Kafka followers never elect themselves: a separate controller quorum (KRaft, see KRaft & Controllers) picks the leader and writes the decision to a replicated metadata log. That avoids two masters (split brain).

Durability comes from copies, not from fsync

A surprise for kernel people: by default Kafka does not call fsync for every message. A write lands in the leader's OS page cache, and the kernel flushes it to disk later. Kafka is safe anyway because the record is also in the memory of other machines. Losing all three replicas' page caches at the same instant needs three servers to lose power together, which is why you place replicas in different racks or availability zones.

From your world

Think RAID-1 across machines, or a control plane that acks a route only once N route processors have it in their RIB. acks=all + min.insync.replicas=2 means "commit when a quorum of up-to-date copies has it". Where it breaks: RAID mirrors synchronously at the block layer; Kafka followers pull asynchronously, and the leader decides when "everyone in sync has it".
