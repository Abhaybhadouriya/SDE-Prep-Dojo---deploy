Quick quiz

Replica LEOs in the ISR are 120, 118 and 121. What is the high watermark?

  1. 121
  2. 118
  3. 120
  4. 359
HW is the minimum LEO across the ISR: 118. Consumers can read offsets up to 117.

RF=3, min.insync.replicas=2, acks=all. Two brokers go down. What happens to writes?

  1. They succeed with one copy
  2. They succeed but are not visible
  3. They fail with NotEnoughReplicas
Only one replica can be in the ISR, which is below the minimum of 2, so the leader rejects acks=all writes.

When is a follower removed from the ISR?

  1. When it has not caught up to the leader's log end for replica.lag.time.max.ms (30 s)
  2. When it is more than 1,000 messages behind
  3. When a consumer reads from it
ISR membership is time-based, not count-based.

With acks=1, what does the acknowledgement guarantee?

  1. The record is fsynced on 3 disks
  2. The leader appended it to its log (page cache)
  3. All followers have it
acks=1 waits for the leader's append only. If the leader dies before followers fetch, the record can be lost.

Who chooses the new leader when a broker dies in Kafka 4.x?

  1. The followers vote among themselves
  2. ZooKeeper
  3. The active KRaft controller
ZooKeeper was removed in 4.0. The active controller elects leaders and records the decision in the metadata log.

What does enabling unclean leader election trade?

  1. Throughput for latency
  2. Disk space for memory
  3. Ordering for parallelism
  4. Possible data loss for faster availability
An out-of-sync replica can become leader so the partition comes back online, but records it never received are lost.

What problem do leader epochs solve?

  1. Knowing exactly where a returning replica must truncate its uncommitted tail
  2. Encrypting replication traffic
  3. Balancing consumers
Epochs (KIP-101) let a replica find where its history diverged from the current leader, avoiding loss and divergence.
