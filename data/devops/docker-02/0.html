The story: a stack of see-through stickers

Explain like I'm 5

Take a clear plastic sheet and draw a house on it. Put a second clear sheet on top and draw a tree. Put a third on top and draw the sun. When you look down through all three, you see one picture: a house, a tree and the sun.

Now here is the rule: you can only ever add a sheet on top. You can never erase a lower sheet. If you want the tree gone, you stick a white patch over it on a new top sheet. The tree is hidden, but it is still there underneath, and your stack is now one sheet thicker, not thinner.

A Docker image is exactly this: a stack of see-through sheets called layers. Each layer adds or changes some files. Looking through all of them gives you one complete set of files.

There is a second part to the story. When you play with the picture, you do not draw on the stack itself. You lay one more sheet of tracing paper on top and draw on that. When you are done, you throw the tracing paper away and the stack is like new. Your friend can take the same stack and lay their own tracing paper on it at the same time.

That tracing paper is the container layer. Every container gets its own thin writable sheet on top of the shared, read-only image layers. This is why ten containers from one image do not need ten copies of the image.

And one last picture: a cake-mix packet. The Dockerfile is the recipe (next page), the image is the packet you can copy and share forever, and the container is the cake you actually bake. One packet, many cakes.
