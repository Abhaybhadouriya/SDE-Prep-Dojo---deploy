Approaches: brute -> better -> optimal

Hint 1

Write the profit of one trade bought on day i and sold on day j as a sum of daily changes.

Hint 2

If you can trade every day, which of those daily changes would you ever want to skip?

1. Brute force - try every decision

Idea: recurse over days with a flag "holding or not"; on each day choose buy/sell or rest. Two choices per day gives O(2n). Memoising on (day, holding) already turns it into the DP below.

O(2n)time
O(n)recursion

2. Better - state-machine DP (cash / hold)

Idea: keep two numbers while scanning: cash = best profit with no share after today, hold = best profit while holding a share. Each day: cash = max(cash, hold + p) (sell or rest), hold = max(hold, cash - p) (buy or rest). Start with cash = 0, hold = -infinity; the answer is cash.

O(n)time
O(1)space

Why mention it: it is correct by construction (it considers every legal plan) and every follow-up is a one-line change: subtract a fee on sell, read cash from two days ago for a cooldown, or keep k pairs of states for k trades.

3. Optimal - sum of positive differences (greedy)

profit = 0
for d in range(1, len(prices)):
    profit += max(0, prices[d] - prices[d - 1])
return profit
O(n)time
O(1)space

Why it works: one trade from day i to day j earns p[j] - p[i] = (p[i+1]-p[i]) + (p[i+2]-p[i+1]) + ... + (p[j]-p[j-1]) (telescoping). Trades cannot overlap, so any plan earns a sum of daily changes over disjoint day ranges, each change used at most once. That sum can never beat "take every positive change, no negative one". And that best case is achievable: buy before each rising day and sell after it, which is legal because you may sell and buy again on the same day. So the greedy total is both an upper bound and reachable. (It equals the peak-valley plan: a run of rises is the one trade valley -> peak.)
