60-second recall

  • Cue: unlimited trades, one share, no fee -> sum of positive daily changes.
  • Proof: a trade i -> j telescopes into its daily changes; take all positive ones, none negative.
  • DP: cash = max(cash, hold + p), hold = max(hold, cash - p); answer cash.
  • Variants: fee -> subtract on sell; cooldown -> buy from cash two days back; k trades -> k state pairs.
  • Cost: O(n) time, O(1) space; int is enough (max 3·108).
profit = 0
for d in range(1, n):
    if p[d] > p[d-1]: profit += p[d] - p[d-1]
return profit

What is the answer for prices [3, 1, 4, 1, 5]?

  1. 4
  2. 5
  3. 7
  4. 8
Positive changes: 1 -> 4 (+3) and 1 -> 5 (+4), total 7.

Why does summing positive daily changes give the optimum?

  1. A trade i -> j telescopes into daily changes, and no plan can use a change twice
  2. Prices are always increasing
  3. It is the DP with a fee of 0 only by coincidence
  4. Because n is small
Upper bound: a plan uses disjoint ranges of daily changes. Reachable: trade every rising day.

Which variant breaks the greedy?

  1. Allowing same-day sell and buy
  2. A fee per transaction
  3. Prices up to 105
  4. A single day of prices
With a fee, many small trades each pay it; merging them can be better, so you need the DP.
