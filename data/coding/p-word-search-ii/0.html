The story

Explain like I'm 5

You have a letter grid from a puzzle magazine and a shopping list of 30,000 words to circle. Checking the grid once per word takes forever. Instead you sort the list into a "starts-with" book: every word starting with "ca" is on one page, with sub-pages for "car" and "cat". Now you trace paths on the grid and flip pages as you go. The moment the book has no page for what you have traced, you stop that path. Once you have circled every word on a page, you tear the page out.

From your world

This is the difference between matching packets against each ACL rule in turn and compiling the rules into one decision tree (or a prefix trie, like the kernel's FIB trie for routes). One walk of the input checks every rule at once, and a missing branch is a drop decision made after one lookup. Deleting finished branches is like removing one-shot rules once they have fired.
