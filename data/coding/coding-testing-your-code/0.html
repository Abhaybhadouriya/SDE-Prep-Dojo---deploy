The story: checking the cake before the party

Explain like I'm 5

You baked a cake from a new recipe. Before the guests come, you do three things. First you taste a small slice: is it sweet? Then you think about the guests who are different: the friend who cannot eat nuts, the baby who needs a tiny piece, the uncle who wants three pieces. Last, if something is wrong, you fix only that thing. You do not throw the whole cake away and bake a new one ten minutes before the party.

Testing code in an interview is the same. Taste one normal example by hand. Think about the "different guests": empty input, one item, repeated items, very big numbers. If one of them breaks the code, change the one line that is wrong and taste again.

In an interview nobody runs your code for you (or, on a shared editor that can run code, the interviewer still wants to see that you know what it will do). So "testing" means executing the code in your head and on the whiteboard, out loud, in a way the interviewer can follow. Done well, it takes three to five minutes and it is often the difference between "solved it" and "solved it and I trust this engineer".

From your world

You already do this for a living. Before a driver patch goes out you walk the error paths by hand (what if kmalloc fails here, what if the ring is full, what if the length field is zero), you think about wrap-around at the end of a descriptor ring, and you run kselftest or KUnit cases for the boundaries. Interview testing is the same instinct at a smaller scale: the "ring is full" case becomes "the array is empty", and the "length is 0xFFFF" case becomes "the sum overflows 32 bits".
