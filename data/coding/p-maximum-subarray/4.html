Approaches: brute → better → optimal

Hint 1

Instead of "the best subarray anywhere", ask "the best subarray that ends exactly at index i". How does it relate to the one ending at i-1?

Hint 2

If the best sum ending at i-1 is negative, should index i keep it?

1. Brute force - every start, running sum

Idea: for each start i, extend j to the right with a running sum and keep the maximum. (Re-summing each subarray from scratch would be O(n³).)

O(n²)time
O(1)space

What is wasted: the run that starts at i and the run that starts at i+1 share almost all their numbers, yet we add them again for each start.

2. Better - divide and conquer (the original's follow-up)

Idea: split the array at the middle. The best subarray is entirely in the left half, entirely in the right half, or crosses the middle. The crossing one is the best suffix of the left plus the best prefix of the right, found in O(n). Recurse on both halves.

O(n log n)time (T(n) = 2T(n/2) + O(n))
O(log n)stack

What is wasted: it recomputes the best prefix and suffix at every level. Returning four values per segment (total, best prefix, best suffix, best) makes it O(n), and that is the idea behind a segment tree for range queries. For one query, a left-to-right scan is simpler.

3. Optimal - Kadane (DP on "ends here")

cur = best = nums[0]          # core: 4 lines
for x in nums[1:]:
    cur = max(x, cur + x)     # extend, or start fresh at x
    best = max(best, cur)
  1. State: cur(i) = the largest sum of a subarray that ends exactly at i.
  2. Transition: cur(i) = max(nums[i], cur(i-1) + nums[i]). A subarray ending at i is either just [i] or a subarray ending at i-1 plus nums[i], and for the second kind the best one ending at i-1 is the best prefix to attach.
  3. Base case: cur(0) = nums[0], and best starts at nums[0] too (not 0 and not "minus infinity plus x" arithmetic).
  4. Answer: best = max over i of cur(i), because every subarray ends somewhere.
  5. Space optimisation: cur(i) needs only cur(i-1), so the dp array becomes one variable.
O(n)time, one pass
O(1)space

Why it works: the sum of nums[i..j] is P[j+1] - P[i] with prefix sums P. For a fixed end, the best start is the one with the smallest prefix before it. Kadane's cur is "current prefix minus the lowest prefix so far": restarting when cur + x < x is the same as "a new lowest prefix was just found". So one scan checks, for every end, the best possible start.

Proof (optional)

Induction on i. Any subarray ending at i is [i] or S + [i] for S ending at i-1. Its sum is nums[i] or sum(S) + nums[i] ≤ cur(i-1) + nums[i] by the hypothesis. Both bounds are reached ([i] itself, and the optimal S for i-1), so cur(i) is exactly the maximum of the two. Taking the maximum of cur(i) over all i covers every non-empty subarray.
