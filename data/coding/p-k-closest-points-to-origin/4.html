Approaches: brute -> better -> optimal

Hint 1

Do you need the square root to decide which of two points is closer?

Hint 2

You need the k best, unordered. Which element of your current k best is the one a new point must beat?

1. Brute force - sort by distance, take k

points.sort(key=lambda p: p[0] * p[0] + p[1] * p[1])
return points[:k]
O(n log n)time
O(n)or O(log n) for an in-place sort

What is wasted: it orders all n points although we need neither the order nor the far ones. It also needs every point up front. Perfectly fine to say and even to code first: two lines.

2. Better - max-heap of size k

for x, y in points:
    d = x * x + y * y
    if len(heap) < k: heappush(heap, (-d, x, y))
    elif d < -heap[0][0]: heapreplace(heap, (-d, x, y))
  1. The heap holds the k best seen so far. Its root is the worst of them (largest distance).
  2. A newcomer competes only with the root. If it is closer, the root cannot be in the final answer (k points beat it now): evict it. Otherwise skip in O(1).
  3. The heap never grows past k. Each operation is O(log k), not O(log n).
O(n log k)time
O(k)space

Why it works: invariant: after reading t points, the heap contains the k nearest among those t (or all t if t < k). A new point p enters only by displacing the current k-th nearest, which is exactly when p is nearer than it; otherwise the k nearest of the first t + 1 points are unchanged. At the end, t = n.

When it is the right answer: points arrive as a stream, n is huge and k small, or memory is limited to O(k). That is the version most interviewers want first.

3. Optimal (average) - quickselect on squared distance

while lo < hi:
    store = partition(pts, lo, hi)     # random pivot; closer points to the left
    if store == k - 1: break
    if store < k - 1: lo = store + 1
    else:             hi = store - 1
return pts[:k]
  1. Partition puts the pivot at its final rank. Everything left of it is closer, everything right is farther.
  2. Recurse into one side only. If the pivot's rank is k-1 we are done; otherwise the cut is on one side. Expected work n + n/2 + n/4 + ... = 2n.
  3. Pick the pivot at random. A fixed pivot gives O(n²) on sorted or adversarial input.
O(n)average time
O(n²)worst case
O(1)extra space (in place)
Proof (optional)

With a uniformly random pivot, the chance that the pivot lands in the middle half of the current range is 1/2, which shrinks the range to at most 3/4. So in expectation every two partitions shrink the range by 3/4, and the total work is a geometric series bounded by a constant times n. The O(n²) worst case needs consistently bad pivots, which has vanishing probability. Median-of-medians makes it worst-case O(n), but nobody codes that in an interview.
