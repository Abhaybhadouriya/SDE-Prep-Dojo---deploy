The story

Explain like I'm 5

A delivery hub sits at the centre of a city map, and orders come from many addresses. The dispatcher has k drones and wants the k nearest addresses. They do not need to rank the whole city. They keep a short list of k addresses on a clipboard and remember which one on the list is the farthest. Every new address is compared with that farthest one only: if it is nearer, it replaces it; if not, it is ignored.

From your world

This is the "top-k" job a kernel or a load balancer does all the time: keep the k least-loaded backends, the k oldest timers, the k heaviest flows. A heap is the standard structure for "the next one to act on": libevent and Go's runtime keep timers in a min-heap. The trick of comparing x² + y² instead of √(x² + y²) is the same as comparing squared magnitudes in fixed-point code to avoid a slow, inexact square root.
