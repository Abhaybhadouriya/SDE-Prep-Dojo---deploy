Longest window with a budget

Many "longest" questions hide a small budget. Max Consecutive Ones III (LC 1004): you may flip at most k zeros, so the window is valid while it holds at most k zeros. Fruit Into Baskets (LC 904): two baskets, one fruit type each, so the window is valid while it holds at most 2 distinct types. Longest Subarray of 1s After Deleting One Element (LC 1493) is LC 1004 with k = 1, minus one at the end because the deleted cell does not count.

The state is one counter (zeros, or a map of types). The loop is the "longest" shape. The only real work in the interview is the restatement: say "this is the longest window with at most k zeros" and the interviewer knows you have it.

The window that never shrinks

A well-known shortcut in the top LC 1004 solutions replaces the while with an if: when the window is invalid, move left once, together with right. The window then slides at its best size instead of shrinking, and the answer is the final right - left + 1 (or n - left). It is correct because we only ever care about beating the best length, and a window that is not growing cannot beat it. It is shorter, but the plain while version is easier to defend under questions. Give the while version first and mention the shortcut as an aside.

Longest Repeating Character Replacement (LC 424) uses the same never-shrink idea with a twist: it keeps maxFreq as the highest count ever seen and never lowers it when letters leave. That looks like a bug but is not: a stale maxFreq can only keep the window at its current size, never make the answer bigger than the truth. If you use it, say why.
