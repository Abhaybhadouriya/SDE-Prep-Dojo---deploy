The story

Explain like I'm 5

A warehouse floor is a square of tiles. Some tiles have boxes on them. A robot starts on the top-left tile and must reach the bottom-right tile, stepping to any of the 8 tiles around it (diagonals too), never onto a box. You pour water on the start tile: in one second it spreads to every free tile around it, in the next second one ring further, and so on. The second in which the water first touches the goal tile tells you the shortest walk.

From your world

This is hop-count routing. With every link cost equal to 1, a breadth-first flood from the source (like a TTL-limited flood, or RIP's distance vector with unit metrics) reaches each router first along a minimum-hop path. Dijkstra is only needed when link costs differ; with equal costs the FIFO queue is already the priority queue.
