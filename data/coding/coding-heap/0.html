The story: the hospital waiting room

Explain like I'm 5

In a hospital waiting room, people do not see the doctor in the order they came. The nurse always calls the most urgent person next. When a new patient walks in, the nurse does not re-sort the whole room. She only checks: "Is this person more urgent than the ones at the front?"

A priority queue is that nurse. It never keeps everyone in perfect order; it only keeps the most urgent person easy to find. That is why it is fast.

A heap is the usual way to build a priority queue. It gives you three operations:

  • push(x): add an item, O(log n).
  • peek(): look at the best item (smallest in a min-heap), O(1).
  • pop(): remove the best item, O(log n).

That is all. There is no fast "find any item" and no sorted walk. If an interview problem keeps asking "the smallest / largest / earliest / most frequent right now" while items keep arriving or leaving, a heap is the tool.

From your world

You already use priority queues in the kernel. CFS picks the task with the smallest virtual runtime from a red-black tree (leftmost node cached), and hrtimers keep timers ordered by expiry the same way. The timer wheel instead drops timers into buckets by tick: the same choice as "heap vs bucket sort" in Top-K problems (see the end of this page).
