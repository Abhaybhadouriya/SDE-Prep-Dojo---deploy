Quiz

You need the 10 largest values from a stream of a billion numbers. What do you keep?

  1. A min-heap of size 10
  2. A max-heap of size 10
  3. A sorted array of all numbers
  4. A max-heap of all numbers
The root of a size-10 min-heap is the smallest of the top 10, which is exactly the one to compare against and evict. O(n log 10) time and O(10) memory.

In the array form of a heap, where are the children of index 4?

  1. 8 and 9
  2. 2 and 3
  3. 9 and 10
  4. 5 and 6
Children of i are 2i + 1 and 2i + 2, so 9 and 10. Its parent is (4 - 1) / 2 = 1.

Merging k sorted lists with N elements in total using a heap costs:

  1. O(N log N)
  2. O(N log k)
  3. O(N k)
  4. O(k log N)
Each of the N elements is pushed and popped once on a heap that never holds more than k entries.

Tasks A A A A B B C, cooldown n = 2. What is the minimum time?

  1. 7
  2. 9
  3. 11
  4. 10
m = 4 (A), ties = 1: (4 - 1) x (2 + 1) + 1 = 10, and 10 > 7 tasks. Schedule: A B C A B _ A _ _ A.

In Meeting Rooms III all rooms are busy when a meeting starts. Which room does it get?

  1. The least used room
  2. The room that frees first (lowest number on ties), with its end extended by the meeting's duration
  3. Room 0
  4. It is dropped
The meeting waits for the earliest-ending busy room and keeps its length, so the new end is old end + duration. This is the busy heap's top.

Why does the Linux timer wheel use buckets rather than a heap?

  1. Heaps cannot hold timers
  2. Heaps need more than O(n) memory
  3. Buckets give exact ordering
  4. Most timers are cancelled before they fire, so O(1) insert and cancel matter more than exact order
Network and timeout timers are mostly cancelled. The wheel makes insert and cancel O(1) and gives up exact order within a bucket, the same deal bucket sort makes.
