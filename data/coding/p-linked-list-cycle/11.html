Return the node where the cycle begins (LC 142).

After slow and fast meet, move one pointer back to the head and advance both one step at a time; they meet at the entry. Reason: when they first meet, slow has walked μ + k and fast 2(μ + k), so μ + k is a multiple of λ. From the meeting point (k into the loop), walking μ more steps lands on k + μ, which is a multiple of λ from the entry: the entry itself.

How to answer

Write the two distances, derive "μ + k is a multiple of λ", then the second phase.

Likely follow-ups
  • Remove the loop: stop the second phase one step early on the loop side and set that node's next to null.
Find the length of the loop.

Once they meet, keep one pointer still and move the other one step at a time until it comes back; the number of steps is λ. O(λ) extra time, O(1) space.

How to answer

Reuse the meeting point; one more lap.

Likely follow-ups
  • Find μ (the part before the loop) with the LC 142 phase.
Why speed 2? Would speed 3 be faster?

The meeting is guaranteed when the relative speed is 1. With speeds 1 and 3 the gap shrinks by 2 per round; on a loop of even length with an odd starting gap, the two pointers are never on the same node, and the loop runs forever. Any pair works if you also compare at intermediate steps, but that is just speed 2 in disguise.

How to answer

Talk about relative speed and gap parity.

Likely follow-ups
  • Brent's algorithm: teleport slow to fast at powers of two; fewer pointer moves, also finds λ directly.
Can you do it by modifying the list?

Yes: reverse the list as you walk. If there is a loop, the reversal eventually brings you back to the head; or point each visited node's next at a sentinel node and detect landing on a node that already points at it. Both are O(n), O(1), but destroy (or need to restore) the list, which is rarely allowed.

How to answer

Mention it to show breadth, then say why Floyd is preferred.

Likely follow-ups
  • Restore the list afterwards: reverse again.
Detect a cycle in a directed graph instead of a list.

A node can have many outgoing edges, so two pointers are not enough. Use DFS with three colours (white, grey = on the current path, black = done): an edge to a grey node is a back edge, which means a cycle. Or Kahn's topological sort: if fewer than V nodes are removed, there is a cycle. O(V + E).

How to answer

Say why Floyd needs out-degree 1, then switch tools.

Likely follow-ups
  • LC 207 Course Schedule is exactly this check.
