Interview follow-ups

The words may contain any Unicode characters.

Replace the 26-slot arrays with hash maps. Compare the key sets directly and sort the value lists: O(n + k log k) for k distinct characters. The proof does not depend on the alphabet.

How to answer

Say that only the counting structure changes; the two invariants stay the same.

Likely follow-ups
  • Avoid the sort? Compare count-of-counts maps (how many letters have count c) in O(k).
They are close. Print one sequence of moves that turns word1 into word2.

Sort the present letters of each word by count and pair them up: the i-th letter of word1 must end up with the count of the i-th letter of word2. That pairing is a permutation of the letters; walk each of its cycles and apply one move 2 per swap (a cycle of length L takes L - 1 relabels). Now word1 is an anagram of word2: fix positions left to right, swapping the needed character into place (move 1), at most n - 1 swaps.

How to answer

Turn the sufficiency half of the proof into code: it already says "relabels realise the count permutation, then swaps reorder".

Likely follow-ups
  • Fewest swaps to apply a permutation? Elements moved minus number of cycles.
Move 2 may now turn a letter into ANY letter, even one that is absent. What changes?

The letter-set invariant disappears: you can rename into fresh letters. Only the multiset of counts (and so the length) remains, so the test becomes "sorted counts are equal".

How to answer

Show that each invariant comes from one restriction of the moves; drop the restriction and the invariant goes with it.

Likely follow-ups
  • Now moves may also merge two letters into one? Then counts can be added; the question becomes a partition problem.
Many words arrive; group the ones that are pairwise close.

"Close" is an equivalence relation, so build a canonical key: (sorted tuple of the letters present, sorted tuple of counts). Hash the key and group, exactly like Group Anagrams with a different key. O(n) per word plus O(26 log 26).

How to answer

Point out reflexive, symmetric, transitive first; that is what makes a key exist.

Likely follow-ups
  • Key size? At most 52 small numbers; a bitmask of present letters plus the sorted counts.
The two strings are streams that you can read only once each. Memory limit is O(1).

The solution already is one pass per string with 52 counters, so it works on streams. The length check becomes part of the counts (sum of counts) at the end.

How to answer

Say that the invariants are aggregates, and aggregates can be computed online.

Likely follow-ups
  • Streams spread over machines? Each machine counts its chunk; add the 26-vectors.
