Technique: BFS where every level is an index range, so "farthest reach from the current range" gives the next range in one pass.

Closest match: GfG Minimum Jumps

  • Same: fewest jumps from the first to the last index, nums[i] = max jump.
  • Different: the end may be unreachable (return -1), and arrays are longer.
  • So: same scan plus "if far <= i at a level end, return -1".

How to recognise it:

  • "Fewest steps / intervals / clips / taps" to cover or reach a point on a line.
  • Each choice covers a range that starts at or before the current frontier.

Extra practice (competitive programming, optional - not interview questions): Codeforces 1729C Jumping on Tiles (Minimum cost first, then the most jumps at that cost: sorting, not BFS. Good contrast).

Practice order:

  1. Jump Game (reachability).
  2. This problem.
  3. Video Stitching, then Minimum Taps (same scan after a conversion).
  4. Jump Game III and VII (real BFS).
