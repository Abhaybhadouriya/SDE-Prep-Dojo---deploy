The story

Explain like I'm 5

A row of 32 lamps, some on. You could walk past every lamp and count the lit ones. Or you could use a magic button that switches off the rightmost lit lamp, wherever it is, and press it until the row is dark. The number of presses is the number of lit lamps, and you never visit a dark lamp.

This is the "hello world" of bit manipulation, and in a kernel/networking background you already use it: popcount appears in CPU-mask weights, bitmap allocators, Hamming distance between hashes and subnet-mask prefix lengths. Interviewers ask it to check that you know n & (n - 1) and can explain why it works, and then to see how you think about a function called billions of times.

From your world

Linux has hweight32() / bitmap_weight(), and GCC has __builtin_popcount, which becomes a single POPCNT instruction with -mpopcnt. Without it, the kernel's generic version is the SWAR bit-twiddling shown in the follow-ups.
