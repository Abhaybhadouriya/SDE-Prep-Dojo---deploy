  3. Level 1: freeze width = 2 (7 and 1). Pop 7, row = [7], push its left child 3. 3 now sits in the queue behind 1, but it is outside the frozen width.
  4. Pop 1 (the second and last of the width), row = [7, 1], push 2 and 6. Two pops done: levels = [[4], [7, 1]].
  5. Level 2: freeze width = 3 (3, 2, 6). Pop 3, row = [3], push 9. Without the frozen width, the loop would not know that 9 belongs to the next level.
  6. Pop 2 and 6 (leaves, nothing to push). Three pops: row [3, 2, 6] is closed. The queue holds exactly level 3.
  7. Level 3: width = 1. Pop 9: row [9]. The queue is empty, so return [[4], [7, 1], [3, 2, 6], [9]]. Each node was pushed once and popped once: O(n).
