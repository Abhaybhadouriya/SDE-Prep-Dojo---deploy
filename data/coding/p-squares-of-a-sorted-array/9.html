60-second recall

  • Cue: sorted input, transform with one turning point, sorted output.
  • Insight: the largest remaining square is at lo or hi; the smallest is in the middle.
  • State: lo, hi = unread ends; w = next slot to fill, from the back.
  • Trap: filling from the front, or lo < hi that skips the last element.
  • Cost: O(n) time, O(1) extra. Next: ax² + bx + c (LC 360), the sign of a picks the fill end.
lo, hi = 0, n - 1
for w in range(n - 1, -1, -1):
    if nums[lo]**2 > nums[hi]**2: out[w] = nums[lo]**2; lo += 1
    else:                         out[w] = nums[hi]**2; hi -= 1

Why does the algorithm fill the output from the back?

  1. To save memory
  2. The largest remaining square is always at an end; the smallest is not
  3. Python lists append faster at the end
The ends hold the extremes in magnitude, so you always know the maximum.

On [-6, -3, -1], which pointer moves?

  1. Only lo
  2. Only hi
  3. They alternate
Every |nums[lo]| is larger than |nums[hi]| until they meet.

f(x) = -x² + 3. Where do you start filling?

  1. From the back, as before
  2. From the middle
  3. From the front: the ends now hold the smallest values
A negative leading coefficient flips the valley into a hill.
