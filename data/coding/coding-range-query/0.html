The story: the shop ledger

Explain like I'm 5

A shop writes its sales for each day in a ledger. The owner keeps asking "how much did we sell from day 20 to day 45?". The clever clerk keeps a running total on every page, so each answer is one subtraction (that is a prefix sum).

Then the accountant starts fixing old days: "day 3 was wrong, add 10". Now every running total after day 3 is wrong and the clerk has to rewrite hundreds of pages. So the shop hires a few helpers instead. One helper remembers the total of days 1-4, another days 5-6, another day 7, another days 1-8. To answer a question you ask three or four helpers and add up what they say. To fix a day you tell the three or four helpers whose block contains that day. Nobody rewrites hundreds of pages any more.

That is the whole idea of this page. When an array changes between questions, you store sums of well-chosen blocks so that both a question (query) and a change (update) touch only O(log n) blocks. The Fenwick tree and the segment tree are two ways to choose the blocks. The ordered map is a different tool for a related question: "which booked interval is just before or just after this time?".
