Why prefix sums stop being enough

Recall the prefix sum: P[i] = sum of the first i values, and sum(l..r) = P[r + 1] - P[l]. A query is O(1). But a[3] += 10 changes P[4], P[5], ... P[n]: O(n) per update. With q updates and q queries mixed together on n = 105, both "no structure" and "prefix sums" cost about 1010 steps, far too slow.

In interviews the line is simple. If nothing changes, use prefix sums. If single values change and you need sums or counts, use a Fenwick tree. If you need min or max over a range, or updates that hit a whole range, use a segment tree. If the "positions" are times or interval ends that can be as large as 109, use an ordered map (TreeMap, C++ std::map).

How often does this come up? Fenwick and segment trees are rare in SDE-1 loops and show up mostly at Google, sometimes at Amazon, Uber and Meta, usually as the follow-up "now the array changes". The ordered-map problems are common at every level: My Calendar I has 7 companies in our problem database, Uber most often, and an Uber SDE-2 onsite asked it in September 2026.
