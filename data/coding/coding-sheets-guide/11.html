Q&A: choosing and using lists

Is Blind 75 still enough in 2026?

As a minimum, yes: all 75 are in the Dojo 450 and they keep appearing in reports. As a complete plan, no. Of the 553 problems named in 2024-26 reports, many are outside Blind 75 (Valid Word Abbreviation, Random Pick with Weight, Rotting Oranges, Making a Large Island). NeetCode 150 or the Dojo 450 closes most of that gap.

How to answer

Use Blind 75 as the core you can re-solve cold, then extend by pattern.

Likely follow-ups
  • "Then NeetCode 150 or Striver SDE?" Either; they share 57 problems. Pick by style.
Striver's sheets or NeetCode: which is better for Indian product companies?

Both cover the core. Striver's SDE sheet includes more GfG classics that Indian OAs like (job sequencing, N meetings in one room, aggressive cows); NeetCode is pure LeetCode and grouped by pattern. Flipkart's saved list leans on sliding window, binary search on answer and two pointers (Maximum Points from Cards, Koko Eating Bananas, Container With Most Water), which both cover.

How to answer

Pick one; add the other's unique problems in your weak topics. The Dojo 450 already includes the six TUF classics with no LeetCode twin.

Likely follow-ups
  • "What about Love Babbar 450?" Good for GfG-style tests; only 199 of its 448 are on LeetCode.
How much should I trust company frequency lists?

As a signal of what is common, a lot; as a prediction of your exact question, not much. A frequent problem has been reported often, but interviewers also change details or pick from a private pool. For Amazon, 194 of 209 frequent problems are in Tier A anyway; for Uber only about half are, so the company list adds more there.

How to answer

Use company lists to find gaps in your pattern coverage, not to memorise answers.

Likely follow-ups
  • "Why does a 6-month window matter?" It shows what is being asked now, not in 2019.
Why does the Dojo 450 include problems that no famous sheet lists?

Because the score also counts company frequency and recent reports. 71 of the 450 are on none of the nine big lists; most are Meta, Amazon or Google favourites that became popular after the sheets were written. Valid Word Abbreviation is the clearest example: 25 Meta reports in 2024-26, rank 178.

How to answer

Explain the three inputs (lists, companies, recent) and the cap that stops any one input from dominating.

Likely follow-ups
  • "Why cap the list score at 16?" So popularity among sheet authors cannot outweigh what companies actually ask.
I have two weeks and 200 solved problems. What should I do?

Stop adding breadth. Re-solve your Tier A problems that are due, timed, from a blank editor; then do the target company's frequency 4+ problems that are new to you. In the Microsoft example that is 11 new problems plus revision of the rest.

How to answer

Two numbers per day: new problems (5-8) and revised problems (5-10). Do one full mock with a person in each week.

Likely follow-ups
  • "What if a pattern is still weak?" Take one day for that group's lesson and its 5 easiest Tier A problems.
How do I use a sheet without just memorising solutions?

Solve each problem in three passes. First pass: try for 25-30 minutes, then read one top solution and write it yourself. Second pass (3 days later, from the revision queue): solve from scratch, saying the pattern first. Third pass (2 weeks later): solve under a timer and explain the complexity. If pass two fails, the problem goes back to day 1.

How to answer

Write the "cue" (what in the statement tells you the pattern) in your notes, not the code.

Likely follow-ups
  • "How long before reading the solution?" About 25-30 minutes of real trying; then learn, do not suffer.
