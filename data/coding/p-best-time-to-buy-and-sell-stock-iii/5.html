Dry run

On [3, 8, 2, 6, 1, 7]:

  1. Day 0, price 3. Buying costs 3, so both buys sit at -3; selling on the same day earns 0. (Starting the buys at -prices[0] avoids an overflow from MIN_VALUE + p.)
  2. Day 1, price 8. sell1 = -3 + 8 = 5: one trade 3 → 8. buy2 = 5 - 8 = -3 (spend the profit on a 2nd buy) is no better yet, and sell2 also reaches 5.
  3. Day 2, price 2. A cheaper first buy: buy1 = -2. Better: buy2 = sell1 - 2 = 3, meaning 'I made 5 and now hold a share bought at 2'. Each row reads the row above it on the same day.
  4. Day 3, price 6. sell1 stays 5 (2 → 6 is only 4). sell2 = buy2 + 6 = 9: trades 3 → 8 and 2 → 6.
  5. Day 4, price 1. Cheapest day: buy1 = -1. buy2 = 5 - 1 = 4 (first profit 5, now holding a share bought at 1).
  6. Day 5, price 7. sell1 = -1 + 7 = 6 (one trade 1 → 7). sell2 = 4 + 7 = 11: trades 3 → 8 and 1 → 7. The answer is sell2 = 11.
