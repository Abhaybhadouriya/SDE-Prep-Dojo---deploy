Parsing and validation: a tiny state machine

atoi, valid number, version compare, IP validation: these test whether you read the rules and cover every branch, not clever algorithms. Method: list the parts of a valid input in order, then walk the string once with an index, consuming one part at a time.

The overflow check is where most candidates slip. You cannot compute val * 10 + d and test it afterwards in a 32-bit int: it already overflowed (undefined behaviour in C and C++). Rearrange the inequality so nothing overflows: val * 10 + d > MAX is the same as val > (MAX - d) / 10 with integer division. The negative side has one extra value (-231), and clamping to INT_MIN as soon as the magnitude passes INT_MAX handles it, because -231 is exactly the clamp value.

From your world

This is kstrtoint() / simple_strtoul() with the range check the kernel added after years of bugs, or parsing a TLV length field before you memcpy with it. You already know the rule: check before you multiply, not after.

Three more parsers, same method

  • Compare version numbers (165): "1.01" equals "1.001", and "1.0" equals "1". Walk both strings with two indices; at each step read the next number up to a dot (missing parts count as 0), compare, continue. No split needed, O(n + m) time, O(1) space. Leading zeros vanish on their own when you build the number digit by digit.
  • Valid number (65, Hard, a Meta favourite): keep three flags, seenDigit, seenDot, seenExp. A sign is allowed only at the start or right after e; a dot only before any dot or exponent; e only once and only after a digit, and it resets seenDigit because the exponent needs its own digits. At the end, the answer is seenDigit. One pass, no regular expression.
  • Validate IP address (468): IPv4 = 4 parts of 1-3 digits, no leading zero, at most 255; IPv6 = 8 parts of 1-4 hex digits. "1.2.3.4." must fail, and some split functions silently drop that empty last part.

Interviewers love this: before coding a parser, say your list of cases out loud ("leading spaces, sign, digits, garbage after digits, overflow both ways, empty string") and ask which ones apply. It shows you test like an engineer, and it often reveals that the interviewer wants fewer cases than you feared.
