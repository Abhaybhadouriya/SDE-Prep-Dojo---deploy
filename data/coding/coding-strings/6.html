Pattern matching: a first look

"Find the first index of pat inside text" (LeetCode 28) is Easy because the naive method passes: try each start position and compare up to m characters, O(n · m). In an interview, write the naive version first (or call the library and say so), then be ready for the follow-up: "can you do it in linear time?" Two answers are worth knowing.

KMP: never re-read the text

When a comparison fails after k matched characters, the naive method throws those k characters away and restarts one position later. KMP (Knuth-Morris-Pratt) keeps them: it already knows the longest part of the matched pattern prefix that is also a suffix (a border), and it continues from there. Those border lengths are precomputed once in a table called lps (longest proper prefix that is also a suffix), sometimes called the failure function.

Why O(n)? k rises by at most 1 per text character and every fall-back lowers it, so there are at most n fall-backs in total (an amortised argument).

Rolling hash (Rabin-Karp) and friends

  • Rolling hash: hash the first window of length m, then slide: remove the leaving character's contribution, multiply, add the entering one, all modulo a large prime. Equal hashes mean "probably equal"; confirm with a real compare. Good for Repeated DNA sequences (187) and for comparing many substrings at once (Longest duplicate substring, 1044, with binary search on the length).
  • Z-function: z[i] = length of the longest substring starting at i that matches a prefix of the string. Equivalent power to KMP; pick the one you can write without notes.
  • The doubling trick: goal is a rotation of s exactly when the lengths match and goal occurs inside s + s (Rotate string, 796). s is made of a repeated block exactly when s occurs in (s + s) with the first and last characters removed (Repeated substring pattern, 459).

From your world

A rolling hash is the incremental checksum update of RFC 1624: adjust by the old and new values instead of recomputing over the whole packet. DPI engines match many patterns with Aho-Corasick, built on KMP's failure links.
