The story

Explain like I'm 5

A row of jars holds red, white and blue marbles, one per jar, all mixed up. You may only swap two jars at a time, and you want all reds on the left, whites in the middle, blues on the right. Put one hand at the left end (where the next red goes), one at the right end (where the next blue goes), and walk with your eyes from the left. See a red: swap it into the left hand's jar and move both. See a white: just move on. See a blue: swap it into the right hand's jar and move that hand, but look again at the jar in front of you, because a new, unchecked marble just landed there.

The puzzle is Edsger Dijkstra's "Dutch national flag" problem (the flag is red, white and blue). It is a three-way partition, the same step that makes quicksort fast on inputs with many equal keys.

From your world

Think of a ring of descriptors you must classify in place into "done", "in flight" and "free" groups with no scratch buffer. Each group is a contiguous range between two indices, and one scan pointer moves items to the right range with one swap each. The invariant "everything between mid and hi is unclassified" plays the same role as the head/tail invariants of a ring buffer.
