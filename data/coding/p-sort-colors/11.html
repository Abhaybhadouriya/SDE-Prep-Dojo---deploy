Interview follow-ups

Now there are k colours (1..k). What do you do?

Counting sort with k counters is O(n + k) time and O(k) space. In place with O(1) space, you can run the flag repeatedly: each pass places the smallest and largest remaining colours at the two ends, so k/2 passes, O(n·k). For large k, an in-place quicksort-style recursion on the colour range is O(n log k).

How to answer

Give the counting answer first, then ask whether memory O(k) is allowed.

Likely follow-ups
  • The GfG "sort colors II" form with O(1) space: the k/2-pass version.
Each item is an object with a colour field. Does your solution still work?

The flag does: it swaps whole items, so it works on any record type with a three-way key. Counting and overwriting does not, because you cannot rebuild objects from counts. Neither keeps the original order inside a colour; if that is required, use a stable counting sort with an output array.

How to answer

Separate "moves the real items" from "stable"; the flag has the first, not the second.

Likely follow-ups
  • Which standard partition schemes are stable? (None of the in-place swap ones.)
Why does three-way partitioning matter for quicksort?

With two-way partitioning, an array of many equal keys splits badly and quicksort degrades towards O(n²). Splitting into <, ==, > puts all equal keys in their final place at once, and the recursion only runs on the < and > parts. This is exactly the Dutch flag step with "pivot" in place of 1.

How to answer

Link it back: "Sort Colors is one round of three-way quicksort with pivot 1."

Likely follow-ups
  • Bentley-McIlroy partitioning (equal keys swapped to the ends, then to the middle).
Count the swaps your solution makes. Can you make fewer?

The flag swaps once per 0 and once per 2 it meets, including swaps of an element with itself; you can skip those with a check (if lo != mid). The minimum number of swaps to sort three values is a known counting problem: fix pairs that are in each other's zone with one swap, then cycles of three with two swaps.

How to answer

Say that the flag is optimal in passes, not in swaps, and sketch the pair-then-cycle count if asked.

Likely follow-ups
  • Minimum adjacent swaps instead? (Count inversions between classes.)
Only 0s and 1s. What is the simplest one-pass version?

One write pointer w: scan, and whenever you see a 0, swap it to w and advance w. Or two pointers from the ends that swap a 1 on the left with a 0 on the right. Both are O(n), O(1).

How to answer

Show that the flag with no 2s reduces to the single write pointer.

Likely follow-ups
  • Keep the 1s in their original order? (Move Zeroes style, which is stable for the moved class.)
