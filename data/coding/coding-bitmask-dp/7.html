How to recognise a bitmask DP

  • The constraint says n <= 12, 14, 16 or 20 on the items you choose, assign, visit or partition. Nothing else in DSA has those odd small limits.
  • The brute force is "try every permutation" but the future only depends on which items are used (and maybe the last one). n! becomes 2n or n 2n.
  • "Visit every node / collect every key / cover every skill": the mask is the set of things already collected, paired with a position (BFS if unit weights, Held-Karp if weighted).
  • "Split into k groups" or "each person takes a subset": subset sums plus submask DP, or the "fill one bucket at a time" trick when all groups have the same target.
  • "Pair everyone up" / "assign each of n to one of n": dp[mask] with the next person = popcount(mask).
  • A small alphabet (26 letters, 10 digits, 40 hat types, up to 10 primes below 30): the mask is over that alphabet even when n is large (Number of Ways to Wear Different Hats, Stickers to Spell Word, The Number of Good Subsets).
  • A game where any unused item can be picked (Can I Win): memo on the mask of used numbers; the turn is implied by popcount.

Not bitmask DP: "n <= 20" with a plain subset-sum target and no interaction between items is often meet in the middle (split into two halves of 210 each). And n = 40 is almost always meet in the middle (240 is too many masks).
