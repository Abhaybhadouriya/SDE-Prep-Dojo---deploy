The problem (in our words)

A word can be shifted in two ways:

  • Right shift: replace every letter with the next letter of the alphabet, with 'z' becoming 'a'. For example "pqr" becomes "qrs", and "wxz" becomes "xya".
  • Left shift: replace every letter with the previous letter, with 'a' becoming 'z'. For example "qrs" becomes "pqr", and "xya" becomes "wxz".

Shifting again and again in either direction gives an endless shifting sequence, for example ... ↔ "pqr" ↔ "qrs" ↔ ... ↔ "yza" ↔ "zab" ↔ "abc" ↔ ... ↔ "pqr" ↔ .... You are given an array of words strings. Group together all words that belong to the same shifting sequence. The groups, and the words inside each group, may be returned in any order.

  • Input: an array of strings strings.
  • Output: a list of lists of strings, one list per shifting sequence, every input word in exactly one group (duplicates included). Signature: List<List<String>> groupStrings(String[] strings).

Constraints

  • 1 <= strings.length <= 200
  • 1 <= strings[i].length <= 50 (no empty words)
  • strings[i] consists of lower-case English letters.
