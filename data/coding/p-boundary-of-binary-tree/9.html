  • Cue: tree outline / perimeter / anti-clockwise boundary.
  • Insight: four disjoint parts; leaves belong only to the leaf walk.
  • State: edge walk = "left if any else right" (mirror on the right), stop at a leaf.
  • Trap: lone root, empty left subtree, reverse the right edge.
  • Cost: O(n) time, O(h) space.

Root 1 has only a right child 2, and 2 has children 3 and 4. What is the boundary?

  1. [1, 3, 4, 2]
  2. [1, 2, 3, 4]
  3. [1, 2, 4, 3]
  4. [1, 3, 4]
There is no left child, so the left boundary is empty. The leaves are 3 and 4. The right edge is [2] (the walk stops before leaf 4), added in reverse.

Why does the left or right edge walk never add a leaf?

  1. The leaf walk adds every leaf; adding them on the edge too prints them twice
  2. Leaves are not on the boundary
  3. It keeps the order sorted
  4. Edge walks cannot reach leaves
The four parts must not overlap: root, left edge without leaves, all leaves, right edge without leaves (reversed).
