The story

Explain like I'm 5

A tree-shaped garden has a fence to build around it. You stand at the top gate (the root) and walk anti-clockwise: down the left side, along the bottom past every plant at the very edge of the soil (the leaves), and back up the right side to the gate. You write down every post you touch, and you never write the same post twice.

The algorithm is easy once you see the four parts; the difficulty is all in the definitions. Which nodes count as "left edge"? What if the root has no left child? Is a leaf at the bottom of the left edge part of the edge or part of the leaves? Interviewers ask this one to see whether you pin down a definition before coding.

From your world

It is like dumping a ring buffer that wraps around: you print the head segment, the middle, and the tail segment, and the hard part is the boundary indices, so the element at the seam is printed exactly once. Here the seams are the bottom-left and bottom-right leaves.
