One day, one week: the session shape

Every study day has the same shape, so you never waste time deciding what to do. Reviews come first because they are short and they keep everything you already paid for.

Inside the 25 minutes for a new problem: 5 minutes to understand and write the brute force, up to 15 to find and code the better idea, 5 to test. If you have no idea at minute 15, read the hint or the first paragraph of the explanation, close it, and finish yourself. Then write the five-line card on the problem page's Notes box.

Step through week 2 to see how the counts, difficulty and revision fit together.

  1. Monday: read the Two pointers lesson (15 min), then four new problems, Easy first. Week 1's problems come up for review before you start.
  2. Tuesday: Monday's four are due (1-day gap). Review them first in 1-2 minutes each, then four new two-pointer problems.
  3. Wednesday: switch to the Sliding window lesson. The two-pointer problems you met on Monday and Tuesday make the window easy to understand: it is two pointers moving the same way.
  4. Thursday: four Medium windows. Wednesday's four are due (1-day gap).
  5. Friday: window variants (at most K, exactly K) and the first Hard of the week. Monday's problems are due again (reviewed Tuesday, then a 3-day gap); note how much faster the review is now. A Hard is allowed 40 minutes; reading the explanation after 25 is fine.
  6. Saturday: the timed set. Pick two unseen problems from this week's groups, set a 45-minute timer, talk out loud, no hints. Then two more new problems at normal pace.
  7. Sunday: no new problems. Clear the review queue, do full re-solves of anything you graded Hard or Again, write a short retro (what was slow, what to change) and look at next week's row.
  8. End of week: 24 new problems, mostly Medium, and every one is scheduled for revision. That is the week's real output.
