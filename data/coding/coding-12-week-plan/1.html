Four rules the whole plan is built on

Before the week-by-week table, here is why it looks the way it does. If you ever change the plan, keep these four rules.

  1. Patterns in dependency order. Each group uses ideas from earlier ones. Sliding window is two pointers plus a hash map. Backtracking is tree recursion plus "undo". DP is backtracking plus memory. Learn them in that order and each new group feels like a small step.
  2. A fixed daily number, not a weekly goal. "3 or 4 new problems today" is something you can finish. "25 this week" turns into zero on Monday and ten on Sunday night.
  3. Revision before new problems. Every problem you solve comes back on the revision ladder (1, 3, 7, 14, 30 days). The plan leaves time for that every day and one lighter day each week with no new problems.
  4. Test under interview conditions. Weeks 5, 9 and 12 are mock weeks: fewer new problems, timed rounds with a partner, and a re-solve sprint. They tell you what you can actually do in 45 minutes, which is a different thing from what you have seen.
