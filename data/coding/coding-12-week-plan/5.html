Mock-round weeks: 5, 9 and 12

A mock round is a practice interview: someone (a friend, a colleague or a paid mock service) gives you a problem you have not seen and watches you solve it in 45 minutes while you talk. You can also self-mock with a timer and a voice recording, but a human interviewer finds gaps a timer cannot, such as long silences or unclear explanations.

Step through one 45-minute round. The time boxes come from the usual round structure described in How coding rounds work.

  1. Minutes 0-5, clarify. Ask about input size, value ranges, duplicates and edge cases. Write the agreed signature.
  2. Minutes 5-10, example and brute force. Do one example by hand and state the simple solution with its complexity. This proves you understood the problem.
  3. Minutes 10-15, the better idea. Name the pattern, say why it applies (the cue), and the target complexity. Wait for the interviewer to agree before coding.
  4. Minutes 15-35, code. Use the template shape you know, keep talking, and write small helpers rather than one long function.
  5. Minutes 35-40, test. Walk your example through the code line by line, then two edge cases. Fix bugs out loud.
  6. Minutes 40-45, follow-up. Interviewers often change one constraint. Your revision card's insight line is what you adapt.
  7. After the round, the debrief. Score problem solving, coding, communication and testing from 1 to 4. Every score under 3 becomes a concrete task for next week.

The rest of a mock week: fewer new problems (16 and 14 instead of 22-26), and a re-solve sprint: re-solve cold every anchor problem from the phase you just finished. Anything you fail goes back to the start of the revision ladder and its lesson goes on next week's reading list.
