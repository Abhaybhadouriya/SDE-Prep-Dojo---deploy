The story: learning to drive before the highway

Explain like I'm 5

Nobody learns to drive on the highway. First you learn the pedals in an empty car park. Then quiet streets, then turning at junctions, then a busy road, and only then the highway. Each step uses everything from the step before.

Coding problems work the same way. "Arrays and hash maps" are the pedals. "Two pointers" and "sliding window" are quiet streets. Trees and graphs are junctions. Dynamic programming is the highway. If you start with the highway, you crash and decide you are "bad at coding". You are not. You just skipped the car park.

This plan is the driving school timetable: what to practise each week, how many problems a day, and when to take a test drive (a mock interview) to see if you are ready.

From your world

You would not bring up a new NIC driver by starting with RSS and XDP offloads. You get the PCI probe working, then register access, then one TX/RX ring with interrupts, then NAPI polling, then the offloads. Each stage needs the one before it to be solid. A DSA plan is a bring-up sequence for your interview skills, and the mock weeks are your integration tests.
