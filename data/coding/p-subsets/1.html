The problem (in our words)

A test engineer has a list nums of distinct integer option codes and wants to try every possible combination of options, including using none of them. Return all subsets of nums (the power set). The list must not contain the same subset twice; the subsets, and the numbers inside each subset, may come in any order.

Signature: List<List<Integer>> subsets(int[] nums) (LeetCode style; return a list of integer lists).

Constraints:

  • 1 <= nums.length <= 10
  • -10 <= nums[i] <= 10
  • All values in nums are distinct.

Practice it: LeetCode 78 · GfG Subsets

Constraints tell you: n <= 10 means at most 210 = 1024 subsets with at most 10 numbers each, about 5,000 numbers of output. Exponential time is not a problem here; it is required, because the answer itself is exponential. "Distinct" means no duplicate handling is needed (that is LeetCode 90).
