The story

Explain like I'm 5

A shop shelf holds tins sorted by label, and some labels repeat many times. The rule is "at most two tins of each label on the front shelf". You walk along the tins and pack the ones you keep to the left. Before you put a tin down, you look at the tin two places to the left of the free spot. If it has the same label, you already have two of these (the one next to it must match too, because the shelf is sorted), so you leave this tin behind.

One glance two places back answers "how many copies do I already have?" without any counter. This is the only new idea on top of the "keep one copy" version.

From your world

Think of rate-limiting duplicate log lines: the kernel's printk_ratelimit lets a burst of a few identical messages through and then drops the rest. Here the "burst" is 2, the log is sorted so duplicates arrive back to back, and the decision for each line only needs the history buffer you already wrote, compacted in place like a ring buffer that never wraps.
