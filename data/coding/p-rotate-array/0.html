The story

Explain like I'm 5

Six friends sit on a bench in a line. The teacher says: "the last two of you, move to the front, everyone else slide along". Moving one child at a time is slow. A trick: everyone turns the whole line around (the order flips), then the first two swap with each other and the other four reverse their small group. Now the last two are in front and everyone is in the right order again.

From your world

Rotating a buffer in place is what you do when a ring buffer has to be "linearised" so that the oldest entry sits at offset 0, for example before handing a log ring or a receive ring to code that expects a flat array. Without a second buffer, the three-reversal trick (or the gcd-cycle walk, which is what std::rotate uses on random-access ranges in some libraries) does it with one temporary.
