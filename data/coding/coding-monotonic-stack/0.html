The story: who can see the stage?

Explain like I'm 5

Children stand in a line facing right, towards a wall of windows. Each child wants to know: "Who is the first child to my right who is taller than me?" That taller child blocks the view.

The slow way: every child looks right, one by one, until they find someone taller. The clever way: walk along the line from left to right, keeping a little group of children who are still waiting for their answer. When a new child arrives, every waiting child shorter than the new one has found their answer: it is the new child. They leave the group. The new child joins the group and waits too.

Notice something about the waiting group: it is always sorted. Tall children at the back, shorter ones in front. If a short child were behind a taller waiting child, the short one would already have been answered. That sorted group is the monotonic stack.

From your world: wait queues and the run queue of unanswered requests

The waiting stack is a wait queue with a wake-up condition. Each element on the stack sleeps on the condition "a value bigger than me arrives". A new value is the event; it wakes every sleeper whose condition it satisfies, and because the stack is sorted, those sleepers are exactly a run at the top, so the waker stops at the first one it cannot satisfy. Unlike wake_up() on a real wait queue, nobody has to scan the sleepers who stay asleep.

The O(n) argument is the one you already use for a packet path: each skb is enqueued once and dequeued once, so however bursty a single softirq run is, total work is linear in packets. "Each index is pushed once and popped at most once" is the same accounting.

The histogram has a systems face too. Picture per-column "free run length" in a 2-D allocation map (or consecutive free pages per zone over time): the largest all-free rectangle is LC 85. And the greedy stack is a reorder buffer in reverse: it holds back committed results only while a better (smaller) one could still replace them, within a fixed budget of discards.

Every problem on this page is that story with a different question asked at the moment a child leaves the group.
