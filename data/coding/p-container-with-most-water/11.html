Interview follow-ups

Prove that moving the shorter pointer never skips the optimum.

For the shorter post l, every unchecked pair (l, k) with k < r is narrower and its level is at most h[l], so its area is at most the area of (l, r), which we just computed. Dropping l loses nothing. The pair grid shows it: each step deletes a row or column whose cells are all dominated by the checked cell.

How to answer

Say it in two lines: "narrower, and no deeper than the short wall". Then draw the 4×4 grid if asked for more.

Likely follow-ups
  • What about ties? (Both posts are finished; move either.)
Return the pair of indexes, not just the area.

Store (l, r) whenever best improves. Same loop, same cost.

How to answer

Mention which pair you return on ties (the first found, the widest, ...) and ask which one they want.

Likely follow-ups
  • Return all optimal pairs? (Can be O(n²) in the worst case, e.g. all heights equal 0.)
Can you skip several posts at once?

Yes: after dropping the shorter post, keep moving that pointer while the next post is not taller than the dropped one, because such a post is also capped at a lower level with less width. It is a constant-factor speed-up, still O(n) in the worst case.

How to answer

Present it as an optimisation, not a change in complexity.

Likely follow-ups
  • Does it change the answer on ties? (No, as long as you compare with <=.)
The posts have different x positions (not 0..n-1). What changes?

Width becomes x[r] - x[l]. If the posts are given in x order, the same two-pointer proof holds, because width still shrinks as the window shrinks. If they are unsorted, sort by x first: O(n log n).

How to answer

Point at the one fact the proof needs: width is monotone in the window.

Likely follow-ups
  • Posts arrive as a stream? (Then the sort-by-height idea with min/max index fails; you need all posts first.)
Heights go up to 109 and n up to 106. What breaks?

The area can reach 1015: use long / int64 for the product (cast before multiplying). Time stays O(n).

How to answer

Do the bound out loud: max height × max width.

Likely follow-ups
  • Where exactly must the cast go in Java/C++? (On one operand, before the multiplication.)
