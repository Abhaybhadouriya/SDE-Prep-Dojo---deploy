Approaches: brute → better → optimal

Hint 1

Start with the widest tank, posts 0 and n-1. Every other tank is narrower. What must it have to beat the widest one?

Hint 2

It needs a higher water level. Can a tank that still uses the shorter of the two end posts ever have a higher level?

1. Brute force - try every pair

Idea: two nested loops over i < j, compute min(h[i], h[j]) * (j - i), keep the max.

O(n²)time
O(1)space

What is wasted: most pairs are provably worse than one we already checked. Once a short post has been paired with the farthest post still in play, all its other pairs are dominated. -> rung 2.

2. Better (a dead end worth naming) - sort posts by height

Idea: process posts from tallest to shortest; for a post of height h, every post already seen is at least as tall, so the best partner is the one farthest away. Keep the min and max index seen so far: area = h × max(i - minIdx, maxIdx - i).

O(n log n)time
O(n)space

What is wasted: the sort. It is correct and fast enough, and a good thing to mention if you blank on the two-pointer proof, but the problem never needs the global order: the two ends already tell us which post is the limit. -> rung 3.

3. Optimal - two pointers, drop the shorter wall

l, r, best = 0, len(height) - 1, 0
while l < r:
    best = max(best, min(height[l], height[r]) * (r - l))
    if height[l] < height[r]: l += 1
    else: r -= 1
  1. Start as wide as possible. Posts 0 and n-1. Any other candidate is narrower, so it can only win with a higher level.
  2. The shorter post is finished. Say h[l] <= h[r]. Every remaining pair (l, k) with l < k < r has level min(h[l], h[k]) <= h[l] and a smaller width, so its area is at most the one we just computed. Drop l.
  3. Repeat on the smaller window. The problem shrinks by one post per step; after n - 1 steps nothing is left.
  4. Ties are safe. When h[l] == h[r], both posts are finished (each is the short side), so moving either one is fine.
O(n)time
O(1)space

Why it works: we never skip the best pair. A pair is skipped only when one of its posts was dropped, and a post is dropped only after it was paired with the farthest partner still in play while being the shorter one. That checked pair is at least as good as every skipped pair that uses the post.

Proof (optional)

Suppose the optimum is (a, b) and the loop never evaluates it. Then at some step one of them was dropped first; say a was dropped while the window was (a, r). b was still in play, so r >= b, and r > b because (a, b) itself was never checked. Since a was dropped, h[a] <= h[r], so area(a, r) = h[a] × (r - a) >= min(h[a], h[b]) × (b - a) = area(a, b). The loop evaluated (a, r), so it saw an area at least as large as the optimum. The case where b is dropped first is the mirror image.
