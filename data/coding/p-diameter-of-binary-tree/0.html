The story

Explain like I'm 5

A family tree is painted on a wall, and two cousins want to know who in the family lives "farthest apart", counting one step for each parent-child line on the wall. Any trip between two people goes up to some ancestor and then down again. So stand at each person in turn and ask: "how far down can I go on my left side, and how far on my right side?" Add the two. The biggest sum anywhere on the wall is the answer, and it does not have to be at the great-grandparent at the top.

The catch that makes this a classic: each person must tell their parent only one number, their longest single branch down (a path that goes up to the parent cannot use both of your branches), while the two-branch sum is recorded separately as a candidate answer.

From your world

The diameter of a network topology (the longest shortest path between two nodes) bounds the worst-case hop count, which is what spanning tree timers and TTL defaults are sized against. On a tree topology it is exactly this computation: depth per subtree, best sum of the two deepest branches at each switch.
