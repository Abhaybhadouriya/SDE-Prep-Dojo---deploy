The story

Explain like I'm 5

An old clock tower shows the year carved in Roman letters. Each letter is a coin of fixed value: I is 1, V is 5, X is 10, and so on. Normally the coins are laid out from biggest to smallest and you just add them up. The only twist: when a small coin is placed just before a bigger one, it means "take this much away from the next coin". So IV is 5 - 1 = 4, not 1 + 5.

From your world

It is a tiny tokenizer with one token of lookahead, like parsing a protocol header where the meaning of a byte depends on the next byte. No backtracking, no buffering: read symbol i, peek at symbol i + 1, decide the sign, move on. The same "peek one ahead" shape shows up in decoding variable-length fields.
