The story

Explain like I'm 5

You are hiking in thick fog along a ridge trail with posts every few metres, and you want to stand on any hilltop, not the highest one. You cannot see far, but at any post you can feel which way the ground slopes. If the ground rises to your right, walk right: you are going up, and since the trail ends in a cliff, the climb has to stop somewhere, and the point where it stops is a hilltop. With a helicopter you could even skip to the middle of the remaining trail, feel the slope there, and throw away the half behind you.

The surprise is that binary search does not need sorted data. It needs a question about mid whose answer tells you which half is guaranteed to contain an answer. Here the question is "is the next post higher?"

From your world

It is like bisecting a regression with git bisect: you never look at every commit, you only need a test at the midpoint that tells you which side the change you want is on. Here the "test" is one comparison, nums[mid] < nums[mid+1], and the side it points to always has a peak.
