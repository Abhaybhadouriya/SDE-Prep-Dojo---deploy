Approaches: brute -> better -> optimal

Hint 1

All windows have the same length. Do you need to compare averages, or is something simpler enough?

Hint 2

Two neighbouring windows share k - 1 elements. What is the difference between their sums?

1. Brute force - sum every window

Idea: for each start i from 0 to n - k, add up nums[i..i+k-1] and keep the largest.

O(n·k)time
O(1)extra space

What is wasted: neighbouring windows re-add the same k - 1 elements.

2. Better - prefix sums

Idea: build pre[i] = sum of the first i elements; the window starting at i has sum pre[i+k] - pre[i]. O(n) time, O(n) space. This is LeetCode's first editorial approach and the form you need for the "at least k" follow-up, where window lengths vary.

3. Optimal - sliding window sum

s = sum(nums[:k]); best = s
for i in range(k, n):
    s += nums[i] - nums[i - k]
    best = max(best, s)
return best / k
O(n)time: each element enters and leaves once
O(1)extra space

Why it works: invariant: after processing index i (i >= k - 1), s equals the sum of nums[i-k+1..i], the window that ends at i, and best is the largest sum of any window ending at or before i. Moving from i - 1 to i adds nums[i] and drops nums[i-k], which is exactly the difference between the two windows. Every window ends at some index, so every window's sum is compared once. Since all windows have length k, the largest sum gives the largest average: dividing by the same positive k does not change the order.

Comparing sums instead of averages avoids k - n + 1 floating-point divisions and any rounding questions; the top community solutions all divide once at the end.
