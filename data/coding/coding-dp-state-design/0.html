The story: what do you need to remember?

Explain like I'm 5

You are playing a board game and your mum calls you for dinner. You want to finish the game after dinner, so you take a photo of the board. Do you need a video of every move you made so far? No. The photo is enough, because the rest of the game depends only on where the pieces are now, not on how they got there. A DP state is that photo: the smallest set of facts about "where you are" that decides everything that can still happen. Find the right photo and the problem becomes easy. Take a photo that misses something (whose turn it is!) and your answers come out wrong.

The previous page, the DP primer, gave a four-step recipe: state, transition, base cases, answer. Steps two to four are almost mechanical once step one is right. So the real question in every DP problem is: after I have made some of the decisions, what is the least I must know to finish optimally?

Two histories that leave you with the same "photo" have the same best future. That is the whole reason DP works. If you have chosen the first 5 items of a list and used 7 units of budget, it does not matter which 5 choices got you there; the best way to continue is the same. So you solve "continue from (index 5, budget 7)" once and reuse it.

From your world

A context switch is the exact analogy. The kernel does not save the whole history of a task; it saves struct pt_regs, the stack pointer and a few flags, because that is all the future of the task depends on. A DP state is the "saved registers" of a recursive search. And just like a task whose saved context forgot one register, a state that forgets one fact (a cooldown, whose turn it is, how much budget is left) resumes into the wrong behaviour without crashing.
