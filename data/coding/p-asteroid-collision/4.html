Approaches: brute → better → optimal

Hint 1

Which pairs of directions can ever meet? Write down the four combinations of signs for a left rock and a right rock.

Hint 2

Scan left to right and keep the rocks that have survived so far. A new left-mover can only hit the last survivor. What happens after it destroys it?

1. Brute force - replay until nothing changes

Idea: repeatedly scan the row for an adjacent pair (positive, negative), resolve it (remove the smaller or both), and start again. Stop when no such pair is left.

O(n²)time
O(n)space

What is wasted: each pass rescans the whole row from the start, although the only place a new collision can appear is right where the last one happened. -> rung 2.

2. Optimal - a stack of survivors

for a in asteroids:
    while a < 0 and st and st[-1] > 0 and st[-1] < -a: st.pop()   # newcomer wins
    if a < 0 and st and st[-1] > 0:                              # newcomer meets an equal or bigger one
        if st[-1] == -a: st.pop()
        continue
    st.append(a)
  1. The stack is always stable. Read bottom to top, it is some left-movers followed by some right-movers: no + is ever directly below a -.
  2. Right-movers and lonely left-movers just go on top. A positive newcomer moves away from everything on its left. A negative newcomer with no positive top (empty stack or a negative top) moves away too.
  3. A negative newcomer fights the top. Top smaller: pop it and fight the next one. Top equal: pop it and drop the newcomer. Top bigger: drop the newcomer.
  4. The stack at the end is the answer, already in left-to-right order.
O(n)time
O(n)space

Why it works: collisions only happen between a + on the left and a - on the right, and the first rock a left-mover can reach is the nearest surviving right-mover, which is the stack top. Everything below the top is shielded by it, so it does not matter in which global order the real collisions happen in time: each newcomer's fate is decided by the top-down sequence of right-movers it meets. Amortised cost: each rock is pushed once and popped at most once, so the inner while runs at most n times in total.

Proof (optional)

Invariant: after processing a prefix, the stack equals the final state of that prefix alone (as if no more rocks came), and it has the form (negatives)(positives). Base: the empty prefix. Step: a positive newcomer collides with nothing in the prefix (everything to its left moves left or moves right at the same speed), so push. A negative newcomer can only meet the positives on the stack, nearest first; resolving them in that order gives the same survivors as the physical process, because each collision involves the newcomer and the nearest live right-mover. After it stops, the stack still has the (negatives)(positives) shape. Rocks that come later cannot change collisions already decided inside the prefix except through the same top-down process, so the invariant holds for the whole array.
