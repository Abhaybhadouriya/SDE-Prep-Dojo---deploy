The story: getting dressed in the morning

Explain like I'm 5

You cannot put your shoes on before your socks, and you cannot put your jacket on before your shirt. But socks and shirt? Either can go first. If you wrote every "this before that" rule on a card, a topological order is any way of lining up your clothes so no card is broken. To find one, keep picking a piece of clothing that has no rule still waiting on it: socks or shirt first, then whatever those freed up, and so on. If you ever get stuck with clothes left over and every one of them waits on another, your rules are impossible: someone wrote "shoes before socks" and "socks before shoes".

Formally: a directed graph has edges with a direction, written u → v. Read it as "u must come before v". A topological order is a list of all vertices where every edge points forward, from an earlier position to a later one. Such an order exists if and only if the graph has no directed cycle. A directed graph with no cycle is called a DAG (directed acyclic graph).

Why the "only if" half? Take a cycle a → b → c → a. In any line-up, a must sit before b, b before c, and c before a, so a sits before itself. Impossible. Why the "if" half? Every DAG has at least one vertex with no incoming edge (otherwise you could walk backwards forever and, with finitely many vertices, you would revisit one, which is a cycle). Put that vertex first, delete it, and the rest is still a DAG. Repeat. That proof is Kahn's algorithm, as you will see.

From your world

You run topological sorts every day without calling them that. make builds a target only after its prerequisites, and reports a circular dependency when the rules loop. depmod writes modules.dep so that modprobe can load a module's dependencies first. systemd turns After= / Before= into an ordering graph and complains about an "ordering cycle" when units loop. The kernel's lockdep records "lock A was held while taking lock B" as an edge and warns about a possible deadlock the moment a new edge would close a cycle: that is online cycle detection in a directed graph. And coreutils even ships the algorithm as a command, tsort.
