The story

Explain like I'm 5

Your desk has room for only three books. Each time you read a book, you put it on top of the pile. When a new book arrives and the desk is full, the book at the bottom of the pile goes back to the shelf: it is the one you have not touched for the longest time. To grab a book quickly you also keep a sticky note on the wall: "Maths book: 2nd from top", so you never dig through the pile.

The pile is a doubly linked list, the sticky note is a hash map, and "least recently used" (LRU) is the bottom of the pile.

From your world

The Linux page cache keeps pages on LRU lists built from struct list_head; list_move() is O(1) because each node carries prev and next, exactly the move-to-front here. The kernel uses approximations (active/inactive lists, a CLOCK-like second chance) because a true LRU update on every access is too costly under contention, which is a good follow-up answer.
