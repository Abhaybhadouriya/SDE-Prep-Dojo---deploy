Fill order, memo, and costs

For any interval table, write the loops in one of two shapes and you will not read an empty cell:

  1. By length: for len in 1..n: for i in 0..n-len: j = i + len - 1. This is the clearest when you explain it: one diagonal at a time.
  2. i descending: for i in n-1 down to 0: for j in i..n-1. Row i only reads row i+1 (below) and its own earlier columns (left). Two plain loops, and it allows a 1-D rolling row for LPS.

Or skip the order question with memoised recursion: solve(i, j) calls shorter ranges and caches results. It is easy to get right in an interview. The costs are recursion depth (up to n, fine for n <= 1000 but check Python's limit) and a slower constant.
