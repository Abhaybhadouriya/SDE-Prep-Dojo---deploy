  • Cue: numbers as digit lists/strings, add them; digits stored ones-first.
  • Insight: schoolbook addition; finished list = 0; carry ∈ {0, 1} since 9+9+1 = 19.
  • State: a, b, carry, dummy, tail.
  • Trap: loop while a or b or carry; never convert to long; guard a.next on null.
  • Cost: O(max(m, n)) time, O(1) extra; recursion O(max(m, n)) stack.
carry = 0
while i_has_digit or j_has_digit or carry:
    total = carry + digit_i_or_0 + digit_j_or_0
    carry, d = divmod(total, base)
    emit(d)
    advance i and j if they have digits

Both lists are exhausted but carry = 1. What happens?

  1. The carry is dropped
  2. The last node's value becomes 10
  3. One more node with value 1 is appended
Loop on l1 or l2 or carry so the final carry becomes its own digit, e.g. 5 + 5 = 10 gives [0,1].

What is the largest value the carry can take?

  1. 1
  2. 9
  3. 18
The column sum is at most 9 + 9 + 1 = 19, so sum / 10 is at most 1.

Why not convert both lists to integers and add?

  1. It is slower than O(n)
  2. Up to 100 digits overflows 64-bit integers
  3. Lists cannot be read twice
A 64-bit integer holds about 19 digits; the lists can have 100. Digit-by-digit addition has no size limit.
