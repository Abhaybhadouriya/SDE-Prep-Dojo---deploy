Common mistakes

Greedy matching. Matching each letter of a with the first free equal letter of b fails: for a = "xab", b = "abx", greedy matches 'x' with the last letter of b and then nothing else fits, giving 1; the real LCS "ab" has length 2. An early match can block better later ones, which is why both "drop" choices must be tried.

Confusing subsequence with substring. For the longest common substring, a mismatch resets the cell to 0 and the answer is the max over all cells, not dp[m][n].

Off-by-one indexing. With a (m+1) × (n+1) table, cell (i, j) compares a[i-1] and b[j-1]. Mixing the two conventions reads row -1.

Losing the diagonal in the 1D version. Reading row[j-1] after it was overwritten gives the current row's value, not the old diagonal. Save up before writing.

Python grid aliasing. [[0] * n] * m creates m references to one row; use a comprehension.
