Where the numbers come from

Every number on this page comes from one file, recent-coding.json, built by the site's data scripts from public LeetCode Discuss posts. Here is how 4,217 posts became 553 problems.

The method in five lines

  • Posts. For each company tag, the newest posts back to 2024 whose title looks like an interview or OA report. Up to 520 posts for Amazon, Google, Microsoft and Meta, up to 220 for every other company.
  • Problems. A problem counts when the post links to it, gives its number, or uses its exact title on a question line. Posts that name more than 10 problems were dropped as copied lists.
  • Sections skipped. Prep tips, resources, results, and LLD / HLD / behavioural rounds, so system-design names do not leak in.
  • Level comes from the post title (L3 / E3 / SDE I = SDE-1; L4-L5 / E4-E5 / SDE II = SDE-2; L6 / E6 / senior / staff = SDE-3). Round comes from the nearest round heading.
  • One report = one post naming one problem. A post with three problems gives three reports.

Read the counts with care. The extraction is automatic: expect a few false positives (a title mentioned in passing) and misses (a question told in prose). Meta posts name their problems far more often than Google posts, so Meta is over-represented: it has 577 reports, Google 84. A low count for Google means "Google candidates rarely name the problem", not "Google asks few problems". Level is missing on 473 reports and round on 748.

The problem explorer uses the same file: every problem page under /coding/p/... shows its recent reports with links to the posts, and those counts refresh each time the data build runs. The tables on this page are a snapshot from the 2026-11-24 build.
