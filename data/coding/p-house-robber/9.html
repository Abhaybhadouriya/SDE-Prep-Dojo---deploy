Revision card

  • Cue: items in a line, no two adjacent (rule looks 1-2 steps back), maximise the sum.
  • Insight: last item skipped -> best[i-1]; taken -> best[i-2] + a[i]; take the max.
  • State: best[i] = best total using items 0..i; keep only prev1, prev2.
  • Trap: parity greedy fails ([4,1,1,4]); update prev1/prev2 together.
  • Cost: O(n) time, O(1) space; plain recursion O(1.6^n).
prev2 = prev1 = 0                     # best up to i-2, i-1
for x in items:
    prev2, prev1 = prev1, max(prev1, prev2 + x)   # skip vs take
return prev1

What does best[i] mean in the cleanest state definition?

  1. Best total if item i is taken
  2. Best total using items 0..i (i may or may not be taken)
  3. Sum of items 0..i
With "using 0..i", the answer is simply best[n-1] and the transition has two terms.

For [5,6,5], what does the DP return?

  1. 6
  2. 11
  3. 10
best = 5, 6, max(6, 5+5) = 10. Taking the largest item first would give 6.

Why can prev1 and prev2 both start at 0?

  1. They stand for the empty prefix, whose best total is 0
  2. Because values are at most 400
  3. Because n ≥ 2
best[-1] = best[-2] = 0 means "no items yet", so n = 1 works with no extra code.
