The story

Explain like I'm 5

A shopkeeper reads a till slip aloud: "8, minus 9 split 4 ways, times 3, plus 1". She keeps two numbers in her head: the money that is already counted, and the item she is working on now. "Times" and "split" only change the item in her hand. "Plus" and "minus" mean "this item is done, drop it in the counted pile and pick up the next one". At the end she drops the last item in and reads the pile.

This is the question Meta asks most often in its phone screens and onsites for this pattern (six reports in 2025 alone in our DB). Nobody wants a full parser: they want to see that you handle precedence with a tiny amount of state, parse multi-digit numbers and spaces cleanly, and get division toward zero right.

From your world

It is a one-token-lookbehind state machine, like a protocol parser that only acts when a field ends. The "operator before the number" is the pending action, applied when the delimiter (the next operator, or end of input) arrives. No backtracking, no buffer beyond one value.
