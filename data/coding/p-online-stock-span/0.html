Explain like I'm 5

Children stand in a line, and each new child asks "how many of us, counting me and going backwards, are no taller than me?". A tall child does not need to look at every short child behind someone: if the child right in front of her already "covers" three shorter children, she can count all four in one go. So each child, when she is covered, hands over her count and steps out of the way for good.

This is "previous greater element", asked as a design problem: the prices come one by one and you must answer immediately. The interview checks whether you know the monotonic stack, can argue the amortised bound, and handle equal prices correctly.

From your world

Think of a ring buffer of samples where you must report, for each new sample, how long the signal has been at or below the current level. Storing the run length with each kept sample is a skip list of one level: you hop over whole runs instead of walking samples.
