60-second recall

  • Cue: online "how many days back until a higher price" -> previous greater element.
  • State: stack of (price, span), prices strictly decreasing bottom -> top.
  • Step: span = 1; pop while top.price <= price, adding top.span; push (price, span).
  • Trap: <= (equal prices count) and carry the popped spans, not just the pop count.
  • Cost: amortised O(1) per call (each day pushed and popped once), O(n) space.
def next(self, price):
    span = 1
    while self.st and self.st[-1][0] <= price:
        span += self.st.pop()[1]
    self.st.append((price, span))
    return span

Quick quiz

Prices so far 70, 60, 65. What does next(66) return?

  1. 1
  2. 2
  3. 3
  4. 4
Stack is (70,1) (65,2); 65 ≤ 66 pops with span 2, 70 > 66 stops: 1 + 2 = 3.

What is the stack after the calls 3, 1, 2?

  1. (3,1) (1,1) (2,1)
  2. (3,1) (2,2)
  3. (2,3)
  4. (3,3)
2 pops (1,1) and gets span 2; 3 > 2 stays.

Why is the total work O(n) over n calls?

  1. Each day is pushed once and popped at most once
  2. The stack never exceeds log n
  3. Prices are bounded by 105
  4. Each call pops at most one entry
Pops are paid for by earlier pushes: at most n pops in total.
