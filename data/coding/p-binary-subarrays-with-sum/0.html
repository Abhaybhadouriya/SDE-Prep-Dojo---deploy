The story

Explain like I'm 5

A strip of tiles is either blank or has a star. How many stretches of neighbouring tiles hold exactly two stars? Counting "exactly two" directly is fiddly, because blank tiles at both ends can be added or left out. Counting "at most two" is easy: stand at a tile, walk left until you would see a third star, and every stretch ending here and starting anywhere you walked over counts. Do the same for "at most one", and subtract: what is left holds exactly two.

That subtraction is the whole trick, and it reappears in a dozen problems ("exactly k odd numbers", "exactly k distinct values").

From your world

It is the same move as counting packets in a size band from a histogram: packets of size exactly s = packets ≤ s minus packets ≤ s - 1. A "≤" query is the easy, monotonic one (a cumulative count); "exactly" is the difference of two of them.
