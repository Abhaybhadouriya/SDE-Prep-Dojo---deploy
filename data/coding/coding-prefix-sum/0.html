The story: the odometer

Explain like I'm 5

Your car has an odometer: the number that shows how far the car has gone since it was new. It never resets. If you want to know how far you drove on Tuesday, you do not need a diary of every trip. You read the odometer on Tuesday night (say 1,250 km) and on Monday night (1,180 km). Tuesday was 1,250 - 1,180 = 70 km.

One subtraction. It does not matter if Tuesday had one trip or forty.

A prefix sum is the odometer of an array. Next to the values a[0], a[1], ... you keep the running total P: how much has been added up so far. The total of any block of neighbours (a subarray, also called a range) is then "odometer at the end minus odometer at the start".

The second idea on this page is a small twist. If you remember every odometer reading you have seen, you can ask: "was there an earlier reading exactly 70 km less than now?" Each yes is a stretch of driving that was exactly 70 km long. That question, asked with a hash map, solves a whole family of interview problems that a sliding window cannot touch.
