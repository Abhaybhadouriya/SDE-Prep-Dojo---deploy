The story

Explain like I'm 5

Fold a strip of paper with letters on it so the two halves line up. Where the letters on both sides match all the way, you have found a word that reads the same both ways. Pick a fold line, then check the letters one step left and one step right of it, then two steps, until a pair does not match. The fold can sit on a letter ("level", fold on the "v") or between two letters ("noon", fold between the two "o"s). Try every fold line and remember the longest match.

That is the whole algorithm. The work is not missing the "between two letters" folds, and computing where the best palindrome starts from its centre and length.

From your world

The DP version is the same shape as validating nested structure from the inside out: a range is good only if its two ends agree and the inner range is good, like a frame whose header and trailer match around a payload that is itself valid. The centre version is a symmetric scan with two cursors moving apart, like comparing a buffer against its own reverse from the middle.
