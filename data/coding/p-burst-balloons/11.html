Interview follow-ups

Return the order of bursting, not just the total.

Store arg[l][r] = the k that won. Reconstruct recursively: order(l, r) = order(l, k) + order(k, r) + [k] (k is burst after everything on both sides). O(n) extra work after the DP.

How to answer

Same as parent pointers in any DP; note k comes LAST in the emitted order.

Likely follow-ups
  • Count how many orders reach the maximum (keep a count next to each best).
Bursting earns left + i + right (sum) instead of the product. Same approach?

Yes: the "last balloon in (l, r) sees l and r" argument only needs the gain of k to depend on k and its two final neighbours. Replace the product by the sum; the DP and its proof are unchanged.

How to answer

State the property that makes the DP valid rather than re-deriving everything.

Likely follow-ups
  • What if the gain depended on all remaining balloons? Then interval DP breaks and you are back to subsets.
Can you speed up O(n3)?

O(n3) is the expected answer. Knuth's optimisation (the best k moves monotonically with l and r) needs the quadrangle inequality, and that is not known to hold for this product cost, so do not claim it. A practical saving: burst every zero-valued balloon first. It earns 0 and only ever makes its neighbours' products 0, so removing zeros up front never lowers the total and shrinks n.

How to answer

Be honest: name Knuth / divide-and-conquer optimisation and the condition they need.

Likely follow-ups
  • Which classic interval DP does satisfy it? (optimal BST, merging stones with k = 2.)
Write the top-down version and give its complexity.

f(l, r) with a memo table of size (n + 2)2; each state loops over r - l - 1 choices: O(n3) time, O(n2) memory, recursion depth O(n). Same as the table.

How to answer

Interviewers often accept top-down; it is the clearest way to show the "last balloon" choice.

Likely follow-ups
  • Convert to bottom-up: what order must the cells be filled in, and why?
The balloons form a circle (the first and last are neighbours).

There are no virtual 1s. Fix which balloon is burst last overall (it sees only itself when it bursts, or define the rule for the last two), cut the circle there into a line with that balloon at both ends as walls, and run the line DP: n runs of O(n3), or one O(n3) DP over a doubled array.

How to answer

Clarify the scoring for the last one or two balloons first; the doubling trick is the standard way to turn a circle into intervals.

Likely follow-ups
  • Same doubling for circular merge-stones.
