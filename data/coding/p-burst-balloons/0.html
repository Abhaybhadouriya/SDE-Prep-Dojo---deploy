The story

Explain like I'm 5

A row of jars each holds a number. When you smash a jar, you win (left neighbour × jar × right neighbour) sweets, and the two neighbours slide together. The order you smash in changes the total. Thinking "which jar first?" is confusing, because after it breaks the left and right parts squeeze together and affect each other. Thinking "which jar is smashed LAST between these two walls?" is easy: by then everything else between the walls is gone, so its neighbours are just the two walls.

This "decide the last action, not the first" flip is the main idea of interval DP and the reason this problem is famous. Once you see it, the code is ten lines.

From your world

It is like the matrix-chain multiplication problem you may know from compiler or BLAS optimisation: the cost of the final multiplication depends only on the outer dimensions, and the two sub-chains are optimised independently. Here the "outer dimensions" are the two balloons l and r that are still standing.
