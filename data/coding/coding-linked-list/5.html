Move 4: merging by splicing

To merge two sorted lists you do not copy values into a new list. You keep a tail pointer (starting at a dummy), compare the two front nodes, hang the smaller one on tail, and advance that list. When one list runs out, hang the whole other list on in one step. No node is allocated; the merge is O(n + m) time and O(1) extra memory.

Merging is a building block. Sort List (148) is merge sort on a list: find the middle with fast/slow (first middle, so two nodes split into one and one), cut the list in two, sort each half, merge. Merge k Sorted Lists (23) merges with a min-heap of the k front nodes (O(N log k)), or merges pairs of lists in rounds. Add Two Numbers (2) walks two lists together with a carry and appends new digit nodes to a dummy tail. Palindrome Linked List (234) and Reorder List (143) are the combination play: find the middle, reverse the second half, then walk the two halves together.

When you cut a list in half, set the first half's last next to null. If you forget, the "first half" still runs into the second half and your merge or comparison walks the same nodes twice or loops forever.
