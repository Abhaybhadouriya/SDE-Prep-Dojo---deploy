Three digit-loop rules cover Reverse Integer, String to Integer (atoi), Palindrome Number and Plus One:

  1. Pop and push. d = x % 10; x /= 10; takes the last digit. r = r * 10 + d appends it.
  2. Check before you push. If r > INT_MAX / 10, or r == INT_MAX / 10 and d > 7, the push would overflow. Mirror it for negatives (INT_MIN / 10 and d < -8).
  3. Know your %. In C, C++, Java and Go, -7 % 10 == -7 (the sign of the dividend). In Python, -7 % 10 == 3. For a non-negative modulo in C-like languages, write ((a % m) + m) % m.

Modular arithmetic. When a problem says "return the answer modulo 109 + 7", reduce after every + and *: (a + b) % m, (a * b) % m in 64-bit, and (a - b + m) % m for subtraction. Division is the exception: a / b becomes a * inverse(b), which comes from fast power in the next section. 109 + 7 is prime, and the product of two values below it fits in a signed 64-bit integer.
