The story

Explain like I'm 5

You walk along a mountain trail and write down your height above the start after every stretch, up or down. A friend asks: "How many times did a run of consecutive stretches climb exactly 3 metres?" You do not re-walk anything. At each point you look at your notebook: if you are at 5 m now, every earlier moment when you stood at 2 m starts such a run. Count those moments, then write down your current height and keep walking.

The notebook is a hash map from "height reached" (prefix sum) to "how many times", and the climb of exactly k between two moments is a subarray that sums to k.

From your world

Think of a byte counter on a NIC: the bytes sent between two timestamps are counter[t2] - counter[t1], you never re-add the packets in between. A prefix sum is that monotonic counter, except here values can be negative, so it can also go down.
