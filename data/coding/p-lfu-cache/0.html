The story

Explain like I'm 5

A small library shelf has room for two books. Every time a book is borrowed it gets one more sticker. When a new book arrives and the shelf is full, the librarian removes the book with the fewest stickers; if two books have the same number, the one nobody has touched for longer goes. To do this fast, she keeps the books in rows by sticker count, newest at the front of each row, and remembers which row is the lowest one that is not empty.

Rows by sticker count are the frequency buckets, the order inside a row is an LRU list, and "lowest non-empty row" is minFreq.

From your world

Pure LFU is rare in kernels because old popularity never decays (a page hot an hour ago stays "hot"). Linux's page cache uses two LRU lists (active and inactive) as a cheap frequency signal, and ZFS's ARC balances a recency list against a frequency list. Saying that LFU needs ageing is a strong follow-up answer.
