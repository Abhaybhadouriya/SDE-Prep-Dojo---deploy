60-second recall

  • Cue: grid + "surrounded / enclosed / reaches the edge" -> flood from the border, not from each region.
  • Insight: survives = reachable from a border O; one multi-source BFS answers that for every cell.
  • State: queue of cells + a third marker '#' in the board itself (no visited grid).
  • Trap: tag on push, decide before flipping, restore '#', avoid deep recursion.
  • Cost: O(m·n) time, O(m·n) worst-case queue.
for (r, c) on the border: keep(r, c)        # 'O' -> '#', push
while q:
    r, c = q.popleft()
    for nr, nc in 4 neighbours: keep(nr, nc)
for each cell: '#' -> 'O', anything else -> 'X'

Where should the flood fill start?

  1. From every O inside the board
  2. From every O on the border
  3. From the top-left cell only
Survivors are exactly the cells reachable from a border O, so seed the BFS with all border O cells at once.

In example 2, why does (2,3) survive although it is not on the edge?

  1. Diagonals count as connections
  2. It has two O neighbours
  3. It connects through (3,3) to (3,4) on the edge
Reachability is transitive: a path of O cells to the border is enough.

Why tag safe cells with '#' instead of flipping during the BFS?

  1. Decide first, flip later: the final pass needs to tell safe O from captured O
  2. '#' is faster to compare
  3. LeetCode requires '#'
The third marker separates 'safe' from 'not reached' without a visited grid; the last pass restores '#' to O.
