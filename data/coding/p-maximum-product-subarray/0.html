The story

Explain like I'm 5

You walk along a row of cards and multiply the numbers on a stretch of cards next to each other. You want the biggest result. A big negative number looks bad, but if another negative comes later, the two cancel and the result jumps up. So while you walk, you remember two things: the biggest result so far ending at your card, and the most negative one, because the most negative one might become the biggest after the next negative card. A zero wipes everything, and you start again after it.

Sums only grow when you add a positive number. Products can flip sign, so "the worst run so far" is a candidate for "the best run" one step later.

From your world

Think of a chain of gain stages where each stage multiplies the signal and may invert it (negative gain). The strongest positive output of a contiguous chain is either a chain with an even number of inverters, or it comes from the most negative partial chain meeting one more inverter. A zero-gain stage is a dead link: nothing that passes through it survives, so the chain splits into independent segments there.
