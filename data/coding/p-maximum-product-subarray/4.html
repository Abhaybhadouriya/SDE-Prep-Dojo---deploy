Approaches: brute -> better -> optimal

Hint 1

Inside a block with no zeros, the sign of the product only depends on how many negatives it holds. With an even count, is it ever worse to take the whole block?

Hint 2

For the product of a subarray ending at i, you need the subarray ending at i-1 that gives the largest product when multiplied by nums[i]. Which one is that when nums[i] is negative?

1. Brute force - every start, running product

Idea: fix the start i, extend the end j to the right, and multiply the running product by nums[j] each time. Keep the largest product seen.

best = nums[0]
for i in range(n):
    p = 1
    for j in range(i, n):
        p *= nums[j]; best = max(best, p)
O(n²)time
O(1)extra space

What is wasted: blocks are not independent. After the first zero-free stretch, most of the products are repeated work, and only two kinds of block can ever win. -> rung 2.

2. Better - prefix and suffix products (two scans)

Idea: split the array at zeros. Inside one zero-free segment, if the number of negatives is even, the whole segment is the best. If it is odd, you must drop exactly one negative "end": either everything up to and including the first negative, or everything from the last negative on. Both candidates are a prefix or a suffix of the segment. So scan left to right with a running product (reset to 1 after a zero), scan right to left the same way, and take the largest value either scan ever holds.

best, pre, suf = nums[0], 1, 1
for i in range(n):
    pre = (pre or 1) * nums[i]          # reset after a zero
    suf = (suf or 1) * nums[n - 1 - i]
    best = max(best, pre, suf)
O(n)time
O(1)extra space

What is wasted: nothing in time, and it is a neat proof, but it depends on a counting argument that only works for products. It does not extend to "longest subarray with positive product" style questions as directly, and the prefix product can grow large before a negative brings it back (community comments report needing 64-bit or double for it on LeetCode). -> rung 3 is the method interviewers expect.

3. Optimal - max and min product ending here

hi = lo = best = nums[0]
for x in nums[1:]:
    if x < 0: hi, lo = lo, hi
    hi = max(x, hi * x)
    lo = min(x, lo * x)
    best = max(best, hi)
  1. State. hi = the largest product of a subarray that ends exactly at the current index; lo = the smallest (most negative) one.
  2. Transition. A subarray ending at i is either [x] alone or a subarray ending at i-1 extended by x. Multiplying by x > 0 keeps the order, so the new max comes from the old max. Multiplying by x < 0 reverses it, so the new max comes from the old min: swap first, then use the same two lines.
  3. Base case. hi = lo = best = nums[0]: the only subarray ending at index 0 is [nums[0]].
  4. Zero. With x = 0 both lines give 0, so the next step effectively restarts from the next value. No special case needed.
  5. Answer. The best subarray ends somewhere, so best = max over i of hi.
O(n)time
O(1)extra space

Why it works: for a fixed x, the function p -> p·x is increasing when x > 0 and decreasing when x < 0. So over all products p of subarrays ending at i-1, the largest p·x is reached at the largest p (x > 0) or at the smallest p (x < 0). Keeping the two extremes is therefore enough to know the largest and the smallest product ending at i, by induction over i.

Proof (optional)

Claim: after processing index i, hii = max and loi = min of P(j, i) over all j ≤ i, where P(j, i) is the product of nums[j..i]. Base: i = 0, only P(0, 0) = nums[0]. Step: every P(j, i) with j < i equals P(j, i-1)·x, and P(i, i) = x. The set {P(j, i-1)·x} is the image of an interval-like set under a monotone map, so its max and min are the images of hii-1 and loi-1 (swapped if x < 0; with x = 0 all are 0). Adding x itself gives the formulas. The answer is the max over i of hii, because the best subarray has some end index.
