The story

Explain like I'm 5

A shop clerk reads prices off messy handwritten tags. She ignores blank space at the start, notes a minus sign if there is one, then reads digits until she hits something that is not a digit, like a letter or a dot, and stops right there. If the tag says a price bigger than the till can show, she just types the till's biggest number.

The algorithm is exactly that clerk: a fixed order of small rules, one character at a time, never looking back.

From your world

This is strtol() / the kernel's kstrtoint() family with saturation instead of -ERANGE, the code you write when parsing a sysfs write, a /proc tunable or a command-line option. The overflow check before the multiply is the same one you use for safe size arithmetic (check_mul_overflow()), just done by hand.
