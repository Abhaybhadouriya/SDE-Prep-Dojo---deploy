The story: the driving test

Explain like I'm 5

In a driving test you do not win by reaching the shop fastest. The examiner sits next to you with a sheet of paper. She ticks boxes: did you look in the mirror, did you signal, did you stop at the line, did you stay calm when a dog ran out.

Two kids reach the shop. Kid A drove fast, said nothing, and missed a stop sign. Kid B drove at a normal speed, said "checking the mirror, turning left now", and stopped at every line. Kid B passes.

A coding round is that test. The shop is a working solution. The sheet of paper is a scorecard. The interviewer is watching how you got there as much as whether you arrived.

From your world

You already know this kind of test: it is a patch review on a kernel mailing list. Nobody merges a patch only because it compiles. Reviewers want a commit message that explains the why, a clear diff, a note on the edge cases (what if the ring buffer is full, what if the lock is already held), and proof that you tested it. A coding round grades the same four things in 45 minutes, live: explain, solve, write clean code, test.
