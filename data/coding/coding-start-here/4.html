The 45-minute budget for one question

Most coding slots are 45 or 60 minutes, and 5-10 of those go to introductions and your questions at the end. That leaves about 35-45 minutes for the problem. Step through how a strong candidate spends them, and which box on the scorecard each part fills.

  1. Minutes 0-2: a one-line intro. Do not tell your life story; the clock is running.
  2. Minutes 2-7: restate the problem in your own words, ask about input size, value range, duplicates, empty input, and what to return if there is no answer. Write one example and its expected output.
  3. Minutes 7-15: say the brute force and its cost, then the better idea and its cost. Get a "yes, go ahead" before you type.
  4. Minutes 15-35: code it, narrating as you go ("this map stores the last index of each value"). Short helper functions, real names.
  5. Minutes 35-41: dry-run your own example line by line, then try the edge cases you listed. Fix bugs out loud.
  6. Minutes 41-45: the follow-up. This is where SDE-2 and SDE-3 signal is decided. If time runs out, describe the change in words.
