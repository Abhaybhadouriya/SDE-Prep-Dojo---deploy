How this module and the Dojo Curated 450 are organised

The module holds every problem from the well-known sheets plus the company lists: 3,408 problems in total. You will not solve them all, and you should not try. They are organised in three ways so you can always find the next useful one.

1. By pattern: group → subgroup → variant

Every problem sits in one place in a three-level tree. The group is the broad tool (sliding window), the subgroup is the shape (variable-size window), and the variant is the exact question type (longest valid window). Problems in the same variant share one template, so solving two or three of them teaches you the rest.

2. By priority: the Dojo Curated 450 (Tier A)

Every problem gets a priority score from three things: how many respected sheets include it, how often companies ask it (from saved company frequency lists), and how many 2024-26 interview reports name it. The top 450 by score form Tier A, the Dojo Curated 450. The next 600 are Tier B, the rest Tier C. Tier A contains every Blind 75 problem and 149 of the NeetCode 150. The sheets guide explains the formula in detail.

The Dojo Curated 450 list (/coding/list/dojo-curated-450) is ordered by group: Arrays & Hashing (35), Two Pointers (18), Sliding Window (17), Binary Search (24), Linked List (20), Stack & Queue (27), Heap (19), Intervals (10), Greedy (15), Backtracking (19), Trees (52), Tries (6), Graphs (49), Dynamic Programming (47), Bits (8), Math (21), Strings (15), Matrix (4), Sorting (4) and Data Structure Design (20). Six entries are not on LeetCode (TUF classics such as aggressive cows); they link to their source.

3. By list and by company

  • Lists (/coding/list/<id>): Blind 75, NeetCode 150/250, LeetCode 75, Top Interview 150, Striver's A2Z and SDE sheets, Love Babbar 450 and more, each in its original order and sections.
  • Companies (/coding/company/<id>): 101 company lists with a 1-8 frequency bar per problem.
  • Problem pages (/coding/p/<slug>): a data card, and for written problems our restatement, brute-to-optimal approaches, a visual dry run, code in four languages and similar problems.
  • Progress and revision: mark a problem solved and it comes back for review after 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days (spaced repetition). Progress lives in your browser and syncs if you sign in.

A simple plan that works: pick one group in the Dojo 450, read its lesson, solve the group's problems in order, and let the revision queue bring old ones back. Use company lists only in the last two weeks before a specific loop.
