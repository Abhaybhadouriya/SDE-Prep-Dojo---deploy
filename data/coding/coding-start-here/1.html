The rounds, in the order you meet them

Most product companies (Amazon, Microsoft, Google, Meta, Flipkart, Walmart, Uber and many more) run the same pipeline with small changes. The names differ; the shape does not.

RoundWhat it isWhat passes itWhat fails it
Online assessment (OA)A timed test on a platform (HackerRank, Codility, HackerEarth, or the company's own). Hidden test cases grade you. Common for SDE-1, campus and Indian product companies; often skipped for experienced hires with a referral.All hidden tests green, including the big inputs. Speed matters because the clock is short.A correct but slow solution that times out on the large cases; overflow; forgetting the empty input.
Phone / video screenOne engineer, a shared editor (CoderPad, a Google Doc, an in-house tool), usually no compiler. Meta describes its initial tech screen as a 45-minute conversation with an engineer.A working optimal or near-optimal solution, explained while you write, plus a dry run.Silence; jumping into code before agreeing on the approach; not finishing.
Onsite loop3 to 5 interviews on one day (often virtual now): 2-3 coding, 1 design (HLD or LLD), 1 behavioural. Each interviewer writes an independent scorecard.Strong signal on the rubric in each round. One weak round can be balanced; two usually cannot.Solving one problem but going quiet on the follow-up ("now the input is a stream").
Bar raiser / hiring committeeAmazon adds a Bar Raiser, an interviewer from outside the team who can veto. Google sends the packet to a hiring committee that never met you. Others have a hiring-manager round.Consistent scorecards: the same strengths show up in every round.Mixed signals, or the bar raiser's own question going badly (these are often harder: see the data below).

Formats change by team and by year. Before a real loop, read the company page here: Amazon, Microsoft, Google, Meta, Flipkart, Walmart, Uber. Each lists recent reports with links.

Flipkart, PhonePe, Razorpay and Swiggy also run a 90-120 minute machine-coding round; it is covered in the LLD module's machine-coding guide.
