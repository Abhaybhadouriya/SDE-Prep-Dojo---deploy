The story

Explain like I'm 5

You fly over a lake in a plane and look down at a map drawn in squares: some squares are grass, some are water. You want to know how many separate pieces of grass there are. Every time you spot grass you have not coloured yet, you say "a new island!" and colour the whole piece, square by square, walking only up, down, left and right. When the map is fully checked, the number you said is the answer.

From your world

A grid is an implicit graph: each cell is a node and its edges are the four neighbours, computed on the fly, like a router that does not store adjacency but computes next hops from an address. Counting islands is counting connected components, the same job as finding broadcast domains after you merge switches with links (union-find does that in the follow-up).
