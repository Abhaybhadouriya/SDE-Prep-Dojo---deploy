The story

Explain like I'm 5

Three friends each write a word on a strip of paper, and you lay the strips one above the other, left edges lined up. Now slide a ruler from left to right one letter at a time. As long as all letters under the ruler are the same, you keep going. The moment one strip shows a different letter, or one strip has run out, you stop. Everything to the left of the ruler is the shared beginning.

The ruler is the whole algorithm. The only thing to get right is the stopping rule: "a strip ran out" is just as important as "a letter differs".

From your world

It is longest-prefix thinking from routing tables, turned around: instead of finding the route with the longest prefix that matches one address, you find the longest prefix that all addresses share, which is what route summarisation (supernetting) computes from a list of networks, bit by bit.
