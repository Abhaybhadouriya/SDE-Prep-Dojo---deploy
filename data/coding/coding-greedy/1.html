What greedy is, next to DP and backtracking

All three approaches face the same thing: a series of decisions. Where they differ is how many of the possible futures they look at.

ApproachAt each decisionTypical costCorrect when
BacktrackingTry every option, undo, try the nextExponential (2n, n!)Always (it checks everything)
Dynamic programmingTry every option, but remember the best result per stateStates x choices, often O(n2) or O(n x W)The best answer is built from best answers to subproblems
GreedyTake one option (the best-looking) and move onO(n) or O(n log n) for the sortSome best answer always contains the greedy choice

The last cell is the key sentence of this page: greedy is correct when there is always an optimal answer that agrees with the greedy choice. If that holds at every step, then by induction the whole greedy answer is optimal. Two words you will hear for this:

  • Greedy-choice property: some optimal solution starts with the greedy choice.
  • Optimal substructure: after making that choice, what is left is a smaller copy of the same problem.

In practice, a greedy solution in an interview has three parts: the order (what you sort by, or which direction you scan), the rule (what you take or skip at each item), and the reason (one or two sentences on why no better answer is lost). Interviewers grade the reason. "It passed the examples" is not a reason.
