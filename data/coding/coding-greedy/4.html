Shape 2: sort, then assign

Many greedy problems become obvious after one sort. You sort, then walk once and match, pair or group items with a simple local rule.

  • Assign Cookies (LC 455): each child needs a cookie of at least some size; each cookie goes to at most one child; maximise happy children. Sort both lists. Give the smallest cookie that is big enough to the least greedy child. Exchange: a bigger cookie spent on a small need could only hurt a later child. Two pointers, O(n log n).
  • Boats to Save People (LC 881): boats hold at most two people under a weight limit. Sort; pair the heaviest person with the lightest if they fit, else the heaviest goes alone. The heaviest person must go somewhere, and the lightest is the partner most likely to fit.
  • Two City Scheduling (LC 1029): 2n people, exactly n fly to each city. Sort by costA - costB: the first n go to A. The sort key is "how much I save by choosing A", which is the classic way to turn a two-way choice into an order.
  • Partition Labels (LC 763): no sort, but the same flavour. Record the last position of each letter. Scan, stretching the current part's end to the last position of every letter you see; when i equals that end, cut. This is jump-game reach in disguise.

The pattern "the smallest (or largest) remaining item has only one sensible place to go" is a strong greedy signal. Say it out loud: it is your proof.
