How to recognise a greedy problem

You readThinkShape
"can you reach the end", "fewest jumps", "minimum taps to cover"track the furthest reachReachability
circular route, running balance, "starting point"running sum with reset + total checkGas station
"maximum number of non-overlapping", "minimum arrows", "meeting rooms"sort by end (or start + heap for rooms)Interval ends
two lists to match (children / cookies, people / boats)sort both, two pointersSort and assign
"each neighbour must get more", rules from both sidesone pass per direction, take maxTwo pass
brackets with wildcards, minimum insertions or swapscounter, or [lo, hi] rangeBalance counter
"arrange to form the largest / smallest", order of jobspairwise swap test becomes the comparatorComparator
"remove k digits to make it smallest"greedy with a monotonic stackStack greedy

Signals that greedy is probably wrong: the problem asks to count the ways (that is DP), the values are arbitrary weights with a capacity (0/1 knapsack is DP; only the fractional version is greedy), or a tiny counterexample beats your rule in under a minute. Coin change with arbitrary coins is the standard trap: use DP.

Test your greedy rule on 3 to 4 element inputs by hand before coding. Most wrong greedy rules fail on an input that small. If the interviewer asks "are you sure?", that is often a hint to look for one.
