The problem (in our words)

A sensor sends integer readings one at a time. Build a class MedianFinder that can report the median of all readings received so far. The median of a sorted list is its middle value; when the list has an even length there is no single middle, and the median is the average of the two middle values (for [3,5,8] it is 5; for [3,5] it is 4.0).

  • MedianFinder() creates an empty object.
  • void addNum(int num) records one reading num.
  • double findMedian() returns the median of every reading recorded so far. Answers within 10-5 of the true value are accepted.

The judge sends a list of method names and a list of argument lists, and expects a list of results (null for the constructor and addNum).

Constraints:

  • -105 <= num <= 105
  • At least one reading has been added before any call to findMedian.
  • At most 5 * 104 calls in total to addNum and findMedian.

Follow-ups from the original: what if every reading is in [0, 100]? What if 99% of them are?

Practice it: LeetCode 295 · GfG Median in a Stream · TUF article

Constraints tell you: 5·104 calls, and they can be mixed in any order. Re-sorting on every query (O(n log n) each) is about 1010 steps in the worst case: too slow. Insertion into a sorted array is O(n) per add (2.5·109 moves worst case): borderline. You want O(log n) per add. Values are small (|num| <= 105), so the sum of two tops fits in an int, but compute the average in floating point.
