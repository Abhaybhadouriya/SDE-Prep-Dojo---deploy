The story

Explain like I'm 5

Kids arrive one by one at a photo line and you must always know who stands in the middle by height. Instead of re-sorting the whole line each time, you keep two groups: the shorter kids on the left, with the tallest of them at the front, and the taller kids on the right, with the shortest of them at the front. The middle kid is always one of the two at the fronts. A new kid only has to be compared with those two.

That is the whole trick: you never need the full order, only the boundary between the two halves.

From your world

Think of a p50 latency gauge on a router that must answer "what is the median RTT so far?" at any moment. Keeping every sample sorted costs O(n) per insert. Two priority queues (the same structure as a kernel timer heap) keep only the boundary in order, so each sample costs O(log n) and the gauge read is O(1).
