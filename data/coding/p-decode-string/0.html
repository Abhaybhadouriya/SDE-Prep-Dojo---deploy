The story

Explain like I'm 5

A knitting pattern says "2[knit 3[purl]]". You read it as: do the part in the brackets twice, and inside that, purl three times. To follow it you must finish the inner instruction before you can repeat the outer one. So you keep a little note for each bracket you are inside: "when this bracket ends, repeat it this many times and put it after what I had". That pile of notes is a stack.

From your world

It is the same structure as nested TLV or ASN.1 decoding, or nested scopes in a parser: on "open" push the parent's context, on "close" pop it and merge the child's result into it. A recursive descent parser keeps that context on the call stack; an iterative one keeps it on an explicit stack, which also avoids stack overflow on deep input.
