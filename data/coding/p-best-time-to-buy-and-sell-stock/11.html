Interview follow-ups

Return the buy and sell days, not only the profit.

How to answer: keep cheapIdx next to cheapest; when best improves, record (cheapIdx, j). Still O(1) space.

Likely follow-ups: ties - earliest pair or shortest holding period? Decide with the comparison (> vs >=).

The prices arrive as a stream, one per second.

How to answer: the algorithm is already online: two variables, O(1) per tick. Report best at any time.

Likely follow-ups: a sliding window of the last W days - then you need the minimum of a window, which is a monotonic deque.

I give you the day-to-day changes instead of prices.

How to answer: the answer is the maximum subarray sum of the changes, floored at 0 (Kadane with cur = max(0, cur + d)), because consecutive changes telescope to sell - buy.

Likely follow-ups: must trade exactly once (no 0 floor) - use the non-empty Kadane.

You may make up to two (or k) deals.

How to answer: state machine DP: for each deal t keep buy[t] = max(buy[t], sell[t-1] - p) and sell[t] = max(sell[t], buy[t] + p). O(nk) time, O(k) space.

Likely follow-ups: k >= n/2 means unlimited, so switch to the greedy sum of rises.

Each sale costs a fee, or you must rest a day after selling.

How to answer: those are LC 714 and 309: two or three states (hold / free / cooldown) updated per day.

Likely follow-ups: why the order of updates in one day matters (use the old values).

Short selling is allowed (sell first, buy back later).

How to answer: also track the running maximum and the best runningMax - p; answer is the larger of the two directions.
