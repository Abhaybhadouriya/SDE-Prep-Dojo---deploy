Dry run

p = 12, q = 18 on the example tree. Grey nodes have been passed; the ring is the node being compared.

  1. We look for the lowest common ancestor of 12 and 18 (blue). Let lo = 12 and hi = 18. We only ever compare with the current node; no recursion, no stack.
  2. At 20: both 12 and 18 are smaller than 20 (it is enough to check hi < 20), so both live in 20's left subtree. 20 is a common ancestor, but not the lowest one. Move left.
  3. At 10: both are larger than 10 (lo > 10), so both are in the right subtree. Move right. The subtree of 5 and the whole right half of the tree are never looked at.
  4. At 15: 12 is on its left and 18 on its right (lo <= 15 <= hi). Going further would lose one of them, so 15 is the lowest node that has both below it. Answer 15, found in 3 steps = the depth of 15.
