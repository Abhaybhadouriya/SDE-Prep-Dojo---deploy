Taking hints well

A hint is not a penalty in itself; ignoring or fighting one is. Interviewers give hints to keep the session productive, and they note how quickly you use them. A good response has three parts: repeat the hint in your words, connect it to the problem, act on it.

The interviewer saysWhat it usually meansA good reply
"What if the array were sorted?"Sorting or two pointers or binary search is the path."If sorted, equal or overlapping items are adjacent, so I can sweep once. Sorting costs n log n, which fits n = 104. Let me do that."
"Can you do better?"Your complexity is above the target."The cost comes from the inner loop. What I keep recomputing is ___, so I will store it in ___."
"Are you sure about that line?"There is a bug there, now."Let me trace it with the touching example... you are right, it should be <=, not <. Thanks."
"What happens when n is 109?"Memory or time does not scale; a math or streaming idea is wanted."Then I cannot store all of it. I need something O(1) or O(log n) in memory, like a running value or a binary search on the answer."
"Let's move on."Time is short, or they have what they need."Sure. In one sentence, the remaining fix would be ___." Then move.

If you disagree with a hint, test it instead of arguing: "Let me check that against the nested example." Either you find the bug, or you show politely, with evidence, why the code is right. Both are good signals.
