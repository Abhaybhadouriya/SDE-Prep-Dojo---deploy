The story: building with blocks while a friend watches

Explain like I'm 5

You and a friend are building a tower from blocks. If you build in silence, your friend cannot help, and if the tower falls they do not know why. If you say "I am putting the big blocks at the bottom so it does not wobble", your friend nods, or says "careful, that one is cracked". Talking turns a test into teamwork.

Interviewers write notes while you work, and they can only write down what they see and hear. A candidate who silently types a perfect solution leaves an empty notes page for "communication" and "problem solving". A candidate who explains a decent solution, spots their own bug out loud and fixes it often scores higher. Talking is not decoration; it is how your thinking becomes evidence.

From your world

You already do this on a good incident bridge or in a kernel patch series. The cover letter says what the series does and why; each commit message states the invariant it keeps ("this lock now protects the list and the counter"); and when a reviewer on the list says "what about the error path?", you answer the point, not the person. A coding interview is a 45-minute patch review where you write the patch live. Narrate like a cover letter, comment like a commit message.
