Getting unstuck out loud

Everybody gets stuck; the signal is what you do next. Silence for two minutes looks like being lost. Working through a routine out loud looks like engineering.

  1. Say that you are thinking. "Let me take thirty seconds to think about this quietly." Then really take thirty seconds, not three minutes.
  2. Re-read the constraints. n <= 20 suggests 2n; values <= 105 suggest counting arrays; "sorted" suggests binary search or two pointers.
  3. Solve a tiny case by hand. Narrate what you do: "I am looking at the closest earlier bigger element..." That sentence is often the algorithm (a monotonic stack, here).
  4. Name the slow query. "I keep asking for the minimum of the current window" leads to a heap or a deque.
  5. Ask a narrow question. "Is it OK if the output is in any order?" is fine. "What's the trick?" is not.
  6. Fall back to the brute force. With ten minutes left, code the correct slow version cleanly, test it and explain the improvement in words. Correct and slow scores better than clever and unfinished.
