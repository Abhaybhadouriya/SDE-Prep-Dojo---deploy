  • Cue: nearest bigger element to the right, as a distance -> monotonic stack.
  • Insight: today pops every strictly colder waiting day and writes today - idx; then it waits itself.
  • State: stack of indices still waiting; temps non-increasing bottom -> top.
  • Trap: push indices, strict <, leftovers stay 0.
  • Cost: O(n) amortised (push once, pop once); O(1)-space variant hops along answer[] from the right.
st = []                              # indices, values monotone
for i, x in enumerate(a):
    while st and a[st[-1]] < x:      # x is the next greater of the top
        j = st.pop(); res[j] = i - j
    st.append(i)

What is kept on the stack?

  1. Temperatures in increasing order
  2. Indices of days still waiting for a warmer day, temperatures non-increasing bottom to top
  3. All indices seen so far
Store indices so you can compute the distance i - j when a day is resolved.

Why is the whole loop O(n) even though a day can pop many entries?

  1. Each index is pushed once and popped at most once
  2. The stack never holds more than 2 entries
  3. Temperatures are bounded
Total pops <= total pushes = n, so the work is amortised O(n).

A day equal to the top's temperature does what?

  1. Pops it (answer 0)
  2. Does not pop it: 'warmer' means strictly greater
  3. Clears the stack
Use strict < when popping; equal days keep waiting.
