Approaches: brute -> better -> optimal

Hint 1

When day j turns out to be warmer than day i, which other days can day j also answer in the same moment?

Hint 2

Keep the days that have no answer yet. What shape do their temperatures have, read in order?

1. Brute force - scan forward from every day

Idea: for each i, walk j = i+1, i+2, ... until t[j] > t[i]; write j - i, or 0 if you fall off the end.

O(n²)time
O(1)extra space

What is wasted: on [90,89,...,31,100] every day scans to the end. The scan for day i re-reads days that an earlier scan already proved colder. -> rung 2 or 3.

2. Better - "next occurrence" table over the 71 temperatures

Idea: walk from the right and keep next[v] = the nearest index seen so far with temperature v. For day i, the answer is the minimum of next[v] over all v > t[i], minus i. Then set next[t[i]] = i.

O(n·W)time, W = 71
O(W)extra space

Good to mention, because it shows you read the constraints. It breaks as soon as the interviewer says "temperatures are arbitrary integers". -> rung 3 does not care about the value range.

3. Optimal - monotonic stack of waiting days

for day, t in enumerate(temps):
    while pending and temps[pending[-1]] < t:   # today answers the colder days on top
        prev = pending.pop()
        wait[prev] = day - prev
    pending.append(day)                          # today waits for its own warmer day
  1. The stack holds days with no answer yet, oldest at the bottom. Store indices, not temperatures: you need the index to compute the distance and to know where to write.
  2. Their temperatures never increase from bottom to top. A warmer day would have popped the colder ones before being pushed on top of them.
  3. A new day pops every strictly colder day on top and is exactly their first warmer day: anything between them and today was not warmer (otherwise they would already be gone).
  4. Days left on the stack at the end never met a warmer day; their answer stays 0 from the initialisation.
O(n)time
O(n)space (stack)

Why it works: when day j pops day i, no day k strictly between them is warmer than day i: if t[k] > t[i], day k would have popped day i first, and day j could not pop it again. So j is the first warmer day for i. The loop is O(n) by amortisation: each index is pushed once and popped at most once, so the inner while runs at most n times over the whole input.

3b. Same cost, no stack - hop along the answers from the right

Fill answer from right to left. For day i, start at j = i+1. If t[j] is not warmer, you do not have to look at j+1: every day between j and its own answer is no warmer than t[j], hence no warmer than t[i]. So jump straight to j + answer[j]; if answer[j] == 0, nothing to the right is warmer than t[j], so there is no answer for i either.

This is the trick from one of the top community posts. It uses only the output array (O(1) extra space). The hops for day i visit exactly the days a right-to-left stack would pop, so it is still amortised O(n).
