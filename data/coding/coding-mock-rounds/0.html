The story: the fire drill

Explain like I'm 5

At school the teacher explains what to do in a fire: leave your bag, walk, do not run, go to the big tree in the playground. Everybody nods. Then one day the alarm rings for real practice, a fire drill. Suddenly it is loud, people bump into each other, someone goes back for a lunch box. The drill shows what the explanation could not: what you actually do when the bell is ringing.

A mock interview is a fire drill for your coding round. You already know the patterns. The mock shows what happens to that knowledge when a clock is running and someone is watching you think.

Most candidates who fail a coding round did not lack the pattern. They spent 25 minutes on the first problem and never reached the second, or they coded in silence, or they said "done" without a dry run and the interviewer found the bug. None of that shows up when you practise alone with no clock, because nothing forces it to. Mocks force it.

From your world

You would never ship a failover path that has only been reviewed on paper. You pull the cable on the primary in the lab and watch: does the standby take over within the timer, do the counters look right, does the log tell you what happened? A mock round is pulling the cable on your interview skills. The unit tests (solving problems one by one) pass; the mock is the integration test under real timing.
