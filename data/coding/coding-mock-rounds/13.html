Scenario drills: grading your own mock honestly

A mock only helps if the debrief is honest. In each drill the mock went the same way; the weak self-grade and the strong self-grade lead to very different next weeks.

Scenario: solved, but with two hints

You solved the Medium in 38 minutes. The interviewer gave a hint at minute 12 (use a heap) and another at minute 25 (handle the empty case).

Weak: "Solved it, so that is a pass. Next mock." The two hints are exactly what a real interviewer writes down, and they point at what you should practise.

Strong: "Solved with two hints: that is a lean hire at best. Hint 1 says I missed the heap cue ('k largest', 'repeatedly take the smallest'), so I add three heap problems to this week. Hint 2 says my edge-case sweep is not a habit yet, so for the next five problems I write the empty and one-element cases before I code. I retry this exact problem in three days, without hints, in 25 minutes."

Likely follow-ups
  • How do you turn a hint into a practice item? (name the cue you missed, then drill problems with that cue)
  • When does a solved problem still count as a fail in your log?

Scenario: ran out of time on the follow-up

You finished the main problem in 20 minutes, cleanly. The follow-up ("now the input is a stream") took the rest of the time and you did not finish it.

Weak: "The follow-up was unfair; the main part was perfect." Or the opposite: "I failed." Neither says what to fix.

Strong: "Main problem: strong, 20 minutes, tests done. Follow-up: I jumped into code without stating the new limits (O(k) memory, one pass). Next time I spend two minutes on the design out loud first. Grade: hire on the main problem, weak on the follow-up, so a lean hire overall. One action: practise three 'now it is a stream' follow-ups, saying the design before any code."

Likely follow-ups
  • Which follow-ups come up most often? (stream, many queries, very large k, input spread over machines)
  • Why does "design out loud, then code" matter even more in a follow-up?

Scenario: a clean pass that felt easy

You solved an easy Medium in 15 minutes with no hints and good tests.

Weak: "Too easy, this mock was useless."

Strong: "A clean pass in 15 minutes is a data point: this pattern is solid. I log it as a pass with no review for 14 days, and I ask for a harder problem, or two problems in 45 minutes, in the next mock, because real loops at my level often have two."

Likely follow-ups
  • How do you raise the difficulty of a mock without making it unrealistic?
  • When should you stop doing mocks on one pattern?
