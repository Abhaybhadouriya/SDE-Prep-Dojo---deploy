Picking mocks from Tier A by company

Mock problems should be close to what you will actually get. The Dojo has the data for that: the Dojo Curated 450 (Tier A, the top 450 problems by priority) and, for each company, the saved Premium company list with a frequency bar from 1 to 8 (8 = asked most). Open a company's view at /coding/company/<company> to see its Tier A problems in frequency order.

The picking rule has four parts. It is simple enough to apply by hand and is exactly what the template code does:

  1. Company Tier A list, most frequent first. Ties broken by name, so the same inputs always give the same mock.
  2. Drop what you have seen. Anything solved or read in the last 14 days.
  3. Problem 1 by level. SDE-1: the first Easy or Medium. SDE-2 and SDE-3: the first Medium.
  4. Problem 2 from a different group. SDE-1: a Medium. SDE-2: a Medium or Hard. SDE-3: a Hard. The pattern group (arrays, graphs, sliding window...) must differ from problem 1, so one mock always tests two patterns.

Step through the rule on eight of Amazon's most frequent Tier A problems, for an SDE-2 candidate who solved Two Sum and LRU Cache this week.

  1. Start from the company's Tier A problems in frequency order. Saved company pages list the most frequent first; ties are ordered by name so the pick is repeatable.
  2. Remove the seen set. Two Sum and LRU Cache were solved this week, so a mock on them would test memory, not skill.
  3. Problem 1 for SDE-2 is the first Medium. Best Time to Buy and Sell Stock is Easy, so it is skipped; Longest Substring Without Repeating Characters is the pick.
  4. Problem 2 must be Medium or Hard and from a different group than sliding window. Number of Islands (graphs) is the first that qualifies.
  5. The mock is set. For SDE-3 the same rule would demand a Hard for problem 2 and pick Trapping Rain Water; for SDE-1, problem 1 would be the Easy stock problem.

Two things stand out. First, the very top of every list is shared: Longest Substring, LRU Cache, Trapping Rain Water and Median of Two Sorted Arrays appear for several companies. That is why your first mocks will often hit problems you already know; put them in the seen set and the picker moves down the list. Second, company style shows after the first few rows: Meta leans on trees and stacks, Uber on graphs and design, Flipkart on binary search on the answer and greedy. Rotate companies early; use your target company's list in the final three weeks.

From your world

Picking mocks by frequency is the same thinking as choosing which code paths to put under a soak test: you do not test every ioctl equally, you test the ones the field logs say are hit most, then you add one rarely used path so you are not blind to it. Tier A plus company frequency is the field log; the different-group rule is the rare path.
