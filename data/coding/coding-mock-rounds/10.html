How to recognise what your mocks are telling you

After four or five graded mocks, the pattern of scores tells you what to practise. Read it like a set of counters.

Cue in your mock logWhat it meansWhat to do next
P1 solved, P2 never startedTime management, not knowledge.Obey the 12 and 22 minute checkpoints; brute force first if the idea is late.
Problem solving 2 on most mocksRecognition gap.Back to the pattern primers; rewrite Trigger lines; untimed problems for two weeks.
Code 2 with correct approachTemplates not automatic yet.Re-type the lesson templates from memory daily for a week.
Testing stuck at 2You stop when it "looks right".Make the dry-run table a hard rule for three mocks in a row.
Partner grade far below your ownCommunication you think is clear is not.Record and watch; narrate before every block of code.
Same group fails twiceA pattern hole.Five problems of that group from Tier A before the next mock.
Three hires in a row at your levelReady for this level.Move P2 to Hard (or the next level's bar) to build margin.

Cues inside a mock, in the moment:

  • Three minutes of no progress on the idea: say the brute force and offer to code it. It is never wrong to have a working baseline.
  • You are fixing a second bug in the same loop: stop patching; re-state the loop invariant out loud, then rewrite the loop.
  • The interviewer asks the same question twice: they are hinting. Re-read your last line against what they asked.
  • Constraints say n is up to 105 and your idea is O(n2): say so yourself before they do, and look for sorting, hashing or a window.
