The last week: a code freeze for your head

The final seven days are not for new patterns: anything learned now is shallow. The week has three jobs: keep the clock skill warm with a few mocks, re-solve the weakest cards, and arrive rested.

  1. D-7, D-5, D-3: mocks. D-7 on a mixed set, D-5 and D-3 picked from your target company's Tier A list. Each with the full debrief. If D-7 shows a process problem (time, silence), make that the habit for D-5.
  2. D-6 and D-4: card days. Open Revise and work the due queue first. Then re-solve every card graded Again or Hard in the last month, timed at 20 minutes each. On D-4, also re-read the cards of the five groups your company asks most, and do two new untimed problems from those groups.
  3. D-2: read, do not re-solve. Read through cards: pattern, trigger, insight. A quick read of 60 to 100 cards is a pattern-recognition drill. No timed solving; you are protecting confidence now.
  4. D-1: logistics and rest. Check the editor or platform the company uses, your camera, microphone and internet, the time zone of the slot. Skim your weakest 20 cards for 30 minutes. Stop early. Sleep matters more than one more problem.
  5. D-0: warm up. One or two hours before the round, solve one Easy you know, out loud, in the same editor. The goal is to start the round with your voice and typing already warm, not to learn anything.

From your world

This is the code freeze before a release. In the last week before GA you stop merging features and only take fixes for bugs you already know about, because every new change is a risk you have no time to soak. New patterns in the last week are late feature merges; the Again and Hard cards are the known-bug list.

If the D-3 mock goes badly, do not cram. Debrief it like any other; one bad mock says less than the trend of your last five.
