Running a mock: with a partner or alone

With a partner (best)
  • A friend, a colleague, or a peer-mock platform. Swap roles: you interview them next time.
  • The partner picks the problems so you cannot pre-read them, and gives hints only when asked or after three minutes of silence.
  • They fill in the rubric as they watch; you fill it in separately afterwards, then compare. The gap between the two grades is the most useful number you will get.
Solo (still worth it)
  • Let a script or a friend pick problems blind (the template below does it from the company list).
  • Record your screen and voice. Talk to the recording as if it were the interviewer.
  • Plain editor, no autocomplete, no run button until you have dry-run by hand. Grade by watching the first five and the last five minutes of the recording.

Match the environment to the company where you can (the company guides list what candidates reported); when unsure, practise the hardest version: no run button.

Never mock on a problem you have solved in the last two weeks. Recall is not the skill under test. The picker below skips anything in your "seen" set, which should hold every problem you solved or read a solution for in the last 14 days.
