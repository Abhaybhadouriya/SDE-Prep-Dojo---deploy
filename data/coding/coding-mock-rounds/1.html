What a mock tests that solo practice does not

A mock round is a full rehearsal of one interview round: fixed length, problems you have not seen, and either a partner playing the interviewer or a strict solo setup that imitates one. It is not "do two problems quickly". It tests five things that solo practice lets you skip:

  1. Time pressure. Two problems in about 40 minutes of working time leaves roughly 20 minutes each, questions and testing included. Most people find their first mock much slower than expected.
  2. Talking while thinking. The interviewer grades what they hear. Silence for three minutes reads as "stuck", even when you are making progress.
  3. No running the code. Many rounds use a plain shared editor or a whiteboard. You need to be sure the code is right before anyone runs it.
  4. Unseen problems. Recognising a pattern from a fresh statement is a different skill from recalling a solution you wrote last week.
  5. Recovery. Noticing a wrong turn, saying it, and switching to a fallback quickly.

Rule of thumb: once you can solve most Tier A Mediums untimed, start one mock per week. In the last three weeks, go to two or three per week. A mock without a written grade and a debrief afterwards is just a timed practice session; the grading is where the learning happens.
