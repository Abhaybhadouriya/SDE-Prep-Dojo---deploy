The self-grading rubric, per level

Grade every mock on the same four signals the real scorecard uses (see what each level is graded on): communication, problem solving, code and testing. Each gets a score from 1 to 4, and you also count how many problems you finished and tested.

ScoreCommunicationProblem solvingCodeTesting
1 missingLong silences; coded before agreeing an approach.No working approach, even with hints.Does not compile in your head; logic missing.Said "done" with no dry run.
2 with helpTalked, but the interviewer had to pull the plan out of you.Reached a working approach after a hint, or only the brute force.Works with several bugs that you did not find.Dry-ran only when asked; missed an obvious edge case.
3 meets barStated plan and cost before coding; checked in at each step.Optimal or near-optimal alone; complexity correct.Clean, correct, readable; at most one small bug, found by you.Unprompted dry run with a table plus an edge sweep.
4 strongDrove the session; explained trade-offs and why the solution is optimal.Optimal without hints and handled a follow-up twist.Idiomatic, modular, no bugs; written at speed.Chose the test set deliberately: boundaries, large input, the adversarial case.

The bar then depends on the level you are interviewing for. These thresholds are the Dojo's own calibration, built from the level descriptions on How coding rounds work, not an official company rule; use them to compare your mocks with each other, not to predict an offer.

Count how many of the three conditions you missed. Zero misses is a hire (a strong hire if the total is 15 or 16), one miss is a lean no hire, two or more is a no hire. The picture below grades a typical SDE-2 mock.

Grade the recording, not your memory. Right after a mock everyone remembers being clearer than they were. If you have no recording, grade only from your raw notes written at minute 45, and round every in-between score down.
