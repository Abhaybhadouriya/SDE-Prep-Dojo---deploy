Approaches: brute -> better -> optimal

Hint 1

Can you take the 2nd card from the left without taking the 1st? What does the set of taken cards look like, then?

Hint 2

How many different final sets are there? How does the score change from one to the next?

1. Brute force - recursion over left / right

Idea: at each step, try taking the left end and the right end, recurse with k - 1. Memoising on (cards taken from the left, cards taken from the right) brings it to O(k2), still 1010 for k = 105.

O(2k)time (O(k²) with memo)
O(k)recursion depth

What is wasted: different orders reach the same final set; only the split point i matters.

2. Better - prefix and suffix sums

Idea: the score of the split "i from the left, k - i from the right" is prefix[i] + suffix[k - i]. Build both sums for k elements and take the max over i = 0..k. O(k) time, O(k) space. This is the most-voted community explanation.

3. Optimal - trade one card at a time (or minimise the leftover window)

cur = sum(cardPoints[:k]); best = cur
for i in range(1, k + 1):
    cur += cardPoints[n - i] - cardPoints[k - i]
    best = max(best, cur)
return best
O(k)time
O(1)extra space

Why it works: any legal sequence of k picks takes a prefix of length i and a suffix of length k - i, because a card in the middle can only be reached after every card between it and an end is gone. All k + 1 splits are legal and reachable in some order, so the answer is the max over the splits. Going from split (i left, k - i right) to (i - 1 left, k - i + 1 right) removes exactly one card, cardPoints[i - 1], and adds exactly one, cardPoints[n - (k - i + 1)], so cur is updated in O(1) and always equals the current split's score.

The complement view (the top Python community solution): the cards you do not take are one contiguous block of length m = n - k. Score = total - (block sum), so slide a window of length m over the array and find its minimum sum. O(n) time. Same answer, and it is the form that generalises to LC 1658. With k = n the block is empty (m = 0), which needs a guard in that version; the trading loop handles it with no special case.
