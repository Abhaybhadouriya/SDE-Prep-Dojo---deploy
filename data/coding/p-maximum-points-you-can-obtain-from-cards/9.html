60-second recall

  • Cue: take k from the two ends; maximise the sum.
  • Insight: any pick = i from the left + k - i from the right; the cards left are one window of n - k.
  • State: cur = score of the current split, best; step cur += a[n - i] - a[k - i].
  • Trap: greedy on the ends is wrong; DP is O(k²); loop i = 1..k inclusive.
  • Cost: O(k) time, O(1) space (complement window: O(n)).
cur = sum(cardPoints[:k]); best = cur
for i in range(1, k + 1):
    cur += cardPoints[n - i] - cardPoints[k - i]
    best = max(best, cur)

How many different sets of taken cards are possible?

  1. 2k
  2. k + 1
  3. n choose k
The set is fixed by i, the number taken from the left, for i = 0..k.

In the complement view, what do you minimise?

  1. The sum of a contiguous window of length n - k
  2. The sum of the k smallest cards
  3. The smaller of the two ends
The cards left behind are contiguous, and score = total - their sum.

Why does greedy fail on [1, 50, 2, 2, 3], k = 2?

  1. It overflows
  2. It takes too long
  3. The 50 is reachable only by first taking the smaller end card 1
Greedy gets 3 + 2 = 5; the best split is 1 + 50 = 51.
