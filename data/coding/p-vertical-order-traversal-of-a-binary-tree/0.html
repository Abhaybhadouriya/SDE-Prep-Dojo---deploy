The story

Explain like I'm 5

A family tree is printed on a wall, and the painter has drawn vertical stripes across it. Each child hangs one step down and one stripe to the left (for a left child) or right (for a right child) of its parent. Now read the wall stripe by stripe, from the leftmost stripe to the rightmost, and in each stripe from the top down. If two people are painted on exactly the same spot, read the smaller name-number first.

The catch that makes this one "Hard": two nodes from different subtrees can land on the exact same spot, and the order inside a column is by row first and only then by value. Most wrong answers come from getting that tie rule wrong, not from the traversal.

From your world

It is the classic "group by key, then sort inside the group" job you do on packet captures: tag every record with (flow, timestamp, seq) and one sort by that tuple gives per-flow, time-ordered output. Here the tag is (column, row, value).
