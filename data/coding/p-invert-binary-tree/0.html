The story

Explain like I'm 5

Draw a family tree on a window and look at it from outside the house. Everything is the same people at the same heights, but whoever was on the left is now on the right, at every branch, all the way down. Grandma's left child is now on her right, and that child's own kids are also flipped. That flipped drawing is the inverted tree.

The whole trick is to see that "mirror the tree" is the same small job repeated at every node: swap its two children. The recursion does the "at every node" part for you.

From your world

Think of a kernel red-black tree or a routing trie you want to walk in reverse order. Instead of copying it, you can flip the meaning of left and right at every node; an in-order walk then yields keys in descending order. The recursion depth is the tree height, so on a degenerate (list-shaped) tree it is O(n) frames: the same reason kernel code prefers explicit stacks over deep recursion.
