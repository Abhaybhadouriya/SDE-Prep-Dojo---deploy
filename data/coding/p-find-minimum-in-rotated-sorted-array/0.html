The story

Explain like I'm 5

A shelf of numbered books was in order, 1 to 100. Someone lifted the last few books and put them at the front, so the shelf now reads 73, 74, ..., 100, 1, 2, ..., 72. You want book 1, and you may only peek at a few spines. Peek at the middle and at the right end: if the middle number is bigger than the right-end number, the "jump back to 1" must happen between them. If it is smaller, the stretch from the middle to the end is still in order, so book 1 is at the middle or to its left. Every peek throws away half the shelf.

From your world

This is finding the oldest record in a ring buffer that was dumped linearly. A log ring (think dmesg or a trace buffer) stores increasing sequence numbers, but the write head has wrapped, so the array reads "newest-ish, then oldest". The oldest entry is the minimum, and because both pieces are sorted you can bisect for the wrap point instead of scanning the whole buffer.
