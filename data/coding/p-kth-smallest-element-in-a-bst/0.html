The story

Explain like I'm 5

A librarian files books on a family tree of shelves: every shelf to the left of a shelf holds smaller numbers, every shelf to the right holds bigger ones. You want the 5th smallest book. Walk to the far left (the smallest), then read the shelves left-to-right, counting as you go: one, two, three, four, five - stop, that is your book. You never need to look at the shelves after it.

From your world

This is an ordered map lookup by rank instead of by key. The Linux kernel's rbtree has rb_first() + rb_next(), which is exactly the walk below; and an "order-statistic" tree (each node caching its subtree size, like the augmented rbtrees used for interval and timer bookkeeping) answers "the k-th entry" in O(log n) without walking.
