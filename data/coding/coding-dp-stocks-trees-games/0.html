The story: a trader with a mood ring

Explain like I'm 5

A girl sells lemonade cups at a fair. Each morning the price of a lemon changes. She can buy one lemon, keep it, and sell it later. At the end of every day she is in exactly one "mood": holding a lemon, just sold one, or resting with empty hands.

Her trick is a notebook with one line per mood: "the most money I can have tonight if I end the day in this mood". Tomorrow she only needs tonight's three numbers to fill tomorrow's three numbers. She never has to remember the whole history of what she bought and when.

That notebook is a state machine DP. The "state" is not just the day number; it is the day plus a small tag that says what situation you are in (holding or not, how many trades used, whether you are cooling down). Once you list the tags and the arrows between them, the code writes itself.

The same thinking works on trees (the state is "this node is robbed or not", "this node has a camera or not") and on two-player games (the state is "which part of the array is still left", and the value is "how far ahead the player to move can get").

From your world

You have written this a hundred times in C: the TCP state machine (LISTEN, SYN_RCVD, ESTABLISHED, FIN_WAIT...), a link FSM in a driver, a PPP or BGP session FSM. A state, a set of events, and a switch that computes the next state. State-machine DP is the same table, except each state also carries a number (the best profit so far), and each transition keeps the max of all ways to arrive.
