Stock I and II: two states are enough

Stock I (LeetCode 121), told our way: you get the price of one share for each of n days. You may buy once and sell once, on a later day. Return the largest profit, or 0 if no trade makes money. Constraints: 1 <= n <= 10^5, prices between 0 and 104. Example: prices [8, 3, 6, 2, 7, 5] give 5 (buy at 2, sell at 7).

The greedy answer is "keep the lowest price seen so far, try selling today". The DP answer is the one that generalises. Keep two numbers at the end of each day:

  • hold = the most cash you can have while owning the share (cash is negative right after buying).
  • cash = the most cash you can have while owning nothing.

Each day: hold = max(hold, -price) (keep it, or buy today with zero cash, because only one trade is allowed) and cash = max(cash, hold + price) (stay out, or sell today). Start with hold = -infinity, cash = 0.

Stock II (LeetCode 122): same input, but you may trade as many times as you like, holding at most one share at a time. The only change is the buy arrow: you now buy with the cash you already earned, hold = max(hold, cash - price). (It also equals the sum of every positive day-to-day rise, which is the one-line greedy.)

Look at what changed between I and II: one arrow. That is the point of the state-machine view. Every stock variant is "the same two or three boxes, with a different rule on one arrow".
