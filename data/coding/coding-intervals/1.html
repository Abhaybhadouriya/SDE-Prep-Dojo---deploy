The one test you need: do two intervals overlap?

Two intervals either sit apart, touch at one end, partly overlap, or one sits inside the other. You do not need four cases in code. They are disjoint only when one ends before the other starts, so they overlap when that is not true:

def overlaps(a, b):                 # closed intervals [s, e]
    return a[0] <= b[1] and b[0] <= a[1]
# common part, if any: [max(a[0], b[0]), min(a[1], b[1])]

Closed or half-open? Read the examples to decide whether touching counts. In Merge Intervals, [1,4] and [4,5] merge into [1,5]: closed, test with <=. In Meeting Rooms and Non-overlapping Intervals, [1,2] and [2,3] do not clash: a meeting that ends at 2 frees the room for one that starts at 2. That is half-open [s, e), and the test becomes <. Most interval bugs are this one character.

After sorting by start, the test gets even simpler. If cur.start <= last.end, they overlap. We already know last.start <= cur.start.
