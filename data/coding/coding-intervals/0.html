The story: one meeting room, many bookings

Explain like I'm 5

Your school has one music room. Children write their bookings on cards: "from 2 to 3 o'clock", "from 2:30 to 4". The teacher wants to know three things. Do any bookings clash? If we join clashing bookings together, which blocks of time is the room busy? And if the clashes are real, how many rooms would we need so that nobody waits?

The teacher's trick is always the same. First she puts the cards in order of start time. Then she reads them once, left to right, and only compares each card with what she has just seen. She never compares every card with every other card.

An interval is a pair [start, end]: a meeting, a booking, a range of IP addresses, a range of memory. Problems give you a list of them and ask you to combine them, count them, or pick some of them. The brute force compares every pair, which is O(n2). After a sort (O(n log n)), one pass is enough, because an interval can only interact with its neighbours in sorted order.

From your world

You have merged intervals for years. Route aggregation joins adjacent prefixes (10.0.0.0/24 and 10.0.1.0/24 become 10.0.0.0/23). The kernel merges neighbouring vm_area_struct regions when their flags match, and a buddy or free-list allocator coalesces adjacent free blocks. /proc/iomem is a list of non-overlapping intervals. Every one of these keeps the ranges sorted by start, then fixes things locally.
