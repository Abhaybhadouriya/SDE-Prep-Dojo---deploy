The story: the tallest kid in the photo frame

Explain like I'm 5

Kids walk past a photo frame one by one. The frame always shows the last 3 kids. Your job: shout the name of the tallest kid in the frame, every time a new kid steps in.

Here is a smart trick. When a tall kid steps in, every shorter kid who came before her can go home. Why? She will stay in the frame longer than they will, and she is taller. While they are in the frame, she is too, so they can never be the tallest. So you keep a short line of kids, tallest at the front, and each new kid sends the shorter ones at the back home. The tallest in the frame is always the first kid in your line. When that kid walks out of the frame, she leaves the front of the line.

That line is a monotonic deque: a double-ended queue whose values only go down (or only go up) from front to back. It answers "what is the max of the last k items?" in O(1) amortised time, where a heap needs O(log n) and a rescan needs O(k).

From your world

The Linux kernel needs a windowed max too: TCP BBR estimates the bottleneck bandwidth as the max delivery rate over the last ~10 round trips, and the min RTT over a time window. lib/win_minmax.c keeps that running max/min with just three samples (best, 2nd best, 3rd best) because it can accept an approximation. In an interview you need the exact answer, and the exact version of that idea is this page: keep every sample that could still become the max, drop the rest.
