Why a plain queue or a heap is not enough

The classic problem (LeetCode 239, Sliding Window Maximum), told our way: a sensor gives you a list of n integer readings and a window size k. Slide a window of exactly k consecutive readings from the left end to the right end, one step at a time, and report the largest reading in each window. Output the n - k + 1 maxima in order. Constraints: 1 <= n <= 10^5, readings between -104 and 104, 1 <= k <= n. Example: readings [4, 2, 12, 3, 8, 1, 7], k = 3 give [12, 12, 12, 8, 8].

ApproachTimeWhy it is weak (or fine)
Rescan each windowO(n k)105 x 105 = 1010: too slow.
Running max variable-Wrong: when the max leaves, you do not know the new max. A sum can be undone (sum -= out); a max cannot.
Max-heap with lazy deletionO(n log n)Works: pop the top while its index is outside the window. Fine as a fallback, but the interviewer will ask for O(n).
Balanced BST / multisetO(n log k)Works, and gives max and min. Good for the sliding window median, overkill for max.
Monotonic dequeO(n)Keeps only the candidates; each index enters once and leaves once.

The key fact is dominance. Item j dominates item i if j came later (so it leaves the window later) and its value is at least as large. A dominated item can never be the answer again, so we may delete it right now.

After deleting every dominated item, what is left? Each survivor is bigger than everything after it. So the survivors, read from oldest to newest, are strictly decreasing. The oldest survivor is the biggest: it is the window max.
