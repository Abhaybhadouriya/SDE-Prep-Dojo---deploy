The story

Explain like I'm 5

You walk through a maze of rooms. In each room there are up to nine doors, numbered 1 to 9, but some are locked because that number is already used nearby. You open the lowest unlocked door and keep going. If you reach a room where every door is locked, you walk back to the previous room and try its next door. You never need a map of the whole maze: you only remember the path you came by.

That walk-back is backtracking: a depth-first search over partial boards, where each level of recursion fixes one blank. Sudoku is the textbook "constraint placement" version: the constraints (row, column, box) let you reject a bad digit the moment you place it, instead of after the board is full.

From your world

The used-digit masks are like a per-CPU bitmap of allocated slots: row | col | box is the set of busy slots, ~busy the free ones, and freeing is the same bit flip as allocating. Backtracking is a transaction log: every placement is undone in exact reverse order.
