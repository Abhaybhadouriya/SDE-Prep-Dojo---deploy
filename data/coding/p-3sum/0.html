The story

Explain like I'm 5

A shop has a shelf of price tags, some positive (you pay) and some negative (coupons). You want every way to pick three different tags that cancel out to exactly zero, but each combination of values only once. Line the tags up from most negative to most positive. Hold one tag in your hand; now walk one finger in from the left and one from the right. Too negative? Move the left finger right. Too positive? Move the right finger left.

The whole trick is turning a three-way search into "one fixed choice + a two-pointer scan", and doing the de-duplication by position instead of with a hash set of results.

From your world

It is the same move as merging two sorted lists in a kernel routine: because both ends are ordered, one comparison tells you which end can be discarded for good. No index, no hash table, just two cursors over sorted memory, which is also cache-friendly.
