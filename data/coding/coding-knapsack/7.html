How to recognise a knapsack

  • "Choose a subset" plus a budget, a capacity, a target sum or a required total: 0/1 knapsack.
  • "Split into two groups" with equal sums or the smallest difference: subset sum up to total/2.
  • "Assign + or -" to each number: rewrite as a subset with sum (total + target)/2.
  • "Unlimited supply", "you may reuse", coins, cuts, tiles, squares: unbounded knapsack.
  • "Number of combinations" (order does not matter): items outside. "Number of sequences / ways to climb" (order matters): amounts outside. Climbing stairs with step sizes {1, 2} is Combination Sum IV with nums = [1, 2].
  • Constraints: n up to a few hundred and a sum or capacity up to about 104-105. If n ≤ 20 and the sums are huge, think bitmask or meet in the middle instead.
  • Two limited resources (zeros and ones, people and profit): the state has one dimension per resource.

Say the classification first: "each item at most once, and we count, so it is a 0/1 counting knapsack: downward loop, ways[0] = 1". That one sentence tells the interviewer you know the whole family.
