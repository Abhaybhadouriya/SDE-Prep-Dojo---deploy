  • Cue: fewest unit-cost steps between two states -> BFS on an implicit graph.
  • Insight: neighbours = change each letter to a..z + hash-set lookup; meet in the middle.
  • State: front, back, shared unused set, words (starts at 1).
  • Trap: endWord must be in the list; mark used on enqueue; answer counts words.
  • Cost: O(N · L² · 26) time, O(N · L) space; all-pairs build O(N² · L).

while front and back:
    if len(front) > len(back): front, back = back, front

Does the answer count words or steps?

  1. steps (edges)
  2. words in the sequence, so beginWord alone counts 1
  3. letters changed
  4. unique letters
cold → cord → card → ward → warm is 5 words, 4 steps; the answer is 5.

Why mark a word used when you enqueue it, not when you dequeue it?

  1. it saves memory
  2. the same word could be queued many times from one level
  3. it is required for bidirectional search only
  4. to keep the order
Marking on enqueue gives each word one queue entry; the distance is still the shortest because BFS reaches it first at its lowest level.
