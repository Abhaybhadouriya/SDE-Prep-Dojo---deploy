The story

Explain like I'm 5

Two friends pass secret notes where every letter is replaced by its position in the alphabet: A is 1, B is 2, ..., Z is 26. The sender forgot to put spaces between the numbers, so "126" could be A-B-F (1 2 6), A-Z (1 26) or L-F (12 6). You want to know how many different messages the note could mean. Some digits cause trouble: a 0 can never stand alone, it must be the end of 10 or 20.

From your world

This is counting the parse trees of a byte stream with variable-length tokens of size 1 or 2, where some tokens are illegal. It is the same shape as counting how many ways a framing decoder could split an unframed buffer. The DP over prefixes is exactly what a streaming parser would do: carry the counts for the last two boundaries, nothing more.
