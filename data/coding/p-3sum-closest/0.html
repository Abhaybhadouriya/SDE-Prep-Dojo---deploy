The story

Explain like I'm 5

You have a pile of weights, some light, some heavy, and you must put exactly three on the scale to get as close as possible to a mark. You line the weights up from lightest to heaviest. You pick one weight for sure, then try the lightest and the heaviest of the rest together. Too light? Swap the light one for the next heavier one. Too heavy? Swap the heavy one for the next lighter one. Each swap gets you closer, and you remember the best reading you ever saw.

From your world

Think of picking three buffer sizes from a sorted free list so that together they come closest to a request without the "must not exceed" rule. Sorting turns "try every triple" into a monotone search: on a sorted array, moving a pointer changes the sum in a known direction, the same reason binary search works on a sorted table. Two cursors walking toward each other over one array is also how you merge-scan sorted ranges in C.
