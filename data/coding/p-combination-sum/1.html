The problem (in our words)

A shop sells parcels whose weights are given in an array candidates of distinct positive integers, and a customer wants a total weight of exactly target. You may take any parcel weight any number of times.

Return a list of all unique combinations of weights from candidates that add up to target. Two combinations are the same if every weight is used the same number of times in both (order does not matter). You may return the combinations, and the numbers inside each, in any order. The inputs are such that there are fewer than 150 unique combinations.

Signature: List<List<Integer>> combinationSum(int[] candidates, int target).

Constraints

  • 1 <= candidates.length <= 30
  • 2 <= candidates[i] <= 40
  • All elements of candidates are distinct.
  • 1 <= target <= 40

Constraints tell you: the output is the work. With fewer than 150 answers and target ≤ 40, exhaustive search is expected; the smallest value is 2, so a combination has at most 20 numbers and the recursion is at most 20 deep. There is no clever polynomial trick for listing all answers; the skill being tested is generating each one exactly once and pruning dead branches.
