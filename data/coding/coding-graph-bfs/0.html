The story: ripples in a pond

Explain like I'm 5

Drop a stone in a still pond. A ring of ripples moves out, then a bigger ring, then a bigger one. Every leaf floating on the pond gets touched by the first ring that reaches it, and the number of that ring tells you how far the leaf is from the stone.

Now drop three stones at once. Three sets of rings spread together, and each leaf is touched first by the rings of the nearest stone. That is multi-source BFS.

And if part of the pond is covered by ice (walls), the rings flow around it, so the ring number is the distance around the ice, not through it. That is BFS on a grid.

From your world

You already know BFS as flooding. A link-state routing protocol floods its updates hop by hop; a packet with a TTL can reach exactly the routers within TTL hops; a broadcast storm expands in rings. "Hop count" is BFS distance. When links get weights, OSPF switches to Dijkstra's shortest-path-first, and the 0-1 BFS later on this page is the special case where every link costs 0 or 1. The visited set is the same idea as a duplicate-suppression cache for flooded packets: without it, everything is re-flooded forever.
