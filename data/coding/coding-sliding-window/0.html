The story: the train window

Explain like I'm 5

You sit in a train and look out of a window. The window shows exactly three houses. When the train moves, one new house appears on the right and one old house disappears on the left. You do not need to look at the middle houses again: they are still there.

If someone asks "how many red doors can you see right now?", you keep a running count. A red door comes in: add one. A red door goes out: take one away. You never count the whole view again.

That is the whole idea. A window is a block of neighbouring items in an array or a string: indexes left to right. A sliding window moves that block forward and keeps a small summary of what is inside, updating it with only the item that came in and the item that went out.

Some windows have a fixed size (always 3 houses). Others stretch and shrink, like a camera zoom: "show me the longest stretch of street with at most two colours of door". Both kinds are on this page.
