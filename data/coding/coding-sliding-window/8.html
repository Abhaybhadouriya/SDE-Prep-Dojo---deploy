How to recognise a sliding window problem

When you see "contiguous subarray / substring" and "longest / shortest / count / maximum of" and a condition that gets more broken (or more satisfied) as the block grows, think sliding window.

  • Words in the statement: substring, subarray, consecutive, window, "at most k", "at least k", "contains all of", "k consecutive".
  • Fixed k given and a score per window: fixed window.
  • "Longest ... such that": variable window, shrink while invalid.
  • "Smallest ... that covers / reaches": variable window, shrink while valid.
  • "Number of subarrays with exactly K": atMost(K) - atMost(K - 1).
  • "Max / min of every window": monotonic deque.

Not a sliding window: subsequences (items need not be neighbours: think DP or two pointers on sorted data); sums with negatives and an exact target (prefix sums); "median of every window" (two heaps, since a median is not monotone either).

Questions with a window are common in real interviews. In our scan of 4,217 LeetCode Discuss interview reports (January 2024 to November 2026), Max Consecutive Ones III was named in 20 reports (Meta 12, LinkedIn 7), Longest Substring Without Repeating Characters in 17, Minimum Window Substring in 15 and Sliding Window Maximum in 14 (Flipkart 6).
