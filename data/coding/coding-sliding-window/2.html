Fixed-size windows

The window length k is given. The recipe:

  1. Build the first window of k items the normal way.
  2. Slide: for right from k to n - 1, add a[right], remove a[right - k].
  3. Score each window after the update and keep the best.

Click through the slide for k = 3:

  1. Build the first window of size k = 3 the slow way, once: 4 + 2 + (-1) = 5. Best so far = 5.
  2. Slide one step: 7 enters on the right, 4 leaves on the left. Sum = 8 with two operations, not three. New best = 8.
  3. Slide one step: 3 enters on the right, 2 leaves on the left. Sum = 9 with two operations, not three. New best = 9.
  4. Slide one step: 1 enters on the right, -1 leaves on the left. Sum = 11 with two operations, not three. New best = 11.
  5. The right edge reached the end. Every window was scored with O(1) work after the first, so the whole scan is O(n). Answer = 11.

Typical fixed-window questions: maximum average of k neighbours, "does any window of length |p| hold an anagram of p" (keep 26 letter counts and a count of letters that match), maximum vowels in a window of k, and "take k cards from the two ends" (which is the same as "drop the window of n - k cards with the smallest sum").

The anagram family has a neat O(1) check: keep a counter matches = how many of the 26 letters have the same count in the window and in the pattern. Each slide changes two letters, so matches changes by at most 2, and "window is an anagram" is just matches == 26.
