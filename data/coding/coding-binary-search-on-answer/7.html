Median of two sorted arrays: binary search on a cut

LeetCode 4, told our way: two sorted integer arrays A (size m) and B (size n) are given. Return the median of all m + n values together (for an even total, the average of the two middle values). Required time: O(log(m + n)). Constraints: 0 <= m, n <= 1000, 1 <= m + n <= 2000, values between -106 and 106. Example: [1, 3] and [2] give 2.0; [1, 2] and [3, 4] give 2.5.

Merging is O(m + n). The log version searches a different answer: how many items i does A put in the left half? If the left half must hold half = (m + n + 1) / 2 items, B gives j = half - i. Let aL = A[i-1], aR = A[i], bL = B[j-1], bR = B[j] (minus or plus infinity past the ends).

  • Cut is correct when aL <= bR and bL <= aR (A's own halves and B's own halves are sorted already).
  • aL > bR: A gave too many; move i left (hi = i - 1).
  • bL > aR: A gave too few; move i right (lo = i + 1).
  • Median: odd total, max(aL, bL); even total, (max(aL, bL) + min(aR, bR)) / 2.

Why is this monotone? As i grows, aL grows and bR shrinks, so "aL > bR" is F..F T..T in i, and the correct i is just before it turns true. Search the shorter array: i ranges over [0, m], and j = half - i stays inside [0, n] only when m <= n. Time O(log min(m, n)).

A friendlier generalisation that interviewers accept: write kth(A, B, k) that drops k/2 items from one array per step (the one whose (k/2)-th item is smaller), O(log k). The median is kth of the middle. The same function answers "k-th element of two sorted arrays" (TUF).
