The problem (in our words)

You get an array height of n non-negative integers. Bar i is a wall of width 1 and height height[i], standing next to its neighbours with no gaps. After rain, water collects in the dips between walls; water that reaches either end of the row runs off. Return the total number of unit squares of water that stay trapped.

Signature: int trap(int[] height) (LeetCode style; read the array, return one integer).

  • n == height.length
  • 1 <= n <= 2 * 104
  • 0 <= height[i] <= 105

Practice it: LeetCode 42 · GfG Trapping Rain Water · TUF article · CodeChef TRAPRW

Constraints tell you: n up to 2·104 means even O(n²) (4·108 simple steps) is too slow in most judges, so aim for O(n). The answer is at most n · max height = 2·109 in theory, which is close to the 32-bit limit; real inputs stay far below, but say "I would use a 64-bit sum if heights could be larger" in the interview.
