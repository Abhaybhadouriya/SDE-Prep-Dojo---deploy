The story

Explain like I'm 5

A talent show gives medals to the top 3 scores, and the judges keep announcing "the bronze score right now". Contestants keep arriving. You only need to remember the three best scores, and you look most closely at the lowest of them, the bronze. A new score below bronze changes nothing and you forget it. A new score above bronze pushes the old bronze off the podium, and you work out the new bronze.

A min-heap is the podium: it hands you its smallest element in O(1) and replaces it in O(log k). The values that fell off are gone for good, since there are always k better values above them, so the memory stays at k.

From your world

This is how you keep "top talkers" in a flow monitor or the p-th percentile watermark of a counter: a bounded min-heap keyed on the metric. Each packet costs O(log k) at most, and most packets are rejected with one comparison against the root, like a fast-path check before the slow path.
