The story

Explain like I'm 5

A librarian keeps the books of one shelf in number order. One night someone lifts a block of books off the left end and puts it back on the right end, without changing the order inside either block. Now the shelf reads 40, 45, ... 70, then 2, 9, ... 33. You want book 9 and you are only allowed to open a few books. Open the middle one. One side of it is in perfect order, start to end. Looking at the first and last number of that tidy side tells you at once whether book 9 can be there. If not, it is on the other side.

The whole problem is that one observation: you lost global order, but at every split you still have a sorted half that can answer "is it in here?" with two comparisons.

From your world

A ring buffer of increasing sequence numbers (a TCP retransmit queue or a log ring indexed by slot) is a rotated sorted array once it has wrapped: slot 0 is not the oldest entry. Finding a sequence number by binary search over the slots is this problem; the "find the pivot first" variant is finding the head of the ring.
