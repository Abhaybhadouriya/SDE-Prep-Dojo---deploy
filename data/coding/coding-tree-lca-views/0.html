The story: the office building and the lift

Explain like I'm 5

A big company has one boss at the top. Every person has one manager, and each manager has at most two direct reports. Two people, Asha and Ben, have a fight. Who is the first manager that both of them report to, directly or indirectly? Walk up from Asha writing down her managers, walk up from Ben, and the first name on both lists is the answer. That person is their lowest common ancestor.

Now somebody asks "which people can you see if you stand at the right side of the org chart?" or "who is two handshakes away from Asha?" or "fax this whole chart to the other office, as one line of text". Those are the other questions on this page.

Everything here assumes the basics from Binary trees: recursion that returns a value up, BFS by levels with the frozen queue size, and the three DFS orders. If any of those feels shaky, do that page first. This page is about the named problems built on those moves, the ones interviewers reuse because each has one key idea and several easy-to-miss edge cases.

From your world

You already walk trees by parent pointer: dentry->d_parent to build a path, task->real_parent up the process tree, a cgroup's parent chain. cgroup v2 even uses an LCA: to move a process between two cgroups, you need write access to cgroup.procs of their common ancestor. Serialization is what a flattened device tree (DTB) does: a preorder walk with explicit begin-node and end-node tokens, so the reader can rebuild the tree without any pointers.
