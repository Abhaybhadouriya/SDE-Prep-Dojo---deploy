The story

Explain like I'm 5

A conga line of kids walks through the park, each kid holding the shoulder of the kid in front. The teacher wants the line to walk the other way. She goes from the front to the back, and tells each kid in turn: "let go, turn around, and hold the shoulder of the kid who used to be in front of you". Before a kid lets go, the teacher keeps an eye on who was behind them, or she would lose the rest of the line.

This is the first linked-list question for many interviewers, and the reversal loop shows up inside a dozen harder problems (palindrome check, reorder, k-group reversal). It is worth being able to write it without thinking.

From your world

The Linux kernel has this exact loop: llist_reverse_order() in lib/llist.c. A lock-less list (llist) is pushed at the head, so llist_del_all() hands back the entries newest first; consumers that need arrival order reverse the detached chain with prev/next pointers, in place, with no allocation.
