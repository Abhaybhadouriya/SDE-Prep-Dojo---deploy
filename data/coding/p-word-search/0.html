The story

Explain like I'm 5

A tray of letter tiles lies in a grid. Your friend asks: can you spell a secret word by putting your finger on one tile and sliding it, one tile at a time, up, down, left or right, never touching the same tile twice? You put your finger on a tile with the first letter, then look around for the second letter, and so on. If you get stuck, you lift your finger back one tile and try a different neighbour. If every attempt from every starting tile gets stuck, the word is not there.

"Lift your finger back" is backtracking: the search keeps exactly one path, and undoing the last step restores the board, so the next try starts clean.

From your world

It is a depth-first route walk with a loop guard, like a path-vector protocol rejecting a route that already contains its own AS number: a cell already on the current path cannot be used again. The difference is that here the guard is undone when you backtrack, because a cell that was useless on one path may be needed on another.
