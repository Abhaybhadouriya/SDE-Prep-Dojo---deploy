Dry run

Example 1. P = reached by the Pacific flood, A = by the Atlantic flood, PA = both.

  1. The grid. Instead of asking every cell "where does your water go?", we start at the seas and walk uphill.
  2. Pacific seeds: every cell of the top row and the left column drains straight into the Pacific. All 7 go into the queue, marked when pushed.
  3. One BFS layer up: a neighbour joins if it is at least as high as the cell we came from. (1,1), (1,2) and (2,1) join. Nothing else climbs further.
  4. The Pacific flood stops. (2,2) is low: toward the Pacific it is walled in by 5 and 6, so the uphill search never enters it, and its rain never reaches the Pacific.
  5. Second, independent BFS with its own visited grid: the bottom row and right column drain into the Atlantic.
  6. First layer up: (1,2), (2,1) and (2,2) join. (2,2) joins from (2,3) because the heights are equal, and equal heights let water flow.
  7. Second layer: (1,1) joins from (1,2) (equal again). The queue empties. The top-left cells stay out: their only downhill paths end in the Pacific.
  8. Intersect the two visited grids: 5 cells reach both seas. Each BFS visits a cell at most once, so the total is O(m·n).
