The story

Explain like I'm 5

A hilly farm is drawn as a chessboard of fields, each with a height. Along the top and left edge runs one canal, along the bottom and right edge another. When it rains, water rolls from a field to a side neighbour that is not higher. The farmer wants to know which fields can send water into both canals. Asking each field "try every path" is slow. Instead, stand in each canal and walk inland, only stepping onto fields that are the same height or higher: every field you can reach can drain back down to where you started.

From your world

It is the same move as computing reachability on the reverse graph: to find every node that can reach a sink, you do one BFS from the sink over reversed edges instead of one search per node. Routing does this when it builds a tree rooted at a destination (shortest-path trees toward a prefix), and a garbage collector does the dual (marking from the roots). Two sinks means two reverse searches and an AND of the two mark bitmaps.
