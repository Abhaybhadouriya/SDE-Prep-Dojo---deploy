The story

Explain like I'm 5

A ferry must carry a line of crates across a river, in the order they stand, within a fixed number of days. It makes one trip per day and loads crates from the front of the line until the next crate would be too heavy. You are buying the ferry: what is the smallest weight limit that still gets all the crates over in time? Guess a limit, simulate the loading, count the days. Too many days: the limit was too small, guess higher. Few enough: maybe a smaller ferry would also do, guess lower.

The answer lies between "the heaviest crate" (otherwise that crate never fits) and "all crates together" (one trip). Halving that range each guess takes about 25 guesses even for the largest inputs.

From your world

Same question as "what is the smallest link bandwidth that drains this ordered queue of packets within D time slots, with no packet split across slots?" You cannot compute it directly, but you can simulate a given bandwidth cheaply, and more bandwidth never needs more slots, so you bisect on the bandwidth, like bisecting a commit range.
