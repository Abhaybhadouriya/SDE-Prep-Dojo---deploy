The problem (in our words)

You get a list accounts. Each element accounts[i] is a list of strings: accounts[i][0] is a name, and the rest are emails of that account.

Two accounts belong to the same person if they have at least one email in common. Two accounts with the same name may still belong to different people, since people can share a name. Every account of one person always has that person's name. Merge the accounts: if account A shares an email with B and B with C, all three are one person.

Return the merged accounts. Each one must be a list whose first element is the name, followed by that person's emails in sorted order (each email once). The accounts themselves may be returned in any order.

Signature: List<List<String>> accountsMerge(List<List<String>> accounts).

  • 1 <= accounts.length <= 1000
  • 2 <= accounts[i].length <= 10
  • 1 <= accounts[i][j].length <= 30
  • accounts[i][0] consists of English letters.
  • accounts[i][j] for j > 0 is a valid email.

Practice it: LeetCode 721 · GfG Account Merge

Constraints tell you: at most 1,000 accounts x 9 emails = 9,000 emails. Any near-linear grouping plus a sort is far below the limit; even O(N²) pairwise comparison of accounts would pass but is not what the interviewer wants. The real work is modelling: what are the nodes, and what creates an edge?
