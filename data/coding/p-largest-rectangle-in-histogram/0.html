The story

Explain like I'm 5

A row of buildings of different heights stands side by side, each one block wide. You want to hang the biggest possible rectangular banner across their fronts, with its bottom on the ground and its top no higher than any building it covers. If you pick a building and say "the banner is exactly this tall", the banner can slide sideways over every neighbour that is at least that tall, and must stop at the first shorter one on each side.

So the whole problem is: for every bar, find the first lower bar on its left and on its right. Doing that naively is O(n) per bar. The stack trick does it for all bars together, because a bar only waits on the stack until its right wall shows up.

From your world

It is like a kernel completion queue where each pending request waits for "the first event smaller than me". Requests that arrive later and are taller sit on top; one small event retires all the taller pending entries in LIFO order, and each retired entry learns both of its boundaries at that moment. Every entry is pushed once and popped once: amortised O(1).
