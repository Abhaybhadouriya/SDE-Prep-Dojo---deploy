Dry run

Example 1. Top: preorder with the cursor. Middle: inorder with the current range ringed and the root's cell filled. Bottom: the tree built so far.

  1. next = 0: preorder[0] = 7 is the root of the whole tree. In inorder, 7 sits at index 3, so inorder[0..2] = {9,4,5} is its left subtree and inorder[4..5] = {6,1} its right subtree.
  2. Build the left side first (preorder is root, left, right). next = 1: 4 is the root of inorder[0..2]. It sits at index 1: left part [0..0], right part [2..2].
  3. next = 2: 9 is the root of inorder[0..0]. Both of its ranges, [0..-1] and [1..0], are empty, so it is a leaf.
  4. Back at 4, build its right part. next = 3: 5 owns inorder[2..2]; a leaf. The left subtree of 7 is complete, and exactly 3 preorder entries (4, 9, 5) were used: the size of the range.
  5. Now the right side of 7, inorder[4..5]. next = 4: 1 is its root, at inorder index 5: left part [4..4], right part [6..5] (empty).
  6. next = 5: 6 owns inorder[4..4]; a leaf and the left child of 1. Then 1's right range is empty: null.
  7. All 6 preorder entries are used, each exactly once, and every inorder lookup was one hash-map read. Result in level order: [7,4,1,9,5,6]. Total O(n) time.
