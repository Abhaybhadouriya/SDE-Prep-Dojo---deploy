Common mistakes

  • Storing values, not handles, in the map. Then moving an item in the list needs a search: O(n).
  • The list node does not store its key. When you evict the tail, you cannot delete it from the map.
  • One structure left stale. put on an existing key updates the value but forgets to move it to the head; an eviction removes from the list but not from the map.
  • Evicting after inserting. In LFU you evict the key you just added; in LRU the size check is off by one.
  • Randomized set delete order. Deleting idx[x] before writing idx[last] breaks when x is the last element. In Java, list.remove(int) versus remove(Object) with an Integer: remove by index.
  • Pouring two stacks too often. Moving in to out on every pop, even when out has items, breaks the queue order.
  • Iterators that pre-compute everything when the interviewer asked for lazy, or hasNext() that returns true for an empty nested list.
  • Unbounded memory in stream designs. A logger map that never forgets old messages; a hit counter queue under a burst of a million hits per second (use buckets).
