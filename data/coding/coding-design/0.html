The story: the hotel front desk

Explain like I'm 5

A small hotel has 3 rooms. The desk clerk keeps two things. A key board on the wall: one hook per guest name, so she finds any guest's room at once. And a line of cards on the desk, ordered by who used the lounge most recently. When a guest walks by, she pulls their card out of the line and puts it at the front.

When a fourth guest arrives and the hotel is full, she looks at the last card in the line, the guest who has been quiet the longest, and asks them to leave. She takes their key off the board too, because the card has the name written on it.

The key board is fast but has no order; the card line has order but is slow to search. Together, every job takes one step.

That clerk just built an LRU cache: a hash map (the key board) plus a doubly linked list (the card line), where the map stores a handle straight into the list. Almost every design problem on this page is that move: two or three plain structures, each answering one question fast, glued by handles.

From your world

You have coded this in C. The kernel's page cache keeps pages on LRU lists made of struct list_head, and list_move(&page->lru, &lru_list) is exactly "pull the card out and put it in front": O(1) because the node carries its own prev/next. The lookup side is a hash or tree (the page cache uses an XArray keyed by file offset). An interview LRU cache is that pair, minus the locking.
