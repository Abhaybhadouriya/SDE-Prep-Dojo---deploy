How to recognise these problems

You read...Think
"longest / most items you can pick, keeping order, each bigger than the last"LIS. O(n2) for n <= ~3000, patience for 105.
Items with two sizes that must both grow (envelopes, boxes, people on shoulders)Sort by one key, LIS on the other. Watch the tie-break.
"chain" where each item comes from the previous by one small changeSort by size, dp[item] = 1 + best predecessor (LIS on a DAG).
Two strings and "common", "minimum operations", "convert", "delete to make equal"A table dp[i][j] over prefixes. Decide what the three arrows mean.
"number of ways / distinct subsequences" over two stringsSame table, add instead of max/min. Check overflow.
A pattern with ?, . or * matched against a whole stringMatching DP over (position in text, position in pattern).
Can string C be woven from A and B keeping each order?Interleaving: dp[i][j], and i + j is the position in C.

Size hints help too. Two strings of length up to 1000 means about 106 cells: an O(nm) table is what they want. A single array up to 105 rules out O(n2) and points to patience sorting.
