The story

Explain like I'm 5

Kids stand in a row after a spelling test, each with a score. Every kid gets at least one sticker, and a kid who scored higher than the kid right next to them must get more stickers than that neighbour. The teacher wants to buy as few stickers as possible. Picture the scores as a mountain range: every valley kid gets 1, and the count climbs by 1 per step up each slope. A peak kid stands on two slopes and needs to be above both, so they get the taller of the two climbs.

The trap is to handle both neighbours in one sweep: when you walk left to right, you do not yet know how long the downhill on your right will be. Splitting the two rules into two sweeps removes that problem.

From your world

It is like computing a value that depends on a chain in both directions, such as hop counts in a line of routers measured from both ends: one forward pass, one backward pass, combine with max. Each pass only needs the neighbour it just visited.
