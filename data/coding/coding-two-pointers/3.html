Opposite ends 2: container with most water

The problem, in our words. You get n fence posts of heights h[0..n-1], standing one unit apart. Pick two posts; with the ground they form a tank whose water level is the shorter post. Return the largest amount of water (width x shorter height) any two posts can hold. Posts cannot be tilted.

  • n == h.length; 2 <= n <= 105; 0 <= h[i] <= 104.
  • Example: h = [2, 7, 3, 6, 4, 1, 5] -> 25 (posts 1 and 6: width 5, level min(7, 5) = 5). Example: h = [3, 3] -> 3.

Practice it: LeetCode 11 - Container With Most Water · our write-up: Container With Most Water.

Invariant: "the best tank not yet measured uses posts inside [lo, hi]". Say h[lo] < h[hi]. Any tank that uses post lo with some other post j inside the range has width < hi - lo and level <= h[lo], so it is no better than the tank (lo, hi) we just measured. Post lo is finished: lo++. On a tie, moving either side is safe by the same argument.
