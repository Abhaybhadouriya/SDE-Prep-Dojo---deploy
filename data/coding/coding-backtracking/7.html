Pruning and complexity

Backtracking is exponential by nature. You cannot change the worst case much, but pruning decides whether the real run is milliseconds or minutes. The common prunes, from cheapest to strongest:

  • Sort and break. With sorted candidates, once one is too big, all later ones are too (combination sum).
  • Validity before recursion. Check the piece, the cell or the queen before the call, not at the leaf (palindrome partitioning, N-Queens).
  • Feasibility bound. Stop when even the best case cannot succeed: in Generate Parentheses, never let closes exceed opens; in Matchsticks to Square, a stick longer than a side fails at once.
  • Ordering. Try the most constrained choice first (Sudoku's fewest options; big sticks first in partition-to-k-subsets).
  • Symmetry. In partition into k equal subsets, if putting an item into an empty bucket failed, putting it into another empty bucket fails too.
  • Memoisation. If the result depends only on a small state (start index in Word Break II), cache it. At that point you are doing DP.
