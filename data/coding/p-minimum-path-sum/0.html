The story

Explain like I'm 5

A robot walks across a field of square tiles from the top-left tile to the bottom-right tile, and every tile charges a toll. The robot may only step right or down. Which walk pays the least? Stand on any tile and ask: "how did I get here?" Either from the tile above or from the tile on the left. If you already know the cheapest bill for those two tiles, take the smaller one and add this tile's toll. Do that for every tile, top to bottom, and the last tile holds the answer.

This is the standard "grid DP": the state is a cell, the choice is the last move, and the order of filling (row by row, left to right) guarantees both inputs of a cell are ready before the cell is computed.

From your world

It is shortest path on a DAG. A grid where edges only go right or down has no cycles, so you do not need Dijkstra's heap: processing cells in row-major order is already a topological order, and a single relaxation per edge finishes the job - like computing worst-case latency through a pipeline of stages where each stage only feeds the next.
