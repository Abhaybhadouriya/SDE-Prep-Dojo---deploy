The story: the family tree on the wall

Explain like I'm 5

Grandma is at the top of the family tree. She has at most two children, and each child has at most two children of their own, and so on down to the babies at the bottom who have no children yet.

If you want to count everyone, you can ask Grandma, "how many people are in your family?" She does not count them herself. She asks each of her two children the same question, adds their answers, and adds one for herself. Each child does the same thing with their own children. The babies just say "one, me!"

That is the whole trick of trees: ask the same question of each child, then combine the answers. Sometimes the answer flows up (counting the family), sometimes a message flows down (Grandma whispers her surname and every child passes it on), and sometimes you visit people row by row (a group photo: grandparents first, then parents, then kids).

From your world

You have walked trees for years. The kernel's struct rb_node (red-black tree behind CFS run queues, epoll, and many timer and memory-region lookups) is a binary search tree node with left and right pointers. fib_trie is a prefix tree for route lookup, the dentry cache is an n-ary tree, and the device tree is literally a tree. Recursion on a tree is the call stack doing your bookkeeping; the iterative versions on this page just manage that stack by hand, the way you would in kernel code where the stack is small and recursion is frowned upon.
