Interview questions

Design a structure that returns the median of a stream of integers. Why two heaps, and what are the invariants?

A max-heap lo holds the smaller half and a min-heap hi the larger half. Invariants: top(lo) <= top(hi), and |lo| equals |hi| or |hi| + 1. The median is top(lo) for an odd count, else the average of both tops. Add = push to lo, move lo's top to hi, move hi's top back if hi got bigger: O(log n). Query O(1).

How to answer

Start with the sorted-array baseline (O(n) insert) to show you know why it fails, then state the two invariants before writing code. Write the three-move add; it has no branches to get wrong.

Likely follow-ups
  • All values in [0, 100]? (101 counters, walk to the middle.)
  • 99% in [0, 100]? (Counters plus overflow counts or lists for both sides.)
  • Support deleting a value? (Lazy deletion, or two multisets.)
Sliding window median: how do you remove the item that leaves the window?

A binary heap cannot remove an arbitrary item in O(log n) without an index. Lazy deletion: count the leaving value in a hash map, adjust a balance variable for the side it belonged to (decided with <= top(lo)), insert the new value, rebalance once if needed, and pop stale values only when they surface at a top. O(n log n). In C++ use a multiset with a middle iterator, or two multisets: O(n log k).

How to answer

Name the problem with heaps (no delete), give both fixes, pick one for your language. Mention overflow when averaging two large ints.

Likely follow-ups
  • Why can the heaps grow beyond k? (Stale items under the tops.) Does it hurt? (Memory up to O(n); time still O(n log n).)
  • Could you use an indexed heap? (Yes: store positions in a map and sift after a swap-with-last delete; more code, O(log k).)
IPO: prove that always taking the largest affordable profit is optimal.

Capital never decreases. Suppose an optimal plan's first pick has profit p while a larger affordable profit P exists. Swap: doing P first gives capital at least as high at every later step, so every later project in the plan is still affordable, and the total is no smaller. Repeat for each step. Implementation: projects sorted by capital with a pointer, a max-heap of unlocked profits, k pops, stop early if the heap is empty.

How to answer

Say "two keys" first: capital gates, profit ranks. Then the two structures, then the exchange argument in two sentences.

Likely follow-ups
  • Capital is spent when you start a project? (No longer monotone; becomes a knapsack-like problem, greedy fails.)
  • k is larger than n? (The loop stops when the heap empties; at most n pops.)
Merge k sorted linked lists. Compare the heap with divide and conquer.

Heap: push the k heads, pop the smallest, append it, push its next. O(N log k) time, O(k) extra space. Divide and conquer: merge lists in pairs per round, log k rounds of O(N) each: also O(N log k), O(1) extra if done iteratively. One-by-one merging into an accumulator is O(N k).

How to answer

State why only heads can be next. Mention the Python tie-break and the empty-list cases (k = 0, some lists empty).

Likely follow-ups
  • The lists are files bigger than RAM? (External merge: buffered reader per run, heap of size k, several passes if needed.)
  • Return an iterator instead of a list? (Keep the heap as the iterator's state.)
Smallest range covering elements from k lists: why do you always advance the list holding the minimum?

The current heads cover every list, and the window is [min, max]. Advancing any other list can only keep or raise the max while the min stays: never narrower. Only advancing the min's list can raise the left end. So each pop is the only useful move, and after it we check the new window. Stop when the min's list is exhausted. O(N log k).

How to answer

Draw three short lists and do two pops by hand. Explain the stop condition and the tie rule (strict < keeps the earlier start).

Likely follow-ups
  • Another approach? (Merge all numbers tagged by list, then a classic "window covering all k colours" with counts: O(N log N).)
  • Why track max separately? (A min-heap only gives the min; the max only rises, so a variable is enough.)
Find k pairs with the smallest sums from two sorted arrays.

View row i as the sorted list a[i] + b[0..]. Seed the heap with (a[i] + b[0], i, 0) for the first min(k, |a|) rows. Pop k times; after popping (i, j), push (i, j + 1). O(k log min(k, |a|)). Sums need 64 bits.

How to answer

Draw the 3 x 3 sum grid; show that rows and columns are sorted; explain why seeding column 0 means no visited set.

Likely follow-ups
  • Only the k-th smallest sum, k up to |a| · |b|? (Binary search on the sum with a two-pointer count.)
  • Three arrays? (Merge two first, keep k, then merge with the third.)
Minimum cost to hire k workers: where do the sort and the heap come from?

All hired workers share one pay rate per unit of quality, and it must be at least each one's wage / quality. So the rate is the group's maximum ratio, and cost = rate × quality sum. Sort by ratio; the current worker sets the rate; among earlier workers keep the k smallest qualities in a max-heap with a running sum; answer = min over positions of ratio × sum. O(n log n).

How to answer

Derive "cost = max ratio × sum of quality" from the two rules first; the algorithm follows from it. Name the family: Maximum Performance of a Team and Maximum Subsequence Score.

Likely follow-ups
  • Why is it fine that the captain might not be among the k smallest qualities? (It is pushed first, then the largest quality is popped; if the captain itself is popped, that group is evaluated at a ratio that is too high, which only overestimates and never beats the true best.)
You need the median latency of the last 5 minutes of requests on a busy server. What do you build?

An exact answer is a time-based sliding window median: two heaps with lazy deletion (or an order-statistic tree), evicting by timestamp from a FIFO queue. At high rates that costs memory per request. In practice latency is bucketed: a fixed histogram (for example log-scaled buckets) per second in a ring of 300 slots, summed on query, gives the median within one bucket width in O(buckets) time and constant memory. Mention that this is what monitoring systems do.

How to answer

Give the exact data-structure answer first (it shows you know the pattern), then the engineering answer with the error bound, and ask which the interviewer wants.

Likely follow-ups
  • Merge medians from many servers? (Medians do not merge; histograms and sketches like t-digest do.)
  • p99 instead of p50? (Same histogram, walk to 99%.)
