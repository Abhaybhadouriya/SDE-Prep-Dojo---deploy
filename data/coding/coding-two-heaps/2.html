How to recognise these patterns

Cue words: "median of a stream / of every window", "middle element after each insert", "balance two groups", "maximise capital / profit with at most k picks", "choose k workers / engineers / items where cost depends on the worst one", "merge k sorted", "smallest range that includes one from each list", "k smallest sums / pairs", "k-th smallest in a sorted matrix", "next ugly number".

  1. You need a middle, not an end. One heap gives you the min or the max. A median or "the boundary between two halves" needs two heaps facing each other.
  2. The data keeps changing. If all the data is there up front, quickselect finds the median in O(n) average. Two heaps earn their place when items arrive (stream) or arrive and leave (window).
  3. Two keys pull in different directions. "You may only take what you can afford (key 1), and you want the best one (key 2)." Sort by key 1, heap by key 2.
  4. Several sorted sources, and you want them in global order. Heap of heads, each entry remembering where it came from.

Counter-cue: if the question asks for the k-th smallest in one static array, a single heap or quickselect is enough (see the heap primer). If values are small integers, counting beats any heap.
