Designing with stacks and queues

Three small design questions come up again and again, often as a warm-up:

  • Queue using two stacks (232). Push onto in. To pop or peek, if out is empty, move everything from in to out (this reverses the order), then use out's top. Each element moves once, so every operation is amortised O(1), even though one pop can cost O(n).
  • Min Stack (155). Store the current minimum alongside each value (push (x, min(x, previousMin))), or keep a second stack of minimums. getMin is the top's saved minimum, O(1).
  • Circular queue (622). A fixed array with head and count; the tail is (head + count) % capacity. Keeping a count avoids the "head == tail: full or empty?" ambiguity.
