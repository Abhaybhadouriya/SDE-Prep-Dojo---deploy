The re-solve schedule: 1, 3, 7, 14, 30 days

The numbers are gaps between reviews, not dates. Solve on day 0, review after 1 day, then 3 days after that, then 7, then 14, then 30. After that the site keeps going to 60 and 120 days. Each review you pass pushes the next one further out; a review you fail pulls the card back to tomorrow. Step through one card's life.

  1. Day 0: you solve the problem (with or without help) and write its card. The site schedules the first review for tomorrow.
  2. Day 1: recall the card from the title alone, then check. Good: the next gap is 3 days.
  3. Day 4: same again, a 1-2 minute card review. Good: the next gap is 7 days.
  4. Day 11: the first real test. Open a blank editor, set a 25-minute timer and solve it again without looking. Good: next gap 14 days.
  5. Day 25: card review; if anything feels shaky, write just the core loop. Good: next gap 30 days.
  6. Day 55: by now most problems are solid. The ladder continues at 60 and 120 days, so a mastered card costs you a minute every two to four months.
  7. If you fail at any point, grade Again. The card drops back to a 1-day gap and climbs the ladder again. That is not a punishment: the first rungs are short, so it recovers quickly.

How the four grades on the Revise page move a card (this is exactly what the site stores):

GradeUse it whenNext gapExample from a 7-day gap
Again (key 1)You could not get the approach, or your code had a real bug you did not find.Back to 1 day7 → 1
Hard (key 2)You got there, but slowly or with a peek at the card.Same gap again7 → 7
Good (key 3)You recalled the idea and could code it.Next rung7 → 14
Easy (key 4)Instant: pattern, insight and edge cases came out together.Skip a rung7 → 30

A Good or Easy grade on a review whose gap was already 30 days or more, after at least four reviews, marks the problem Mastered - so the earliest is about two months after you first solved it (1, 3, 7, 14, 30 days, then the 30-day review). A later Again drops it back to Solved. This is a simplified version of the SM-2 algorithm that the SuperMemo and Anki flashcard programs grew from: fixed rungs instead of a per-card "ease" number, so you can predict your own schedule in your head.

From your world

The Linux timer wheel keeps near timers in fine-grained buckets and far timers in coarse ones, because a timer two minutes out does not need millisecond precision. The ladder works the same way: tomorrow's review matters to the day, a 120-day review can slip a week without harm.
