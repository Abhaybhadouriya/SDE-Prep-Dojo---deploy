The revision card: a five-line memory hook

You cannot re-solve 200 problems from scratch every week, and you do not need to. What you need to keep is the hook: the few facts that let you rebuild the solution in a minute. Write them as five lines the moment you finish a problem, while the "aha" is still warm.

LineWhat it answersBad (too vague)Good
1 PatternWhich group, subgroup and variant? Use the breadcrumb on the problem page."strings""sliding window, variable size, longest valid"
2 TriggerWhich words in the statement should have told you? This is what makes the next unseen problem easier."saw it before""'minimum speed such that ... within H hours' = binary search on the answer"
3 InsightThe one idea the brute force misses."use a map""if speed s works, every speed above s works, so the answers are monotonic"
4 Template / invariantThe loop shape or invariant you code from. Point at the lesson template rather than copying code.(the whole solution pasted)"lo = 1, hi = max(pile); first s with hours(s) <= H"
5 Traps + costWhere you got the bug, and the complexity you must say aloud.(empty)"ceil(p / s) without floats; hours can overflow int · O(n log max)"

Where to keep the cards: every problem page in this module has a Notes box (up to 4,000 characters, saved in your browser and synced when you sign in). Paste the five lines there. A paper notebook works too; what matters is that the card exists and is short.

From your world

A revision card is a good commit message for your own brain. The diff (your code) shows what; the message says why, which edge case bit you, and what not to break. Six months later nobody reads the diff first; they read the message.
