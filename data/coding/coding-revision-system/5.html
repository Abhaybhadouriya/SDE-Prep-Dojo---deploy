A revision session, step by step

Do revision before new problems, every day, while you are fresh. The queue is sorted most-overdue first, so if you only have 15 minutes you still clear the cards most at risk. Here is a short session with four due cards on day 10.

  1. Open Revise. Four cards are due. LRU Cache is the most overdue, so it comes first.
  2. See the title only. Say the card out loud in under a minute: pattern, insight, cost. Then show the answer and compare. You had it: Good, next gap 14 days.
  3. 3Sum: you remembered the shape but forgot the duplicate skip, a real bug. Hard keeps the 3-day gap, and you note a full re-solve for after the queue.
  4. Two Sum: instant and complete. Easy skips a rung: 1 day becomes 7.
  5. Coin Change: blank. Grade Again honestly (back to 1 day) and write one line on why. The failure section below says what to do next.
  6. Card reviews are fast because you only recall the hook. Spend the saved time on full re-solves of the cards you graded Hard or Again, then move on to new work.

Card review or full re-solve?

Card reviewFull re-solve
What you doSee the title, say the five lines out loud, check against the card and your old code.Blank editor, timer on, solve and dry-run without looking, then compare.
Time1-2 minutes15-25 minutes (Medium), up to 35 (Hard)
WhenGaps of 1, 3, 14, 30 days and beyondThe 7-day review; any card you graded Hard or Again; the week before a loop for your target company's list
CatchesForgotten pattern or insightBugs, slow coding, missed edge cases

Interviewers love a candidate who can say, after two minutes of thought, "this is binary search on the answer, because feasibility is monotonic". Card reviews train exactly that sentence. Full re-solves train the 20 minutes of clean code that follow it.
