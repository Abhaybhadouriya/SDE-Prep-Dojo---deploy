The story: the spice rack

Explain like I'm 5

Grandma teaches you a curry. You cook it once with her and it is perfect. A month later you try alone and you cannot remember: was it cumin first or mustard seeds? You stand in front of the spice rack, lost.

Now imagine you cooked it again the next day, then three days later, then a week later, then two weeks later. Each time you only needed a quick look at grandma's little card: "seeds first, wait for the pop, then onions". After the fifth time you never need the card again.

Coding problems are the same. Solving a problem once is cooking with grandma. The card is the five lines that remind you of the trick. The schedule is cooking it again just before you would forget.

From your world

DRAM cells leak charge, so the memory controller refreshes every row before the charge drops too far (the usual DDR rule is every row within a 64 ms window). Nobody calls that wasteful: without refresh the data is gone. Your memory of a solution leaks the same way, just over days instead of milliseconds. Spaced repetition is a refresh schedule for it, and like a good controller it refreshes a row just before it would fail, not every cycle.
