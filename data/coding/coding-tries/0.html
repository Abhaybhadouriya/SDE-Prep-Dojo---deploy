The story: the phone that guesses your friend's name

Explain like I'm 5

You type "S" into your phone's contacts and it shows Sam, Sara and Sunil. You type "Sa" and Sunil disappears. You type "Sar" and only Sara is left. The phone did not read every name each time you pressed a key. It keeps the names in a kind of tree of letters: from "S" you can go to "a" or "u", from "Sa" you can go to "m" or "r", and so on.

Every key press moves you one step down the letter tree. Whatever is below where you are standing is exactly the list of names that start with what you typed. And if you try to step to a letter that is not there, you know at once that no name starts that way.

From your world

You already know the most famous trie in networking: the routing table. Longest prefix match walks the destination address bit by bit from the most significant bit, and the deepest node that holds a route wins. Linux's IPv4 FIB is an LC-trie (fib_trie, a path- and level-compressed trie). The page cache's XArray (formerly the radix tree) is another trie, keyed by file offset bits. Interview tries are the same idea with letters instead of bits, and without the compression tricks.
