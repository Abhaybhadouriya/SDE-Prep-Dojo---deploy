The story

Explain like I'm 5

You play a game with a number: split it into digits, square each one, add them up, and that is your new number. Some numbers land on 1 and stay there forever (1 squared is 1). Others go round and round in a loop and never land. To find out which, send two runners along the chain: one walks, one runs twice as fast. If there is a loop, the runner catches the walker from behind. If the chain ends at 1, the runner just sits on 1.

The interview value is not the digit arithmetic but two ideas: (1) seeing an implicit linked list where there is no list at all, and (2) arguing that the process must end in 1 or in a cycle, so the loop terminates. Without (2), "repeat until 1" looks like it could run forever.

From your world

The same shape shows up in a routing-loop detector or in walking a chain of descriptors that might be corrupted into a loop: you cannot afford to remember every node, so you run two cursors at different speeds and check whether they meet. Floyd's algorithm is also how Pollard's rho finds factor collisions.
