The story: the lift operator with a notebook

Explain like I'm 5

Long ago, a lift had a person inside called the operator. People on each floor shouted "going up!" or "going down!" and the operator wrote the floor in a notebook. People inside said "fifth floor, please" and that went in the notebook too.

The operator had one simple rule: keep going the way you are going while someone still needs you that way; then turn around. She never went up one floor, down two, up three, because that wastes everyone's time. And she never, ever moved the lift with the door open.

When the building got a second lift, a manager in the lobby decided which lift should answer each shout. He did not drive the lifts; he only wrote in their notebooks.

That is the whole design. The notebook is a set of pending stops per car. The rule "keep going while there is work ahead" is the LOOK policy. The operator's moods (standing still, moving, door open, closed for repair) are states. The shouts are request objects. The lobby manager is the controller, and his way of picking a lift is the dispatch strategy.

From your world

You already know the elevator algorithm: it is the disk-arm scheduler. SCAN and LOOK are disk-head schedulers: serve requests in sector order while sweeping one way, then turn. Linux still calls its I/O schedulers elevators (block/elevator.c), the deadline scheduler keeps requests sorted by sector for exactly this reason, and the old elevator= boot parameter picked one. The structure also matches a protocol stack: each car is a TCP-style state machine driven by one event loop, and buttons are interrupts that only enqueue work for that loop, like a top half handing off to a softirq.
