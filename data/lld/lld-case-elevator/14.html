Interview follow-ups (Q&A)

What is the difference between a hall call and a car call, and why model them separately?

A hall call is a floor plus a direction and asks the system for any car; it must be dispatched. A car call is a floor pressed inside one car and belongs to that car; it is never dispatched. They also stop the car under different rules: a car call always stops it, a hall call only when the direction matches or the car is turning. Two types make both rules explicit and let dispatch accept only hall calls.

How to answer

Give the two rules, then show the two records.

Likely follow-ups
  • How does destination dispatch change this? (A third request type with both floors.)
Why the State pattern instead of an enum and a switch?

With four states and one event (tick), an enum plus switch is acceptable and I would say so. The State pattern pays off when states get their own data and events: door obstruction, fire recall, overload. Each state class decides its own next state, so a new state is one class plus a transition, and the rule "only MOVING moves the car" lives in one place. I start with classes here because the follow-ups in this question always add states.

How to answer

Do not oversell; say when the switch is fine and what tips it over.

Likely follow-ups
  • Where do transitions live: in the states or in a table?
Explain SCAN versus LOOK. Which do you use?

Both serve stops in the current direction before turning. SCAN sweeps to the end of the shaft before turning; LOOK turns as soon as there is nothing more ahead. From floor 4 with calls at 6 and 2, LOOK travels 6 floors and SCAN 14. LOOK wastes less travel and energy; SCAN gives more uniform worst-case waits at the ends. I default to LOOK and keep both as a MovementPolicy strategy, so a test compares them on the same input.

How to answer

One example with numbers beats a definition.

Likely follow-ups
  • Why not shortest-seek-first? (It starves floors far from the current cluster.)
How do you choose which car answers a hall call?

Through a DispatchStrategy. Nearest-by-distance is the baseline but ignores direction. My default estimates travel: an idle car costs the distance; a car moving toward the caller in the caller's direction costs the distance; any other car costs finishing its sweep to its farthest stop and coming back. Break ties by id so tests are stable. Production systems add load, the number of stops already assigned, and zones; all fit the same interface.

How to answer

Show the bad case for nearest (one floor away, going the wrong way), then the cost function.

Likely follow-ups
  • Should a call be re-dispatched if a better car appears later?
The building has N floors. How do you find the next stop in O(log N)?

Keep the stops in sorted sets per car (car calls, hall-up, hall-down): Java TreeSet, C++ std::set. The next stop above is higher(floor), below is lower(floor), both O(log N). Adding and removing are O(log N) too. A boolean array of N floors would need an O(N) scan for "anything above me?"; a bitmap with find-next-set-bit is the C answer and is fast in practice.

How to answer

Name the data structure and the exact method.

Likely follow-ups
  • Why three sets and not one? (Direction-aware stopping.)
Many people press buttons at the same time. How is your design thread-safe?

Single writer. submit() can run on any thread: it validates against immutable fields and puts an immutable command in a BlockingQueue. One controller thread drains the queue on each tick and is the only code that reads or writes car state, so cars have no locks. Listeners are in a copy-on-write list. My test runs 4 button threads with 800 presses while the controller ticks, and asserts exactly 800 dispatches and that every stop is served.

How to answer

Say who owns the state, how work reaches the owner, and what the test proves.

Likely follow-ups
  • Why not a lock per elevator? (Dispatch would need all of them at once.)
  • How does a status API read car positions safely? (Snapshot after each tick.)
Add a capacity limit of 20 people per car.

The car tracks load (from a weight sensor, or from boarding and leaving events). When it is full, shouldStopHere() ignores hall calls but still stops for car calls (people must get out), and the skipped hall call goes back to the controller for re-dispatch. Dispatch skips full cars or adds a large penalty to their cost. Nothing else changes, which shows the seams were right.

How to answer

Name the two places that change (stop rule, dispatch) and the one that must not (car calls still stop).

Likely follow-ups
  • What if the full car is the only one in service?
100 floors, 20 cars, and zones. How does your design change?

Each car gets a served range. A ZonedDispatch wraps the cost strategy: it filters candidates to cars whose range includes the call floor, then delegates. Car calls outside the car's range are rejected in submit. The lobby is usually shared by all zones, so a lobby UP call goes to the zone of the destination, which is the argument for destination dispatch at that scale.

How to answer

Decorator over the existing strategy; no change to cars or states.

Likely follow-ups
  • Express cars that skip floors?
Can a request be starved? Prove it cannot.

With LOOK a car only reverses when nothing remains ahead, so it cannot oscillate around one region. A stop behind the car is reached after at most one sweep to the farthest stop ahead and back, which is bounded by twice the shaft length. Hall calls never get lost because a call no car can take waits in unassigned and is retried each tick. Shortest-seek-first, by contrast, can starve a far floor forever if nearby calls keep arriving.

How to answer

A bound (two shaft lengths) plus the "never dropped" rule.

Likely follow-ups
  • Does dispatch preserve the bound across cars?
How do you test an elevator system deterministically?

Time is a tick I control, so a test sets up cars, submits requests, calls tick() a known number of times and checks positions and states. Randomness uses fixed seeds. Invariants are checked with a listener: on every move, assert the state is MOVING and the floor is in range. Liveness is checked with a simulated passenger load that must finish. Concurrency is checked with real threads, asserting counts rather than an order.

How to answer

Ticks for determinism, listeners for invariants, a load test for liveness.

Likely follow-ups
  • How would you test with real time? (Inject a clock.)
