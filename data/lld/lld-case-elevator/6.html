Core flows

A hall call, from button to doors

  1. Asha presses UP on floor 3. Her (button) thread calls submit(new HallCall(3, UP)). The controller validates it (floor in range, a real button) and puts it in the inbox. The call returns at once: the button thread never touches a car.
  2. On the next tick(), the controller thread drains the inbox and runs each command. HallCall.execute() calls assignHallCall(this).
  3. The controller asks the dispatch strategy to choose among cars that accept new calls. E1 is idle at 0 (cost 3); E2 is idle at 8 (cost 5). E1 wins.
  4. The call is added to E1's hallUp set and listeners hear onDispatch: the display can light "E1 coming".
  5. On each following tick E1's state runs: IDLE sees a stop above and becomes MOVING; MOVING moves one floor per tick and tells listeners.
  6. At floor 3, shouldStopHere() is true (a hall call in E1's direction). serveHere() clears the call, starts the door dwell and the state becomes DOORS_OPEN.
  7. Asha gets in and presses 8. That is a CarCall(E1, 8), submitted the same way and executed on the next tick. It is never dispatched: it already belongs to E1.

Should this car stop here?

The rule in shouldStopHere() is short and worth saying out loud in the interview:

  1. Car call here always stop: someone inside wants this floor.
  2. Hall call here in my direction stop: they want to go my way.
  3. Hall call here in the other direction stop only if I am about to turn around anyway (nothing more ahead). Otherwise pass it and pick them up on the way back.

When does the car turn? LOOK versus SCAN

  1. LOOK: one car at floor 4, car calls for 6 and 2. It goes up to 6, sees nothing more above, turns at once and goes down to 2: 6 floors.
  2. SCAN: same calls, but SCAN keeps sweeping to the top floor before turning, then comes down to 2: 14 floors. SCAN gives more predictable wait times at the ends of the building at the cost of empty travel; LOOK is what real controllers and our default use.

Both are one method: LookPolicy.continueAhead returns "is there any stop beyond this floor in this direction?"; ScanPolicy.continueAhead returns "are there any stops at all, and am I not at the end yet?". The test travel(new LookPolicy()) == 6 and travel(new ScanPolicy()) == 14 proves the difference.

Which car? The dispatch cost

NearestCarDispatch picks the car with the smallest floor distance. It is easy and wrong in a classic way: a car one floor away that is going the other direction may need to travel to the top and back. DirectionalCostDispatch estimates floors to travel before reaching the caller:

  • Idle car: distance to the call.
  • Moving car, call ahead and in the same direction: distance to the call (pick up on the way).
  • Otherwise: finish this sweep to its farthest stop, then come back: |farthest - floor| + |farthest - callFloor|.

The test sets E1 at floor 2 going up to 10 and E2 idle at 5, then presses DOWN on floor 3. Nearest picks E1 (one floor away, wrong way: cost 15 by the second rule). Directional picks E2 (cost 2). Ties go to the lower car id, so tests are deterministic.
