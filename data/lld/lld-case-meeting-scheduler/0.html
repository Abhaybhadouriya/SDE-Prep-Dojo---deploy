The story: the room register at school

Explain like I'm 5

Your school has three special rooms: a small reading room, a music room and the big hall. Next to the office there is a big register. Each room has its own page, and each page has lines for the times of the day. When a teacher wants a room, she looks at that room's page. If the lines for her time are empty, she writes her name there. If someone else's name is already on any of those lines, she must pick another room or another time.

Two rules make it work. First, a room page can never have two names on the same line. Second, only one teacher may write in the register at a time, so two teachers cannot both see an empty line and both write on it.

That is the whole problem. A meeting scheduler is the register: one page per room (a room calendar), one page per person (a personal calendar), and a clerk (the scheduler) who checks and writes. The follow-ups interviewers love (repeat every Tuesday, pick the smallest room that fits, send invites, find a free slot for five busy people) are all new pages or new rules in that register.

From your world

A room calendar is a VMA list. The kernel keeps a process's memory regions (vm_area_struct) sorted by start address in a tree, and mmap(MAP_FIXED_NOREPLACE) must fail with EEXIST if the new range overlaps an existing one. To check, it does not scan every region: it finds the neighbour just before and just after the new address. Our RoomCalendar does exactly that with a sorted map, and the same "half-open range" rule applies: [start, end), so a region ending at 0x2000 does not overlap one starting at 0x2000.

Where this breaks: a process has one address space, but a meeting touches many calendars at once (one room plus every participant). That is where lock ordering comes in, and it is the part of this problem that most candidates get wrong.
