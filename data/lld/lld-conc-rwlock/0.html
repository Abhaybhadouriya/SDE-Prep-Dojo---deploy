The story: the notice board

Explain like I'm 5

The school notice board shows today's timetable. Any number of children can read it at the same time; reading does not change anything. But when the teacher repaints it, nobody may read, or they would see half of Monday and half of Tuesday.

Rule one: many readers together, or one painter alone. That is a read-write lock.

Rule two: if the teacher is waiting with her paint, new children must stop walking up, or there will always be someone reading and she never gets a turn. That is writer starvation, and the fix is to block new readers while a writer waits.

A cleverer teacher paints a new board in the staff room and swaps it in with one move. Children reading the old board finish reading the old board. Nobody ever waits. That is copy-on-write, and in the kernel, RCU.

From your world

The kernel has all three tools. rwlock_t (read_lock/write_lock) and struct rw_semaphore (down_read/down_write) are read-write locks. seqlock_t (read_seqbegin/read_seqretry) protects timekeeping data, where readers retry instead of blocking the writer. RCU (rcu_read_lock, rcu_dereference, rcu_assign_pointer, synchronize_rcu) protects the routing tables and the network-device list that every packet reads. Java's ReentrantReadWriteLock, StampedLock and "immutable object behind an AtomicReference" are the same three ideas.
