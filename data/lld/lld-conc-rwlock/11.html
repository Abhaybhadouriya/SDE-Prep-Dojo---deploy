In interviews

Read-write locks are asked in two forms: build one (with synchronized, wait and notifyAll, no library classes), or choose one for a read-heavy component and defend the choice. Real reports:

  • Microsoft: create a simple read-write lock with synchronized, wait() and notify(); writers exclusive, readers shared, no ReadWriteLock or atomics allowed (LeetCode Discuss, 2019-07-14).
  • Dropbox onsite: "Reader-Writer Lock" as one of four onsite rounds (LeetCode Discuss, 2021-02-26).
  • A senior SDE question: optimise a getMax() / update() class for read throughput, then for write throughput; the candidate's ReentrantReadWriteLock answer was not what the interviewer wanted (LeetCode Discuss, 2021-07-19). A volatile field with a CAS loop (AtomicInteger.accumulateAndGet(x, Math::max)) makes reads free, which is the point of the choosing section: a one-word value does not need a read-write lock.
  • Rubrik: "implement reader writer lock, various cases" (LeetCode Discuss, 2026-04-16); our worked answer, including the cancelled-writer case, is in the concurrency interview page.

Where it fits in the case studies: the read-mostly config or catalogue in a key-value store, route or rule tables in a rate limiter, and the leaderboard reads in Cricbuzz.

Strong 30-second answer: "Readers share, writers exclude. I keep a reader count, a writing flag and a waiting-writers count under one monitor; new readers wait while a writer is waiting, so writers cannot starve. Every wait is in a while loop, unlock calls notifyAll, and a writer that is interrupted while waiting undoes its count and wakes the readers behind it. Before using one, I check the read side is long enough to matter: for a single value, a volatile or an AtomicReference to an immutable snapshot is faster and simpler."
