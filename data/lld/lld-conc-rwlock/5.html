When a read-write lock is slower than a mutex

An RW lock is not free speed. Every readLock().lock() still writes to the shared lock state (it increments the reader count with a CAS), so all readers still fight over one cache line. It only wins when:

  • The read section is long enough (microseconds, not nanoseconds) for parallel reading to outweigh the extra bookkeeping. For a HashMap.get, a plain lock or a ConcurrentHashMap is often faster.
  • Reads far outnumber writes. With 50% writes, readers mostly wait anyway.
  • The data is not already in a concurrent structure. If ConcurrentHashMap fits, use it: reads take no lock at all.

Measure before you choose. In an interview, say: "RW lock if reads dominate and the read section is not trivial; otherwise a mutex or a concurrent map; and for the hottest paths, no lock on reads at all", then explain the next two sections.
