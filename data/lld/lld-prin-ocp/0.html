The story: the wall socket

Explain like I'm 5

Your house has wires hidden inside the walls, and a few sockets on the wall. When you buy a new lamp, you do not call an electrician to break the wall and add a wire just for the lamp. You plug it into a socket. The lamp has the right plug, the socket has the right holes, and that is enough.

The wiring inside the wall is closed: nobody touches it. The house is still open: you can add a fan, a phone charger or a new TV tomorrow. The socket is the agreement between them. That is the Open/Closed Principle: build your code with sockets, so new things can plug in without breaking the walls.

In code, "breaking the wall" means opening a class that already works and is tested, adding another else if, and hoping nothing else breaks. A socket is an interface (an abstract type that lists methods but not how they work). New behaviour arrives as a new class that implements it.
