The story: the sandwich counter

Explain like I'm 5

At a sandwich shop you do not shout "brown, yes, no, cheddar, yes, no, mayo!" in one breath. You go step by step: "brown bread", "cheddar", "add lettuce", "no onion", "mayo please". The person behind the counter builds it as you talk.

Then you say "wrap it". Only now do you get a sandwich. Before that, it is a half-made thing that stays behind the counter. If you forgot the bread, they stop you: "You need bread!"

The person behind the counter is the builder. "Wrap it" is build(). The wrapped sandwich is the product. And "Veggie Delight, please" (a fixed recipe from the menu) is a director.

Three things make this work, and they are exactly the three things the Builder pattern gives you:

  1. Readable steps. Each choice has a name (.ttl(32)), not a position in a long list.
  2. One check at the end. All the rules ("bread is required", "cheese only if not vegan") run once, in build().
  3. No half-made objects escape. The finished product can be immutable (cannot change after creation) and is safe to share.
