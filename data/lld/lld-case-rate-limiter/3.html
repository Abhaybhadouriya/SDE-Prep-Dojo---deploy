Identify the entities (nouns and verbs)

Take the requirement sentence and underline nouns (candidate classes) and verbs (candidate methods). Then throw away the nouns that are only data.

  1. Start from one requirement sentence. Read it slowly.
  2. Nouns are candidate classes. Coloured ones carry behaviour or rules; grey ones may be plain data.
  3. Verbs become methods: allow on the service, refill and expire inside each algorithm, configuration on the service.
  4. The core four: a service (entry point), a rule (what the limit is), a Limiter interface (one key's state and algorithm), and one class per algorithm.
  5. Three supporting classes appear from non-functional needs: a factory (build a limiter from a rule), a clock (testable time), and a composite (combined limits).
  6. Grey nouns are dropped. A client and an API are identifiers; making a Client class here is over-design.
