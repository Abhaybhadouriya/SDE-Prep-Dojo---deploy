Noun and verb extraction, step by step

Here is a requirement paragraph in the style an interviewer reads aloud:

"Design a library system. Members can search books by title or author. A book can have several copies. A member can borrow up to 3 copies for 14 days. If a copy is returned late, the member pays a fine of 5 rupees per day. Librarians add new copies and register users. The system sends a reminder email when a loan is overdue."

Underline every noun and noun phrase, circle every verb. You get roughly: nouns library, system, member, book, title, author, copy, day, fine, rupee, librarian, user, reminder, email, loan; verbs search, borrow, return, pay, add, register, send. Now filter. The stepper shows the sorting in the order you should do it.

  1. Drop the frame nouns. "Library" and "system" describe the whole thing you are building. At most they become the orchestrator's name; they are not domain objects.
  2. Merge synonyms. The paragraph says "member" in one sentence and "user" in another. Ask once ("are users and members the same?") and pick one word. Two names for one idea become two classes that drift apart.
  3. Demote nouns that are just data. Title and author have no behaviour; they are fields of Book. "5 rupees" and "14 days" are values: a Money value object and a constant or policy. A tiny, validated value (ISBN) earns its own value class.
  4. Separate roles and side effects. Librarian is an actor. You need a class for it only if librarians have data the system tracks (permissions, shift). The reminder email is a side effect of a use case: model it as a Notifier interface so the core does not depend on SMTP.
  5. What is left is your class list. Note the split the paragraph hid: "book" really meant two things, the title in the catalogue (Book) and the physical item on the shelf (BookCopy). Spotting such splits is where interviewers see real modelling skill.
