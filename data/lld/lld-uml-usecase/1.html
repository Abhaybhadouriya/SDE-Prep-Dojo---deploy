Actors and use cases: who uses the system, for what

A use case is one goal that someone outside the system wants to reach with it, written as a short verb phrase: "Borrow a copy", "Pay a fine". An actor is that "someone": a role that sits outside the system and talks to it. Three things trip people up:

  • An actor is a role, not a person. Priya can be a Member on Monday and a Librarian on Tuesday. You draw two actors, not one Priya.
  • Actors are not always humans. A payment gateway, a cron-style scheduler that sends overdue reminders, or another service can all be actors.
  • Actors live outside the boundary. If something is inside your code, it is a class, not an actor. "Member" the actor and Member the class can share a name: the actor is the real person, the class is your record of them.

The two relationships you may meet

  • «include»: a step that always happens inside another use case and is shared by several of them ("Borrow" and "Reserve" both include "Look up member"). Arrow points from the base use case to the included one.
  • «extend»: an optional add-on that happens only under a condition ("Pay a fine" extends "Return a copy" when the book is late). Arrow points from the extension to the base.

How much to draw in an interview: almost never the full UML diagram. Write the actors and use cases as a bulleted list in two minutes and ask "is this the scope?" That list is the contract for the rest of the round, and the interviewer will usually trim it ("skip reservations"). The formal notation is in the uml-diagrams.org reference if you ever need it.

Write each use case as one sentence with a subject

Turn every bullet into a full sentence: actor + verb + object + condition. "A member borrows an available copy if they have fewer than three active loans."
