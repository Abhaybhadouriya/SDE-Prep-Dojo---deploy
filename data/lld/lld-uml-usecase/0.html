The story: putting on a school play

Explain like I'm 5

Your class is putting on a play. The teacher hands you the story: "The wolf knocks on the door. The pig opens the door and shouts." Before anyone can act, you must decide two things. Who is in the play? The wolf, the pig. Those are your characters. What do they do? Knock, open, shout. Those are their actions. The door is a prop: it does not act on its own, but the story needs it.

You would never hire four actors called "Wolf A", "Wolf B", "Wolf C" and "Wolf D" if the story has one kind of wolf who appears four times. You hire one wolf costume and put a name tag on it.

Low-level design starts exactly like this. The requirements are the story. The nouns (wolf, pig, door) are candidates for classes. The verbs (knock, open, shout) are candidates for methods. The people outside the story who watch it and react are the actors. The rest of this page turns that child's game into an interview technique with rules you can say out loud.

From your world

You already do this when you read an RFC or a hardware datasheet. "The sender MUST retransmit the segment when the retransmission timer expires." Nouns become structs (struct tcp_sock, struct sk_buff, struct timer_list); verbs become functions that take the struct as their first argument (tcp_retransmit_skb(sk, skb)). OOP just moves that first argument inside: sock.retransmit(segment).
