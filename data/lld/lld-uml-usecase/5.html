Walking one use case through the classes

A class list is only a guess until you push a use case through it. Pick the most important one ("Borrow a copy"), and trace the calls. If a step has no obvious owner, you are missing a class or a method. If one class appears in every step, it may be doing too much. This is the moment a sequence diagram earns its place.

Notice what the trace told us: the service is the only class that touches three objects, which is correct for an orchestrator. Member and BookCopy each answer one question about themselves. If you had put canBorrow in the service by reading member.getActiveLoans().size(), the trace would show the service reaching into another object's internals: a small coupling smell interviewers notice.

Do the same trace for "Return a copy" and you find a missing piece: you need to find the loan from the barcode the librarian scans. That is a lookup the service needs (Map<barcode, Loan>, or a LoanRepository). Traces discover data structures, not just classes.
