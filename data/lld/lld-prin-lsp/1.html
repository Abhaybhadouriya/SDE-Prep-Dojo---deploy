The definition, plain and formal

Barbara Liskov (Turing Award 2008) introduced the idea in a 1987 keynote on data abstraction and hierarchy. With Jeannette Wing she made it precise in the 1994 paper A Behavioral Notion of Subtyping. Their formal rule says, in our words: anything you can prove about objects of the base type must also be true for objects of the subtype.

Plain version: if code works with a Base, it must keep working, unchanged, when you give it any subtype of Base.

The key word is behaviour. The compiler checks that Square has a setHeight method. It cannot check that setHeight keeps the promise "the width stays the same". LSP is about those promises, which is why it is also called behavioural subtyping. Inheritance that passes the type checker but breaks promises is the most common way to violate it.

From your world

A struct file_operations is a C interface. Every driver that fills .read is a "subtype". The signature is fixed by C, but the real contract is in the documentation and in what callers rely on: return a byte count no larger than requested, 0 means end of file, a negative errno means an error, and with O_NONBLOCK return -EAGAIN instead of sleeping. A driver that sleeps on a non-blocking read compiles fine and passes the type checker. It still breaks every event loop that uses it. That is an LSP violation in C.
