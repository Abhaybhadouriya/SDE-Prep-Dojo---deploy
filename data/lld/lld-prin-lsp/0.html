The story: the spare tyre

Explain like I'm 5

Your family car gets a flat tyre. Dad puts on the spare from the boot. The car drives home. That is a good substitute: you swap it in and nobody in the car has to do anything different.

Now imagine a spare tyre that fits the wheel, but bursts if the car goes faster than 30 km/h. It looks like a tyre and bolts on like a tyre. But on the highway it surprises everyone. It is not a real substitute, because it breaks a promise that every tyre makes: "you can drive normally on me".

The Liskov Substitution Principle says: if a thing claims to be a kind of tyre, it must work everywhere a tyre is expected, with no surprises.

In code, the "car" is client code written for a base type (a parent class or an interface). The "spare tyre" is a subtype (a child class or an implementation). If swapping in the subtype makes the client misbehave, the subtype is lying about what it is.
