Interview questions

Walmart LLD rounds often drift into "multithreading and locks" questions after the design (LC 6104099), and Cleartrip's 2026 follow-up round asked about synchronisation strategies and item-level vs restaurant-level locking (LC 8411392).

synchronized vs ReentrantLock: when would you choose each?

Both give mutual exclusion, reentrancy and the same memory visibility. synchronized is block-scoped and releases automatically, so it cannot leak. ReentrantLock adds tryLock (with or without timeout), lockInterruptibly, optional fairness, and multiple Condition objects, at the cost of a mandatory try/finally.

How to answer

Say "synchronized by default", then list the four features that make you switch, and give one concrete example (bounded queue with notFull/notEmpty, or a transfer with tryLock).

Likely follow-ups
  • Is ReentrantLock faster? (Not meaningfully on modern JVMs; choose on features.)
  • What does "reentrant" mean and why does it matter for recursion or a method calling another locked method?
Why must wait()/await() be called in a loop?

Three reasons: spurious wakeups are allowed by the spec; another thread can grab the lock between the signal and your re-acquire and change the state back; and with one wait set, you may be woken for someone else's condition. The loop re-checks the predicate while holding the lock, so returning from it always means the condition is true.

How to answer

Write the four-line pattern (lock; while(!p) await; act; unlock), then give the three reasons. Mention the lost-wakeup problem that atomic release-and-sleep prevents.

Likely follow-ups
  • What happens if you call wait() without holding the monitor? (IllegalMonitorStateException.)
  • notify vs notifyAll with producers and consumers on one monitor?
Your transfer method deadlocks under load. How do you fix it?

The cycle comes from locking in argument order. Impose a global order (lock the lower account id first), which breaks the circular-wait condition. Optionally use tryLock with a timeout and random back-off as a second defence, returning "busy" instead of hanging. For one hot pair, a single lock per pair or per shard is also fine.

How to answer

Draw the two threads and two locks, name the cycle, apply ordering, then add tryLock. Mention a thread dump shows "waiting to lock ... held by" chains.

Likely follow-ups
  • What if accounts have no natural order? (Use System.identityHashCode plus a tie-breaker lock.)
  • What is livelock and how can tryLock + retry cause it? (Random back-off.)
Semaphore vs mutex: is a binary semaphore a lock?

Functionally it can be used like one, but a mutex has an owner and a semaphore does not. Only the owner may unlock a mutex, and a reentrant mutex lets the owner lock again; any thread may release a semaphore. Ownership enables debugging, priority inheritance and reentrancy. No ownership makes semaphores good for signalling and for counting N resources.

How to answer

Lead with "ownership", give one use for each (mutex: protect a map; semaphore: cap DB connections at 10), and mention the "release without acquire" bug.

Likely follow-ups
  • Build a connection pool: which primitive? (Semaphore for the count + a concurrent queue of connections.)
What is a fair lock and should you use one?

A fair lock grants access in arrival order, so no thread starves. It reduces throughput because a running thread cannot take a free lock if others are queued; every handoff wakes a parked thread. Use the default unfair lock unless you have observed starvation that matters, such as a long-waiting writer.

How to answer

Define it, state the throughput cost and why (context switches), and say you would measure first.

Likely follow-ups
  • Does tryLock() respect fairness? (The untimed one does not; the timed one does.)
Spinlock vs mutex: when would you spin?

Spin when the critical section is a handful of instructions, the holder is running on another core, and the holder can never sleep. Otherwise sleep. Modern mutexes (futex-based pthread mutexes, JVM monitors, the Linux kernel mutex) spin briefly and then block, so application code should almost never write its own spinlock.

How to answer

Compare the hold time with the cost of a sleep/wake round trip, mention single-core and "holder sleeps" as the two ways spinning goes very wrong, then mention adaptive locks.

Likely follow-ups
  • Why does the kernel disable preemption or interrupts with spinlocks?
  • What is a futex?
Make this ParkingLot (or BookMyShow seat map) thread-safe. Where do the locks go?

Find the invariant first ("a spot is held by at most one vehicle"). Lock at the smallest unit that owns that invariant: per spot or per seat, not the whole lot. For "find a free spot and take it", make the check and the take one atomic step: either lock the floor's free list, or use a ConcurrentHashMap.putIfAbsent/CAS on the spot state. Never hold the lock during payment or I/O; use a temporary hold with an expiry instead.

How to answer

Invariant, then granularity, then atomic check-then-act, then lock order if more than one lock, then "what I do not do under the lock".

Likely follow-ups
  • Two users book the last seat at the same time: walk through it.
  • What changes when the seat map lives in a database? (Row locks or optimistic version numbers.)
