The story: four rules from a kitchen

Explain like I'm 5

DRY. Mom writes "2 spoons of sugar" on five recipe cards. Grandpa becomes diabetic, so she changes it to one spoon - on four cards. The fifth card still says two. Write the sugar rule once, on the fridge, and point every card to it.

KISS. Your little brother asks for a sandwich. You could build a sandwich-making robot. Or you could make a sandwich. Make the sandwich.

YAGNI. You are cooking for 4 people tonight. Do not buy a 50-litre pot "in case we host a wedding one day". Buy it when the wedding is real.

Law of Demeter. At the shop you pay the shopkeeper. You do not let the shopkeeper reach into your bag, open your purse and take the notes out himself. You are told the price; you pay.

These are not SOLID principles, but interviewers use them all the time, mostly in the form of feedback: "duplicated logic", "too complex", "over-engineered", "train-wreck calls". This page teaches each one so you can avoid those comments, and argue about them when they conflict.
