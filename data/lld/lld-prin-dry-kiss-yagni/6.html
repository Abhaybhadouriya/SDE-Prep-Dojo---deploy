When the rules fight each other

Senior engineers are not the ones who know the rules. They are the ones who know which rule to break, and say why. The common fights:

ConflictExampleHow to decide
DRY vs decouplingTwo microservices share a common-models library so the Order DTO is written onceAcross service or team boundaries, prefer a little duplication: a shared library forces lock-step releases. Share the contract (schema, API spec), not the class.
DRY vs KISSTwo 4-line methods merged into one generic method with a mode flagIf the merged version needs flags or if (caller == X), keep the copies. Wait for the rule of three.
YAGNI vs Open/ClosedStrategy interface for pricing when only one price rule existsAdd the seam when the problem statement or the interviewer signals variation ("different vehicle types", "more payment methods later"). Otherwise a concrete class, refactored when the second case arrives.
Demeter vs middle manWrapping every getter of every child objectForward behaviour clients need (pay()), not every field. Read-only navigation of value objects is usually fine.
KISS vs performanceSimple O(n) scan vs an indexed structureSimple first, measure, then optimise the measured hot path. In an interview, state the complexity and the upgrade path.
