The story: the sealed envelope

Explain like I'm 5

You are building a big tower of blocks and your mum calls you for dinner. Your little brother might knock it over. So you take a photo of the tower, put the photo in an envelope, seal it, and give it to grandpa. Grandpa does not open it; he just keeps it safe.

After dinner the tower is a mess. You ask grandpa for the envelope, open it, and rebuild the tower exactly as in the photo. Only you know how to read your own photo. Grandpa only keeps envelopes, and if you ask him, he can keep one for every hour of the day.

In code, you are the Originator (the object whose state matters, like a text editor). The sealed envelope is the Memento (a snapshot that only the originator can read). Grandpa is the Caretaker (an undo history or a list of save slots) that stores envelopes but never opens them.

From your world

You have used mementos at the lowest level of the machine. When an interrupt arrives, the CPU and entry code push the interrupted task's registers into a struct pt_regs on the kernel stack; the handler runs; then the saved registers are restored and the task continues as if nothing happened. In user-space C, setjmp(env) saves the execution context into a jmp_buf and longjmp(env, 1) returns to it. Your code never looks inside jmp_buf: its layout is private to the C library. That is an opaque memento with you as the caretaker.
