The stock model: onHand, reserved, available

Two counters per (store, SKU). onHand is what is physically in the store. reserved is how much of it is promised to unpaid orders. available = onHand - reserved is what you may promise next. Every operation changes these in a fixed way:

OperationonHandreservedavailableWhen
restock(q)+q-+qsupplier delivery
reserve(q)-+q-qorder created (only if available >= q)
commit(q)-q-q0payment succeeded
release(q)--q+qpayment failed, cancel before payment, expiry
return(q)+q-+qcancel after payment, before dispatch

Watch it with the demo's race: 5 milk cartons, 20 shoppers.

  1. Restock 5. All five are available.
  2. Twenty threads call createOrder at once; the SKU lock lets exactly five reservations through. The cartons have not moved; they are promised.
  3. The other fifteen are rejected because available is 0. This is the behaviour you want: "in the building" is not "for sale".
  4. Three customers pay: commit lowers both counters. Three cartons leave with riders.
  5. Two never pay. After the hold, the sweeper releases them: available goes back to 2 and the next shopper can buy. The invariant 0 <= reserved <= onHand held at every step.

Why not just decrement onHand at create time? You can (some teams do: "deduct at create, add back on failure"). It also prevents overselling. But you lose the ability to tell "physically present" from "promised", which pickers, audits and restock alerts need, and every failure path must remember to add back. Two counters make the state explicit. Say which you chose and why.
