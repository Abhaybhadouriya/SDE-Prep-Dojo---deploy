Interview follow-ups

When do you decrement inventory: at add-to-cart, at order creation, or at payment?

Not at add-to-cart: browsers would lock stock they never buy. Not only at payment: two people could both pay for the last unit. Reserve at order creation (checkout) with a short hold, deduct at payment success, release on failure, cancel or timeout. The hold length trades payment time against stock locked by people who abandon.

How to answer

Give the three options with the failure of each, then the reservation lifecycle and the invariant 0 <= reserved <= onHand.

Likely follow-ups
  • Flash sale with 10x traffic? (Shorter hold, a queue in front of checkout, per-user limits.)
Twenty users race for the last five units. Show that your code cannot oversell.

The only place that increases reserved is reserveAll, and it checks onHand - reserved >= q while holding that SKU's lock. So no two threads can both see the last unit as free. The demo runs 20 threads with a start latch and asserts exactly 5 orders and reserved == 5.

How to answer

Point at the single mutation site and the lock around it, then show the test. "Here is the test" beats "trust me".

Likely follow-ups
  • Why is hasAll() in the selector not enough? (It is unlocked; it can be stale by the time you reserve. It is a hint, the reserve is the decision.)
Baskets contain several SKUs. How do you lock without deadlock?

Acquire per-SKU locks in one global order (sorted by SKU id), release in reverse. With a total order there is no circular wait, so no deadlock. Alternatives: one lock per store (simpler, less parallel) or tryLock with timeout and back-off (no deadlock, but livelock risk and more code).

How to answer

Draw the two-thread cycle, then the sorted fix. Mention de-duplicating SKUs (a set) so you never lock the same one twice.

Likely follow-ups
  • What if SKUs live on different servers? (No shared lock; use per-line conditional updates and compensate, or a single-writer actor per store.)
The payment webhook arrives twice, and once after the hold expired. What happens?

Twice: onPayment holds the order's lock; the second call sees CONFIRMED and returns it without touching stock. In a real system the dedup key is the gateway's payment id, stored with a unique constraint. After expiry: the call checks now > expiresAt (or finds EXPIRED, set by the sweeper) and rejects; the stock has already gone back on sale, so the right action is an automatic refund, not a late confirm.

How to answer

Separate "duplicate" from "late". Both are normal for webhooks (at-least-once delivery).

Likely follow-ups
  • Could you instead extend the hold when payment starts? (Yes: a "payment in progress" state with a longer timeout, set when the gateway session opens.)
How do you pick the dark store, and what if the nearest one is missing one item?

A StoreSelector strategy. Default: nearest store within the delivery radius that has the full basket. If the nearest lacks an item, the next nearest with everything wins. If none has everything: reject, offer substitution, or (if allowed) split. Because the check is a hint, a failed reservation drops that store and asks again.

How to answer

Strategy plus the retry loop. Mention that real systems map a customer to a serviceable store zone first (geo-polygon), then check stock.

Likely follow-ups
  • Peak-time load balancing? (Score = distance + w x open orders; a new selector class.)
SQL or NoSQL for each part?

Stock and orders: SQL, because the invariant needs atomic conditional updates, check constraints and transactions across order and stock rows. Catalog: SQL or a document store plus a search index. Holds: DB rows with an expiry index, or Redis with TTL for speed (with a DB record as the source of truth). Order events: a log/queue (Kafka) for notifications and analytics.

How to answer

Tie each choice to a property (atomicity, query shape, TTL, fan-out), as the Zepto 2024-09 interviewer asked per component.

Likely follow-ups
  • Why a message queue at all? (Decouple order placement from notifications, picking and analytics; absorb peaks; retry failed consumers.)
Your sweeper thread and a confirm race. Prove nobody releases stock that was just sold.

Both paths begin with order.moveTo() under the order's lock: confirm wants CREATED to CONFIRMED, expiry wants CREATED to EXPIRED. Only one can see CREATED. The loser's transition throws (expiry catches it and skips). Inventory is touched only after a successful transition, so release and commit can never both run for one order.

How to answer

"The state transition is the lock-protected decision; inventory effects follow the winner."

Likely follow-ups
  • Lock ordering between order lock and SKU locks? (Always order first, then SKUs; nothing takes them the other way.)
Support cash on delivery and wallets without touching createOrder.

Introduce PaymentMethod with start(order). Card and wallet start a gateway session and wait for the webhook. COD confirms immediately (commit at once, no hold) and marks the order "collect cash". createOrder only calls method.start(order).

How to answer

Strategy again; note COD's different inventory timing.

Likely follow-ups
  • COD refused at the door? (Return flow: units come back to the store's onHand after inspection.)
The server restarts. What happens to in-memory holds?

They are lost, and so are the reserved counts, which is actually safe for overselling (stock looks more available) but wrong for orders already promised. So in production holds are persisted (order rows in CREATED with expires_at, or Redis keys with TTL), and on startup the reserved counter is rebuilt from active holds, or kept in the DB in the first place.

How to answer

Say what breaks, then where the truth lives.

Likely follow-ups
  • Reconciliation with the warehouse system? (Periodic count sync; physical counts adjust onHand, never reserved.)
Why is stock not a field on Product?

Because a product exists once in the catalog but has a different quantity in every dark store. Stock depends on two keys, (store, SKU), so it is its own entity. Putting quantity on Product is the most common modelling mistake on this problem.

How to answer

"What does this field depend on?" If the answer is two things, it belongs to an association class.

Likely follow-ups
  • Where does price live? (Catalog, possibly overridden per city or store.)
