The story: the corner shop with a waiting shelf

Explain like I'm 5

A tiny shop has five milk cartons. You phone and say "keep two for me, I am coming to pay". The shopkeeper moves two cartons to a waiting shelf with a sticky note: your name and "5 minutes". Now only three cartons are for sale, even though five are in the shop.

Three things can happen. You arrive and pay: the cartons leave the shop. You call and say "never mind": the cartons go back on the sale shelf. Or you never come: when the 5 minutes are up, the shopkeeper peels off the note and puts them back. The waiting shelf is what stops the shop from promising the same carton to two people.

A quick-commerce company has hundreds of these shops (called dark stores: small warehouses that only serve online orders). When you order, it picks the nearest shop that has everything in your basket.

From your world

You have met reserve-then-commit in the kernel. ext4's delayed allocation reserves blocks when write() returns (so a later ENOSPC cannot surprise the application) and allocates real blocks only at writeback; a truncate before writeback releases the reservation. Memory overcommit accounting (vm_committed_as) charges at mmap, long before pages exist. The timeout on a hold is a conntrack entry: it has an expiry, a garbage-collection worker sweeps stale ones, and a late packet must check the entry is still alive. And the deadlock-free way to lock a basket of SKUs is the rule lockdep enforces: every path takes locks in one global order (as lock_rename() does for two directories).
