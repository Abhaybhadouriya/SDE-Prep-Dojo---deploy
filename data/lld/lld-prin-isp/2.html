The four costs of a fat interface

Throwing exceptions is only the cost you can see. There are three more, and interviewers like candidates who can name them.

  1. Lying types. Callers cannot trust the interface. They start writing if (machine instanceof OfficeMachine) or wrapping calls in try/catch "just in case". That is a sign the abstraction has failed.
  2. Change ripple. If the fax team changes fax(doc, number) to fax(doc, number, coverPage), every class that implements Machine must change, and in a compiled language every client of Machine must rebuild, even the print queue that never sends a fax.
  3. Hard tests. To unit-test a print queue you must build a fake Machine that stubs scan and fax too. With a 20-method interface, test doubles turn into walls of empty methods.
  4. Hidden coupling. Two clients that have nothing to do with each other (print queue, fax gateway) are now tied together through one shared type. Change for one of them is a risk for the other.
  1. Left: three clients all depend on one fat Machine. Right: each client depends on one small role interface.
  2. The fax team adds a cover-page parameter to fax().
  3. With the fat interface, every client of Machine sees a changed type: rebuild, retest, redeploy - even the print queue.
  4. With role interfaces, only FaxGateway depends on Fax, so only it is affected.
  5. This is the real reason ISP exists: limit the blast radius of change, the same goal as SRP but seen from the caller's side.
