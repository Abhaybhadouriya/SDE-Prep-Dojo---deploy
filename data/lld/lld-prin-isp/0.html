The story: the TV remote with 80 buttons

Explain like I'm 5

Grandma wants to watch the news. Her TV remote has 80 buttons: input source, picture mode, sleep timer, Bluetooth pairing, three "smart" buttons. She only needs four: power, volume up, volume down, channel. Every time she presses the wrong button the TV goes into a strange menu and she has to call you.

Now imagine a second, tiny remote with only those four buttons. Grandma is happy. The big remote still exists for you. Both remotes talk to the same TV.

That is the whole idea: give each person a remote with only the buttons they use. In code, the "remote" is an interface, the "person" is the class that calls it, and the "TV" is the class that does the work.

The Interface Segregation Principle is the "I" in SOLID. Robert C. Martin wrote it down in the 1990s after working on a printing system at Xerox, where one huge Job class was used by every part of the system, so a tiny change forced everything to rebuild. His one-line rule:

Clients should not be forced to depend on methods they do not use.

The important word is client. A client is any piece of code that calls an interface. ISP is not about how many methods a class has. It is about how many methods each caller is made to see.
