Identify entities: nouns, verbs and what changes

Underline nouns (candidate classes) and verbs (candidate methods), then mark what will change. Click through.

  1. Nouns: Listing (one home), User (owner or seeker), SearchQuery (filters, sort and page in one object), the indexes, and the Shortlist.
  2. Verbs: listing and marking sold go through the platform; searching is the engine's job; filtering is a Specification's job; sorting is a comparator's job.
  3. What changes: interviewers add filters ("pets allowed"), sort orders ("newest"), smarter matching (prefix) and promoted results. Each gets its own seam.
  4. Not a class: a price range is two numbers; BHK is an int; sold is a status; units are parsed into sq ft and rupees at the edge and never stored.

The key modelling choice is to separate candidates from filters. Indexes answer one question fast ("which ids mention pool?", "which ids are in Bellandur?"). Everything else (price, size, BHK, status) is checked on that short list with plain predicates. You do not build a sorted index for every field on day one; you build the one or two that cut the list the most.

Why is Listing immutable? An edit builds a new object (withPrice, withStatus) and the engine swaps it in under the write lock. A search that already holds the old object keeps a consistent snapshot of it, and the old object's fields tell the engine exactly which index entries to remove.
