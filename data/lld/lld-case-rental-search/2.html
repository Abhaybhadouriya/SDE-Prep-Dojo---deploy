Clarify requirements (first 5-10 minutes)

Search problems are vague on purpose. The interviewer wants to see you turn "search should be efficient" into decisions.

Functional requirements

  1. Users register, log in and log out (the name is the id; no passwords). Only logged-in users list, shortlist or mark sold.
  2. List a property: title, description, locality, optional nearby localities, rent or sell, price, size, BHK.
  3. Search with any mix of: locality (one or more, case-insensitive), price range, size range, type, BHK set, and free text (all words must match). Only available listings appear.
  4. Sort by relevance, price or size ascending, or newest; paginate with a page number and page size.
  5. Shortlist a listing; the shortlist shows sold listings too, marked SOLD. Owners see their own listings, sold ones included.
  6. Mark sold and change price (owner only); the change is visible to the next search.
  7. Remove a listing (or document) so it is gone from every index.
  8. Bonus: sizes in sq ft / sq m / sq yd, prices like 40k / 60L / 2Cr, nearby localities ranked last, search-as-you-type on the last word.

Non-functional requirements

  • Fast reads: search must not scan every listing when a text or location filter is given.
  • Correct updates: an edit or delete is never half-visible (in the text index but not in the store).
  • Extensible: a new filter or sort order without touching search().
  • Many readers, few writers: searches run in parallel; edits are rare.

Out of scope (say it out loud)

  • Persistence, typo tolerance (fuzzy match), synonyms, geo distance in km, images, payments and the HLD of a sharded search cluster. Each gets a line in Extensibility.
