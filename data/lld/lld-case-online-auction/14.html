Interview follow-ups (Q&A)

Why does Auction keep both "live bids" and "participants"?

They answer different questions. The winner is chosen from live bids, so a withdrawal removes the buyer from live. The seller's share depends on everyone who paid the participation cost, and that money is not refunded, so a withdrawal does not remove the buyer from participants. Sample 2 (profit 0.40 after a withdrawal) only works with two collections.

How to answer

Point at the sample that proves it. Graders like answers tied to the given test cases.

Likely follow-ups
  • What if the cost is refunded on withdraw? (Remove from participants too; the formula is unchanged.)
Why not use double for the profit?

Because 0.2 is not exact in binary floating point, so 17 + 0.6 - 30 can print as -12.399999999999999 and comparisons in tests become flaky. Keep money in the smallest unit as a long (paise): 1700 + 3 x 1 x 20 - 3000 = -1240, and format to "-12.40" only when printing. BigDecimal also works and is the Java answer for arbitrary rates.

How to answer

Name the bug, then the fix and where formatting happens.

Likely follow-ups
  • What if commission is 17.5%? (Paise still works with a rounding rule; or BigDecimal with HALF_EVEN.)
The interviewer says: "now make it a normal highest-bid auction". What changes?

One new class, HighestBidRules, that returns the maximum amount and breaks a tie by the smaller sequence number. Auction, the house, the driver and the existing tests are untouched, because the winner rule is behind AuctionRules. I would show it by creating one auction of each type in main.

How to answer

This is the Open/Closed principle in one sentence. Link the follow-up to the seam you built at minute 15.

Likely follow-ups
  • Second-price (Vickrey) auction? (Winner is the highest bidder, price is the second-highest bid: the rule returns both.)
Is this Mediator or Observer?

Both, for different jobs. The house is a Mediator: buyers, sellers and auctions do not reference each other; every request goes to the house, which validates and routes it. Listeners are Observers: after something happens, the house broadcasts an event to whoever subscribed. Observer is one-to-many notification; Mediator is many-to-many coordination through a hub.

How to answer

Give one line per pattern and point to the class that plays each role.

Likely follow-ups
  • Does the house become a God class? (It only validates and routes; rules, data and locking live elsewhere.)
How do you implement "preferred buyer" without the rules knowing about users?

The house passes a Predicate<String> into pickWinner: name -> buyers.get(name).isPreferred(). The rule only asks "is this buyer preferred?". The counter of past auctions lives on Buyer and moves when an auction closes, for every participant. The current auction is not counted, because the count is taken before it is updated.

How to answer

Interface segregation in practice: pass a small function (one question, "is this buyer preferred?"), not the whole registry, so the rules depend on nothing they do not use. The dependency inversion version of the same idea: AuctionHouse defines the AuctionRules and AuctionListener interfaces it needs, and concrete rules and notifiers depend on those, not the reverse.

Likely follow-ups
  • Two auctions close at the same moment. Does the count race? (The counter is atomic; ordering between two closes is not defined by the statement, so say so.)
Two buyers bid 160 at the same time on a 150 lead. What happens?

Both requests reach Auction.bid(). The first takes the lock, sees 150, accepts 160 and releases. The second then takes the lock, sees 160, and its 160 fails the "best + increment" check, so it is rejected and the client can retry with 170. Read-best, validate and store are one critical section; that is what prevents two leaders or a lost update. The lock is per auction, so other auctions are not slowed down.

How to answer

Name the check-then-act race, then show the critical section. Mention the test that proves it.

Likely follow-ups
  • Could you do it lock-free? (An AtomicReference<Bid> with compare-and-set for the leader works for English auctions; the sealed-bid map still needs the lock or a concurrent map.)
How do auctions close on time, and what if the timer is late?

The house schedules close(id) on a ScheduledExecutorService at creation. Two safety nets: every bid checks the injected clock against endsAt, so a late timer cannot let a late bid in; and close() is idempotent, so a manual close and the timer can race safely. Tests use a manual clock, so time is a parameter, not a sleep.

How to answer

"The timer is an optimisation for closing; the clock check is what enforces the deadline."

Likely follow-ups
  • A million auctions with end times? (One timer wheel or a delay queue ordered by end time, not a thread per auction.)
Add auto-bidding: each buyer sets a secret maximum. Keep it efficient.

Store each buyer's maximum. The visible price is always min(highest maximum, second-highest maximum + increment), and the leader is the buyer with the highest maximum (earliest on a tie). So a new maximum only needs the top two values, kept in two fields or a small heap: O(log n) or O(1) per bid. Simulating bid-counter-bid in a loop is the slow answer the question warns about.

How to answer

Derive the price formula first, then the data structure.

Likely follow-ups
  • The buyer is online and bids by hand. (The statement in LC 1309282 disables the proxy while the buyer is online: a flag on the proxy.)
Stop "sniping" (bids in the last second).

Soft close: if a bid arrives within the last N minutes, move endsAt to now + N. Inside the locked bid() step, update endsAt; the timer then either reschedules or, simpler, re-checks endsAt when it fires and sleeps again if it moved. The deadline check in ensureOpen() already uses endsAt, so nothing else changes.

How to answer

Show that the existing "clock check on every bid" makes this small.

Likely follow-ups
  • Cap the extensions? (Count them in the auction; the rule is a constant or part of the auction type.)
Why are listeners called after the lock is released?

A listener is foreign code: it may send an email, write to a disk, or call back into the house. Under the auction lock it would make every other bidder wait, and a call back into the same auction from another thread could deadlock. So Auction.bid() returns an outcome, the lock is released, and the house notifies. The cost: a listener may see events slightly after a later bid; if order matters, events carry the sequence number.

How to answer

"Never call out of a critical section." Then name the trade-off.

Likely follow-ups
  • A listener throws. (Catch per listener and log, so one bad listener cannot break the others or the bid.)
How does this LLD change at eBay scale?

The per-auction lock becomes a single writer per auction: route every bid for an auction id to one partition or actor, which validates and appends to a bid log. Reads (current price, bid history) come from a cache updated by that writer. Notifications go through a queue to a fan-out service. The end-of-auction timer becomes a delay queue. The rule classes stay the same; they just run inside the writer. Details are in HLD: Online Auction.

How to answer

Map each LLD piece to its HLD counterpart instead of starting over.

Likely follow-ups
  • The writer for a hot auction crashes. (Replay its bid log from the last checkpoint; bids are idempotent by (buyer, seq).)
