Identify entities: nouns, verbs and what changes

Underline the nouns (candidate classes) and the verbs (candidate methods). Then ask which parts the interviewer will change; those get an interface. Finally cross out nouns that are only numbers. Click through.

  1. Nouns: the platform (AuctionHouse), one Auction with its limits and cost, the two roles Buyer and Seller, a Bid, and the outcome (AuctionResult).
  2. Verbs: each lands on the class that owns the data. Creating an auction is the house's job; bidding and withdrawing change one auction; picking a winner is a rule; profit is computed once, at close.
  3. What changes: the winner rule (sealed highest-unique, open ascending, lowest unique) becomes a Strategy; who gets told becomes listeners; when it closes becomes a timer plus an injected Clock.
  4. Not a class: the participation cost and the commission are numbers; the timer is a scheduled task; the status is an enum.

The key modelling choice is two collections inside Auction: live (buyer to current bid; withdraw removes from it) and participants (everyone who paid; withdraw does not remove). The two sample test cases only agree with each other if you keep them apart: sample 1 counts 3 bidders, sample 2 counts a bidder who withdrew. A single "bids" list cannot answer both questions.

Should Bid be mutable so "update" can change its amount? No. An update replaces the buyer's entry with a new immutable Bid with a new sequence number. Listeners and the history log can then keep old bids safely, and "who bid first" is just the smaller seq.
