The story: the auction hall

Explain like I'm 5

Imagine a school fair with a big hall. A child brings a toy to sell. A grown-up at the front desk writes the toy on the board: "bids between 10 and 50 coins". Other children walk up to the desk, never to each other, and whisper a number. The desk writes it down. A child can change their number, or take it back, until the bell rings.

When the bell rings, the desk looks at all the numbers and says who won, using the rule of the day. On some days the biggest number wins. On other days the rule is funny: the biggest number that only one child said. The desk then shouts the winner to the whole hall, and tells the seller how many coins they made.

From your world

AuctionRules is an ops table. In the kernel, struct file_operations lets the VFS call ->read without knowing which driver is behind it; here Auction calls rules.validate() and rules.pickWinner() without knowing whether it runs a sealed-bid or an open ascending auction. The listener list is a notifier chain: blocking_notifier_call_chain() walks registered callbacks without knowing who they are, exactly like the house calling every AuctionListener after a bid.

Interfaces in C terms: every interface on this page is, in C, a struct of function pointers; each implementation fills one in, like file_operations. Java and C++ build that table (the vtable) for you, and every object carries a pointer to it. More in OOP from C structs and polymorphism and vtables.
