Core flows: picking the winner, and one bid end to end

First, the rule that makes this problem different: highest unique bid, using the example from the statement. Click through.

  1. Six live bids at close. Withdrawn bids are already gone from the live map.
  2. Group by amount and walk from the highest amount down (a TreeMap in descending order).
  3. 100 was bid by C and F: not unique, skip.
  4. 90 was bid by B and D: not unique, skip.
  5. 70 was bid by E alone: E wins. We stop at the first unique amount.
  6. The bonus rule plugs into the same loop: a tied amount with exactly one preferred buyer goes to that buyer.

Now one bid in an open ascending auction, where order matters: quinn raises to 160 while rae leads at 150.

  1. The buyer (console line, test, or REST call) asks the house: updateBid("quinn", "E1", 160).
  2. The house checks that quinn is a registered buyer. Requests from unknown names stop here.
  3. The house looks up E1 by id and calls bid(quinn, 160, update). The buyer never touches the auction object.
  4. The auction takes its own lock, then checks it is OPEN and that the clock is before endsAt.
  5. Under the same lock it asks the rules for the current best (rae at 150) and validates 160 against the floor 150 + 10.
  6. It stores a new immutable Bid with sequence number 3, records quinn as a participant, releases the lock and returns "rae was outbid".
  7. Only now, outside the lock, the house notifies listeners: onBid and onOutbid(rae, 160).
  8. The caller gets the stored bid back.

The two classic bugs: removing a withdrawn buyer from the bidder count (sample 2 then prints 0.00 instead of 0.40), and computing profit with double (17 + 0.6 - 30 printed as -12.399999999999999). Both show up the moment you run the samples, which is why Main asserts them.
