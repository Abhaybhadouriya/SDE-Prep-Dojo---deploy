The story: learning to cook a whole dinner

Explain like I'm 5

Imagine you want to cook a full dinner for your family, all by yourself, in one hour. You cannot start with the dinner. First you learn to hold a knife and to chop an onion. Then you learn a few recipes: rice, dal, a salad. Then you learn to do two things at once, like stirring the dal while the rice boils, without burning anything. Only then do you try the full dinner with a clock on the wall. The first time it is messy and late. The fourth time it is on the table in 55 minutes, and you know exactly which step slows you down.

Low-level design (LLD) prep works the same way. The knife skills are OOP and design principles: classes, interfaces, composition, SOLID. The recipes are design patterns such as Strategy, Observer and State. Doing two things at once is concurrency: locks, thread-safe classes, no double booking. The full dinner with a clock is the interview round itself: a problem like "design a parking lot", solved and explained in 45 to 120 minutes. This plan gives one week to each stage.

From your world

You already think in low-level design when you write a driver: you pick a struct layout, fill a const struct file_operations table, decide who owns a buffer and who frees it, and take a spinlock around shared state. LLD interviews ask for the same decisions, only in Java or C++ classes instead of structs and function pointers. So week 1 is mostly renaming things you know; weeks 2 and 3 are where new material lives. Start with From C Structs to Objects, written for exactly this jump.
