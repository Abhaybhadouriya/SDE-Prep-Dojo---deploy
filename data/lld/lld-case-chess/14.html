Where does move validation live: in Piece, Board or Game?

Split by the data each rule needs. "How does a knight move" needs only the piece and the board's occupancy, so it lives in the Knight. "Is this square attacked" needs every piece, so it lives in Board. "Whose turn, does this leave my king in check, is the game over" needs the whole game, so it lives in Game. That keeps each class small and each rule in one place.

How do you check that a move does not leave the king in check?

Execute the candidate move on the board, ask board.isAttacked(kingOf(mover), opponent), then undo. Because Move is a Command that saves what it overwrote, this works for captures, castling and en passant with no special cases. It is O(pieces x moves) per check, which is fine for a game (an engine would use incremental attack maps or bitboards).

How to answer

The execute-test-undo trick and why Command makes it exact.

Inheritance or Strategy for piece movement?

Inheritance, because a piece's rule is fixed for its lifetime and each subclass is tiny. Promotion does not change a pawn's rule; it replaces the pawn with a new Queen object. Strategy (or composition of movement components) wins when rules are configured at runtime, e.g. fairy-chess pieces built from parts: "moves like a rook plus a knight". Then a piece holds a list of MovementRule objects.

How to answer

How do you implement undo, and what must a Move remember?

Each Move stores its from and to, kind and promotion, and during execute records what it changed: the captured piece and the square it was on (different for en passant), the previous en-passant square, the rook squares for castling, and it bumps the moved pieces' move counters. Undo reverses each in reverse order. The Game keeps a stack of executed moves.

How to answer

How do you know your move generator is correct?

Perft: count all legal move sequences to a fixed depth from known positions and compare with published numbers (20, 400, 8,902, 197,281 from the start). A mismatch tells you a rule is wrong; splitting the count by first move finds which one. Add unit tests for each special rule and each terminal state.

Checkmate vs stalemate: how do you tell them apart?

After each move, compute two booleans for the side to move: in check, and has at least one legal move. Has a move: ACTIVE or CHECK. No move and in check: CHECKMATE (the other side wins). No move and not in check: STALEMATE (draw).

How to answer

The 2x2 table; status derived, never set by hand.

How do you add a computer opponent without changing Game?

Why is Position an immutable record?

Positions are compared (t.equals(enPassantTarget)), used in lists and maps, and shared between moves. Mutable positions would let one move's "to" silently change another's. A record gives value equality, hashing and immutability for free.

How to answer

Value semantics, sharing safety, equality.
