Read the table for what it repeats. Zepto (both reports) wants requirements, classes, schema and APIs in one hour, and the patterns the SDE-3 report lists are Decorator and Observer, followed by locks (the report does not say where they were used; price-rule stacking and notifications are the natural places). Expedia's twist is price breakdown and the "available on every night" rule. Wayfair's is several providers, which is an Adapter around each supplier. Every follow-up below comes from one of those threads.

Say this in the first minute: "I will keep inventory as counts per room type per night, sell a stay only if every night has a room, hold it for a few minutes while payment runs, and keep pricing and cancellation behind interfaces because each hotel has its own rules."
