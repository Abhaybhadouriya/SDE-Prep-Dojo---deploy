Interview follow-ups

How do you check whether a hotel can sell a room for a stay?

Keep a count of reserved rooms per room type per night. For a stay [check-in, check-out), the free count is the total for that type minus the largest reserved count across the nights in the range. If that minimum is at least the rooms asked for, the stay fits. Check-out day is not a night of the stay, so back-to-back bookings share it.

How to answer

Say "minimum over the nights" and "half-open range" explicitly, then give the cost: O(nights) per hotel per search, which is tiny because stays are short.

Likely follow-ups
  • How would you make search fast across 10,000 hotels? (Precompute per-night free counts, filter by city first, cache search results for a few seconds; bookings still re-check under the lock.)
  • Expedia's twist: only hotels with continuous availability. (That is the same minimum-over-nights rule.)
Two guests try to book the last room at the same time. Walk me through your locking.

The check and the take happen in one critical section on that hotel's lock: compute free over all nights, compare, increment every night's count, create the booking, unlock. The second guest waits for the lock, then sees zero free and gets an error. One lock per hotel means bookings in different hotels never wait for each other, and since a booking touches one hotel only, there is no lock ordering to get wrong.

How to answer

Name the race (check-then-act over several nights), the lock granularity and why, then mention the DB version (guarded UPDATE ... WHERE reserved + n <= total, or SELECT ... FOR UPDATE ORDER BY night).

Likely follow-ups
  • Why not a lock per night? (Several locks per stay, ordering needed, and all-or-nothing rollback.)
  • What if two app servers run this code? (The lock must move to the database or a distributed lock; the DB constraint is the final guard.)
Why hold rooms before payment instead of booking after the payment succeeds?

Payment takes seconds to minutes. If we only take inventory after payment, two guests can both pay for the last room and one must be refunded with an apology. A short hold takes the room first, so the guest who is paying knows the room is theirs, and the hold expires if they give up. The price is that held rooms are briefly unavailable to others, so the hold must be short (we use 15 minutes) and must expire reliably.

How to answer

Give the failure without holds, the TTL trade-off, and how expiry works without depending on a timer (lazy checks on every read and on confirm).

Likely follow-ups
  • The payment succeeds one second after the hold expired. What happens? (Confirm fails, the booking is EXPIRED, the caller refunds the payment; or extend the hold when the guest enters the payment page.)
Pricing differs on weekends and for long stays, and hotels combine rules. How do you model it?

A PricingStrategy interface with a base-rate implementation, and decorators that each add one rule and call the strategy they wrap: WeekendSurcharge adds a percentage for each Friday and Saturday night; LongStayDiscount takes a percentage off stays of 7 nights or more. A hotel's pricing is a chain built at configuration time. A new rule is one new class; combining rules needs no new code.

How to answer

Show the one-line chain, explain why order matters, and contrast with the class-explosion alternative.

Likely follow-ups
  • Show a breakdown (base, surcharge, tax). (Return a quote object with lines; each decorator appends its line.)
  • Prices change after the guest booked. (The price is stored on the booking at hold time; later rule changes do not touch it.)
Where does Observer fit, and what must a listener never do?

Every status change (held, confirmed, cancelled, expired) is published to registered BookingListeners: email, SMS, analytics, the hotel's channel manager. The service does not know which exist. Listeners are called after the hotel lock is released, because a slow listener inside the lock would stall every booking in that hotel. A listener must never call back into the service in a way that needs the same lock while we hold it, and in production it should only enqueue work.

How to answer

Name the events, the decoupling benefit, and the "outside the lock" rule with its reason.

Likely follow-ups
  • What if the email service is down? (Queue with retries; a booking must not fail because an email failed.)
Model the cancellation rules. Different hotels refund differently.

A CancellationPolicy strategy per hotel, called by cancel() with the booking and the current time. FlexibleCancellation(48) refunds everything until 48 hours before check-in (14:00) and keeps the first night after that; NonRefundable returns zero. A hold that was never paid refunds zero without asking the policy. The policy reads the booking but never changes it; the service does the state change and releases inventory.

How to answer

Show the interface, two implementations with numbers from the demo (8000 booked, 4000 refunded late), and who owns the state change.

Likely follow-ups
  • The policy changes after booking. (Snapshot the policy on the booking at confirm time.)
The payment gateway calls confirm twice for the same payment. How do you avoid a double booking or a double charge?

Make confirm idempotent per payment reference: if the booking is already CONFIRMED with the same reference, return it unchanged. A different reference on a confirmed booking is an error, not a second booking. Over HTTP, add an Idempotency-Key header and store the first response; in the database, a unique constraint on payment_ref makes the duplicate fail even across servers.

How to answer

Say why retries happen (timeouts, at-least-once callbacks), then the three layers: method, HTTP key, DB constraint.

Likely follow-ups
  • The first confirm timed out on the client but succeeded on the server. (The retry returns the stored result.)
Now the platform sells rooms from several hotel providers, each with its own API.

Put an InventoryProvider interface between the service and inventory: search(city, type, stay), hold(...), confirm(...), cancel(...). Our in-memory hotels become one implementation; each supplier gets an Adapter that translates its API, ids and errors into ours. Search fans out to providers in parallel with a timeout and merges results; hold goes to the provider that owns the hotel, and its hold expiry becomes ours.

How to answer

Name Adapter, the fan-out with timeouts, and the fact that consistency now lives at the provider (we cannot lock their inventory, only call their hold).

Likely follow-ups
  • A provider is slow or down. (Timeout and partial results; circuit breaker per provider.)
  • Two providers list the same hotel. (De-duplicate by a canonical hotel id.)
The guest must choose a specific room. What changes?

Only the inventory model. Instead of counts per type, each room keeps its booked ranges in a sorted map from check-in to check-out. A new range [s, e) fits if the booking starting at or before s ends at or before s, and the next booking starts at or after e: two O(log n) lookups (floorEntry, ceilingEntry). "Any double room" then means trying the double rooms in order. The rest of the service is unchanged because it only calls free, take and release.

How to answer

Reduce to interval overlap, give the O(log n) check, and show the change is contained in one class.

Likely follow-ups
  • Uber's conference-room variant: return any free room for a time range. (Same structure; pick the first room whose map has no overlap.)
Why is a hold not its own class?

A hold has the same data as a booking (guest, hotel, type, nights, price) and turns into a booking when paid. Two classes would mean copying data on confirm and keeping two maps in sync. One Booking with a status field and a table of legal moves is simpler, and makes "confirm after expiry" impossible by construction.

How to answer

"Same data, different stage of life: that is a state, not a type."

Likely follow-ups
  • When would you use the full State pattern? (When each state has different behaviour, for example different pricing or emails per state.)
Why store money as paise in a long and not as a double?

Binary floating point cannot represent most decimal fractions exactly, so sums of prices drift by fractions of a paisa and refunds stop matching charges. Integer minor units are exact; percentages are applied with integer arithmetic and one rounding rule. In Java, BigDecimal with an explicit rounding mode is the other correct choice.

How to answer

One sentence on the float problem, one on the fix, and state the rounding rule you chose.

Likely follow-ups
  • Multiple currencies? (Store amount plus currency code; convert at the edge with a dated rate.)
