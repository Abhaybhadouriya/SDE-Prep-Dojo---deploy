Identify the entities

Underline the nouns (candidate classes) and the verbs (candidate methods) in the agreed requirements, then throw out actors, outside systems and plain values. Click through.

  1. Underline every noun in the requirements. Each is a candidate class.
  2. Underline every verb. A verb goes to the class that owns the data it needs: free() needs the per-night counts, so it lives in Inventory.
  3. Prune. The guest and the payment gateway call the system from outside. "Hold" is not a separate object: it is a booking that has not been paid yet, so it is a state. Things that differ per hotel become interfaces.
  4. The final list: 7 classes, 2 enums, 4 interfaces. No Guest, Payment, Invoice or Receptionist class unless the interviewer asks.
EntityKindResponsibility (one line)
BookingServiceService / facadesearch, hold, confirm, cancel, expireHolds. Owns the hotels and bookings maps.
HotelEntity (aggregate root)Rooms, rates, inventory, its pricing and cancellation rules, and the lock that guards its inventory.
InventoryEntity, owned by HotelCounts per (room type, night): free, take, release.
RoomEntityNumber and type. Assigned at check-in, so it carries no booking state.
BookingEntity with a state machineWho, where, which type, how many, which nights, price, hold expiry, status, payment reference.
DateRangeValue objectHalf-open [checkIn, checkOut); validates itself; lists its nights.
OfferValue objectOne search result: hotel, type, free count, price.
PricingStrategy + decoratorsInterfacePrices a stay; rules stack.
CancellationPolicyInterfaceComputes the refund for a cancelled confirmed booking.
BookingListenerInterfaceHears every status change (notifications, analytics).
TimeSourceInterface"What time is it?", faked in tests.

Why counts per type and not a calendar per room? Hotels sell "a double room", and the front desk assigns room 102 at check-in. Counting per type lets the hotel move guests between rooms (for maintenance, upgrades) without touching bookings, and the availability check is one subtraction per night. If the interviewer says "guests pick a room on a floor plan", switch to a calendar per room; we show that change in extensibility.
