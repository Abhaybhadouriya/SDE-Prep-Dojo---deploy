The story: three ways to school

Explain like I'm 5

Every morning you need to get to school. On a sunny day you walk. When it rains, Dad drives you. When Dad is busy, you take the school bus. The job is always the same: "get me to school". Only the way changes.

You do not become a different child on rainy days. You stay the same you, and you just pick a different way to travel. If a new way appears (a bicycle for your birthday), nothing about you changes. You simply have one more way to choose from.

That is the Strategy pattern. One object (you) needs a job done. Several interchangeable ways to do it (walk, car, bus) each live in their own small object with the same shape. The object holds one of them and can swap it at any time. Each "way" is called a strategy; the object that uses it is called the context.

From your world

You have used Strategy in C for years. Every TCP socket in Linux points at a struct tcp_congestion_ops: a table of function pointers (ssthresh, cong_avoid, undo_cwnd, ...) with a name like "cubic" or "bbr". The TCP core never asks "which algorithm is this?"; it just calls icsk->icsk_ca_ops->cong_avoid(sk, ack, acked). A module registers a new algorithm with tcp_register_congestion_control(), and one socket can pick it with setsockopt(fd, IPPROTO_TCP, TCP_CONGESTION, "bbr", 3). The ops table is the strategy interface, cubic and reno are concrete strategies, and the socket is the context.
