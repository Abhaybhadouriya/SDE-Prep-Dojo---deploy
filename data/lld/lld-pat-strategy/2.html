Intent

Strategy (GoF behavioral pattern): when one job can be done several ways, write each way as a separate class that implements one shared interface. The object that needs the job keeps a reference to that interface, and whoever builds it decides which implementation goes in. Swapping the choice, even at runtime, never touches the object that uses it.

In plain words: find the part that varies, give it an interface, and inject it. The context keeps the part that stays the same (when to charge, what to print, who to notify); the strategy owns the part that changes (how much to charge). It is the most direct use of "program to an interface, not an implementation" and of "favour composition over inheritance".
