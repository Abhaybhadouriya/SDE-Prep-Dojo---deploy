Clarify requirements (first 5-10 minutes)

Never start coding from the one-line prompt. Say the rules back to the interviewer and write them down as a list. These are the classic rules plus what to ask.

Functional requirements

  1. Board 100 cells numbered 1..100 (configurable size is a common follow-up).
  2. Snakes and ladders given as input (start, end). Landing on a snake head moves you to its tail; landing on a ladder bottom moves you to its top. The end of one may be the start of another, so follow the chain.
  3. Players two or more, each with a name, all starting at 0 (off the board).
  4. Turns round robin. Roll one six-sided die and move forward by the value.
  5. Exact finish a roll that would go past 100 is not applied; the player stays.
  6. Winning the first player to land exactly on 100 wins and the game ends.
  7. Output print every move as "name rolled a X and moved from A to B", and "name wins the game".

Non-functional requirements

  • Extensible: new dice, new rules and new outputs without editing the game loop (this is what you are graded on).
  • Testable: dice can be scripted so a test knows every move in advance.
  • Correct input: reject boards that are impossible (two jumps on one cell, a snake on 100, a loop).
  • Single process, in memory, no UI, no database.

Out of scope (say it out loud)

  • Graphics, networking, saving a game to disk, user accounts. Mention that an online multiplayer version is an HLD question, not this round.
