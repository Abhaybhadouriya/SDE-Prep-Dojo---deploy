The story: a board game with a referee

Explain like I'm 5

You and your friends sit around a board with 100 squares. Everyone starts off the board. When it is your turn you throw the die and walk forward that many squares. If you stop at the bottom of a ladder you climb up. If you stop on a snake's head you slide down to its tail. The first one to stop exactly on square 100 wins.

Now imagine a referee who sits at the table. The referee keeps the queue of who plays next, hands the die to that child, checks the board for snakes and ladders, moves the piece and shouts out what happened. The children never argue about rules because only the referee applies them.

In code, the referee is the Game class. The board, the die and each player are small objects that do one job each. The whole design question is: who owns which rule? If you get that right, every follow-up the interviewer adds ("now use two dice", "a six gives another roll") is a small new class or one new flag, not a rewrite.

From your world

The game loop is a round-robin scheduler. turnOrder is a run queue: take the task at the head, give it one time slice (one turn), and put it back at the tail unless it has exited (won). A six that grants another roll is like a task that keeps the CPU for one more slice. The dice are a struct of one function pointer, int (*roll)(void *ctx); the game calls it without caring whether it is a real random source or a scripted one, just like a driver calls ops->read().
