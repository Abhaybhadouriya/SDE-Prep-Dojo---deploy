Identify entities: nouns, verbs and the rules that change

Underline the nouns in the problem statement: they are candidate classes. Underline the verbs: they are candidate methods. Then look for the sentences that start with "optionally" or "the interviewer may ask": those are the parts that vary, and they deserve an interface or a rule object. Click through the stepper.

  1. Nouns become classes: Game (the referee), Player, Board, Snake/Ladder, Dice.
  2. Verbs become methods, and each method lives on the class that owns the data it needs: rolling on Dice, moving on Player, sliding on Board, taking turns on Game.
  3. Rules that the interviewer will change become a Rules object or a new implementation of an interface, never an if/else inside the loop.
  4. Not every noun is a class. A cell is just an int. A snake and a ladder have the same data (start, end), so one Jump type covers both. A turn is a method call, not an object.

The most common over-design here is 100 Cell objects, each with an optional snake or ladder pointer, plus separate Snake and Ladder classes with identical fields. If the interviewer pushes for Snake and Ladder classes ("what if snakes later bite and cost you a life?"), say: "today they differ only in direction, so one value type; the day they get different behaviour, Jump becomes an interface with two implementations." That shows you know YAGNI ("you aren't gonna need it": do not build it until a requirement asks for it) and know how to grow the design.
