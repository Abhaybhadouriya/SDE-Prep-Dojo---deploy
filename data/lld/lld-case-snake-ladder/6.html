Core flow: one turn

Here is Bob's turn from the demo: he is on 4, rolls a 6, lands on the ladder at 10, which ends on the snake head at 29, so he comes to rest on 9.

  1. The driver (or a test) asks the game to play one turn.
  2. The game takes the player at the head of the queue. Whoever is at the head plays; no index arithmetic, no "current player" field to keep in sync.
  3. The game asks the dice strategy for a number. It does not know if the dice are random or scripted.
  4. The board reports the jump at the landing cell and resolves the full chain: 10 goes to 29, 29 goes to 9.
  5. The player's position is updated through one method, so there is exactly one place that changes position.
  6. Every listener hears the event. The console printer formats it; a test spy could record it.
  7. Bob did not win, and he did not roll a six that grants another roll, so he goes to the back of the queue.

With the follow-up rules switched on, the turn becomes a small loop. Notice that the rule checks sit in one method in one order, so you can explain the precedence: the three-sixes check comes first, then overshoot, then the move, then the extra roll.
