Structure: five participants

You know all five already: struct work_struct is the Command; INIT_WORK(&w, fn) is the Client building a ConcreteCommand; the device fn touches is the Receiver; the workqueue that later calls w->func(w) is the Invoker. Here are the same five with the book's names, on our text editor:

  • Command: the interface, usually just execute(), plus undo() if you support undo.
  • ConcreteCommand (InsertText, DeleteText): stores the receiver and the arguments. Its execute() calls the receiver. It also stores whatever undo() will need (DeleteText keeps the removed text).
  • Receiver (Document): the object that really knows how to do the work. It has no idea commands exist.
  • Invoker (Editor, a button, a queue, a scheduler): triggers commands and maybe keeps history. It never looks inside a command.
  • Client: creates concrete commands, connects them to receivers, and gives them to invokers.

From your world

Map it to C: Command is a struct with a function pointer; ConcreteCommand is that struct embedded in a bigger struct that carries the arguments; Receiver is the device or object the function touches; Invoker is the workqueue or the timer wheel that calls w->func(w). The "arguments live inside the request" idea is also exactly how struct bio works: the block layer carries the sectors, pages and a completion callback around as one object.
