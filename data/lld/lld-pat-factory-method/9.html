When to use it, and when not

Use it when
  • A base class or framework has a workflow that needs an object whose concrete type only its users know.
  • You want users of your library to extend it by subclassing, without editing or forking it.
  • Each variant differs in what it creates and maybe in one or two other hooks (formatting, validation).
  • You want to keep the workflow and its invariants (auditing, retries, locking) in one tested place.
Avoid it when
  • Only one product exists. The hierarchy is ceremony.
  • The choice comes from data at run time (a string in a request). A Simple Factory or registry fits better; you do not want to pick a subclass by switch just to get a product.
  • Every new product forces a new creator subclass that does nothing else (a "parallel hierarchy explosion"). Pass a function or use a registry instead.
  • Products must match as a family (button + checkbox + menu of one theme). That is Abstract Factory.

Trade-off in one line: Factory Method buys Open/Closed and Dependency Inversion (the workflow depends on Channel, not on SmsChannel) at the price of more classes and the coupling that comes with inheritance.
