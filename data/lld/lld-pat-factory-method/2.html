Intent in one line

Factory Method (GoF, creational): in our words, the base class declares a "make me a product" method and runs its workflow through it, and each subclass overrides that one method to choose the concrete product. The base class's own logic calls the factory method and works only with the product interface.

Two details make it Factory Method and not something else:

  1. The creator usually uses the product itself. Notifier.notifyUser() calls createChannel() and then calls send() on the result. The creator is not "a factory"; it is a class with real work that happens to need an object.
  2. The decision is made by inheritance. Which product you get depends on which creator subclass you instantiated, not on a key passed at call time.

Why "method"? Because the pattern is a single overridable method, not a separate factory object. That is the contrast with Abstract Factory, where creation is delegated to another object that can make a whole family.
