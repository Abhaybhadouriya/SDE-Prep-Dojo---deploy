The story: boxes inside boxes

Explain like I'm 5

You are moving house. Some things go straight into the truck: a lamp, a chair. Other things go into a box first. Some boxes hold smaller boxes, and one small box holds your marbles.

The truck driver asks one question: "How heavy is it?" For the lamp, you just weigh the lamp. For a box, you add up the weight of everything inside it, and if something inside is another box, you ask that box the same question. The driver never needs to know whether he is holding a lamp or a box of boxes. He just asks "how heavy?" and gets a number.

That is the Composite pattern. Single things (leaves) and groups of things (composites) share one interface. A composite answers by asking its children and combining their answers. The client asks the top of the tree once and the recursion does the rest.

From your world

The VFS dentry cache is a composite tree. Every struct dentry has a d_parent pointer and a list of children (d_subdirs in older kernels, d_children since 6.8), whether it names a directory or a regular file. Path walk, du, and rm -r all treat every entry the same way: "visit me, and if I have children, visit them". The inode behind each dentry carries an inode_operations table; a directory's table has lookup, mkdir and unlink, a regular file's table does not. That split, "common ops for every node, child ops only for containers", is the exact design choice this page calls safety vs transparency. The device tree (struct device_node with parent, child and sibling pointers) and the kobject / sysfs hierarchy are the same shape.
