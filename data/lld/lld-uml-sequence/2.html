Sync vs async: who waits?

The most important thing a sequence diagram shows is blocking. A synchronous message is a normal method call: the caller's activation bar keeps going while it waits, and nothing else happens on that lifeline until the reply comes. An asynchronous message is "fire and forget": the caller hands off the work (to a queue, a thread pool, a Kafka topic) and continues at once. The receiver runs at its own time, often in parallel.

Async is a design decision you should say out loud. "Sending the confirmation email is async, so a slow SMTP server never adds latency to the booking, and a failed email does not roll back a paid booking." That sentence, attached to one open arrowhead, shows you thought about latency and failure isolation.
