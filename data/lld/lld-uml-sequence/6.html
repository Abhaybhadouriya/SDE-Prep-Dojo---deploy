Drawing one fast on a whiteboard

  1. Pick one use case. "Book seats", not "the whole app". One diagram per important flow.
  2. List participants left to right in call order. Actor first, then the entry point (controller/API), the orchestrating service, then helpers, then external systems on the far right.
  3. Draw the happy path first. Only solid call arrows top to bottom. Skip obvious returns.
  4. Add one or two fragments that carry design weight: the conflict (alt), the retry (loop), the payment failure (break).
  5. Mark async arrows and say why they are async.
  6. Check against the class diagram. Every arrow needs a method on the receiver's class, and the sender needs a reference to the receiver (a field or a parameter). A missing method or reference here is a missing piece of your design.

Text-first tools. Mermaid (sequenceDiagram) and PlantUML (@startuml) render sequence diagrams from a few lines of text, so they live in design docs and pull requests. In a timed interview a hand sketch is faster; in a take-home, a Mermaid block in the README looks professional.
