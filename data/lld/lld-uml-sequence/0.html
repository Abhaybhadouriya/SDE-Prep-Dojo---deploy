The story: ordering at a restaurant

Explain like I'm 5

You sit at a table and tell the waiter, "One pizza, please." The waiter walks to the kitchen and tells the chef. The chef makes the pizza and hands it to the waiter. The waiter brings it to you.

Now draw it. Write You, Waiter and Chef across the top of a page. Under each name draw a long line going down: that is time passing. Every time someone talks to someone, draw an arrow from one line to the other. Arrows lower on the page happen later.

Sometimes the waiter also shouts "table 4 wants water!" to a helper and does not wait for an answer. That is a different kind of arrow: a message you send and forget.

That drawing is a sequence diagram. It answers three questions that a class diagram cannot: in what order things happen, who calls whom, and who is blocked waiting. In an LLD interview it is the second most useful picture after the class diagram (see Class Relationships), and you draw it for the one flow that is tricky: booking a seat, paying, matching a driver, retrying a failed call.
