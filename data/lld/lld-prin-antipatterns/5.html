Golden hammer and speculative generality: pattern-itis

The golden hammer (from the "law of the instrument": if all you have is a hammer, everything looks like a nail) is using a familiar tool for every problem. In LLD prep it has a special form: after learning 23 patterns, candidates fit as many as possible into every answer. Its cousin speculative generality adds abstractions for requirements nobody stated ("in case we need another database later").

PatternEarns its place when...Is a golden hammer when...
FactoryCreation logic varies by input, or callers must not know concrete classesThere is one product and new would do
StrategyA rule really varies (pricing, allocation, split type) or the interviewer hints it willOne implementation, no hint it will change
BuilderMany optional fields, or a validated immutable objectTwo required fields
ObserverSeveral independent reactions to one eventExactly one listener that is always the same
Interface per classA second implementation exists or a test double is neededFooService + FooServiceImpl for every class
Generic repositorySeveral entities really share storage behaviourA 12-method Repository<T, ID> for one map

Evidence from real rounds points the same way. The workat.tech machine-coding guide and HelloInterview's LLD guide both warn against forcing patterns; a Razorpay candidate who asked about future features so they could add interfaces was told to cover the basic requirements first (2025-06). Unfinished P0 features fail a machine-coding round; a missing Visitor never does.

Rule of three: write it plainly once, tolerate a copy twice, abstract on the third case. In an interview, "the second implementation" is often the interviewer's next follow-up - that is the moment to introduce the interface, and say so out loud: "now that there are two pricing rules, I'll extract a PricingPolicy".

From your world

Kernel maintainers apply the same rule: a new internal API or abstraction layer is not merged without an in-tree user, and "infrastructure for one driver" usually gets pushed back. Abstractions are paid for by their second and third users. See also DRY, KISS, YAGNI.
