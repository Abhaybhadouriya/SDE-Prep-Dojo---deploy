Remember this

  • An anti-pattern is a tempting, common solution that predictably costs more than it saves, and it has a known cure.
  • God object: many reasons to change in one class. Cure: split by responsibility, keep a thin coordinator, do not overshoot into ravioli.
  • Anemic model: entities with no behaviour. Cure: if an entity has a rule, the rule lives in the entity; services coordinate several entities.
  • Singleton abuse: getInstance() as a global. Cure: inject through constructors; main decides how many instances exist.
  • Golden hammer: patterns without requirements. Cure: YAGNI, rule of three, introduce a seam when the follow-up arrives.
  • Midlayer mistake: shared code imposed on everyone grows special cases. Cure: thin top layer plus a library of optional helpers (composition).
  • Working code is the entry ticket; modularity and extensibility are what the grader writes down.
