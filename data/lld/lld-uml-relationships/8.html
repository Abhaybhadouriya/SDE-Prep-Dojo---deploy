Worked example: an e-commerce checkout, all six lines

A typical Walmart-style prompt: "Design the classes for placing an order: customer, cart lines, payment by card or UPI, and a confirmation message by email or SMS." Step through how a strong candidate builds the diagram, one relationship at a time. The runnable Java is lld-code/lld-uml-relationships/Relationships.java.

  1. Customer and Order: a 1-to-many association. Both live on their own; an order keeps a reference to its customer for delivery and invoices.
  2. Order and OrderLine: composition. The order creates its lines; a line belongs to one order and is deleted with it.
  3. OrderLine and Product: a one-way association. The line points to the product it sells; the product does not know which lines mention it. (Copy the price into the line: the product's price may change tomorrow.)
  4. Category and Product: aggregation. A category groups products, but deleting the "Diwali sale" category must not delete the products.
  5. Payment with CardPayment and UpiPayment: generalization. Payment is abstract (italic): there is no "plain" payment.
  6. Notifier with EmailNotifier and SmsNotifier: realization. Any channel that keeps the contract can be plugged in (Strategy pattern).
  7. Checkout: dependencies on Payment, Notifier and Order. It holds no state; each call is given what it needs, which makes it easy to test with fakes.

Notice what the finished diagram tells the interviewer without a word: what is owned (lines), what is shared (products, categories), what varies (payment, channel) and what is stateless glue (checkout). That is the real reason to get the lines right.
