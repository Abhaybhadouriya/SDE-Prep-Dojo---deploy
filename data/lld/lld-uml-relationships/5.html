Dependency: "A uses B, briefly"

A dependency is the weakest relationship: a dashed line with an open arrow from the user to the used. It means a change in B might force a change in A, but A does not keep B. You see it when B is only:

  • a method parameter: checkout.run(order, payment, notifier);
  • a local variable or a return type;
  • a static call: Math.max, UUID.randomUUID();
  • something A creates and hands off without keeping (a factory creating products). UML sometimes tags this line «create».

The field test. The fastest way to tell dependency from association is to look at the fields. If B appears as a field of A, it is at least an association. If B appears only inside method signatures and bodies, it is a dependency.

Do not draw every dependency. A real class depends on String, List, a logger and twenty other things. In an interview diagram, draw dependencies only when they carry design meaning: "the service depends on the PaymentGateway interface, not on RazorpayGateway". That one dashed arrow shows you understand the Dependency Inversion Principle.
