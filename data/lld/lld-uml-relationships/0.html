The story: one house, six kinds of "together"

Explain like I'm 5

Think of your house. The rooms are part of the house. If the house is knocked down, the rooms are gone too. Nobody carries a kitchen to a new house.

The sofa is in the house too, but it is different. When you move, you take the sofa with you. The sofa was bought somewhere else and it can live in any house.

Your neighbour is not inside your house at all. You just know each other. You wave, you borrow sugar. Each of you lives your own life.

The postman comes for one minute, hands you a letter and leaves. You needed him for that minute only.

You are a person, a kind of animal. And at the swimming club you promised "I can do the lifeguard job"; anyone who keeps that promise can be today's lifeguard.

Those six kinds of "together" are the six relationships of a UML class diagram:

  • rooms in a house = composition (strong part-of, same lifetime);
  • sofa in a house = aggregation (weak part-of, separate lifetime);
  • you and your neighbour = association (they know each other);
  • the postman = dependency (used for a moment);
  • person is an animal = generalization (inheritance, is-a);
  • the lifeguard promise = realization (implementing an interface).

On a class diagram (see Class Diagrams for the boxes themselves) every one of these is a line between two boxes. The only thing that tells them apart is the line style (solid or dashed) and the end shape (nothing, open arrow, hollow triangle, hollow diamond, filled diamond). Learn six line endings and you can read any design.
