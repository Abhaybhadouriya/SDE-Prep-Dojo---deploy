How it works: evaluating one car against a tree

  1. The client calls familyTrip.isSatisfiedBy(c3). The root is an AndSpec; it asks its left child first.
  2. The left child is another AndSpec. It asks InCity("BLR"): true.
  3. Then SeatsAtLeast(7): true. So the inner AND is true.
  4. The root now asks its right child, PriceAtMost(3500): 4000 is too much, false.
  5. true AND false is false. C3 is filtered out. The client never saw any of this; it got one boolean.
  6. Short-circuit: for a car that fails an early test, later leaves are skipped. Put cheap and highly selective tests on the left.

Building the rule and evaluating it are separate steps. new InCity("BLR").and(new SeatsAtLeast(7)).and(new PriceAtMost(3500)) only builds a tree of objects; nothing is tested until a candidate arrives. That is what lets you store a rule, print it, send it to a database, or evaluate it against a million cars.
