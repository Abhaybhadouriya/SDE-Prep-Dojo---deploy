When to use it, when not to

Use it when
  • Users or admins combine filters freely: product search, rental search, hotel search, job search.
  • Business rules are reused for filtering, validation and alerts.
  • Rules must be data: built on an admin screen, stored, versioned, explained ("why was this coupon rejected?").
  • New criteria arrive often and should be new classes, not edits.
Avoid it, or watch out, when
  • One fixed condition used in one place: a plain if or lambda is clearer.
  • Large data sets evaluated in memory: push the spec down to the store or you will scan everything.
  • The tree grows into a home-made query language with joins, sorting and paging. Stop and use the store's real query language.
  • Rules need ordering, side effects or "stop at the first match with an action": that is Chain of Responsibility or a rules engine, not a boolean spec.

Performance rule of thumb: put cheap and selective leaves first in an AND (city before a full-text match), and the most likely-true leaf first in an OR. Short-circuiting then skips most of the work.
