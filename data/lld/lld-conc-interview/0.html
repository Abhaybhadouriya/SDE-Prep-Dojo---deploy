The story: one shop, many hands

Explain like I'm 5

A toy shop has one last teddy bear. Two shop assistants each look at the shelf, both see one bear, and both promise it to a child. Now two children are crying over one bear. The fix is not to hire fewer assistants. The fix is a rule: "before you promise a bear, put your hand on it". Only one hand fits on the bear at a time.

Concurrency interview questions are about that rule. Interviewers rarely ask "define a semaphore" and stop. They give you a design you just drew (a parking lot, a cab allocator, a booking system) and say: "a thousand requests arrive at once. What breaks, and how do you fix it?" This page trains that move, then backs it with the theory questions that come up in Java, C++ and OS rounds.

From your world

You already think in these terms. "Two assistants promise the same bear" is a check-then-act race on SMP. A lock is a spinlock or mutex; AtomicInteger.compareAndSet is cmpxchg; a BlockingQueue is an skb queue with a wait queue; ThreadPoolExecutor is a workqueue; lock ordering is what lockdep enforces; immutable snapshots are RCU. Say these mappings out loud in the interview: one line of kernel experience is worth more than a textbook definition.

Where this breaks: the JVM's memory model, not the CPU's, decides what another thread can see. In Java you get visibility only through volatile, locks, atomics or safe publication, never from "the store already happened on x86".
