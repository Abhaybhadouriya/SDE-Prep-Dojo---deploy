The story: the photocopied colouring page

Explain like I'm 5

Your teacher spent an hour drawing a beautiful castle for colouring. Thirty children want to colour it. She does not draw it thirty more times. She puts it in the photocopier and makes thirty copies. Each child colours their own copy, and the teacher's drawing stays clean.

Now imagine a magic copier that did not really copy. It handed out thirty "windows" that all look at the teacher's one drawing. When Riya colours the tower red, everybody's tower turns red, and so does the teacher's. That is the bug we will learn to avoid.

The teacher's drawing is the prototype. Copying is cloning. A real photocopy is a deep copy. The magic window is a shallow copy.

Software does this all the time. A cinema screen has a seat layout: 120 seats, rows, types and prices, loaded from a database with pricing rules. Every show on that screen needs its own copy of the layout so that booking A1 for Monday 6 pm does not book A1 for Tuesday. Loading and pricing it again for every show is slow. Copying a prepared layout is fast.
