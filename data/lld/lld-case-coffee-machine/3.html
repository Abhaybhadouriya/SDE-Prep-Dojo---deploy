Identify the entities (nouns and verbs)

  1. Write the whole problem in two sentences, including the operator and the indicator.
  2. Underline the nouns: customer, beverage, add-on, ingredient, recipe, outlet, indicator, operator.
  3. Underline the verbs. Order is the customer's event; refill is the operator's; create, reserve, pour and release happen inside.
  4. Reshape the nouns. Customer and operator are actors who call methods. An ingredient is just a name and a number, so a map key, not a class. An add-on wraps a beverage (Decorator). An outlet is a worker thread in a pool of N. The indicator is an Observer of the inventory.
  5. The final list. Note what is missing: no Latte class, no LatteWithShot class. Drinks are data (recipes) registered in a factory; only the add-on behaviour is a class.

The most common first draft has class Latte, class Cappuccino and class LatteWithExtraShot. With 4 drinks and 4 add-ons that can each be on or off, inheritance needs 4 x 24 = 64 classes, and a double shot breaks it. Recipes as data plus add-ons as decorators need 4 entries and 4 small classes.
