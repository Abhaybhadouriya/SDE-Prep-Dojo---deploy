Core flow: one order

  1. The customer (or a test thread) calls order("latte", "shot").
  2. The factory looks up "latte" in the menu and wraps it in ExtraShot. An unknown name fails here, with a clear message.
  3. The machine asks the inventory to reserve the final, decorated recipe. Inside one lock the inventory checks every ingredient, then subtracts every one. If a level crosses its low mark, listeners are told after the lock is released.
  4. If anything is short, the order is refused immediately with all the reasons. Nothing was taken, and no outlet was used.
  5. Otherwise the pour task goes to the pool of N outlet threads. The caller gets a Future at once; if all N outlets are busy, the task waits in the pool's queue.
  6. A free outlet thread runs pour: it counts itself as busy and calls the brewer (the hardware). The ingredients are already reserved, so this step cannot fail for lack of milk.
  7. The result completes the Future and revenue is added. If the brewer had thrown, the outlet would release the recipe back to the inventory and report the error.

The key ordering decision: reserve on the caller's thread, brew on the outlet thread. Reserving first means the answer "can I have this?" is immediate and final, and the order of acceptance is the order of calls. Brewing later means slow hardware never holds the inventory lock.
