The story: the class piggy bank

Explain like I'm 5

The teacher keeps a piggy bank for every child and a notebook. When your mum gives 10 rupees, the teacher writes two lines: "Mum's purse: minus 10" and "Your piggy bank: plus 10". When you give 3 rupees to your friend, again two lines: "You: minus 3", "Friend: plus 3". Every story has a minus line and a plus line of the same size, so if you add up every line in the notebook you always get zero. If you ever get something else, someone made a mistake.

One day you ask "did my 3 rupees go?" and, because you did not hear, you ask again. The teacher checks the note number you gave ("note #7") and says "yes, #7 is already done", instead of taking another 3 rupees from you.

The notebook is a double-entry ledger. The "same note number" trick is an idempotency key. Mum's purse is the outside world: a system account for the bank or payment gateway. Those three ideas are the whole design; the rest is careful locking and clean interfaces to UPI and card providers.

From your world

A transfer that must move both legs or neither is the same shape as updating two linked kernel structures under two spinlocks: take them in a fixed global order (by address, or here by wallet id) or two CPUs doing opposite moves deadlock. An idempotency key is TCP's sequence number: a retransmitted segment with a sequence number already ACKed is recognised and not delivered twice. And the payment gateway adapter is a driver ops table: one struct net_device_ops interface, many vendor chips with their own register layouts behind it.
