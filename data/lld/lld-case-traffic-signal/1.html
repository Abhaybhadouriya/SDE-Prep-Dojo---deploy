Before you start: this case assumes OOP from C structs (objects as structs with an ops table) and interfaces vs abstract classes. The patterns it uses are explained on their own pages: State, Strategy, Observer. The threading model builds on thread-safety basics.

Reports are short; the follow-ups are where the marks are. The most recent asks add real-time traffic data; older ones add pedestrians and rush hours.

Our research list ranks it 44th (score 3): rarer than the parking lot or elevator, but a favourite in analyst and new-grad loops because it fits in 45 minutes. Open-source collections such as awesome-low-level-design include it with a State-based solution. The 2022 post shows the common trap: reaching for a Singleton timer. Our design has no global timer at all; a clock calls tick(), which also makes it testable.

Say in the first two minutes: "two independent things vary here: what the controller is doing (normal, emergency, fault) is a State; how long a green lasts (fixed, adaptive, rush-hour plan) is a Strategy." That sentence alone answers the Goldman follow-up.
