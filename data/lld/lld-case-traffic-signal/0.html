The story: the police officer in the middle of the road

Explain like I'm 5

Before traffic lights, a police officer stood in the middle of the crossing. She waves the north-south cars through for a while. Then she raises her hand: "slow down" (that is yellow). She waits until the crossing is empty (everyone stops: all red). Only then does she wave the east-west cars through. She never waves both roads at the same time, because they would crash.

If many cars wait on one road, she gives that road more time. If she hears an ambulance siren from the east, she stops the north-south cars safely (slow down first, then empty the crossing) and waves the ambulance through. If her whistle breaks, she just flashes a yellow torch and everyone drives carefully. A traffic controller is that officer written as code.

This is a classic State-pattern question, usually a level easier than the elevator. The interview tests whether you model modes (normal cycle, emergency, fault) as states rather than flags, whether the safety rule (no conflicting greens, always yellow then all-red between phases) is enforced by the design rather than by luck, and whether the timing is a separate, swappable Strategy so "adjust to real-time traffic" is one new class.

From your world

You have written this machine before as a protocol state machine: a switch (sk->sk_state) in a TCP input path. The all-red interval is TCP's TIME_WAIT (the 2MSL quiet period): before a connection's port pair is reused, the stack waits until every segment still in flight has drained, so an old packet cannot land in the new connection, just as no car from the old phase may still be in the junction when the new phase goes green. The conflict monitor is lockdep: an independent checker of an invariant the main code is supposed to keep.
