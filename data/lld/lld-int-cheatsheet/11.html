Remember this

  • Pick a pattern from what changes in the problem, never from the list. If nothing varies, use a plain class.
  • Creational patterns answer "who makes it", structural "how it fits", behavioural "who does what, when".
  • Look-alikes are decided by intent: who switches it, does it add or control, one interface or many.
  • Every principle has a smell: god class (SRP), repeated switch (OCP), throwing override (LSP), fat interface (ISP), new inside services (DIP).
  • UML: the diamond is at the whole, the triangle points at the parent, dashed means implements or uses.
  • Concurrency: lock the check-then-act at the smallest scope, use atomics for counters and claims, queues for hand-offs, and one global lock order.
  • Most GoF patterns have a kernel twin; naming it is the strongest "real example" you can give.
