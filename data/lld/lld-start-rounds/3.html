Archetype A: the OOD whiteboard round

This is the classic "Object-Oriented Design" round. You get a one-line prompt and talk your way to a design. You draw classes and relationships, write the important methods (sometimes as pseudo-code), and then defend your choices as the interviewer pokes at them.

What reports say, per company:

  • Amazon (29 reports, 24 of them whiteboard/doc): a 60-minute round shared with 2-4 Leadership Principle questions, on a whiteboard tool or CoderPad. Requirements are often withheld on purpose, so clarifying is part of the test. Expect probes on Strategy, State and Observer. A weak LLD can trigger an extra round.
  • Zepto (20 reports, 18 whiteboard/doc): run by third-party interviewers; expects requirements, class diagram, database schema and API design in 60 minutes.
  • Swiggy and Salesforce: mostly 60-minute discussions with class design and APIs; Salesforce sometimes mixes in DSA or HLD.

What "done" means here

  1. A written requirement list the interviewer agreed to, including what is out of scope.
  2. A class diagram with the main entities, their relationships and the interfaces at change points.
  3. The 2-3 methods that carry the logic (for a parking lot: park(), unpark(), fee calculation), written clearly enough to trace.
  4. Answers to "what if": a new vehicle type, a new fee rule, two cars racing for the last spot.

The interviewer cannot run anything, so trace a concrete example out loud: "Car KA-01 enters, SpotFinder returns spot B3, ticket T1 is created at 10:02..." Tracing is how you prove correctness on a board.
