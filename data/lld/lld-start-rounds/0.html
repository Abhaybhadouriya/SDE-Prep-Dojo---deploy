The story: three kinds of cooking test

Explain like I'm 5

Three cooking schools test their students in three ways.

The first school asks you to stand at a whiteboard and explain how you would make a birthday cake: which bowls, which order, what you do if the eggs run out. You might write the recipe for the icing, but nobody eats anything.

The second school gives you a kitchen and two hours. At the end, a judge tastes the food. A beautiful plan with no cake gets zero. A plain cake that tastes right passes, and a cake that is also neat gets top marks. Then the judge asks, "How would you make 50 of these?"

The third school watches you cook one simple dish. Every time you finish, the judge says, "Now make it gluten-free", then "Now make two at once". Your kitchen must be tidy enough that each change is quick.

Those are the three LLD round types. Same skill - designing classes - but different things decide pass or fail. Many candidates prepare for the wrong one. A beautiful class diagram fails a machine-coding round where the evaluator runs your code; a half-finished running program fails a whiteboard round where they wanted to discuss trade-offs.
