The story: the taxi stand with one whistle

Explain like I'm 5

At a taxi stand there is a helper with a whistle. A family walks up and says "a big car, please". The helper looks at the cars waiting, picks the closest big one, and blows the whistle. That car is now taken: its "free" sign goes down. The family gets in, rides, pays, and when they get out the driver puts the "free" sign back up.

Now imagine two families shout at the same moment and there is only one big car. If the helper is careless, both families walk to the same car and there is a fight. A good helper lets only one family take the car, and tells the other "sorry, wait for the next one".

The interview is that helper, as code. Three ideas carry the whole design. Matching: how to pick a driver (nearest, best rated, cheapest). The trip lifecycle: requested, assigned, in progress, completed or cancelled, and nothing else. The free sign: one atomic flag per driver that only one request can flip from "available" to "on trip".

From your world

You have written all three before. The trip lifecycle is the TCP state machine. TCP's legal transitions live in tcp_rcv_state_process() and the close paths, which only call tcp_set_state() for allowed moves (tcp_set_state() itself just records the new state and does the bookkeeping); our canMoveTo() puts that table in one place instead of scattering it across callers. TCP_CLOSE is terminal, just like COMPLETED and CANCELLED. The "free sign" is cmpxchg: atomic_cmpxchg(&drv->state, AVAILABLE, ON_TRIP) succeeds for exactly one CPU. Picking a driver from a list of candidates is like the scheduler picking a runqueue: a policy (sched_class) behind a fixed interface. And telling everyone "trip T1 just started" is a notifier chain: blocking_notifier_call_chain(&trip_chain, TRIP_STARTED, trip).
