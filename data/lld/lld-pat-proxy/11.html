In interviews

In the interview reports we collected, Proxy was named in a handful of posts, linked to Media.net and Amazon, more often than other structural patterns. It appears two ways: as a direct question ("Proxy vs Decorator?", "how does @Transactional work?") and as the shape of a case study. One problem posted on LeetCode Discuss is literally titled Rate Limiter Proxy: requests go through a proxy that enforces several limits at once (per second, per 10 seconds, per minute).

  • Rate limiter: the limiter wraps the downstream service behind the same interface. Strategy picks the algorithm (fixed window, sliding window, token bucket); Proxy is how it is placed in the call path. HLD view: Distributed Rate Limiter.
  • LRU cache and key-value store: a read-through caching proxy in front of a slow store.
  • URL shortener: cache in front of the repository for hot short codes.
  • Notification service: per-vendor rate-limiting proxies around SMS and email adapters.
  • File system and library: protection proxies for permissions, virtual proxies for lazy-loaded content.
  • Stock exchange: quote caching exactly like our demo.

Strong 30-second answer: "A proxy implements the same interface as the real object and controls access to it. The common kinds are virtual for lazy creation, protection for access checks, remote for RPC stubs, caching, and rate limiting. It differs from Decorator by intent: a decorator adds behaviour and is stacked by the client, a proxy controls access and often manages the real object's lifecycle. Spring's @Transactional is a generated proxy, which is why self-calls inside a bean skip the transaction."
