The story: the receptionist

Explain like I'm 5

You want to talk to the head doctor. You do not walk into her room. You talk to the receptionist at the front desk. You say the same things you would say to the doctor: "I need an appointment", "here is my report".

The receptionist decides what happens. If the doctor is still at home, he calls her in only when a patient really arrives. If you are not on the list, he stops you. If you ask "what time does the clinic close?", he answers himself and does not bother the doctor. If fifty people rush in at once, he lets three in per minute. You talk to him exactly as you would talk to the doctor, and he guards the door. That receptionist is a Proxy.

In code, the "doctor" is an object that is expensive to create, lives far away on another machine, holds sensitive data, or cannot take too many calls. The proxy implements the same interface, so callers do not change at all, and it adds a single kind of control in front of the real thing.

From your world

The kernel is full of stand-ins. An mmap()ed file is a virtual proxy: the mapping exists at once, but pages are read from disk only when you touch them and take a page fault. The page cache is a caching proxy in front of filesystem and block I/O; the dentry cache is a caching proxy in front of the filesystem's lookup() (path name to inode). An NFS or FUSE file is a remote proxy: your read() looks local, but the kernel forwards it to a server or to a user-space daemon. A tc token-bucket qdisc in front of an interface is a rate-limiting proxy. And every get_file()/fput() or kref_get()/kref_put() pair is a smart reference that counts users of an object.
