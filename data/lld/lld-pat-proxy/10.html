Related and confused-with patterns

Proxy, Decorator, Adapter and Facade are all wrappers. The code can look identical; the intent separates them:

ProxyDecoratorAdapterFacade
Interface exposedSame as the real objectSame as the wrapped objectDifferent (the one the client wants)New and simpler, over many objects
PurposeControl access: when, whether, whereAdd behaviour (features)Convert an interfaceSimplify a subsystem
Who builds itUsually a factory or framework; the client does not knowThe client composes and stacks them on purposeWiring code, once per adapteeThe subsystem author
Real object's lifecycleOften managed by the proxy (lazy create, remote connect)Always passed in from outsidePassed in or ownedOwns or knows many objects
ExampleLazy image, @Transactional bean, gRPC stubBufferedInputStream, coffee + milk + sugarInputStreamReaderA PaymentService over gateway, ledger and notifier

Our caching and logging proxies could honestly be called decorators too; many real classes sit on the line. In an interview, say what the wrapper is for: "it controls whether we hit the exchange" is Proxy; "it adds price formatting" is Decorator. Other neighbours: a Chain of Responsibility also passes a request along a line of handlers, but any handler may consume it and the handlers do not share the target's interface; a lazy singleton holder is a virtual proxy for a single global instance. The look-alike patterns page puts them all side by side.
