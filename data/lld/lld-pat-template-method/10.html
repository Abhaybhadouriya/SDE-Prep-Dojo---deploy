In interviews

Template Method is rarely the main pattern of an LLD round, but it is the cleanest answer when you notice repeated flows, and "Template Method vs Strategy" is a very common theory question. Places it fits:

  • Coffee machine: every drink runs heat water, brew, pour, add extras; only brew and extras differ. The textbook tea-and-coffee example.
  • Snakes and ladders, tic-tac-toe, chess: a Game.play() loop of setup, turns, winner check.
  • Notification service: validate, render template, deliver, record status; only deliver differs per channel (or use Strategy and say why).
  • Movie ticket booking and hotel booking: hold, pay, confirm, release-on-failure as one fixed booking flow.
  • Logger: a fixed format-and-write sequence with pluggable formatting steps.

Reported: a Walmart SDE-3 round asked for a coffee vending machine (LeetCode Discuss, 2025-05-26); the drink-preparation sequence is where Template Method fits, with payment as a Strategy. Another candidate describes being pushed past "Strategy is just an interface": the interviewer wanted to know what makes it different from an abstract class and how a strategy gets chosen (LeetCode Discuss, 2024-03-07). That is the Template Method vs Strategy contrast in the Related section: inheritance fixes the steps at compile time, composition picks them at runtime.

Strong 30-second answer: "Template Method puts the skeleton of an algorithm in a final method of a base class and lets subclasses fill in specific steps, either abstract steps they must implement or hooks with defaults. The base class calls the subclass, the Hollywood principle. I use it when the order of steps must be enforced and only a few steps vary. If variations multiply or must change at runtime, I switch to Strategy, which is composition instead of inheritance."
