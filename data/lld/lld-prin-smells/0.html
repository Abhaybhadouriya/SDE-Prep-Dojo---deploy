The story: the smell in the fridge

Explain like I'm 5

You open the fridge and something smells bad. The smell is not the problem - it is a hint that something inside is old. You do not throw away the whole fridge. You look, find the old food, and remove it.

Cleaning your room is like that too. You do not throw away your toys; you put them in the right boxes. After cleaning, you have exactly the same toys. They are just easier to find.

Programmers borrowed both ideas. A code smell is a hint in the code that the design has a problem. Refactoring is cleaning up the design while keeping exactly the same behaviour - same toys, better boxes.

Why this matters for interviews: in machine-coding rounds the review call often asks "what would you clean up with more time?", and code-design rounds (Atlassian style) keep adding requirements until your code either absorbs them or falls apart. Spotting smells quickly and knowing the cure by name is how you answer both.
