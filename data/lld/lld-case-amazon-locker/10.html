Extensibility: the follow-ups interviewers ask

Follow-upChangeWhat stays untouched
"Keep large slots for large parcels"New strategy (ReservingBestFitStrategy, already in the code)Station, service
"Use real dimensions"Size.forBox() classifies by the longest side; for true 3-D fit, a FitPolicy that checks sorted dimensions of box versus slot (rotation), and a TreeMap of free slots by volume for best fitCode flow, expiry, listeners
"Reserve a slot at checkout"Add SlotState.RESERVED and a Reservation with a hold timeout; deposit with a reservation id skips the strategy; the expiry sweep also releases stale reservationsPickup, strategies
"Customer returns"A ReturnDrop delivery: customer gets a drop code, courier gets a collect code. Same Delivery lifecycle with roles swappedAllocation, station lock
"Several parcels in one order"Deposit them in one call under the station lock (all or nothing); one notification lists all codes, or one code opens a groupStrategy
"Station is full, now what?"A StationLocator (outside the station) suggests nearby stations with availability; the courier app retries thereEverything inside a station
"Better OTP"A different CodeGenerator: SecureRandom, or HMAC-based codes so the station can verify offline (RFC 6238 style); store only a hash of the codeStation logic
"Survive a restart"A DeliveryRepository written in the same critical section (or write-ahead log); rebuild free lists from slot states on bootStrategies, listeners
"Charge for overdue parcels"A listener on onExpired or a pricing strategy on pickupAllocation

The expiry sweep itself is a periodic job. In production it is one job on a scheduler like the one in the task scheduler case; at large scale, keep a min-heap of deliveries by expiresAt per station so the sweep is O(k log n) for k expired instead of O(n).
