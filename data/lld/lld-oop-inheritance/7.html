When inheritance is the wrong tool

Inheritance is the tightest coupling in OOP: the child depends on the parent's implementation, not just its interface. That causes the fragile base class problem: a harmless-looking change in the parent (say, addAll() now calls add() internally) breaks a child that overrode add() to count elements, which now counts twice. Warning signs in an interview design:

  • Subclasses that exist only to change one small behaviour (CashPaymentParkingLot, CardPaymentParkingLot): use a Strategy field.
  • A combinatorial explosion (ElectricSUV, PetrolSUV, ElectricSedan...): two independent dimensions want composition, or the Bridge pattern.
  • A child that throws UnsupportedOperationException for a parent method (a Penguin that cannot fly()): the is-a promise is broken; see LSP.
  • Inheriting just to reuse a helper method: move the helper into its own class and hold a reference.

Good uses: a genuine taxonomy with shared invariants (Vehicle → Car/Bike with a size used to pick a parking spot), a Template Method where the parent fixes the algorithm skeleton, and framework hooks designed for extension. The full comparison lives on Composition over Inheritance.
