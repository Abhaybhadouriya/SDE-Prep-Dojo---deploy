The four Coffman conditions (all four, or no deadlock)

In 1971 Coffman, Elphick and Shoshani described four conditions that must all hold at the same time for a deadlock to exist. This is the single most useful idea on the page, because it turns "how do I prevent deadlock?" into "which of the four will I break?".

ConditionPlain meaningHow to break itCost of breaking it
Mutual exclusionThe resource can be used by one thread at a time.Do not lock at all: immutable objects, atomic CAS, per-thread copies, a single owner thread that receives messages.Not always possible; CAS loops only work for small state.
Hold and waitA thread keeps what it has while it waits for more.Hold at most one lock at a time, or take every lock you need in one step (a coarse lock that covers both).Coarse locks reduce parallelism.
No preemptionA lock cannot be taken away; only the holder releases it.Use tryLock(timeout): if the second lock does not come, release the first and try again later.Retry logic, and a risk of livelock (fixed with random back-off).
Circular waitA chain of waiting threads closes back on itself.Give every lock a rank and always take locks in increasing rank (by account id, seat id, a lock-level number).Everyone must follow the rule; one careless path breaks it.

Say it in this shape: "A deadlock needs mutual exclusion, hold-and-wait, no preemption and circular wait. I cannot remove mutual exclusion for a balance, so I break circular wait with a global lock order by account id, and for paths where I cannot know the order up front I use tryLock with a timeout and random back-off."
