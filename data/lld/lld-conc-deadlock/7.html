Where deadlocks hide in LLD problems

Interviewers rarely say "now write a deadlock". They ask a follow-up on your design, and the deadlock is waiting inside your answer. Here are the usual places:

ProblemWhere two locks meetSafe answer
Wallet / Splitwise transferDebit one wallet, credit anotherLock both in wallet-id order; or a single-writer ledger thread.
Movie / flight bookingHold seats 9, 3 and 14 in one requestSort seat ids, lock in order; or one lock per show (coarse but deadlock-free).
Food ordering (the Cleartrip ask)Restaurant lock plus item stock lockPick one level (per-item lock or atomic decrement) and never nest restaurant inside item.
Parking lotFloor lock plus spot lockLock only the spot (CAS on its state); floors are read-only structure.
Elevator controllerController lock plus car lock, each calling the otherOne owner thread per car taking commands from a queue; no shared locks.
Observer / event busPublisher lock held while calling subscribersSnapshot subscribers, release, then notify (open call).
Cache with loadercomputeIfAbsent loader that touches the same mapNever modify the map from inside its own compute lambda; load outside, then putIfAbsent.

Nested ConcurrentHashMap.computeIfAbsent: the mapping function runs while a bin of the map is locked. If it updates the same map (for example recursive memoisation), Java 9+ may throw IllegalStateException: Recursive update, and older JDKs could hang. Treat compute lambdas like critical sections: short, no calls back into the map.

Lock hierarchy as documentation

In bigger codebases teams write the order down: "Level 1 Order, Level 2 Inventory, Level 3 Payment. You may take a higher level while holding a lower one, never the reverse." You can even enforce it in tests by wrapping locks in a class that knows its level and throws if a thread takes a lower level while holding a higher one. That is lockdep's rule in Java, and saying it in an interview signals real-world experience.

Avoiding locks entirely

  • Single-writer / actor: one thread owns the state and receives commands on a queue. No two locks, no deadlock (but watch for two actors waiting synchronously on each other, which is the same cycle at a higher level).
  • Optimistic concurrency: read a version, compute, commit with compare-and-set; retry on conflict. No locks are held while waiting, so no hold-and-wait. Covered in the booking lesson.
  • Immutable snapshots: readers never lock; a writer swaps a reference.
