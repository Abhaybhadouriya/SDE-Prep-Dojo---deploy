The story: two cars on a one-lane bridge

Explain like I'm 5

A bridge is only wide enough for one car. A red car drives onto it from the left. At the same moment a blue car drives onto it from the right. They meet in the middle. The red car says "I will go when you back up". The blue car says the same. Nobody backs up. They sit there forever.

That is a deadlock: each one holds something (its half of the bridge) and waits for the thing the other one holds.

Now imagine both drivers are very polite. Both back up at the same moment, then both drive forward again at the same moment, and meet again. Back, forward, back, forward. They are busy all day, but nobody crosses. That is a livelock.

Finally, imagine a big truck that always waits for the bridge to be completely empty. Small cars keep arriving, so the bridge is never empty. The truck is not stuck because of a cycle; it just never gets a turn. That is starvation.

From your world

You have seen this as the classic ABBA bug: spin_lock(&dev->lock); spin_lock(&q->lock); in one path and the reverse order in an interrupt or timer path. On a spinlock both CPUs spin at 100% (the soft-lockup watchdog eventually complains); on a mutex both tasks sleep in D state and the hung-task detector fires after 120 seconds. In Java the threads show as BLOCKED in a thread dump. Same bug, same cure: a fixed lock order.
