The story: the cinema counter with sticky notes

Explain like I'm 5

A cinema has a big seat chart on the wall. You point at two seats. The lady at the counter puts a yellow sticky note with your name on them and says: "They are yours for five minutes. Go get the money." If you come back with the money in time, she paints the seats red: sold. If you do not come back, the sticky note falls off by itself and the seats are white again for the next child.

Two children may point at the same seat at the same moment. Only one sticky note can go on a seat, so only one child gets it. The other child is told "sorry, pick another seat" before anybody pays.

That sticky note is the whole trick of this problem. It is called a hold (or a seat lock) with a TTL (time to live). Everything else (movies, cities, screens, prices) is ordinary modelling. The interview is really about the note: how you place it on several seats at once, how it expires, and how you stop two people from placing it on the same seat.

From your world

A hold with a TTL is a lease. You know the pattern from DHCP (an address is yours until the lease runs out, unless you renew) and from ARP or neighbour-table entries that age out. The seat table is like a connection-tracking table: each entry has a state and a timeout, and an expired entry is simply treated as gone the next time someone looks, without a sweeper having run.

Where this breaks: a DHCP server owns its whole pool on one box. A ticketing site has many app servers, so the lease must live somewhere they all see (a lock per show in one process here; a database row or Redis key with an expiry in production).
