Quiz

A periodic job has period 100 ms and each run takes 30 ms. It first starts at t=0. With fixed delay, when does the second run start?

  1. t=100
  2. t=130
  3. t=30
  4. t=70
Fixed delay counts from the finish: 30 + 100 = 130. Fixed rate would start at 100.

The dispatcher pushes every due job straight into a thread pool's FIFO queue. What breaks first?

  1. Delays
  2. Cycle detection
  3. Cancellation of periodic jobs
  4. Priorities: a later high-priority job waits behind earlier low-priority ones
The decision of which job runs next is made too early. Our semaphore keeps it until a worker is free.

Why is cancel O(1) in our design?

  1. It only flips the state; the dispatcher drops the job when it reaches the top of the heap
  2. It interrupts the worker
  3. Cancelled jobs are moved to a second heap
  4. The heap supports O(1) removal
Lazy deletion: correctness comes from the state check at pop time.

Job D depends on B and C. B and C finish on two workers at the same instant. What guarantees D is scheduled exactly once?

  1. D's priority
  2. A volatile flag on D
  3. Decrementing D's pending count under the lock and scheduling only when it reaches 0
  4. The listener list
The transition to zero happens once, and the lock makes the two decrements one after the other.

A batch of jobs P to Q to R to P is submitted with submitGraph. What should happen?

  1. Only P is rejected
  2. P runs, then the others wait forever
  3. The whole batch is rejected and none of it is admitted
  4. The scheduler breaks the cycle at random
Kahn's algorithm outputs fewer jobs than the batch, so there is a cycle; all-or-nothing keeps state clean.

Why does the scheduler use System.nanoTime() and not currentTimeMillis()?

  1. It is monotonic: a wall-clock step (NTP, manual change) cannot stall or burst the scheduler
  2. It is more precise, nothing else
  3. nanoTime is faster to read
  4. currentTimeMillis is not thread safe
Same as CLOCK_MONOTONIC versus CLOCK_REALTIME.

A job's upstream dependency fails after all retries. What does our design do with the job?

  1. Runs it anyway
  2. Retries the upstream forever
  3. Leaves it WAITING
  4. Marks it SKIPPED, and its own dependents too
Dependents of a failed job can never run; making that explicit prevents jobs waiting forever.
