In interviews

Flyweight is a Tier 3 pattern: in the interview reports we collected it was mentioned only in roadmap-style posts, never as a direct question. It shows up as a follow-up when the interviewer scales your case study: "the board has 10 million cells", "the document is 500 pages", "there are 100,000 vehicles". Recognise the moment and offer the split.

  • Text editor: the textbook case. Character styles (or font runs) are flyweights; text is stored in a buffer, positions are computed at layout.
  • Chess: there are only 12 kinds of piece (6 types x 2 colours). Rules and move generation can be shared flyweights; the board square is extrinsic. Mention it, then say a plain 8x8 game does not need it.
  • Snake and ladder and snake game: shared cell or sprite types on big boards.
  • Parking lot: spot types (compact, large, EV) with their rates and rules can be shared; the spot id and occupancy are extrinsic.
  • BookMyShow: seat categories (pricing, display) shared across thousands of seats per show.

Strong 30-second answer: "Flyweight shares the immutable part of many small objects. I split state into intrinsic, which is the same wherever the object is used, and extrinsic, which the caller keeps and passes in. A factory with a map hands out one shared instance per key. The flyweights must be immutable and the factory thread-safe. Java's Integer cache for -128 to 127 is a flyweight, which is why == on boxed Integers breaks at 128."
