The story: the recipe card robot

Explain like I'm 5

You give a kitchen robot a card that says "if it is cold AND (it is raining OR it is late), make soup". The robot does not understand the whole sentence at once. It breaks the card into little pieces: "it is cold", "it is raining", "it is late", and the joining words AND and OR.

Each piece is a tiny helper that answers just one question. The "it is cold" helper looks at the thermometer. The AND helper asks its two friends and says yes only if both say yes. Ask the top helper, and the question travels down to the little helpers and the answer travels back up. A new card is just a new arrangement of the same helpers.

That is the Interpreter pattern: one class per grammar rule, objects arranged as a tree that mirrors the sentence, and one method, interpret(context), that every node implements by asking its children.

From your world

When you run tcpdump -i eth0 'tcp port 22', the expression is a sentence in a small language. libpcap compiles it into classic BPF instructions, the program is attached to a socket with SO_ATTACH_FILTER, and the kernel runs it for every packet. The kernel converts classic BPF to eBPF internally and runs it either in an interpreter (__bpf_prog_run()) or as JIT-compiled native code. That is an interpreter too, but a bytecode one. The GoF Interpreter is the simpler, slower cousin: it walks the expression tree directly instead of compiling it.
