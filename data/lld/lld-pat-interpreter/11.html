Remember this

  • Interpreter = one class per grammar rule; a sentence becomes a tree; interpret(ctx) recurses.
  • Terminal expressions are leaves (values, comparisons); nonterminal expressions combine children (AND, OR, +).
  • The pattern does not include the parser; write a small recursive-descent one, with precedence in the grammar.
  • Parse once, interpret many times; immutable trees are safe to share across threads.
  • Good for small, stable DSLs: rules, formulas, filters. Bad for big grammars or hot paths.
  • Interviews ask "build an expression evaluator", not "use Interpreter".
  • BPF: same idea compiled to bytecode and JIT, which is what you do when tree walking is too slow.
