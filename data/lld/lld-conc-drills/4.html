Drill 3: counters, from count++ to a sliding window

count++ is three steps: read, add one, write. Two threads that read the same old value both write old + 1, and one increment is lost. In our runs, 8 threads doing a million increments each ended with between about 2.7 and 4.2 million instead of 8 million. Fixes, from simplest to most scalable:

From your world

LongAdder is percpu_counter: writes touch a per-CPU (in Java, per-thread-ish) slot with no shared cache line, and reads pay to sum all slots and may be slightly stale. Same trade-off, same use case (statistics, not decisions).

An expiring counter: "hits in the last 60 seconds"

A frequent ask (LeetCode 362, Design Hit Counter, plus a thread-safety follow-up): count events in a sliding window. Storing every timestamp in a queue works but grows with traffic. The compact design is a ring of W buckets, one per second: bucket i = now mod W stores the second it belongs to and a count. When a hit lands in a bucket whose stored second is old, the bucket is reset and reused. The total is the sum of buckets whose second is within the window.

Thread safety: a hit is "if the slot is stale, reset it; then increment", a check-then-act on two fields, so the simplest correct answer is one lock for hit() and total(). For very high rates, keep a lock per slot (stripes), or use one LongAdder per slot plus an AtomicLong second stamp, accepting that a few hits at a slot boundary may land in the wrong second. Say which trade-off you accept.
