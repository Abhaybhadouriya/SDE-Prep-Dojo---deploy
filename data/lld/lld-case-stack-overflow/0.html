The story: the class question board

Explain like I'm 5

A teacher puts a big board on the wall. Anyone can pin a question card. Others pin answer cards under it. Every child gets one sticker per card to say "this helped" (or a frown sticker to say "this is wrong"), and nobody may put a sticker on their own card. The child who asked can put a gold star on the one answer that solved it, and that card moves to the top.

Stickers turn into points for the card's owner. Children with enough points earn new powers: at 15 points you may give stickers, at 125 you may give frowns. Each card has colour tags ("maths", "space") so you can look only at the colours you like.

From your world

A vote is like a reference held on an object: each voter may hold at most one, keyed by voter id, and the score is the sum. Changing a vote is "drop the old reference, take the new one", and both steps happen under the post's lock, just as kref_get / kref_put keep a count consistent. The "one vote per user" rule is a set keyed by voter, not a counter, for the same reason a refcount alone cannot tell you who holds the references.

Interfaces in C terms: every interface on this page is, in C, a struct of function pointers; each implementation fills one in, like file_operations. Java and C++ build that table (the vtable) for you, and every object carries a pointer to it. More in OOP from C structs and polymorphism and vtables.
