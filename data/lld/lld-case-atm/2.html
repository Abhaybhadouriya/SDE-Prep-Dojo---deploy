Clarify requirements

Spend about 5 minutes here. The ATM looks simple, so candidates skip questions and then discover in minute 50 that they never decided what happens when the cash does not come out.

Functional (P0 - must work in the demo)

  1. Card Insert a card; eject it at any time before money moves.
  2. PIN Verify the PIN with the bank. After 3 wrong tries the ATM keeps the card and the bank blocks it.
  3. Balance Show the account balance (only after a correct PIN).
  4. Withdraw A positive multiple of 100, up to a per-withdrawal maximum; the bank checks funds and the daily limit.
  5. Dispense Pick notes from the cassettes (2000, 500, 200, 100), largest first, but find a combination whenever one exists.
  6. Never lose money If the bank declines, no notes move. If the notes do not come out after the debit, the debit is reversed.
  7. Out of service When cash runs low the ATM stops taking cards; an operator refill brings it back.

Functional (P1 - say you can add them)

  • Deposit (cash or cheque), change PIN, mini statement, transfer.
  • Receipts (print or SMS), language choice, cardless withdrawal with a one-time code.
  • Several banks: on-us cards versus other banks' cards through a network switch.
  • Admin: cash reports, low-cash alerts, remote shutdown.

Non-functional

  • Correctness of money: every withdrawal is debited exactly once, or not at all; retries are safe.
  • Security: the PIN never leaves the ATM in clear text; we model a hashed "PIN block".
  • Thread safety: one ATM serves one customer, but many ATMs hit the same account (a joint account, a stolen card).
  • Extensibility: a new note size, a new transaction type or a new bank protocol is a new class.
  • Testability: the bank and the hardware are interfaces, so tests can fake a decline or a jam.

Out of scope (say it out loud)

  • Real hardware drivers, the real ISO 8583 wire format, real PIN encryption (HSMs, DUKPT).
  • UI screens and printing.
  • The bank's own ledger design (that is the wallet case).
