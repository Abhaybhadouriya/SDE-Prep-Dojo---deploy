So why learn it? Because it is the cleanest practice problem for three patterns at once, and those patterns are what interviewers do ask about. The cash-dispensing part also shows up on its own as a phone-screen coding question. Here is what we found on LeetCode Discuss with a targeted search:

Read the Microsoft example carefully, because it hides the trap of this problem. In the second stock, plain greedy takes the 500, is left with 100, has no 100 notes, and fails. The right answer (three 200s) needs the chain to back off. Most published solutions are greedy only and fail this test.

A strong opening: "The session is a state machine, so illegal actions are rejected by the state, not by if-checks everywhere. Notes are a chain of handlers, one per denomination. The bank is behind an interface with an adapter. And a withdrawal is reserve notes, debit, eject, with a reversal if the eject fails."
