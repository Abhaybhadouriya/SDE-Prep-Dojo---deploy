The story: the shopkeeper with a cash box

Explain like I'm 5

Imagine a shopkeeper who gives people money from their savings. You show your savings card. He asks for your secret number. If you get it wrong three times, he keeps the card. If you get it right, you can ask "how much do I have?" or "give me 1,700".

Before he gives you anything, he does three things in order. First, he looks in his cash box: can he make 1,700 from the notes he has? Second, he phones the bank: "please take 1,700 from this savings". Third, he hands you the notes. If the bank says "not enough savings", he puts the notes back in the box. If the box gets stuck and the notes do not come out, he phones the bank again: "undo that, she did not get the money".

And he only does one thing at a time: he will not count notes while you are still typing your secret number.

That is the whole ATM problem. Here are the real names for the parts of the story; each one is built and explained further down.

From your world

You have written this machine before. A TCP socket is in exactly one state (TCP_LISTEN, TCP_SYN_RECV, TCP_ESTABLISHED ...) and every incoming segment is handled according to that state; tcp_set_state() is the only way to move. An ATM session is the same: IDLE, CARD_INSERTED, AUTHENTICATED, DISPENSING, and an event (a key press) that is legal in one state is an error in another. The cash dispenser is a netfilter chain: each hook (each note size) looks at the packet (the remaining amount), does its part and passes it on.

Where this breaks: TCP's state lives in one sock on one machine. An ATM withdrawal spans two machines (the ATM and the bank) that can fail independently, so "move to the next state" is not enough: you also need a way to undo the bank's half when the ATM's half fails.
