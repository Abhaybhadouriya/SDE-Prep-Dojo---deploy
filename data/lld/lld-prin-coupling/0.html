The story: tangled headphones and tidy drawers

Explain like I'm 5

Throw five pairs of headphones in one bag and shake it. Now try to take out just one pair. You pull, and the other four come with it. That is tight coupling: things are knotted together, so you cannot move one without moving the others.

Now look at a kitchen drawer. The spoon drawer has only spoons. The junk drawer has batteries, a ruler, a birthday candle and a key nobody remembers. Finding a spoon is easy; finding anything in the junk drawer is a hunt. The spoon drawer has high cohesion (everything belongs together); the junk drawer has low cohesion.

Good code is like neatly wound headphones in separate pouches, kept in drawers where each drawer has one kind of thing.

In design reviews and LLD rounds you will hear "this is too tightly coupled" or "this class isn't cohesive". This page turns those vague comments into something you can measure, name and fix.
