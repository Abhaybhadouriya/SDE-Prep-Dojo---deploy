How it is asked

Before you start: this case assumes OOP from C structs (objects as structs with an ops table) and interfaces vs abstract classes. The patterns it uses are explained on their own pages: Strategy. Updating shared counters safely is covered in concurrent collections and atomics.

Atlassian's loop has a dedicated 60-minute "Code Design" round: runnable code, tests, and requirements that arrive in stages. This rating problem is one of its most repeated questions in our data, alongside snake game and the wildcard router. Downlevelling decisions often come from this round, so how cleanly you extend matters as much as finishing. See How LLD rounds work and the Atlassian company guide.

The reports above confirm stage 1 (averages, highest first) and the monthly follow-up; tie-breaking shows up as the natural question once two agents have the same average (the 2025-03 code above breaks ties by name), and exporting the results is a common later stage in prep material for this round. Treat the exact order as "likely", and design so any order is cheap.

The 2025-03 post is the best lesson on this page. Its code kept int rating and computed totalSum / totalCount in integer maths, so ratings 4 and 5 give an average of 4, and its PriorityQueue was printed by iteration, which is not sorted order. Both are classic silent bugs. Write the test "ratings 5 and 4 give 4.50" first and you catch the first one in a minute.
