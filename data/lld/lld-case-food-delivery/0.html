The story: the tiffin counter

Explain like I'm 5

Your street has three small kitchens. Each kitchen has a menu board with prices, and a stove with room for only four pots at a time. You want two dosas and a coffee. A helper looks at all the menu boards, crosses out the kitchens that do not make coffee or have no free pots, and picks the cheapest one left. The kitchen puts your food on its stove (one pot fewer free). When the food is ready, the pots are free again, and a bicycle boy carries the box to you. If all the bicycle boys are out, your box waits on the shelf until one comes back.

That is the whole system: menus, pots (capacity), a rule for picking the kitchen, a box that moves through steps (ordered, cooking, ready, on the bike, delivered), and a queue of boxes waiting for a bike.

From your world

A restaurant's capacity is a credit counter, exactly like TCP's receive window or a NIC's free TX descriptor count: you may submit work only while credits remain, and completion returns them. The order lifecycle is a protocol state machine (think TCP's LISTEN, SYN_RCVD, ESTABLISHED...): a table of legal transitions, and anything else is dropped. The "pick a restaurant" rule is a function pointer in an ops struct, the same way a qdisc or a TCP congestion-control module (struct tcp_congestion_ops) is swapped without touching the caller. And "notify everyone when an order changes state" is a notifier chain (register_netdevice_notifier): subscribers register callbacks, the producer walks the list.
