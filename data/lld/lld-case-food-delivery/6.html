Core flows

The flow that matters most is placeOrder: filter, pick, claim, retry. Then markReady hands the box to a rider or the queue.

  1. The driver (standing in for a customer) calls the facade with a map of item to quantity.
  2. FoodService builds the candidate list: restaurants that serve every item and look like they have room. This is only a hint; another thread may fill the restaurant a microsecond later.
  3. The current strategy chooses one. FoodService does not know or care which rule it is.
  4. The real decision: tryReserve checks and claims capacity inside one lock. If it fails (we lost a race, or the menu changed), remove that restaurant and ask the strategy again.
  5. Only after the claim succeeds is the Order created, moved to ACCEPTED, and listeners told. The total is priced from the menu at this moment and stored.
  6. When the kitchen finishes, markReady moves the order to READY first (so a racing cancel loses), then returns capacity and counts served items.
  7. The dispatcher hands back a free rider chosen by PartnerAssigner, or queues the order. When a rider delivers, next() gives it the oldest waiting order.

Why "pick, then claim, then retry" and not "lock everything, then pick"? Locking every restaurant for each order would serialise the whole city on one order. The optimistic loop locks one restaurant for a few instructions; the retry almost never runs. See optimistic vs pessimistic booking.
