The story: the kitchen counter

Explain like I'm 5

In a restaurant, the cook puts finished plates on a small counter, and the waiter picks them up and carries them to tables. The counter has room for only five plates.

If the counter is full, the cook stops and waits; there is nowhere to put another plate. If the counter is empty, the waiter stands and waits; there is nothing to carry. When the waiter takes a plate, she calls "space!" so the cook can continue. When the cook adds a plate, he rings a little bell so the waiter knows food is ready.

At closing time, the cook puts a card on the counter that says "KITCHEN CLOSED". When the waiter picks up that card instead of a plate, she knows there will be no more food and she can go home. Nobody is left waiting forever.

That is the producer-consumer pattern. The cook is a producer (a thread that creates work), the waiter is a consumer (a thread that processes work), and the counter is a bounded buffer (a queue with a fixed capacity) that sits between them. "Space!" and the bell are condition signals. The closed card is a poison pill. Because the counter is small, a fast cook cannot bury a slow waiter in plates: the counter itself slows the cook down. That is backpressure.

From your world

You have written this a hundred times. A NIC RX ring is a bounded buffer: the hardware is the producer (it DMAs packets into descriptors and moves the tail), the driver's NAPI poll is the consumer (it processes descriptors and gives them back). The TX ring is the same in reverse, and when it is full the driver calls netif_stop_queue() so the stack stops handing it packets, then netif_wake_queue() when descriptors complete: textbook backpressure. Pipes block the writer when the pipe buffer is full. kfifo is a lock-free single-producer single-consumer ring. This page is the same design in Java, with threads instead of hardware.
