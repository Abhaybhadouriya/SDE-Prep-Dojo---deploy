Backpressure: what happens when the queue is full?

Backpressure is any way the slow side pushes back on the fast side. A bounded queue forces you to pick a policy, and the right policy depends on what the items are:

Our demo pairs a fast producer with a consumer that takes one item every 2 ms through a queue of 8. The producer uses offer(item, 1 ms); some offers time out and the queue never grows past 8. In a thread pool the same choice is called a rejection policy (ThreadPoolExecutor's CallerRunsPolicy is "block" in disguise: the submitting thread runs the task itself, so it naturally slows down).

From your world

TCP flow control is backpressure across a network: the receiver advertises a window, and a sender with a full window stops sending. A NIC with a full RX ring drops packets and counts them (rx_missed_errors or similar counters), which is "drop newest". netif_stop_queue() is "block the producer". Kafka moves the queue to disk and lets consumers fall behind; the distance is consumer lag (see Kafka consumers and consumer groups). Same idea at every layer: decide what happens when the buffer is full before it is full.
