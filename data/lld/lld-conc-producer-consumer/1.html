Why put a queue between threads?

  • Different speeds. An HTTP handler produces "send email" jobs in microseconds; sending an email takes hundreds of milliseconds. A queue lets the fast side hand off and return.
  • Decoupling. Producers do not know who the consumers are or how many there are. You can add consumers to drain faster without touching producer code.
  • Bursts. Traffic arrives in spikes. A buffer absorbs a short spike and lets consumers work at a steady rate.
  • Ordering. A FIFO queue keeps work in arrival order, which matters for logs, events in a pub-sub topic, or tasks in a scheduler.

Bounded is the key word. An unbounded queue hides overload: if producers are faster than consumers for long enough, the queue grows until the process runs out of memory, and latency for every item grows with it. A bounded queue turns overload into a visible decision: block the producer, time out, or drop.
