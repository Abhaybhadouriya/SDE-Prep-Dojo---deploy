When to use it, when not to

Use it when
  • Any machine-coding round with "in-memory only, no DB": it is the expected skeleton.
  • Business rules must be tested without a database (fake or in-memory repository in tests).
  • The storage may change (map to SQL, SQL to a key-value store, a cache in front).
  • Several use cases share the same lookups; put them in one place instead of scattering loops over maps.
Avoid it, or watch out, when
  • A 45-minute whiteboard OOD with no storage question: draw it as one box and move on.
  • A thin CRUD app where the framework already is the repository; a wrapper over JpaRepository that only forwards calls adds nothing.
  • Generic "repository for everything" with findBy(String field, Object value): it leaks storage thinking into the domain.
  • Services that are just pass-throughs to repositories while the rules sit in the driver (an "anemic" service). Put the rules in the service or on entities.

The most common machine-coding mistake with this pattern: putting the business check inside the repository (save() refuses a third loan). Then a second use case that saves loans skips or duplicates the rule. Repositories store; services decide.
