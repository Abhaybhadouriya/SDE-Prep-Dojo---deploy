The story: Raju's dosa shop grows up

Explain like I'm 5

Raju opens a tiny dosa shop. At first he does everything: he cooks, takes money, washes plates and writes the accounts. One day he burns a dosa because a customer wanted change. So he hires a cashier and a cook. Each person has one job. That is S.

His menu is painted on the wall. Adding "cheese dosa" means repainting the whole wall. So he buys a board with empty slots: a new dish is a new card slid in, and the old cards stay untouched. That is O.

When the cook is sick, a substitute comes. Raju's rule: anyone who says "I am a cook" must make a dosa that customers would accept. A substitute who can only make tea is not a cook. That is L.

The delivery boy got a remote control with 50 buttons for the whole shop. He only needs "open gate". So Raju gives each helper a small remote with only their buttons. That is I.

Raju used to buy milk only from Sharma Dairy. When Sharma closed, the shop stopped. Now he writes a rule: "any supplier who delivers 20 litres of toned milk by 6 am". Any dairy can plug in. That is D.

Software grows the same way. A machine-coding problem gives you version 1, then the interviewer says "now add surge pricing", "now add SMS", "now add a new vehicle type". SOLID is the set of five habits that make those follow-ups cheap.

The principles were collected by Robert C. Martin in his 2000 paper Design Principles and Design Patterns; the acronym SOLID was coined around 2004 by Michael Feathers (Wikipedia: SOLID). This page is the map. Each letter has a deep page with longer before/after code.
