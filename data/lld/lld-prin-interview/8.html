Remember this

  • Principle questions come as theory, as spot-and-fix code review, as tests to pass, and silently as the grading lens on your machine-coding code.
  • Answer in five beats: define, smell, fix, trade-off, example. The trade-off beat is what makes it an SDE-2 answer.
  • Quick smells: type switches (OCP), throwing overrides (LSP/ISP), new of a detail inside policy (DIP), train wrecks (Demeter), many reasons to change (SRP).
  • SRP means one actor, not one method. DRY means one place per piece of knowledge, not "no similar lines".
  • DIP is about who owns the interface; DI is the technique; IoC is the broad idea.
  • Make extensibility visible: interfaces named after requirements, a second implementation registered in the driver, and one sentence in your review.
  • Your C background is evidence: ops tables, notifier chains and NULL optional ops are SOLID in another language.
