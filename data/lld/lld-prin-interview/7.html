Scenario drills: weak vs strong answers

Scenario: "Now add a new vehicle type"

You finished a parking lot with if (vehicle.type == CAR) checks in Allocator, Pricing and Display. The interviewer adds electric vehicles that need charging spots.

"I'll add another else-if in each of the three places." (Three edits, three chances to miss one - shotgun surgery.)

"That's three switches on the same type code; I'll replace them with polymorphism. VehicleType becomes an enum carrying its spot requirement, and charging spots are a new SpotType. Allocation asks the vehicle which spot types fit. EV support is then one enum constant and one spot class."

Follow-ups
  • What if pricing for EVs also depends on charge consumed?
  • Where do you put the charger state?

Scenario: the interviewer says "this violates SRP"

Your BookingService holds seats, computes price and sends confirmation e-mails.

"It's fine, it's all booking-related."

"You're right - it changes for pricing rules and for messaging too. I'll keep BookingService as the coordinator, extract PricingPolicy and Notifier and inject both. Seat state stays inside Show, which guards double booking." Then do it, in two minutes.

Follow-ups
  • How do you avoid going too far?
  • Which of these would you not split in a 45-minute round?

Scenario: you are accused of over-engineering

You have an AbstractVehicleFactory, a VehicleBuilder and a VisitorReport, and no P0 feature runs yet. 40 minutes are gone.

Defend every pattern and keep building infrastructure.

"Fair point. The factory has one product, so I'll inline it; the builder and visitor serve no current requirement, so they go. I'll get park/unpark running end to end and add a seam if a follow-up asks for one."

Follow-ups
  • Which pattern would you keep, and why?

Scenario: pluggable device capabilities (Amazon SDE-1)

Devices may or may not have a display, a speaker and a battery; a tablet has all three. A real candidate re-implemented the same capability in several classes and the design got messy (2026-08).

One Device interface with showMessage, speak and chargePercent; devices without a speaker throw. (ISP and LSP both broken.)

Small role interfaces (HasDisplay, HasSpeaker, HasBattery) implemented by delegating to reusable components (LcdDisplay, BasicSpeaker, Battery). A Tablet composes three components and forwards calls; behaviour is written once.

Follow-ups
  • How would a caller discover capabilities at runtime?
  • Could you use a capability map instead of interfaces?

Scenario: a pattern your interviewer does not know

You propose Chain of Responsibility for a logger; the interviewer expected Facade + Factory. A Razorpay candidate reported exactly this mismatch (2024-03).

Argue that your pattern is the correct one.

Explain the problem the pattern solves in plain words first ("each handler decides whether to log and passes it on, so adding a level or sink is one new handler"), show the 10-line code, then offer the alternative and the trade-off. You are graded on reasoning, not on pattern names.

Follow-ups
  • When is CoR a bad fit for logging?

Scenario: "what breaks if this changes?"

Your notification design is done. The interviewer asks: a user changes their channel preferences - which components are affected? (Salesforce SMTS, 2026-11)

List every class in the system as "might be affected".

Trace one path: preferences live in PreferenceStore; Dispatcher reads them per message; channels are untouched. So one store write and zero code changes - that is low coupling. Then name the weak spot honestly (a cached preference may be stale for N seconds) and how you would handle it.

Follow-ups
  • How would you make preferences take effect immediately?
