The story: knowing the rules vs driving

Explain like I'm 5

A driving test has two parts. First the examiner asks, "What does a red light mean?" Easy. Then you drive, and the examiner watches whether you actually stop at red lights when nobody reminds you.

Design principle questions are the same. Saying "S is Single Responsibility" is the easy part. The real test is whether your own code, written under a clock, quietly follows the rules - and whether you can spot when someone else's code does not.

The principles themselves are taught on SOLID at a Glance and the five deep pages that follow it, plus DRY, KISS, YAGNI, GRASP and Anti-Patterns. This page is practice.

From your world

You have shipped SOLID in C for years. file_operations is Open/Closed and Dependency Inversion: the VFS owns the table, each filesystem fills it in, and a new filesystem never edits the VFS. Optional members left NULL are the kernel's take on Interface Segregation. Use these as your "example from work" beats - interviewers like hearing a principle recognised in a different language.
