The story: the scorer in the pavilion

Explain like I'm 5

At a cricket match there is one person in the pavilion with a big notebook: the scorer. Every time the bowler bowls, the umpire waves a signal, and the scorer writes one small mark: a dot for no run, a 4, a W for a wicket, a "wd" for a wide. The scorer never writes "the score is now 23 for 2". That number is worked out by adding up the marks.

Outside, a big scoreboard shows the total. Kids in the crowd look at it, a radio man reads it out, and a newspaper copies it at the end. None of them talk to the bowler. They all just watch the scorer's notebook.

That is the whole design. The notebook is a list of Ball events. The rules that turn a mark into runs, balls faced and a change of strike live in one class, Innings. The scoreboard, the radio and the newspaper are listeners (the Observer pattern): they are told when something happens, and they never change the score.

From your world

A NIC driver does not keep a variable called "throughput". It bumps counters per event (rx_packets, rx_bytes, rx_dropped) and tools derive rates from them. A scorecard is the same: per-event counters (runs, balls, fours, extras) and derived values (strike rate, economy, overs as "1.4"). The listeners are a notifier chain: the match calls every registered listener on each event, just like call_netdevice_notifiers() walks its chain without knowing who is on it.
