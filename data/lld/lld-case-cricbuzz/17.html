An over contains: 1, Wd, 4, 0, Nb, 2, 1, 6. How many legal balls is that?

  1. 8
  2. 6
  3. 7
The wide and the no-ball are not legal deliveries, so 8 - 2 = 6 and the over is complete.

Where should a batter's runs and balls faced be stored?

  1. On Player
  2. In the Innings, as a map from Player to BattingStats
  3. On Team
They are facts about one player in one innings; Player stays reusable across matches.

P1 is on strike and scores 1 off the sixth legal ball of the over. Who faces the next ball?

  1. P1, because the odd run and the end of over both swap
  2. The other batter
  3. The next batter in the order
Odd run: swap (P1 to the non-striker's end). Over end: swap again (P1 back on strike).

The interviewer asks for ball-by-ball commentary. With this design, what changes?

  1. An if in Innings.bowl() that prints text
  2. A subclass of Match
  3. A new MatchListener implementation
Output is an Observer; a new output is a new listener and no existing class changes.

Why does Match parse the token before calling Innings.bowl()?

  1. It is faster
  2. Java requires it
  3. So an invalid token is rejected before any score changes
Parse-then-apply makes each ball all-or-nothing.

With 5 players per side, when is a team all out?

  1. At 4 wickets
  2. When the overs end
  3. At 5 wickets
The last batter has no partner, so the innings ends at players - 1 wickets.

Thousands of viewers read the score while the scorer writes. What is the simplest safe design?

  1. One writer per match publishing immutable snapshots that readers fetch
  2. Let readers iterate the live maps
  3. A global lock around every read and write
Single writer plus immutable snapshots gives consistent reads and never blocks the scorer.
