Clarify requirements (first 5-10 minutes)

Cricket has a thick rule book. You will not model all of it, so the first job is to agree on the slice. Say the rules back, write them as a comment at the top of Main, and get a nod.

Functional requirements

  1. Setup two teams with the same number of players, a batting order for each, and the number of overs.
  2. Ball input one token per delivery: 0-6 runs, W wicket, Wd wide, Nb or Nb4 no-ball (with runs off the bat).
  3. Extras a wide or no-ball adds 1 run to the team and to extras, and does not count as one of the six balls of the over (it is bowled again).
  4. Batting stats runs, balls faced, 4s and 6s per batter. A no-ball counts as a ball faced; a wide does not.
  5. Strike batters swap ends after an odd number of runs, and at the end of every over. On a wicket the next batter in the order comes in on strike.
  6. End of innings all out (one batter left without a partner), overs used up, or, in the second innings, the target passed.
  7. Output the scorecard after every over (and when the innings ends mid-over): each batter's line, a * for the two at the crease, total/wickets and overs as 1.4. Finally "Team 1 won the match by 4 runs" or "by 2 wickets" or "Match tied".
  8. Good to have bowler figures (overs, runs conceded, wickets, economy), strike rate, extras total; no bowler bowls two overs in a row.

Non-functional requirements

  • Correct: the Udaan sample must reproduce exactly. Rules live in one place.
  • Extensible: new outputs (commentary, live feed), new ball kinds (byes, leg byes) and new formats (T20, ODI, a super over) without rewriting the scoring loop.
  • Testable: the match is driven by input tokens, so tests are deterministic.
  • Safe to read live: one scorer writes, many viewers read (we come back to this in concurrency).

Out of scope (say it out loud)

  • Run-outs at the non-striker's end, byes and leg byes, overthrows, free hits, the toss, rain rules (DLS), multi-day Tests, fielding stats, persistence and the HLD of pushing scores to millions of phones. Each one gets a line in Extensibility.
