Identify entities: nouns, verbs and what changes

Underline the nouns in the statement (candidate classes) and the verbs (candidate methods). Then ask which parts the interviewer will change later; those get an interface. Finally, cross out nouns that are only numbers. Click through.

  1. Nouns: Match (two innings and a result), Innings (one team batting once), Team and Player, Ball (one delivery) and the score lines for batters and bowlers.
  2. Verbs: each lands on the class that owns the data. Reading a token is Ball's job; applying it is Innings' job; deciding the result and telling the world is Match's job.
  3. What changes: outputs (scorecard, commentary, live feed) become listeners; new ball kinds become a new enum value and parser case; formats are constructor inputs.
  4. Not a class: an Over is legalBalls / 6 unless you need over history; a run is an int; "strike" is two references; the scoreboard is a listener, not part of the model.

The key modelling choice is where the stats live. A common first draft puts runs and ballsFaced on Player. That breaks the moment a player plays a second match. Stats belong to an innings: Innings owns a Map<Player, BattingStats> and a Map<Player, BowlingStats>. Player stays a plain identity that can be reused across matches and seasons.

Should Over be a class? Say: "For the scorecard I only need the count of legal balls, so overs are derived. If you want over-by-over history, maidens or commentary per over, I will add an Over object that holds its balls and its bowler." That shows YAGNI with a clear upgrade path.
