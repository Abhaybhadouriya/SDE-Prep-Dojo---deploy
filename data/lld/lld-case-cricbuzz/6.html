Core flows: one ball, and one over of strike rotation

First, the call sequence for the last ball of the Udaan sample's first innings: P2 hits a six off P9, which is the sixth legal ball of the second and final over.

  1. The scorer (console, test or API) calls match.bowl("6"). Match first picks the current innings from its state.
  2. The token is parsed into an immutable Ball. A bad token throws here, before anything is changed.
  3. Match hands the ball to the current Innings. Match itself has no scoring rules.
  4. Innings updates the team total, the striker's line (runs, balls faced, a six) and the bowler's figures.
  5. It was the sixth legal ball, so the batters swap ends, the bowler is cleared (between overs) and Innings returns overEnded = true.
  6. Match notifies every listener: onBall, then onOverEnd. The printer prints the card; the live feed publishes "Team 1 23/2 (2 ov)".
  7. Match asks whether the innings is complete. Two overs are used up, so yes.
  8. Match ends the innings, creates the second innings with target 24 (first-innings runs + 1) and switches state.

Now the rule that most solutions get wrong: strike rotation. Here is over 2 of the Udaan sample, ball by ball. Watch the wide (re-bowled, no change of strike) and the end-of-over swap.

  1. Start of over 2: P1 is on strike (the batters swapped at the end of over 1). Score 7/0.
  2. W: P1 is out. The next in the order, P3, comes in on strike.
  3. 4: P3 scores four. Even runs, no swap.
  4. 4 again: P3 has 8 off 2 balls.
  5. Wd: one extra to the team, no ball faced, not a legal ball, no swap. It will be bowled again.
  6. W: P3 is out after facing 3 balls. P4 comes in on strike.
  7. 1: one run, so the batters cross. P2 is now on strike.
  8. 6: P2 hits six and has 10 off 4 balls. Total 23/2.
  9. That was the sixth legal ball, so the over ends and the batters swap: P4 would face the next ball.

The two classic bugs: counting a wide as one of the six balls (the over then ends one ball early), and forgetting the swap at the end of the over (the same batter faces every over). Both show up immediately when you run the Udaan sample, which is why the demo reproduces it.
