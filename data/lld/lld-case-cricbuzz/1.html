How it is asked

The cricket question comes in three shapes. Know which one you are in before you design anything:

  • Machine coding / take-home (Udaan style): working code, console input, printed scorecards. Graded on correctness of the cricket rules and on clean, extensible code.
  • LLD discussion with schema and APIs (Zepto, Cricbuzz backend): classes plus tables and REST endpoints; "expandable for future use cases".
  • Live updates (the "real-time notification system like Cricbuzz" variant): this drifts into HLD (fan-out to millions). In an LLD round, show the Observer seam and say where the HLD starts.

In our survey of 16 LLD courses and problem lists, a Cricinfo / Cricbuzz problem appears in 5 of them (Grokking's original list, Educative, AlgoMaster, awesome-low-level-design and the Concept && Coding playlist), and in 5 of the GitHub collections we scanned; the kumaransg/LLD collection tags it as an Udaan problem. In the 311 recent interview reports we collected (2024-06 to 2026-11) it was named once (Zepto), so treat it as a "rank 28" problem: less common than Parking Lot, but very likely at Indian product companies, and a perfect test of whether you can model rules cleanly.

The Udaan statement says: finish the "bare minimum" (team scorecards, strike changes, extras, winner) before adding bowler stats. That is a direct hint about how you are graded: a correct, running core first, extras second.
