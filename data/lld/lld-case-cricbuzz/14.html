Interview follow-ups (Q&A)

Why are batting stats not fields on Player?

Because a player plays many innings. Runs and balls faced are facts about one player in one innings, so Innings owns a Map<Player, BattingStats>. Career stats are then an aggregation over innings, not a field that has to be reset before every match.

How to answer

State the lifetime of the data: "who owns this number, and for how long?"

Likely follow-ups
  • How would you compute a player's career average? (Aggregate batting lines; persist them per innings.)
Is Over a class in your design?

Not in the core. Overs are derived from the count of legal balls (legalBalls / 6 and % 6), which removes a whole class of sync bugs. When we need maidens, over-by-over commentary or a "this over" strip, I add an Over holding its bowler and balls, created in startOver.

How to answer

YAGNI plus a concrete trigger for adding it.

Likely follow-ups
  • How do you detect a maiden? (An over whose balls conceded 0 runs to the bowler.)
Walk me through how a no-ball four is scored.

Nb4 parses to Ball(NO_BALL, batRuns 4, extras 1). Team total +5, extras +1, bowler +5 conceded. The striker gets +4 runs, +1 ball faced and a four. It is not a legal ball, so the over count does not move and it is bowled again. Four is even, so no swap.

How to answer

Split every delivery into "team runs", "batter runs", "extras", "legal ball?", "ball faced?" and "crossed?". The bugs hide in mixing these.

Likely follow-ups
  • What about a free hit after a no-ball? (A flag on the next delivery that blocks most dismissals.)
Where is the strike rotation logic, and why there?

In Innings.bowl(), in a fixed order: apply runs, handle the wicket (new batter on strike) or the odd-run swap, then the end-of-over swap. Innings owns the striker and non-striker references, so it is the information expert. Putting it in the console driver or a listener would duplicate the rule for every input source.

How to answer

Name the order of rules, then test it with the sample's over 2.

Likely follow-ups
  • A batter is run out going for a second run. Who is on strike? (Model which end the wicket fell at.)
Give me the database schema and APIs.

Store events: ball_event(innings_id, seq, over_no, ball_in_over, bowler_id, striker_id, type, bat_runs, extras, wicket_player_id) plus match, innings, team_player. Scorecards are derived by aggregation, or kept in materialised batting_line and bowling_line tables updated in the same transaction. APIs: POST /matches/{id}/balls with a sequence number, GET /matches/{id}/scorecard, and a push channel for live updates.

How to answer

"Events are the source of truth; everything else is a view." Then mention idempotency by sequence number.

Likely follow-ups
  • How do you correct a wrongly entered ball? (Update or void the event, recompute the lines.)
The scorer typed a wrong ball. How do you support undo?

Keep the ball log in the innings. Undo removes the last event and rebuilds the innings by replaying the rest through the same bowl() code (event sourcing). Replay is cheap (at most a few hundred balls) and cannot drift from the forward rules. A Command with a hand-written undo() is the alternative, but every rule then needs a reverse, which is easy to get wrong for strike and over changes.

How to answer

Prefer replay; explain why reverse operations are fragile here.

Likely follow-ups
  • Listeners already printed the wrong ball. Now what? (Emit a "correction" event; the live feed republishes.)
How would you push live scores to every viewer?

In the LLD, the seam is already there: a listener that publishes an immutable snapshot after each ball. Beyond one process it becomes HLD: the listener writes to a topic keyed by match id, a fan-out service holds WebSocket or SSE connections and pushes, and a cache serves the latest snapshot to pollers. Scores are tiny and change at most every few seconds, so snapshots, not diffs, are fine.

How to answer

Show the LLD seam first, then name where the HLD starts. Link it to pub-sub.

Likely follow-ups
  • A viewer reconnects. What do they receive first? (The latest snapshot, then the stream.)
Many matches at once, one scorer each, many readers. What do you lock?

Nothing global. Each match has a single writer (one thread or one queue partition keyed by match id), so Innings needs no locks. Readers never touch the live maps; they read an immutable snapshot published through an AtomicReference after every ball. That gives consistent reads without blocking the scorer.

How to answer

"Single writer per aggregate, immutable snapshots for readers."

Likely follow-ups
  • Two scorer devices for one match? (Pick a leader, or merge by sequence number and reject conflicts.)
Support T20, ODI and Test formats. What changes?

Introduce a MatchFormat: overs per innings (or none), innings per side, max overs per bowler, tie rule (super over or tie). T20 and ODI differ only in numbers. A Test match needs a list of innings, declarations and the follow-on, so Match's "who bats next and when is it over" logic moves into the format as a Strategy, and Match's states grow enough behaviour to justify the State pattern.

How to answer

Separate "numbers that differ" (config) from "flow that differs" (strategy).

Likely follow-ups
  • How is a Test result decided when time runs out? (Draw, a new result type.)
Add a super over when the match is tied.

Add a SUPER_OVER state. On a tie, Match creates two more innings of one over with three batters each (two wickets end it), team 2 bats first, and the result rule compares the super-over totals. Innings is reused unchanged because overs and player count are already parameters; only the player subset is new.

How to answer

Show that parameterising overs and players earlier made this cheap.

Likely follow-ups
  • The super over is tied too. (Repeat, per current rules; make it a loop.)
