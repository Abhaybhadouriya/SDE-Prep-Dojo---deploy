How the ranking was built

We did not guess. Every problem got a score from three independent kinds of evidence, then variants were merged (for example, "concert booking" counts as movie booking, and "doctor appointment" plus "fitness class booking" are one slot-booking case):

  1. GitHub (16 sources) The best-known LLD and machine-coding repositories (for example awesome-low-level-design, the Udit Agarwal reference solutions, the workat.tech Flipkart round repo). One point for each that solves or lists the problem.
  2. Courses and platforms (16 sources) Books, courses and free handbooks, from their public tables of contents (ByteByteGo's OOD course, Hello Interview, AlgoMaster, Coding Shuttle, workat.tech and others). One point for each that teaches it.
  3. Interview reports (311 posts) LeetCode Discuss experience posts dated June 2024 to November 2026, read one by one and normalised to a problem name. One point per report.

The scores are rough, and that is fine: the goal is an order, not a precise number. Two things are clear from the data. A small head of problems dominates (the top 10 have scores of 19 and above), and the long tail is still worth a look because companies like Atlassian, PhonePe and Razorpay have their own favourites that rarely appear elsewhere (rating systems, customer-issue resolution, in-memory search).

Where the reports come from: every report row links to its post on the Recently Asked page. Reports are self-written by candidates, so treat single reports as anecdotes and repeated ones as signal.
