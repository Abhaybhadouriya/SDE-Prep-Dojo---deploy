The story: one last ticket, two counters

Explain like I'm 5

A cinema has two ticket counters and one seat left. Riya walks to counter 1, Kabir to counter 2. Both clerks look at the same paper seat chart on the wall. Both see seat A1 is empty. Both say "yes, it is yours!" and both write a ticket. Two people walk in with tickets for one seat.

How could the cinema fix it? Three ideas:

  • Take the pencil first. Only one pencil for the chart. Whoever holds it may look and write; the other clerk waits. (This is a pessimistic lock.)
  • Check the eraser marks. Each clerk remembers how the chart looked. Before writing, they check nothing changed since they looked. If it changed, they say "sorry, let me look again". (This is optimistic locking.)
  • Put a coin on the seat. The clerk puts a coin on A1 while Riya finds her wallet. If she does not pay in 5 minutes, the coin is removed and the seat is free again. (This is a hold with a timeout.)

From your world

You already solve this every day. Grabbing a free slot in a bitmap with test_and_set_bit() is optimistic booking: one atomic instruction checks and claims, and the loser sees the bit already set. A cmpxchg loop on a descriptor state word is compare-and-set with a version. Taking a per-bucket spinlock before inserting into a hash table is pessimistic per-resource locking. The interview question is the same problem one level up, with users instead of CPUs and a database instead of RAM.
