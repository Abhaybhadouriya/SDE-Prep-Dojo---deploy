Scenario drills: when a question goes sideways

Scenario: you do not know the pattern they named

"Would you use the Specification pattern here?" You have never heard of it.
Weak answer: guessing a definition, or saying "yes" and hoping.
Strong answer: "I have not used it by that name. Can you describe it in a line?" Then reason from the description: "So each filter rule is an object and they combine with and/or. That fits the search filters here; I would put it behind the search service." Honest plus fast reasoning beats a bluff.
Follow-up you can expect: "Code one rule and the and() combinator." Two short classes.

Scenario: the interviewer disagrees with your answer

You said Strategy; they say "I think this is State."
Weak answer: switching immediately to agree, or arguing at length.
Strong answer: test it with the deciding question: "Does the object change its own behaviour as its status changes, or does the caller pick? Here the order moves itself from PLACED to SHIPPED, so you are right, State fits better. The pricing rule is still a Strategy."
Lesson: have a one-line test for every look-alike pair (who changes it, what it adds, what it controls).

Scenario: rapid-fire theory at the start of a design round

"Five minutes before we design: OOP pillars, SOLID, abstract class vs interface, and a thread-safe singleton." Reported in this form at Microsoft and Goldman Sachs.
Weak answer: three minutes on the first question, nothing left for the singleton.
Strong answer: 30 seconds each, one example each, then write the holder singleton in five lines. Say "I can go deeper on any of these" and move on.
Drill: pick five cards at random from the must-know filter, two minutes total, out loud.

Scenario: "make it thread-safe" with ten minutes left

Your parking lot works; the interviewer asks for thread safety.
Weak answer: putting synchronized on every method and calling it done.
Strong answer: name the invariant (one vehicle per spot), the race (two gates find the same free spot), the fix (claim the spot atomically: a per-floor lock or CAS on the spot state), and what stays lock-free (reads of the display board). Code the claim; describe the rest.
Follow-up: optimistic or pessimistic? Answer with the conflict rate.
