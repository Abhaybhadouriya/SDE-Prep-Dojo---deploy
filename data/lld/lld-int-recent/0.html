The story: asking the kids who took the test last week

Explain like I'm 5

Your class has a big test next month. The teacher will not tell you the questions. But kids from other schools took the same kind of test last week, and many of them wrote down what they were asked.

If you read 300 of those notes, you see patterns. "Build a parking lot" shows up again and again. "Split the bill between friends" shows up a lot too. Some questions only one kid ever saw.

So you practise the common ones first, until you can do them fast. Then you glance at the rare ones so nothing surprises you.

That is this page. We collected 311 reports of LLD and machine-coding rounds, wrote each one down in one line, and counted. The first 10 problems cover 87 reports (28%); the top 25 cover 163 (52%).

From your world

This is crash triage. When a kernel team gets thousands of oops reports, nobody reads them in arrival order. You bucket them by signature, sort buckets by count, fix the top buckets first and keep a long tail for later. Problems here are the "signatures"; each report is one crash dump with a date and a link back to the source.
