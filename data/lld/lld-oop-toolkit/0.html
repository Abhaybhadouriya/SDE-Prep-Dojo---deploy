The story: a well-labelled kitchen

Explain like I'm 5

Two friends cook the same dinner. The first one keeps spices in unlabelled bags. When he needs salt he tastes three bags first. When something burns, nothing beeps, so he finds out when the guests cough. He writes the shopping list on a whiteboard, and his little brother keeps rubbing items off.

The second friend has labelled jars, and there are only a few allowed kinds (salt, sugar, chilli). She uses one kind of box for leftovers, whatever the food. Her kitchen has a smoke alarm that says which pan is burning. Her shopping list is printed and laminated: nobody can change it, she prints a new one instead. Every jar has a name tag, so two jars of salt are clearly "the same thing". And her fridge has a rule for the queue: oldest milk in front.

The food tastes the same. But only the second kitchen can survive a busy evening, a new helper, or a surprise guest.

A machine-coding round is that busy evening. Each of the seven habits in the second kitchen has a tool in the language, and the next level names them one by one.
