How to choose: where does the change live?

Do not start from the pattern list. Start from the requirement and ask: what is going to change, or come in many kinds? The answer points to a family, then a pattern.

Walk-through: a vending machine, requirement by requirement

  1. Behaviour depends on the machine's current mode, and the mode changes itself (insert money moves idle to has-money). That is the State pattern, like a protocol state machine.
  2. Several interchangeable algorithms chosen per purchase: Strategy. A small factory turns "UPI" from the keypad into the right strategy object.
  3. Other parts must react to an event without the machine knowing who they are: Observer.
  4. Coin dispensing can be modelled as a chain of handlers (each denomination takes what it can and passes the rest). Many interviewers accept a simple greedy loop; say both.
  5. "Exactly one" is often better handled by creating one instance in main and injecting it. Reach for Singleton only when you must enforce it globally.
  6. Two or three patterns, each justified by a requirement. That is what a strong LLD answer looks like.
