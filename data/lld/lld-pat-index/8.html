When patterns help, and when they hurt

They help when
  • A requirement clearly says "many kinds of X" or "X will change" - payment modes, pricing rules, notification channels.
  • You need a shared vocabulary in a design review or interview ("State for the machine, Strategy for payment").
  • You want to add features by adding classes, keeping tested code untouched.
  • Testing needs seams: an interface you can replace with a fake.
They hurt when
  • Used for their own sake ("pattern-itis"): five interfaces for one implementation each.
  • The variation is imaginary. YAGNI: add the extension point when the second case is real or clearly asked for.
  • They hide simple logic behind indirection, so a reader must open six files to follow one call.
  • Singleton is used as a polite global variable, which hurts testing and hides dependencies.

Interview trap: evaluators check that each pattern solves a stated requirement. Prep guides such as Hello Interview's LLD course warn against forcing patterns in. Say "I'll use Strategy here because pricing has three plans and more are coming", never "I'll add Strategy, Factory, Observer and Builder".
