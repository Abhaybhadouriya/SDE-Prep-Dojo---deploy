How to present one of these in 10 minutes

  1. Restate the invariant (1 min): "At any moment exactly one thread may print" or "every 3 outputs are 2 H and 1 O".
  2. Name the tool and why (1 min): "A different thread hands over the turn, so a mutex is wrong; I will use semaphores."
  3. Write the fields first (1 min), with their starting values. The starting values are the whole design: Semaphore(1) vs Semaphore(0) decides who goes first.
  4. Write the methods (4 min), with every release in finally.
  5. Argue correctness (2 min): no lost wake-up, no deadlock (no cycle), it terminates, and it is fair enough.
  6. Offer the alternative (1 min): "With a single monitor and a stage counter it also works, and it generalises to more threads."
Print odd and even numbers alternately with two threads. Which primitive would you use, and why not a mutex?

Either two semaphores (odd starts with 1 permit, even with 0; each prints and releases the other's semaphore) or one monitor with a shared next counter where each thread waits while next % 2 is not its parity. A mutex alone is not enough: it only gives mutual exclusion, not ordering, and only the owning thread may unlock it, so it cannot be used to hand a turn to another thread.

How to answer

State the invariant (strict alternation), pick the tool, write 10 lines, then mention termination (both loops must stop at the limit and wake the other).

Likely follow-ups
  • Extend to three threads printing 1, 2, 3 in a cycle.
  • How do you cancel both threads cleanly?
In H2O, why is a semaphore alone not enough, and why is a barrier alone not enough?

Semaphores alone cap the group at 2 H + 1 O, but nothing stops a fast H from finishing, releasing its permit and letting a third H in before the O of the same molecule has printed, so groups interleave. A barrier alone makes three threads leave together, but it does not check their types, so H-H-H could form a group. You need the semaphores for composition and the barrier for "leave together".

How to answer

Split the problem into two rules and map each rule to a tool. That shows design thinking, not memorisation.

Likely follow-ups
  • Do it without a barrier (one monitor with two counters).
  • What if a molecule needs 2 C + 6 H + 1 O?
Give three different fixes for Dining Philosophers and name the Coffman condition each one removes.

(1) Global lock order (lower fork first) removes circular wait. (2) A waiter semaphore that lets at most 4 sit removes circular wait too, by making a full cycle impossible. (3) Take both forks atomically (std::lock, or tryLock both and release on failure) removes hold-and-wait. Preemption (stealing a fork) removes no-preemption but is rarely practical with locks.

How to answer

List the four Coffman conditions first, then map each fix to one of them; finish with "I would ship lock ordering".

Likely follow-ups
  • Your fix prevents deadlock; does it prevent starvation?
  • How would you detect a deadlock in a running JVM? (thread dump, jstack, ThreadMXBean.findDeadlockedThreads())
In the multithreaded web crawler, how do you know when the crawl is finished?

Not by an empty queue: a running task may still add links. Keep an atomic count of tasks submitted but not finished. Increment before submitting a child, decrement in finally at the end of a task; the task that brings it to zero opens a latch the caller waits on. Because each child is counted before its parent finishes, the count cannot touch zero while work remains.

How to answer

Name both hard parts (duplicates and termination) before writing code; use seen.add() as the atomic check-then-act.

Likely follow-ups
  • Limit to 10 requests per second per host.
  • One getUrls call hangs: add timeouts.
Why must wait() be called inside a while loop, and why while holding the lock?

While: the thread can wake without its condition being true (spurious wake-up, notifyAll waking everyone, or another thread consuming the state first), so it must re-check. Holding the lock: checking the condition and going to sleep must be one atomic step, otherwise the notify can happen between the check and the sleep and is lost. Java enforces this by throwing IllegalMonitorStateException if you call wait() without owning the monitor.

How to answer

Give both reasons, then the kernel parallel: wait_event() re-checks in a loop and uses prepare_to_wait() to avoid the lost wake-up.

Likely follow-ups
  • When is notify() safe?
  • What does Condition give you over wait/notify?
Design the shared-bathroom scheduler. What policy would you pick and what does it cost?

One lock, one condition, a FIFO queue of tickets, and the state (party, inside count). A person enters only when at the head and the room is empty or same-party with space. FIFO prevents starvation but wastes slots when the head belongs to the other party. Batching (switch sides after K entries when the other side waits) recovers throughput and still bounds waiting. Never hold the lock during the slow "use the room" step.

How to answer

Draw the state machine (Empty, D inside, R inside) first. Then list 2-3 policies with throughput vs starvation, pick one, and code it.

Likely follow-ups
  • Each person's time depends on their name: does that change your policy?
  • Add priorities without starving the low-priority party.
