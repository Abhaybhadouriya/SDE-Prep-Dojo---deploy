Core flow: adding an expense

  1. The driver (or a command parser) calls the facade with raw input: payer, rupees as a decimal, the split type, participants and values.
  2. The service takes its lock, checks every user exists and converts rupees to integer paise (1200.00 becomes 120000).
  3. It asks the factory for the strategy that matches the type. The service never branches on the type itself.
  4. The strategy validates its own inputs and returns the shares map. Shares always add up to the total.
  5. If validation fails, an exception is thrown before anything is stored. The sheet is untouched: all-or-nothing.
  6. Only now is an immutable Expense created, appended to history and recorded in the sheet: for each participant other than the payer, "participant owes payer share". The new expense goes back to the caller.

How the balance sheet updates

Keep one map: owed[creditor][debtor] = paise. For each share (not the payer's own), add the amount to owed[payer][participant] and subtract it from owed[participant][payer]. Both directions are always stored, so the map stays antisymmetric (owed[a][b] == -owed[b][a]) and netting happens by itself: if owed[A][B] is -250 (A owes B 250) and B later owes A 480, the entry moves to +230, which reads "B owes A 230". No separate netting step is needed. Entries that hit zero are removed so "show" never prints "owes 0.00".

Simplify debts

Compute each user's net balance (what they are owed minus what they owe). Put creditors and debtors in two max-heaps. Repeatedly match the biggest debtor with the biggest creditor and transfer the smaller of the two amounts; push back whatever is left. Each round settles at least one person, so there are at most n-1 transfers for n users. This greedy plan is good but not always the absolute minimum: finding the true minimum number of transfers is NP-hard in general (it hides a subset-sum problem; LeetCode 465 "Optimal Account Balancing" solves small cases with backtracking). Say this; interviewers like hearing that you know the limit.
