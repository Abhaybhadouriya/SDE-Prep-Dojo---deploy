How it is asked

Splitwise is the third most-asked LLD problem in our list, after parking lot and movie booking. It shows up in all three round styles (see How LLD rounds work): a 90-120 minute machine-coding round that must run, a 60-minute "LLD with running code" round, and a whiteboard round where classes, schema and follow-ups matter more than running code.

Older Swiggy reports use the same shape: a 2-hour machine-coding round, a short design chat, then the interviewer hands over test cases near the end and asks you to explain the design (LC 1777862). Open-source LLD collections also tag Flipkart, Razorpay and ShareChat for this problem. The free problem statement on workat.tech is the version most Indian candidates practise: command-style input (EXPENSE u1 1000 4 u1 u2 u3 u4 EQUAL, SHOW, SHOW u1) and amounts rounded to two decimals.

Two lessons from these posts. The Meesho candidate wrote clean OOP and was still rejected because one hidden test with an edge case failed (rounding and self-shares are the usual suspects). The Swiggy candidate was told "no running code" and then asked for it. Always aim for a runnable core in the first 45 minutes, whatever the interviewer says at the start.
