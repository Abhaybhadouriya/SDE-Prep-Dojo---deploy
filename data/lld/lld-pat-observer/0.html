The story: the "tell me when it's back" list

Explain like I'm 5

Your favourite toy shop has run out of the red racing car. You could walk to the shop every single day and ask "is it back yet?". That is tiring for you, and annoying for the shopkeeper.

Instead, the shopkeeper keeps a notebook. You write your name and phone number in it. Your friend does too. The day the cars arrive, the shopkeeper goes down the list and calls everyone. If you stop caring, you ask him to cross your name out.

The shopkeeper does not need to know who you are or why you want the car. He only knows "call everyone on the list when cars arrive".

That is Observer. The shop is the subject (the thing that changes). The people on the list are observers. Writing your name is subscribe (attach), crossing it out is unsubscribe (detach), and the calls are notify. Walking to the shop every day is polling, which Observer replaces.

From your world

You have registered observers in the kernel. A driver that cares about interfaces coming up or going down fills a struct notifier_block with a callback and calls register_netdevice_notifier(&nb). When eth0 changes, the core walks the chain and calls every block with NETDEV_UP, NETDEV_DOWN, NETDEV_CHANGEMTU and friends. The core does not know whether the listener is bonding, routing or your module; it knows only the callback signature. Unregistering in your module's exit function is "crossing your name out", and forgetting it is a classic oops after rmmod. Same pattern, same bug class as in Java.
