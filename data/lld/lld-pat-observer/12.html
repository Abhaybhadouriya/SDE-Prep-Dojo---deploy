Interview Q&A

Explain the Observer pattern with an example.

A subject keeps a list of observers behind one interface and calls them when its state changes. Example: a product's stock goes from 0 to 5; every customer on the "notify me" list gets an SMS, and the ops dashboard gets a low-stock check. The product knows only the StockListener interface, so new reactions are added by subscribing, not by editing the product.

How to answer

Subject, observer interface, subscribe, unsubscribe, notify; one concrete example; the decoupling benefit.

Likely follow-ups
  • Push or pull?
  • How does an observer unsubscribe?
Observer vs publish-subscribe: are they the same?

Same idea, different coupling. In Observer, the subject holds references to its observers and calls them, usually synchronously, in one process. In pub-sub, a broker (event bus, Kafka, SNS) sits between them: publishers send to a topic without knowing subscribers, subscribers can be in other processes, and the broker can buffer, persist and retry. Choose Observer for in-process reactions; pub-sub when you need decoupling in time, durability or scale.

How to answer

"Who holds the list?" is the key: the subject, or a broker. Then mention time decoupling and durability.

Likely follow-ups
  • Design an in-memory pub-sub with topics and offsets.
What is the lapsed listener problem and how do you prevent it?

The subject's list keeps a strong reference to every observer. Observers that are never unsubscribed cannot be garbage collected and keep receiving events, a memory and CPU leak. Prevent it by returning a subscription handle and closing it in the owner's lifecycle, binding subscriptions to a scope, and using weak references only as a safety net. In C kernels the same bug is a module unloaded without unregistering its notifier, which then calls freed code.

How to answer

Name it, explain the strong reference, give the handle fix first.

Likely follow-ups
  • Why are weak references not a complete fix? (Timing depends on the GC; an observer referenced only by the subject vanishes at once.)
One observer is slow (it calls an SMS API) and another throws. What happens, and how do you fix it?

With naive synchronous delivery, the slow observer blocks the subject's thread, so every stock update waits for the SMS call, and an exception aborts the loop so later observers are skipped. Fixes: wrap each call in try/catch and log; deliver slow observers asynchronously through an executor or a per-observer queue; add timeouts; for important deliveries, a durable queue with retries and a dead-letter queue.

How to answer

Describe both failure modes, then isolation (try/catch), then async, then durability.

Likely follow-ups
  • How do you keep per-observer ordering with async delivery? (One single-threaded queue per observer, or partition by key.)
How do you make the subject thread-safe?

Concurrent subscribe and notify can corrupt a plain list or throw ConcurrentModificationException. Use CopyOnWriteArrayList when subscriptions are rare and notifications frequent, since iteration works on a snapshot and observers can unsubscribe during the walk. Otherwise copy the list under a lock and call observers outside the lock. Never call foreign code while holding your lock: an observer that calls back into the subject or takes another lock can deadlock.

How to answer

Name the race, give COW with its trade-off (copy on every write), then the "no callbacks under lock" rule.

Likely follow-ups
  • How does the kernel solve it? (Atomic chains walk under RCU; blocking chains under an rw-semaphore.)
Why was java.util.Observer deprecated, and what do you use instead?

Deprecated in Java 9. Observable is a class, so your subject must extend it and cannot extend anything else; its events carry only an untyped Object; it gives no ordering guarantee and is not suitable for a rich event model. Use your own typed listener interface, PropertyChangeSupport for bean properties, or java.util.concurrent.Flow / a reactive library for async streams.

How to answer

Give one or two concrete reasons, then the replacement.

Likely follow-ups
  • What is back-pressure in Flow? (Subscription.request(n): the subscriber controls the rate.)
Design price alerts: users want a notification when a stock crosses a threshold. 1 million alerts, prices tick every second.

Do not make each alert an observer that the ticker calls on every tick; that is a million calls per tick per symbol. Make the ticker publish one event per symbol, and have a single AlertEngine observer per symbol that keeps alerts in two sorted structures (thresholds above and below the current price). On each tick it pops only the alerts that crossed, and hands them to an async notification queue. Each alert fires once and is then removed (one-shot unsubscribe).

How to answer

Show you know naive fan-out does not scale; index observers by what they care about.

Likely follow-ups
  • Prices jump past many thresholds at once?
  • How do you avoid duplicate SMS after a restart?
Where does Linux use Observer?

Notifier chains: register_netdevice_notifier for link events, reboot and CPU-hotplug notifiers, and more. Each notifier_block has a callback and a priority; the chain is walked in priority order and a callback may return NOTIFY_STOP or NOTIFY_BAD to end the walk. Atomic, blocking, raw and SRCU chain types choose the locking. From user space: inotify/fanotify and rtnetlink multicast groups.

How to answer

Name the struct, the register call, the return codes and the chain types.

Likely follow-ups
  • Why must unregister happen before module unload?
