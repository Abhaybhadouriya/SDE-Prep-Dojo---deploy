In interviews

Observer is a Tier 1 pattern. It is the expected answer whenever a requirement says "notify", "alert", "subscribe", "follow" or "when X changes, update Y", and interviewers then probe the hard parts on this page: leaks, threads, sync vs async, and Observer vs pub-sub. Where it appears in our case studies:

Case studySubjectObservers
Notification serviceOrder, account, campaign eventsSMS, email, push channels (often each one a Strategy too)
Pub-sub / message queueTopicSubscribers with their own offsets
Stock exchangeOrder book, tickerPrice alerts, charts, trade confirmations
Online auctionAuction itemBidders told when outbid or when the auction closes
CricbuzzLive matchScoreboard, commentary feed, user alerts
News feed, chat appUser, chat roomFollowers, room members (fan-out)
Elevator, parking lotElevator car, parking floorDisplay panels ("floor 3 full")

Strong 30-second answer: "Inventory shouldn't depend on SMS, search and analytics, so I'll make it a subject with a list of StockListeners. On change it pushes an immutable StockEvent. subscribe returns a handle so callers can unsubscribe and we don't leak. I'll use a CopyOnWriteArrayList and catch exceptions per listener. Slow listeners like SMS go through an executor so stock updates stay fast. If delivery has to survive a crash, I'd move to a durable queue: that's pub-sub, not Observer."
