Race 2: check-then-act (the double booking)

The second shape is a decision based on a value that is stale by the time you act on it. This is the bug interviewers poke at most, because every booking, allocation and inventory problem has it.

boolean book(String user) {
    if (seatsLeft > 0) {        // check
        chargeCard(user);       // any delay here widens the window
        seatsLeft--;            // act: the check may no longer be true
        return true;
    }
    return false;
}

Our demo forces the window open with a 10 ms sleep (standing in for a payment call) and releases both threads at once with a CountDownLatch (demo 2 of the full program):

2) check-then-act WITHOUT a lock
   ravi booked, seatsLeft=-1
   asha booked, seatsLeft=-1
   successful bookings for 1 seat: 2
2) check-then-act WITH synchronized
   asha booked, seatsLeft=0
   ravi sold out
   successful bookings for 1 seat: 1

The fix is to make check and act one atomic unit. Here, synchronized on the method does it. Other common forms of the same bug, with their one-line fixes:

Thread-safe parts do not make a thread-safe whole. ConcurrentHashMap.containsKey and put are each safe, but containsKey followed by put is still check-then-act. Compound actions need their own atomicity: a single atomic method on the collection, or your own lock around both calls.
