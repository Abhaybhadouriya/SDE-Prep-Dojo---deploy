From requirements to a diagram, step by step

Requirements: "A parking lot has several levels. Each level has spots for bikes, cars or trucks. A vehicle parks in a free spot that fits it and gets a ticket. On exit, the fee depends on hours parked; pricing rules will change." Click through:

  1. Nouns from the requirements become boxes: ParkingLot, Level, ParkingSpot, Vehicle. Only the fields and methods you need for the main use case (park, unpark).
  2. Connect them. A lot is made of levels, a level of spots (filled diamond = composition: parts die with the whole). A spot may hold one vehicle (0..1, plain association with an arrow for navigation).
  3. Vehicle kinds differ in behaviour, so Vehicle becomes abstract (italic) with Car and Bike as subclasses. The kind is also an enum, used by spots to decide what fits (dashed dependency).
  4. Pricing will change, so it goes behind an interface. ParkingLot holds one PricingStrategy; HourlyPricing realizes it.
  5. Ticket is a small immutable value created by park(). That is the whole diagram: 10 boxes, about 8 minutes on a whiteboard - enough to start coding.

Notice what is not on the diagram: getters, setters, constructors, logging, a ParkingLotManager with twenty methods. The diagram shows the decisions: what the entities are, who owns whom, where variation lives.
