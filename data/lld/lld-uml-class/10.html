Interview questions on class diagrams

Do they expect UML or code in the LLD round?

Both, in that order, with the balance depending on the round type. Candidates keep asking this on forums (Amazon SDE2, 2026-07; Walmart SDE-3, 2024-04; Microsoft/Expedia/Google, 2026-01). Reports suggest: a whiteboard LLD round (Amazon, Microsoft) wants a quick class diagram, then the key methods in real code; machine coding (Flipkart, Swiggy, PhonePe) wants running code, with the diagram mostly in your head or in a few comments; some interviewers skip the diagram and open the IDE (Walmart SSE, 2024-12).

How to answer

Ask at the start: "Would you like me to sketch the classes first, or go straight to code?" Then draw a minimal diagram (8-10 min) and move on.

Likely follow-ups
  • What do you leave out of the diagram?
  • Would you draw a sequence diagram too?
Should service/manager classes appear on the class diagram?

Yes - the orchestrator (BookingService, ParkingLot) is where the use cases live, and interviewers want to see its public methods. Show it with its collaborators (interfaces it depends on) and its key operations. Skip pure plumbing (controllers, DTO mappers, logging). This exact question is asked often on forums (2024-09; 2020-10).

How to answer

Say which classes are entities, which are value objects, and which is the coordinator - and draw all three kinds.

Likely follow-ups
  • Should bookTicket() live on Ticket or on a service?
How do you show an abstract class, an interface and an enum in UML?

Abstract class: name in italics (on a whiteboard write {abstract} or <<abstract>>); abstract operations also italic. Interface: <<interface>> above the name; implementing classes connect with a dashed line and a hollow triangle (realization), or use the lollipop. Enum: <<enumeration>> with the literals in the second compartment.

How to answer

Draw them quickly while answering; mention the whiteboard shortcut for italics.

Likely follow-ups
  • Generalization vs realization arrow?
What does multiplicity 0..1 vs 1 vs 1..* change in your code?

1: a required, non-null field set in the constructor (validate it). 0..1: nullable or Optional; every use must handle "absent". *: a collection that may be empty. 1..*: a collection that must not be empty - check in the constructor. Multiplicity is a cheap way to communicate invariants before writing code.

How to answer

Point at one link on your diagram and say the field type and the check it implies.

Likely follow-ups
  • Where do you enforce 1..* when items are removed later?
Do you put getters and setters on the class diagram?

No. They add noise and hide the real behaviour. Show attributes with visibility, and operations that carry meaning (park, canFit, cancel). If a field is read-only to the outside, mark it {readOnly} or say so. A diagram full of getX/setX is also a hint of an anemic model.

How to answer

Short answer, plus the anemic-model remark - it shows design taste.

Likely follow-ups
  • How do you show a constructor?
