The story: the chef and the pantry

Explain like I'm 5

A chef has to make soup. In restaurant A the chef walks out every morning, drives to a farm, picks tomatoes, buys salt from one particular shop, and only then cooks. If that farm closes, there is no soup. If you want to taste-test the soup with cheap practice tomatoes, you cannot, because the chef always goes to the same farm.

In restaurant B the kitchen helper puts the ingredients on the counter before the chef starts. The chef just says "I need tomatoes and salt" and cooks with whatever is put in front of her. Real tomatoes on a normal day, plastic tomatoes during training, a different farm next month. The chef never changes. Restaurant B uses Dependency Injection: things a class needs are handed to it, it does not go and get them.

A dependency is any object your class uses to do its job: a payment gateway, a repository, a notifier, a clock. Injection means someone else creates it and passes it in. The "someone else" is usually your main() (or a framework like Spring), and it is the only place that knows which concrete classes are used.

From your world

A Linux network driver does not reach out and grab the PCI device or the network stack. The bus core calls the driver's probe(struct pci_dev *pdev, const struct pci_device_id *id) and passes in the device it should drive. The driver in turn fills a struct net_device_ops and registers it; the core calls ndo_start_xmit when it has a packet. Neither side creates the other. Each receives what it needs through arguments or a registered table. That is Dependency Injection (the device is handed in) and Inversion of Control (the core calls you, you do not run the show) in C.
