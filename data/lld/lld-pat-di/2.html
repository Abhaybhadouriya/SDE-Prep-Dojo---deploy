Intent: three terms people mix up

Dependency Injection (DI): an object receives the objects it depends on from outside (through its constructor, a setter or a method parameter) instead of creating them or looking them up itself. The term was popularised by Martin Fowler's 2004 article on IoC containers.

TermWhat it isOne-liner
Dependency Inversion Principle (the D in SOLID)A design rule: high-level policy and low-level details should both depend on abstractions"Depend on interfaces, not on concrete classes."
Inversion of Control (IoC)A broad idea: a framework calls your code, instead of your code calling the library ("don't call us, we'll call you")"Who is in charge of the flow?"
Dependency InjectionA technique, one specific kind of IoC: control over creating and connecting dependencies moves out of the class"Who calls new?"
DI container (Spring, Guice, Dagger)A tool that does the wiring for you from annotations or config"Automated main()."

You can follow DIP without DI (a factory hands out interfaces), and do DI without a container (pass objects in from main). In LLD interviews you almost always do the second: plain constructor injection, wired by hand.

The DIP page covers the principle; this page covers the mechanics.
