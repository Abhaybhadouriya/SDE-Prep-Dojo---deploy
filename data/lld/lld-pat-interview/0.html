The story: the toolbox test

Explain like I'm 5

A grown-up opens a toolbox and asks you three things. First: "What is this?" (a screwdriver). Second: "This shelf is wobbly. Which tool do you pick?" (the screwdriver, because the wobble is a loose screw). Third: "Why not the hammer?" (a hammer would hit the screw in, and you could never take it out again).

A pattern interview is the same test. A design pattern is a named tool for one kind of change in code. The interviewer checks that you know the names, that you can pick the right one for a problem, and, most of all, that you can say why not the others. A child who brings the whole toolbox to fix one screw does not pass. Nor does a candidate who puts six patterns into a chess game.

This page is the practice room. If a pattern name here is new to you, read its own lesson first: start at the patterns overview, then come back. If you know the names but mix up the pairs (Strategy and State, Decorator and Proxy), the look-alike patterns page goes deeper than the comparison questions here.

From your world

You already use most of these patterns, just without the names. A struct file_operations filled with function pointers is Strategy (or Bridge). A notifier chain (register_netdevice_notifier()) is Observer. Netfilter hooks, where each hook returns NF_ACCEPT, NF_DROP or passes the packet on, are Chain of Responsibility. A TCP connection moving from SYN_SENT to ESTABLISHED is State. In an interview, say the pattern name and then the kernel example. It shows you know the idea, not just the textbook.
