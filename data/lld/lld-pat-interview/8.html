Q&A: judgement questions (using, defending and refusing patterns)

"How many design patterns do you know? Explain a few."

Reported at MakeMyTrip (2025-07-25) and Walmart (2025-05-25). Do not recite 23 names. Say: "The GoF book has 23 in three families: creational, structural and behavioral. The ones I use most are Strategy, Factory, Observer, Builder and Decorator." Then explain two in one line each with where you used them. Pick the two you can defend for five minutes.

How to answer

Number, families, top five, two with real examples. Under a minute. It is an opener; the follow-up is the real question.

Likely follow-ups
  • "Which one is most overused?" (Singleton)
  • "Which did you use in your last project?" (next question)

How to answer

Pick one, describe the problem it solved and the alternative you avoided ("a giant switch on device type in the core"). The patterns in the Linux kernel page has more mappings with sources.

Likely follow-ups
  • "How would you write that in Java?" (interface plus implementations; the vtable is generated for you)
When should you NOT use a design pattern? How do you avoid over-design?

An Amazon SDE-2 candidate used Strategy for chess rules and Factory for pieces; the feedback was that the design was over-designed (2025-02-09). Another Amazon SDE-2 candidate answered a question about taking in query parameters with "decorator, factory and strategy" and calls it a red flag (2025-08-31).

  • Add a pattern only for a change you can name in the requirements ("new vehicle types every quarter"), not for a change you imagine (YAGNI).
  • If there is one implementation and no second in sight, skip the interface. You can extract it later in a minute.
  • Answer the question asked. If they ask how input is parsed, talk about parsing, not about patterns.
  • Count classes against features: 15 classes for a 3-feature problem is a smell.
How to answer

"A pattern is a cost I pay for a change I expect. If I can't name the change, I don't pay." Then give the over-design example. See KISS and YAGNI and common LLD mistakes.

Likely follow-ups
  • "But you'd need it later." ("Then I'd refactor with tests; extracting an interface is cheap.")
Your design uses one pattern, the interviewer clearly expects another. What do you do?

A Razorpay SDE-2 candidate built a logger two ways (Chain of Responsibility, and Facade + Factory) and reports that the interviewer knew only one solution and wanted to hear it (2024-03-05). Do three things: (1) ask what they would like to optimise for ("Are you thinking about adding log levels later?"), (2) briefly compare your choice and theirs in one table row each, (3) offer to switch, and switch cleanly. You score on judgement and collaboration, not on winning.

How to answer

Curious, short, flexible. Never "my way is better". Say "both work; theirs is better if X, mine if Y; since you mention X, let me switch."

Likely follow-ups
  • The interviewer may be testing whether you can defend a good choice: if your reason is strong, state it once, calmly.

A Salesforce SMTS candidate used Factory, Strategy and Singleton "as applicable" and then got concurrency questions (2025-02-22). Parking lot was the most reported LLD problem in our 2024-26 scan.

Tie every pattern to a requirement sentence. The concurrency follow-up (two cars, one spot) is about locking the spot, not about patterns. Full design: Parking Lot.

Should the database connection be a singleton?

Not one connection, no. A single shared connection serialises every query and breaks if the connection drops. What should be one-per-application is the connection pool (HikariCP, for example), which hands out many connections and reuses them. And even the pool is best created once at startup and injected, not reached through a static getInstance(). An old but popular LeetCode thread asks exactly this (2018-01-03).

How to answer

"One pool, many connections, injected." That one line corrects the premise politely. Mention Object Pool by name: Object Pool.

Likely follow-ups
  • How big should the pool be? (small, near the DB's core count times a small factor; measure)
What is MVC, and is it a design pattern?

Discussed in a Razorpay SDE-1 photo-sharing LLD (2024-10-18). MVC is an architectural pattern made of smaller ones: the Model notifies Views when it changes (Observer), the View is often a tree of widgets (Composite), and the Controller is the View's way to handle input (Strategy, swappable per mode). In backend LLD, its cousin is the layering Controller, Service, Repository.

How to answer

Name the three parts and the three patterns inside. Then map it to your design: "my controller is the CLI driver, the service holds the logic, the repository keeps data in maps". See Repository & Service Layer.

Likely follow-ups
  • Where does validation go? (input format in the controller, business rules in the service)
Rapid fire: five look-alike pairs in one sentence each.
  • Composite vs Decorator: both recursive; Composite has many children (a tree), Decorator wraps exactly one (a chain).
  • Chain of Responsibility vs Decorator: both chains; any CoR link may stop the request, every decorator forwards it.
  • Command vs Strategy: both wrap behaviour; Command is what to do (queue it, undo it), Strategy is how to do one job.
  • Observer vs Pub-Sub: Observer's subject knows its observers; Pub-Sub puts a broker between them (and often time).
  • Memento vs Command (undo): store snapshots of state vs store reversible operations.
How to answer

Always the same move: "same shape, different intent", then the one word that differs. Deeper: Look-Alike Patterns.

Likely follow-ups
  • Adapter vs Bridge, Factory Method vs Abstract Factory (covered above)
Which patterns change when the design must be thread-safe?
  • Singleton: lazy creation must be safe (holder, enum, DCL with volatile), and its state must be guarded.
  • Observer: iterate over a copy (CopyOnWriteArrayList), and decide which thread runs callbacks.
  • State: a transition is read-modify-write; guard it with a lock or AtomicReference.compareAndSet.
  • Strategy and Decorator: fine if stateless; a stateful strategy (a rate counter) needs its own synchronisation.
  • Flyweight and immutable value objects: safe to share by design. Immutability is the cheapest thread safety.

Uber reports keep pairing LLD with concurrency (for example ride hailing with multithreading, 2026-08-22).

How to answer

Go pattern by pattern and name the shared mutable state in each. Deeper: Thread-Safe Singleton & Immutability, Concurrency Interview Questions.

Likely follow-ups
  • Producer-Consumer as a pattern (bounded blocking queue)
