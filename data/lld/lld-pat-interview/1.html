The five shapes of a pattern question

In the reports we read, pattern questions came in five shapes. Each shape is testing something different, so each needs a different kind of answer.

ShapeTypical wordsWhat a strong answer has
Define it"What is a singleton class? What is its purpose?"One-line intent, one real use, one cost. Thirty seconds.
Code it"Write a singleton with a driver class. Make it thread-safe." "Write the builder."Compiling code from memory, then the follow-up (volatile, reflection, serialization).
Pick it"Which design pattern is the right fit?" "The problem hints at a pattern."Name what varies first, then the pattern, then a one-line reason the others lose.
Compare it"Strategy vs State", "Decorator vs Proxy", "Factory vs Abstract Factory"Same shape, different intent: who decides, who owns, what changes.
Defend it"Why is this a singleton?" "Isn't this over-designed?"Admit the cost and name the simpler option you rejected.

Many reports mix shapes: a "Code it" (singleton) followed straight away by a "Defend it" (how can it be broken? why do you need it if Spring gives you beans?). Have the follow-up ready before you finish the first answer.
