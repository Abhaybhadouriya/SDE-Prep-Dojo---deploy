The story: the pilot's checklist

Explain like I'm 5

Before every flight, even a pilot with 20 years of experience reads a checklist: fuel, doors, flaps, radio. Not because they forgot how to fly, but because when you are nervous or in a hurry, your brain skips steps. The checklist means nothing important gets skipped, and things happen in the right order - you check the fuel before takeoff, not after.

An LLD interview is a short, nervous flight. The framework on this page is your checklist. Eight steps, in order, each with a time limit, so you never end up with a beautiful diagram and no code, or code that solves the wrong problem.

Without a framework, candidates fail LLD rounds in a small number of predictable ways. They start coding in minute two and discover in minute thirty that they misunderstood the problem. They spend twenty minutes on the class diagram and never write the method that matters. They add five patterns because they memorised five patterns. Or they finish, and the first "what if" question forces a rewrite. Each step below exists to stop one of those failures.

From your world

You already follow this script at work, just slower. A new driver or protocol feature starts with a spec (step 1), a list of the structures you need (step 2), how they point at each other (step 3), the ops table the rest of the kernel will call (step 4), deciding which parts are pluggable (step 5), writing the code (step 6), keeping it open for the next hardware revision (step 7) and choosing the locking (step 8). The interview compresses weeks into 45 minutes; the order is the same.
