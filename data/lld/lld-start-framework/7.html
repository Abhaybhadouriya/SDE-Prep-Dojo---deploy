Step 5 - Patterns at the change points (3-5 min)

Go back to your requirements and ask, for each rule: "Is there more than one way to do this, now or soon?" Where the answer is yes, put an interface and name the pattern. Where the answer is no, do not. That one habit separates a credible pattern choice from a recited one.

Signal in the requirementsPatternExample
"pick / compute / rank in different ways"StrategyRoom selection, parking fees, payment methods, ride matching
"behaves differently depending on its status"StateVending machine, elevator, order lifecycle
"notify / when X happens, tell Y"ObserverNotifications, stock price alerts, auction bids
"create the right kind of thing from a type"FactoryVehicle, spot, chess piece, notification channel
"pass along until someone handles it"Chain of ResponsibilityLog levels, ATM note dispensing, approval flows
"add extras on top" (toppings, discounts)DecoratorPizza or coffee pricing, stacked discounts
"undo / redo / queue an action"CommandText editor, task scheduler
"exactly one shared instance"Singleton (thread-safe)A registry or manager - and say why DI might be better

For the meeting rooms, only one rule varies (how to choose a room), so we use exactly one pattern: Strategy. Saying "I considered Observer for notifications, but notifications are out of scope" is a stronger answer than adding it.

From your world

A Strategy is a function-pointer slot you can swap at runtime. The kernel's I/O schedulers (mq-deadline, bfq, kyber) are a textbook case: the block layer calls through struct elevator_mq_ops and you switch scheduler per device by writing to /sys/block/<dev>/queue/scheduler. BookingService.setStrategy() is the same move.
