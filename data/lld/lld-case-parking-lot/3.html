Identify the entities

Use the oldest trick in object-oriented design: read the requirements and underline nouns (candidate classes) and verbs (candidate methods). Then throw away the nouns that are only actors, outside systems or plain values. Click through the steps.

  1. Underline every noun. Each is a candidate class, but not all of them will survive.
  2. Underline every verb. These become methods; the noun that owns the data a verb needs is the class that gets the method.
  3. Drop actors (driver, gate: they call our API) and turn fixed vocabularies (size, type) into enums. "Fee" changes by rule, so it becomes a strategy.
  4. The final list: 7 classes, 2 enums, 3 interfaces. That is enough; do not add Floor, Row, Barrier or Attendant unless asked.
EntityKindResponsibility (one line)
ParkingLotService / facadeThe API the gates call: park, unpark, free counts. Owns levels and active tickets.
LevelEntityOwns its spots and the free set per size; hands out and takes back spots atomically.
ParkingSpotEntity (immutable)Identity (level, number) and size. Free or taken is tracked by the level, not by a flag on the spot.
VehicleValue objectPlate and type.
Ticket, ReceiptValue objectsProof of entry; proof of payment.
SpotAllocationStrategyInterfacePicks a spot for a vehicle.
FeeStrategyInterfacePrices a stay.
ParkingEventListenerInterfaceHears about entries and exits (display board, analytics).
VehicleFactory, FeeStrategyFactorySimple factoriesTurn raw input and config strings into valid objects.

Why no isFree flag on the spot? If both the spot and the level remember "taken", the two can disagree after a bug or a race. One source of truth (the level's free set) removes a whole class of bugs. Kernel people know this as "do not keep the same state in two places without a lock that covers both".
