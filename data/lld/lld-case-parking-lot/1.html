Three patterns jump out of that table. First, code is expected more often than not, even at Amazon and Microsoft. Second, the follow-ups are always the same few: pricing rules (peak hours, vehicle-type rates), allocation rules (nearest, overflow into a bigger spot), concurrency (two gates, one spot) and reservations. Third, one Uber report says "negative despite working code": working code is necessary, not sufficient. The design must be explainable and extensible.

Interviewers love hearing, in the first minute: "I will keep allocation and pricing behind interfaces, because those are the two rules that change most often in a real car park."
