How it works: pricing a stacked order

The till receives new Milk(new ExtraShot(new Espresso())) and calls pricePaise() once.

  1. The till holds a Beverage. It happens to be a Milk, but the till cannot tell and does not care.
  2. Milk cannot price itself alone, so it first asks the object it wraps. Here the "before" part of the decorator is empty.
  3. ExtraShot does the same and forwards to its inner object.
  4. The Espresso at the bottom answers directly: 12000 paise. Recursion stops at the concrete component.
  5. On the way back, ExtraShot runs its "after" part and adds 3000.
  6. Milk adds 2000 and returns 17000 to the till. Swap the wrappers and the same objects give a different order of work, which matters as soon as one decorator multiplies instead of adds (LargeSize in the code).

Every decorator method follows one template: optional work before, forward to inner, optional work after. Logging and timing wrap both sides; caching may skip the forward entirely on a hit; encryption transforms the data on the way down and on the way back up.
