The story: the coffee counter

Explain like I'm 5

You order a coffee. The barista pours an espresso into a cup. You say "with milk", and she adds milk. "And another shot", and she adds a shot. It is still a cup of coffee you can drink and pay for. Each extra just sits on top of what was already in the cup.

At the till, the cashier does not have a price list with every possible mix ("espresso with milk and two shots and caramel"). She asks the top layer: "What do you cost?" Milk says "20 rupees plus whatever is under me". The shot says "30 plus whatever is under me". The espresso at the bottom says "120". Add them up and you have the price.

That is the Decorator pattern. A decorator looks exactly like the thing it wraps (same interface), holds a reference to it, and does a little extra before or after passing the call down. Because a decorator is itself the same type, you can wrap a decorator in another decorator, in any order, at run time.

From your world

Device-mapper is decorators for block devices. dm-crypt takes a block device and gives you a new block device: same interface (read and write sectors), but every bio is encrypted on the way down and decrypted on the way up. You can stack it on an LVM linear volume, which is itself a dm device over a disk partition. The file system on top neither knows nor cares how many layers are below it. Stackable file systems such as eCryptfs and overlayfs do the same for the VFS: each exposes the normal file interface and forwards to a lower file system with something added. The kernel also wraps an ops table in another ops table: debugfs hands out proxy file_operations that check the file was not removed and then call the real ones.
