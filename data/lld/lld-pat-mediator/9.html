In interviews

Mediator is a Tier-3 pattern: rarely asked by name, but it is the cleanest answer whenever a case study has a "room", a "house" or a "controller" that several actors go through.

  • Online auction: the auction is the mediator between bidders and seller (increments, reserve price, auto-bid, closing). Public LeetCode Discuss threads on designing an online auction list exactly these rules: minimum increment, hidden reserve price, auto-bidding.
  • Chat app: the room or server routes messages, DMs, mutes and joins. A reported round asked for a complete chat-room service, server and clients, in one hour.
  • Elevator: a dispatcher (controller) assigns requests to cars; cars do not negotiate with each other.
  • Traffic signal: an intersection controller coordinates lights so two crossing directions are never green together.
  • Chess and other turn-based games: a game object mediates between players, board and clock.

When you introduce a controller class in a case study, say the word: "The ElevatorController is a mediator; cars only report state and accept commands, so dispatch strategies can change without touching cars." Then pre-empt the follow-up: "To stop it becoming a god class, the dispatch policy itself is a Strategy injected into the controller."

Strong 30-second answer: "Mediator replaces many-to-many links between objects with one coordinator. Colleagues report events to it and it decides who reacts, so the rules live in one place and colleagues are reusable. The risk is a god object, so I keep colleague logic in colleagues and split mediators per use case. Unlike Observer, which only broadcasts, a mediator coordinates; unlike Facade, traffic goes both ways."
