Interview Q&A

What is the difference between Mediator and Observer?

Observer is one-to-many broadcast: the subject announces "I changed" and does not care who listens or what they do. Mediator is many-to-many coordination through a hub: colleagues report events, and the mediator contains the logic that decides which colleagues should do what. A mediator often uses callbacks internally, so the mechanics can look alike; the intent differs. A chat room that applies mute rules and routes DMs is a mediator; a stock ticker pushing prices to charts is an observer.

How to answer

Direction and who owns the logic, then one example each, then "they can be combined".

Likely follow-ups
  • Is an event bus a mediator? (A mediator with no rules: it routes but does not coordinate.)
Mediator vs Facade: both sit in the middle. What differs?

A Facade is a simplified entry point for clients into a subsystem; traffic flows one way and the subsystem classes do not know the facade exists. A Mediator sits between peers that know it and call it; traffic flows both ways and the mediator coordinates them. Removing a facade leaves the subsystem working (clients just call it directly); removing a mediator breaks the colleagues, because they depend on it.

How to answer

Who knows whom, direction of calls, and the "what if you remove it" test.

Likely follow-ups
  • Can one class be both? (Yes, an order service can be a facade for the controller and a mediator between inventory and payment, but name the roles separately.)
Your ChatRoom mediator has grown to 2,000 lines. How do you fix it?

It has become a god object. Move behaviour that belongs to one colleague back into that colleague (formatting, user preferences). Split the mediator by concern: message routing, moderation (mute, ban, rate limits), presence. Make policies pluggable: a ModerationPolicy strategy or a chain of filters (Chain of Responsibility) the router calls. Keep the mediator interface small, a few event methods, and let it delegate.

How to answer

Name the smell, then three refactorings: push down, split by concern, extract policies.

Likely follow-ups
  • How do you test the moderation rules alone?
Design air-traffic control for one runway. Which pattern and why?

Mediator. The tower owns the runway and a landing queue. Planes call requestLanding() and runwayVacated(); the tower answers with clearedToLand() or hold(position). Planes never talk to each other, so the "only one plane on the runway" invariant is enforced in one place. For several runways, the tower holds a runway pool and an assignment strategy; for emergencies, a priority queue instead of a FIFO.

How to answer

State the invariant, show the two-way API, then extend (more runways, priority, concurrency with a lock in the tower).

Likely follow-ups
  • Planes run on separate threads. Where does synchronisation go? (In the mediator, around the runway state.)
How does Linux bind a driver to a device, and what pattern is it?

Devices and drivers both register with a bus. On device_add() the core walks the bus's drivers; on driver_register() it walks the bus's devices. For each pair it calls the bus's match() (PCI IDs, device-tree compatible strings, and so on); on a match it calls the driver's probe(). Neither side looks the other up, so arrival order does not matter and drivers stay independent of particular devices. That is the Mediator pattern, with the bus type supplying the matching rule.

How to answer

Two registration paths, match then probe, then name the pattern and the benefit (order independence, reuse).

Likely follow-ups
  • What happens on hot-unplug? (The core calls the driver's remove() and unbinds.)
Saga orchestration vs choreography: which one is a mediator?

Orchestration is the mediator: a central orchestrator tells the order, payment and inventory services what to do next and handles compensation on failure. Choreography is closer to Observer: each service reacts to events from others and nobody owns the whole flow. Orchestration makes the flow visible and easy to change in one place but adds a central component; choreography removes the hub but spreads the flow across services and is harder to trace.

How to answer

Map each style to a pattern, then one pro and one con each.

Likely follow-ups
  • How do you make the orchestrator reliable? (Persist saga state, idempotent steps, retries.)
