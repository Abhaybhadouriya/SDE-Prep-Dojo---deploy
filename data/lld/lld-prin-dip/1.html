The two rules, in plain words

Robert C. Martin stated DIP as two rules:

  1. High-level modules should not depend on low-level modules. Both should depend on abstractions.
  2. Abstractions should not depend on details. Details should depend on abstractions.

Three words to define first:

WordMeaningExample
High-level moduleThe policy: what the business does and why. The part that would still be true if you changed every technology.OrderService.place(): validate, save, notify
Low-level moduleThe mechanism: how one step is done with one technology.MySQL DAO, SMTP mailer, Razorpay client
AbstractionAn interface (or abstract class) that says what is needed, not how.OrderRepository, Notifier

Why "inversion"? In old layered code, the arrows of "who imports whom" follow the arrows of "who calls whom": the business layer calls the database layer, so it imports it. DIP keeps the call going down but turns the source-code dependency upside down: the database code imports an interface that belongs to the business layer.
