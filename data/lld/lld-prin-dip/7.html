DIP vs DI vs IoC (and Service Locator)

These four names are often mixed up. Interviewers ask about them because the mix-up is common.

NameWhat it isOne-line example
DIP - Dependency Inversion PrincipleA design principle about the direction of source-code dependencies and who owns abstractionsOrderService depends on OrderRepository, which it owns
DI - Dependency InjectionA technique: supply collaborators from outside (constructor, setter, parameter)new OrderService(repo, notifier)
IoC - Inversion of ControlA broad idea: a framework or caller controls the flow and calls your code ("don't call us, we'll call you")Servlet container calls doGet(); a test runner calls your @Test; DI containers are one kind of IoC
Service LocatorAn alternative to DI: the object asks a global registry for its collaboratorsRegistry.get(Notifier.class) inside place() - dependencies hidden

Important: you can do DI without DIP (inject a concrete MySqlOrderDao class - still coupled to MySQL), and you can do DIP without a DI container (plain new calls in main(), often called "pure DI"). In a 90-minute machine-coding round, pure DI in main() is exactly right; pulling in Spring is not.
