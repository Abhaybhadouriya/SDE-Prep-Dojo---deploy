  • DIP: high-level policy and low-level details both depend on abstractions; abstractions do not depend on details.
  • What is inverted is the source-code dependency and the ownership of the interface - not the runtime call.
  • The policy package owns the interface, shaped by what the policy needs (save(Order), not executeSql).
  • Constructor injection by default; wire concrete classes in one composition root (main() in machine coding).
  • DIP = principle, DI = technique, IoC = "framework calls you", Service Locator = alternative that hides dependencies.
  • Kernel: net_device_ops / file_operations are abstractions owned by the core; drivers depend on them.
  • Apply at volatile boundaries (I/O, third parties, clock, varying policies); do not interface every class.
