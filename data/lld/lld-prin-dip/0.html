The story: the lamp and the wall socket

Explain like I'm 5

Imagine if every lamp in your house was soldered straight onto the wires of one power station. Want a new lamp? Call an electrician. Power station changes? Re-solder every lamp. Want to test a lamp? You need a whole power station.

Real houses use a wall socket. The lamp only knows "a socket gives me power". The power company, the solar panel on the roof and the backup battery all plug into the same socket shape. You can test a lamp with a small battery box that has the same socket.

The socket is an abstraction. The lamp (the thing you care about) and the power source (the detail) both depend on the socket, and neither depends on the other. That is the Dependency Inversion Principle.

DIP is the "D" in SOLID. In code, the "lamp" is your business logic (place an order, match a rider to a driver, compute a fare). The "power stations" are details: MySQL, Kafka, an SMTP server, a payment gateway SDK. DIP says the business logic should never be soldered to those details.
