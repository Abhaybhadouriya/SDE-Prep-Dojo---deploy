The story: the school complaint

Explain like I'm 5

You lost your water bottle at school. You tell your class monitor. If the monitor can help (it is under your desk), done. If not, the monitor takes you to the class teacher. If the teacher cannot help either, you go to the principal.

You did not need to know who would solve it. You just told the first person in the line, and each person either solved it or passed it up. Nobody had to know the whole line, only "who is next after me".

That line of people is a chain of responsibility. The sender gives the request to the first handler; each handler decides "I handle it", "I pass it on", or "I stop it here".

From your world

Every IPv4 packet that enters a Linux box walks a chain. At the NF_INET_PRE_ROUTING hook point, every registered struct nf_hook_ops runs in priority order (conntrack, mangle, NAT, filter...). Each hook function returns a verdict: NF_ACCEPT means "fine by me, next please"; NF_DROP means "stop, free the packet"; NF_STOLEN means "I took it, stop walking"; NF_QUEUE sends it to user space. That is Chain of Responsibility in production, running millions of times a second.
