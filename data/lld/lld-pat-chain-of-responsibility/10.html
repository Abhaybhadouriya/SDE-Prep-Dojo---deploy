In interviews

Chain of Responsibility shows up as a named sub-problem more often than Command. The research notes for this module list ATM cash dispensing and approval workflows as the standard CoR prompts, and an ATM with CoR plus State as a common combination. Where to use it:

  • ATM: the note dispenser chain (this page), inside a State machine for the ATM itself.
  • Logger: level-filtered handlers (console, file, remote) as a chain; formatters as a separate concern.
  • Customer issue resolution: escalation from agent to senior agent to manager.
  • Rate limiter: one handler in a request pipeline; explain where in the chain it sits and why.
  • Vending machine: returning change by coin denomination is the ATM problem again.
  • Food delivery or shopping cart: order validation (stock, address serviceable, coupon valid, payment limit) as a chain of validators.
  • Digital wallet: transfer checks (KYC level, daily limit, fraud score) before the money moves.

Strong 30-second answer: "Chain of Responsibility puts handlers in an ordered chain; each one handles the request, passes it on, or stops it, so the sender does not know who handles it and I can reorder or add steps without changing it. I always add a default at the end so nothing falls off. Unlike Decorator, a handler can stop the request. Netfilter hooks are a production example: each hook returns ACCEPT to continue or DROP to stop."
