From your world

Three kernel chains, and which style each one is:

  • Netfilter hooks: pipeline style. Every hook gets a turn in priority order until one returns a verdict other than NF_ACCEPT.
  • Notifier chains (blocking_notifier_call_chain() and friends): callbacks sorted by priority; a callback can return NOTIFY_STOP or NOTIFY_BAD to stop the walk. Usually used as Observer (everyone hears about a reboot or a netdevice going up), but the stop bit makes it a chain too.
  • Shared interrupt handlers: all handlers on a shared IRQ line are called; each returns IRQ_HANDLED or IRQ_NONE, and the core uses the answers to detect spurious interrupts. Nobody stops the walk, so this is the pass-through-to-all style, closer to Observer than to classic CoR.

Same data structure, different stop rule. That is a good sentence to say in an interview. See patterns in the Linux kernel.
