How it works: the ATM dispenser walks the chain (and backs up)

The interview favourite: an ATM holds notes of 2000, 500, 200 and 100, each in limited numbers. Each denomination is a handler; each takes as many notes as it can and passes the rest on. A real prompt reported on LeetCode Discuss (post) includes a trap: with one 500, four 200s and no 100s, a request for 600 must give 200 x 3. A purely greedy chain takes the 500, is left with 100, and fails. So our handlers try the most notes first and back off if the rest of the chain cannot finish. Click through withdraw(600):

  1. The ATM gives 600 to the head of the chain. The 2000 handler cannot take any note (600 / 2000 = 0), so it passes 600 on.
  2. The 500 handler takes as many as it can: one (its whole stock). It passes the remaining 100 on.
  3. The 200 handler cannot make 100 with 200s. It passes 100 on.
  4. The 100 handler is empty. It is the end of the chain and the amount is not zero, so it returns false, and so does the 200 handler.
  5. Back at the 500 handler: the greedy choice failed, so it tries one note fewer (zero) and passes all 600 on.
  6. The 200 handler takes three notes: 600 exactly. The rest is 0, success flows back to the head.
  7. Only now does the ATM commit the plan and reduce stock. Planning first and committing after means a failed withdrawal never leaves the machine half-dispensed.

Say the trade-off out loud: with unlimited notes in "canonical" denominations, greedy is optimal and simple; with limited stock, greedy can fail, and you need backtracking (or a small DP). Interviewers often ask "what if the 500 slot is empty?" just to see if you notice.
