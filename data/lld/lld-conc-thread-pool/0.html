The story: a kitchen with four cooks

Explain like I'm 5

Imagine a restaurant that hired a new cook for every order and sent them home after one dish. Hiring takes longer than cooking, and on a busy night 300 cooks would crowd a tiny kitchen.

A real kitchen keeps four cooks and a rail of order tickets. Waiters clip tickets on the rail. A free cook takes the next ticket, cooks, and comes back for another. If the rail is full, the manager does something on purpose: calls in a part-time cook, tells the waiter "you cook this one yourself", or says "sorry, kitchen is full".

The cooks are worker threads. The rail is the task queue. The manager's rule for a full rail is the rejection policy. The little buzzer the waiter holds, which beeps when the dish is ready, is a Future.

From your world

You already know a thread pool: the Linux workqueue. A driver fills a struct work_struct with a function pointer (INIT_WORK(&w, fn)), calls queue_work(wq, &w), and a kworker thread runs it later in process context. alloc_workqueue(name, flags, max_active) sets how many items may run at once, flush_work() waits for one item, cancel_work_sync() cancels it, and destroy_workqueue() drains and tears down. Java's executor is the same idea: Runnable is the work item, execute() is queue_work(), Future.cancel() is cancel_work_sync(), and shutdown() plus awaitTermination() is drain-and-destroy.
