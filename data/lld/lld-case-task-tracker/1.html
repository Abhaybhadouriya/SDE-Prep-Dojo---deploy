How it is asked

The same domain appears in three formats. Spot which one you are in during the first two minutes, because the grading is different:

  • Machine coding, 90 minutes or take-home (PhonePe, Flipkart): working code, a demo driver, an evaluator reading your classes. Graded on correctness, separation, extensibility and error handling. The PhonePe 2026 take-home was followed by a 45-60 minute code review in which the interviewer asked the candidate to optimise functions written in a brute-force way.
  • LLD whiteboard or doc (Amazon SDE-2): classes, relationships and method signatures, then follow-ups such as subtasks and history. The candidate in the 2025 report felt the round went badly even though the question looked easy, which is the usual trap: a "CRUD" problem where the depth is in the rules.
  • Trello flavour (Flipkart practice problem): boards with privacy and members, lists, cards, assign and move cards between lists. In our model a Trello list is just a workflow state.

In the 311 recent interview reports we collected (2024-06 to 2026-11), "task manager / to-do" was named 3 times (Amazon, PhonePe), most recently in 2026-09. It appears in 3 of the 16 LLD courses and problem lists we surveyed (AlgoMaster, awesome-low-level-design and workat.tech, whose problem set mirrors the Flipkart round) and in 4 of the GitHub collections. That makes it rank 29 in our list: not as frequent as Parking Lot, but a favourite at Indian product companies because it looks simple and quickly shows whether you can model rules, history and concurrency.

Both PhonePe reports stress the same thing: the activity log and the code review. Plan for "every action is logged" from minute one (it is a requirement, not a nice-to-have), and write code you are happy to defend line by line.
