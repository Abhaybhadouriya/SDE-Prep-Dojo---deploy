The story: the sticky-note wall

Explain like I'm 5

A team has a big wall with three columns drawn on it: "To do", "Doing" and "Done". Every job is a sticky note. When you start a job, you move its note from "To do" to "Doing" and write your name on it. When it is finished, it goes to "Done".

There are a few rules. You cannot start a note that has nobody's name on it. A big note with small notes stuck under it cannot go to "Done" while a small one is still in "Doing". And the team captain keeps a diary: every time anybody moves, writes on or throws away a note, the captain writes one line, "Tuesday 10:02, Bob moved 'fix double payment' to Done".

That is the whole design. The wall is a Board. A note is an Issue. The columns and the arrows allowed between them are a Workflow, which is just a table. The rules ("needs a name", "small notes first") are TransitionGuard objects checked before every move. The diary is an append-only list of Events, and the people who want to hear about moves (email, Slack, a webhook) are listeners. A sprint is "the notes we promise to finish in the next two weeks".

From your world

You have built this before. A workflow is a protocol state table: like the TCP state machine, it says which transitions are legal from each state, and anything else is dropped. The guards are LSM hooks: before an operation, the kernel calls every registered security_* hook, and the first one that returns -EPERM stops it. The audit log is the kernel audit subsystem (or a trace ring buffer): records are appended, never edited. And listeners are a notifier chain, called after the change has been made.
