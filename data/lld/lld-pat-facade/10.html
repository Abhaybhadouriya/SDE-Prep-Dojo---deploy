In interviews

Facade is rarely the headline of a case study, but it shows up as the top-level class almost every time: the ParkingLotSystem, BookMyShowService or ElevatorController that the driver code or REST layer calls. Interviewers notice when that class orchestrates cleanly and delegates to well-named parts, and also when it swells into a god class.

  • Logger: a Razorpay machine-coding round debated Chain of Responsibility against Facade plus Factory for the logger design (from our company notes). A Logger facade over formatters, sinks and level filters is a fine answer; say how the parts behind it are structured.
  • Hotel booking and movie booking: a BookingService facade over search, seat or room locking, payment and notification.
  • Food delivery and shopping cart: an order facade like our Java example, with compensation on failure.
  • Parking lot, elevator and ATM: the system class is a facade over floors, spots, fee strategies, cars or cash dispensers.
  • Ride hailing: a trip facade over matching, pricing, payments and tracking.

Strong 30-second answer: "My OrderService is a facade: one placeOrder() that reserves stock, charges, books shipping and notifies, in that order, and releases the reservation if payment fails. The subsystems are injected interfaces and know nothing about it. I keep it thin: no pricing rules inside, and I'd split it per use case if it grew. Unlike a Mediator, nothing calls back into it."
