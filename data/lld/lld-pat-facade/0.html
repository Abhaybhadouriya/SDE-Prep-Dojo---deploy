The story: the hotel reception desk

Explain like I'm 5

A big hotel has a kitchen, a laundry, a housekeeping team, a taxi desk and a billing office. When you want dinner in your room, you do not walk into the kitchen, find the chef, then find a waiter, then go to billing.

You call reception and say "dinner for two in room 304, please". Reception knows who to call, in what order, and what to do if the kitchen is closed. The kitchen is still there, and the staff can still talk to it directly, but guests only need one phone number.

That is the Facade pattern. A facade is one class with a few simple methods that does the common jobs by calling the right parts of a complicated subsystem, in the right order. The subsystem does not change and does not know the facade exists.

From your world

The system call layer is the most famous facade you use every day. A user program calls open("/etc/hosts", O_RDONLY). Behind that one call the kernel walks the path through the dentry cache, checks permissions and LSM hooks, finds the file system's inode_operations and file_operations, allocates a struct file and installs it in the process's file descriptor table. The program sees none of it. Inside the kernel there are smaller facades too: a driver calls request_firmware() and the firmware loader handles search paths, built-in blobs and the fallback mechanism. And libc's fopen() is a facade over open() plus buffering.
