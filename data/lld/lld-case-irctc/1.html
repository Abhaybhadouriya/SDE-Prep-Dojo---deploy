How it is asked

Before you start: this case assumes OOP from C structs (objects as structs with an ops table) and interfaces vs abstract classes. The patterns it uses are explained on their own pages: Strategy, Decorator, Observer, State. The locking is covered in booking concurrency.

IRCTC is rank 31 in our merged LLD list (score 8). It is the Indian-company favourite of the "booking with concurrency" family, next to BookMyShow and hotel booking. What makes it different from those is the partial-route seat reuse, which almost every report mentions.

Three patterns stand out. First, segment reuse is the core requirement in three of the four reports. Second, the Amazon report's "reverse indexing" is an inverted index: the station-to-trains map that makes search fast (we build it). Third, two reports were steered towards schema and SQL, so be ready to show the tables, not only classes (see extensibility). Waitlist, RAC and tatkal are the usual follow-ups once the core is in place; Zepto explicitly said "no waitlist" to keep the round small, so always ask.

The sentence that wins this round: "A berth is sold per segment. Each berth keeps a bitmask of sold segments, so a trip fits when the AND is zero. All allocation for one train, date and class goes through one fair lock, which gives first-come-first-served for tatkal and makes RAC and waitlist promotion on cancellation a simple loop in queue order."
