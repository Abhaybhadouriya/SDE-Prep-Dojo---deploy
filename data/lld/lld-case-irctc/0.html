The story: the conductor's chart

Explain like I'm 5

A train goes Delhi, Agra, Gwalior, Bhopal, Nagpur. The conductor has a big chart. Each bed in the coach is one row, and each piece of track between two stations is one column. When Alice buys a ticket from Delhi to Gwalior, the conductor colours two boxes in bed 1's row. When Bob wants Gwalior to Nagpur, the conductor sees that bed 1's last two boxes are still white, so Bob gets bed 1 too. Alice gets off, Bob gets on, the bed is never empty and never shared.

When all beds are taken, the conductor does two more things. Some small side beds can be shared by two people sitting up (that is "RAC"). After that, people go on a waiting list. If someone cancels, the first sharer gets a full bed, and the first waiting person moves up to sharing. Everyone moves up in the order they came.

That chart is the whole problem. The words have names: a segment is the track between two consecutive stops; a ticket occupies a run of segments; a berth is free for your trip if none of your segments are coloured. CNF = confirmed berth, RAC = Reservation Against Cancellation (half of a side-lower berth, seat guaranteed, berth not yet), WL = waitlisted (no seat yet). Tatkal is a separate quota of berths that only goes on sale the day before the journey, at a fixed minute, with a surcharge.

From your world

This is a bitmap allocator, the same idea as a block bitmap in ext4 or a VLAN bitmap on a switch port: one bit per unit, test with AND, claim with OR, free with AND NOT. The difference is that the "units" are time slices of one resource (the berth), so a berth looks like a timeline that many owners can share without overlap, much like time slots on a TDM link.

Where the analogy ends: a block allocator hands out any free block. Here the choice matters: which berth you give a short trip changes whether a long trip still fits later (fragmentation), so the picker is a strategy you can tune.
