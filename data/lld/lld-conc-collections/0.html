The story: one tally sheet, forty children

Explain like I'm 5

The teacher wants to count how many children like mangoes. She pins one tally sheet on the wall. Forty children run to it at once. Two children read "12" at the same moment, both write "13", and one vote is lost. That is a race.

Fix one: a key to the sheet. Only the child holding the key may write. Correct, but forty children queue for one key. That is a lock.

Fix two: a magic pen. You say "I saw 12, make it 13". If the sheet still says 12, the pen writes 13. If someone changed it, the pen refuses, and you read again and retry. Nobody waits for a key. That is compare-and-swap (CAS).

Fix three: every row of desks gets its own sheet, and the teacher adds the sheets up at the end. Almost nobody bumps into anyone. That is LongAdder, and in the kernel, a per-CPU counter.

From your world

You have used every piece of this. atomic_t with atomic_inc(), atomic_cmpxchg() and try_cmpxchg() are Java's AtomicInteger. On x86 they become lock xadd and lock cmpxchg; on ARM, LL/SC pairs (ldxr/stxr) or the ARMv8.1 cas instructions. kref_get_unless_zero() is a CAS loop: "add one, but only if the count is not already zero". percpu_counter is LongAdder. And RCU-protected lists are the idea behind CopyOnWriteArrayList.
