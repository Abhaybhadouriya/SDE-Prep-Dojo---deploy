The problem: maps and lists shared by threads

In a food-ordering machine-coding round at Cleartrip, a July 2026 candidate reported (LeetCode post) that the whole follow-up round was about this: two users ordering the last item, negative inventory, why HashMap is not thread-safe, item-level vs restaurant-level locking, and Java's concurrent collections. Here are the three bugs behind those questions:

  1. Plain HashMap under writes. Two threads that put at the same time can lose entries, and a concurrent resize can corrupt the table. In Java 7 and earlier, a concurrent resize could even create a cycle in a bucket's linked list, so a later get() looped forever at 100% CPU.
  2. Check-then-act on a thread-safe map. if (!map.containsKey(k)) map.put(k, v) and map.put(k, map.get(k) + 1) are two separate calls. Each call is safe. The pair is not. Our demo ran 800,000 increments as put(get + 1) on a ConcurrentHashMap and ended at 215,252.
  3. Iterating while someone writes. An ArrayList iterator throws ConcurrentModificationException if the list changes under it, even from the same thread.

Collections.synchronizedMap(m) fixes bug 1 by putting one lock around every method. It does not fix bug 2 (you must hold the map's lock yourself around the pair). You must also lock the map by hand while iterating. And every thread queues on that single lock. The java.util.concurrent collections fix all three properly.
