In the interview

Concurrent maps and atomic counters come up as follow-ups in parking-lot, booking, rate-limiter and inventory rounds. Our notes from 2024-2026 interview reports list them among the most common concurrency asks (Cleartrip, Uber's cab allocation, rate limiters). A good answer climbs a ladder and stops at the first step that is enough:

  1. Is it shared and mutable? If you can make it immutable or confine it to one thread, you are done.
  2. One key at a time? ConcurrentHashMap with compute / merge / computeIfAbsent.
  3. One number? AtomicInteger CAS loop for rules ("not below zero"), LongAdder for hot metrics.
  4. Several fields that must change together? One immutable object in an AtomicReference, or a lock.
  5. Several keys or objects? A lock per resource, taken in a fixed order, or lock striping (see double booking).

Strong phrases: "Thread-safe methods do not make a thread-safe sequence of calls." "merge makes read-modify-write one atomic step per key." "CAS is optimistic locking at the CPU level: read, compute, publish if unchanged, else retry." "LongAdder trades exact reads for fast writes, like percpu_counter."
