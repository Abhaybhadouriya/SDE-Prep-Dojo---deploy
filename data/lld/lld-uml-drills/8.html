Drill 5: Doctor appointment booking

Prompt (10 min). Doctors register with one speciality (cardiology, dermatology, orthopedics, general) and declare which 30-minute slots they are free in today, from 9 am to 9 pm; their slots may not overlap. Patients search free slots by speciality, shown in order of start time by default, with other rankings (such as doctor rating) to be plugged in later. A patient may book many appointments in a day but not two at the same time. If the wanted slot is taken, the patient can join that slot's waitlist and gets it if the booking is cancelled. Bonus: show the doctor with the most appointments.

This exact shape appears in several Flipkart SDE-2 machine-coding reports in our research (for example this one), and in Meesho's.

Model answer: doctor appointment booking

Clarify. Actors: Doctor, Patient. Use cases: register doctor, declare availability, search by speciality, book, cancel, join waitlist, view own appointments, trending doctor. Assume one day only and names as ids (the prompt allows it). Out of scope: login.

Sort. Entities: Doctor, Patient, Appointment, AvailabilitySlot (doctor + time range + waitlist). Values: TimeRange (start, end; validates "exactly 30 minutes, on the half hour"). Enum: Speciality (a fixed list in the prompt, so an enum is right here, unlike the gym's workout types). Interface: SlotRankingStrategy. Hidden split: AvailabilitySlot (offered) vs Appointment (taken by one patient).

API. void declare(DoctorId d, List<TimeRange> ranges) fails for non-30-minute or overlapping ranges; Appointment book(PatientId p, SlotId s) fails if taken (offer waitlist) or if the patient already has an appointment at that time; void cancel(AppointmentId a) promotes the first waitlisted patient; List<AvailabilitySlot> search(Speciality s) sorted by the injected strategy. Trending doctor: keep a count per doctor updated on book and cancel, plus a sorted structure or a max-heap with lazy updates.

Compare with drill 2. Same skeleton (offered slot, booking, FIFO waitlist, promote on cancel, ranking strategy). Spotting that two prompts share a shape is the real payoff of drilling: by the fifth booking problem you are reusing a design, not inventing one.

Likely follow-ups
  • Two patients book the same slot at once.
  • Rank by rating, then by time.
