The story: the class notice boards

Explain like I'm 5

Every child in the class has a little notice board. When you draw something, you pin it on your own board. You also have a list of friends whose boards you like.

There are two ways to see your friends' drawings. Way one: every morning you walk to each friend's board and look at the newest drawings. That is a long walk if you have many friends. Way two: whenever a friend draws something, they make copies and drop one into the letterbox of everyone who likes them. In the morning you only open your own letterbox. That is easy for you, but very hard for the most popular child, who must make thirty copies every time.

And when the letterbox is full of drawings, you still need a rule for which one to look at first: the newest? the one with the most stars? the one from your best friend?

That is the whole problem. Way one is a pull feed (also called fan-out on read). Way two is a push feed (fan-out on write). The rule for what comes first is ranking. Showing ten drawings and then "show more" is pagination. Everything an interviewer adds (votes, comments, unfollow, delete, celebrity users, "let me choose my ranking") is a twist on those four ideas.

From your world

Pull versus push is polling versus interrupts. A pull feed is NAPI polling: the reader asks every source when it is ready to read. A push feed is the interrupt path plus a per-consumer queue: the producer does the work at event time and the consumer just drains its queue. The push inbox in our code is a bounded deque that drops the oldest entry when full, the same shape as a socket receive queue or a ring buffer with a fixed number of slots. And the "who gets told when someone posts" list is a Linux notifier chain: register_netdevice_notifier() adds a callback, and NETDEV_UP calls every registered callback in turn. That is the Observer pattern in C.
