In interviews

Visitor is a Tier 3 pattern. In the interview reports we collected, it is almost never the headline of a question; it shows up as the answer to a follow-up such as "now add GST" or "now compute total size and also find all files over 1 MB". When you hear "the types are fixed, but we will keep adding calculations or reports over them", say Visitor, and then immediately say the trade-off. Places it fits:

  • Shopping cart: tax, shipping, discounts or loyalty points per item category, as on this page.
  • File system: size, search by name or extension, permission audit over file/directory nodes (the C++ tab).
  • Text editor: export a document of paragraphs, images and tables to HTML, Markdown or PDF; spell check; word count.
  • Expression evaluators and rule engines (see Interpreter): once you need evaluate and print and validate, move operations into visitors.

Strong 30-second answer: "Visitor moves operations out of a stable class hierarchy into separate classes, one method per element type. It uses double dispatch: accept picks the element's type, visitX picks the operation. New operations cost one class; new element types cost an edit to every visitor, so I use it only when types are stable. In Java 21 I would often use a sealed interface with an exhaustive switch instead."

Common mistake: reaching for Visitor in a 45-minute round where there are two item types and one calculation. The interviewer will ask "why not a method on the class?" and you will not have a good answer. Introduce it when the second or third operation appears, and say why.
