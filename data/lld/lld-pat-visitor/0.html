The story: the inspector who visits every house

Explain like I'm 5

A street has three kinds of houses: a hut, a flat and a farm. Every month a different helper walks down the street: the painter, then the plumber, then the tax collector. Each helper does something different at each kind of house. The painter paints a hut's door blue but paints a farm's barn red.

Do we teach every house how to paint itself, plumb itself and pay tax? No. Then each new helper would mean rebuilding every house. Instead each house has one small doorbell that says "I am a farm, come in". The helper rings, the house answers with its kind, and the helper does the farm job. A new helper (an electrician) needs zero changes to the houses. They just learn the three jobs.

The houses are your element classes (stable). The helpers are visitors (new ones arrive often). The doorbell is a method called accept(visitor), and the "I am a farm" answer is the second half of a trick called double dispatch.

From your world

You have written this in C, probably with a switch. A packet parser or a compiler pass walks a tree of nodes, each with a type tag, and does switch (node->type) { case N_IF: ...; case N_CALL: ...; }. Every new pass (dump, optimise, free) is a new function with the same switch. Visitor is the object-oriented way to write "one function per pass, one case per node type", with the compiler (not a default: branch) telling you when a case is missing.
