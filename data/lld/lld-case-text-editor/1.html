How it is asked

Before you start: this case assumes OOP from C structs (objects as structs with an ops table) and interfaces vs abstract classes. The patterns it uses are explained on their own pages: Command, Memento, Composite, Flyweight. The single-writer document uses locks.

The same problem shows up in three shapes: a machine-coding round where you build a working editor (Razorpay, the TextPad problem), a DSA-flavoured design round where the data structure and complexity matter (Google), and a short undo/redo exercise on a simpler model (Amazon, Microsoft). Prepare the full design and you can shrink it to any of them.

The open-source low-level-design-primer question list also records a menu-driven TextPad machine-coding ask: display all or lines n to m, insert at line n, delete a line or a range, copy lines to a clipboard, paste at a line, undo and redo, all in memory with graceful errors. Our design covers it: a line is the text between two newlines, and copy, paste and range delete are more Commands. LeetCode problem 2296, Design a Text Editor, is the DSA cousin (two stacks around the cursor, which is a gap buffer in disguise).

Two lessons from these reports. The Razorpay round wanted keyboard behaviour (arrows, page keys), not just strings: up and down on lines of different length is where most code breaks. The Google follow-up wanted undo to restore the cursor as well as the text. Ask about both in the first five minutes.
