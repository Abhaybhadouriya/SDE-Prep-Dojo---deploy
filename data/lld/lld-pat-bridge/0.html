The story: remotes and TVs

Explain like I'm 5

Your family has two kinds of remote: a simple one for grandma with four big buttons, and a fancy one with a mute button and a "favourite channels" list. You also have three things to point them at: a TV, a radio and a projector.

A silly factory would build one remote for every pair: simple-TV-remote, simple-radio-remote, fancy-projector-remote... six remotes. Buy a speaker and you need two more. Invent a kids' remote and you need four more.

A smart factory says: a remote only knows how to say "power", "volume up", "next". Every device knows how to do those things in its own way. Any remote can talk to any device through that small list of words. Two remotes plus three devices is five things to build, not six, and a new device works with every remote on day one.

That small list of words is the "bridge". On one side is what you want (simple control, fancy control). On the other side is how it happens (TV, radio, projector). Each side grows on its own.

From your world

You use this every day. read(fd, buf, n) works on an ext4 file, an NFS file, a pipe, /dev/null and a character device you wrote last year. The generic VFS code (the "what": permission checks, file position, fsnotify events) follows file->f_op to a struct file_operations supplied by the filesystem or driver (the "how"). New filesystems plug in without touching vfs_read(), and new VFS features apply to every filesystem. That one pointer, f_op, is the bridge.
