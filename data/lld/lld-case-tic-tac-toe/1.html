How it is asked

Tic-tac-toe is the classic "first" LLD question: most courses and repos teach it first (we counted it in 8 of 16 course and repo sources), and it still shows up in real loops. It comes in two shapes: a running-code round (classes plus a working game in 60 minutes) and a whiteboard round (entities, how the game ends, class diagram, then one method in code, usually the win check). See How LLD rounds work for the difference.

Two related questions travel with it. "Design Tic-Tac-Toe" (LeetCode 348, premium) is the algorithmic core: a move(row, col, player) that returns the winner in O(1). And "decide whether someone has won on a million by million board" (LC 1077138) tests the same idea: never scan the board, only look at what the last move changed. The free workat.tech statement is the version Indian machine-coding rounds use: ask for two names, read moves as "row col" (1-based) until "exit", print the grid after each valid move, print "Invalid Move" otherwise.

The pattern in these reports: the interviewer starts small (3 x 3, 2 players) and then scales (N x N, K players, K in a row). The Amazon candidate was asked only for the game-over method in code, so that method must be your best code. Design for the follow-up from minute one, but code the small version first.
