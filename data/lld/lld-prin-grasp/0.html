The story: who hands out the crayons?

Explain like I'm 5

A class of kids is putting on a play. The teacher has a list of jobs: hand out crayons, count the chairs, ring the bell, tell everyone when to start.

Who should hand out the crayons? The kid who is holding the crayon box, of course. Who counts the chairs? The kid sitting next to the chairs. Who rings the bell to start? One kid only - the "boss of the stage" - and that kid does not paint or count. They just say "go" and everyone else does their own part.

If the bell kid also counted chairs, painted and handed out crayons, they would be tired, slow and every small change would need them. If the crayon kid had to run across the room to ask for chairs first, things would be slow and messy too.

That is all GRASP is: a set of small, common-sense rules for giving each job to the right object. In code, a "job" is a method (or a piece of data to keep). An object-oriented design is good when the jobs sit with the objects that can do them most easily, and when changing one job does not force you to change ten others.

When an interviewer looks at your class diagram and asks "why is calculateFee() in ParkingLot?", they are asking a GRASP question. This page gives you the words and the reasoning to answer it in one sentence.
