Core flows

TTL: lazy reads and the expiry heap (step through it)

  1. Two keys with a TTL: each entry stores an absolute expiry, and the heap holds one (expiresAt, key, version) item per TTL write, earliest first.
  2. session:9 is rewritten with a 60 s TTL. We push a new heap item and leave the old one where it is: finding it would cost O(n).
  3. At 10 s the sweep pops the 5 s item, sees the key's current version is 7, not 6, and skips it. Without the version check, a rewritten key would be deleted early.
  4. At 30 s a read of otp:42 already misses: lazy expiry makes reads correct at the exact deadline even if the sweep is late.
  5. The sweep (every write purges up to 16 due items; a scheduler can call purgeExpired) physically removes it and tells listeners once.

Nested transactions (step through it)

  1. begin() pushes T1. set(a, 11) writes into T1 only.
  2. A nested begin() pushes T2. Reads look at T2, then T1, then the store; the tombstone for b hides the store's b.
  3. rollback() pops only T2. Everything T1 did is still there.
  4. A new T3 is committed: an inner commit just merges its map into T1. Nothing reaches the store yet.
  5. The outermost commit() validates the keys read from the store and applies the whole write set atomically. Other clients see all of it or none of it.
