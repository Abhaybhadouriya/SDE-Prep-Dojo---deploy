When to use it, and when not to

Use it when
  • Two or more product kinds must vary together (vendor, theme, OS, environment, region).
  • Mixing families would be a real bug, not just ugly.
  • The family is chosen once (startup, config, tenant) and then used everywhere.
  • You want unit tests to swap the whole family for fakes in one move.
  • New families are added more often than new kinds.
Avoid it when
  • There is only one product kind. A simple factory or factory method is enough.
  • New kinds are added often. Each new kind means a new method on the interface and an edit to every concrete factory.
  • There is only one family and no real plan for a second (YAGNI, "you aren't gonna need it").
  • Interfaces would multiply faster than features: 4 kinds x 3 families = 12 product classes + 4 factory types.

The asymmetry to remember. Abstract Factory makes adding a row (new family) cheap and adding a column (new product kind) expensive. If the interviewer asks "now add a PayoutClient to every gateway", say clearly that the factory interface grows and every family must implement it. One mitigation: give the new method a default implementation that throws "not supported", or split the factory into smaller ones (Interface Segregation).
