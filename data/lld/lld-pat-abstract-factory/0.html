The story: the toy box that always matches

Explain like I'm 5

You have two big toy boxes. The pirate box gives you a pirate ship, a pirate flag and a pirate captain. The space box gives you a rocket, a space flag and an astronaut.

You never open the pirate box and find an astronaut inside. Whatever box you pick, every toy in it belongs together. If you want to play space today, you do not swap the ship, the flag and the captain one by one. You just pick the other box.

The toy box is the abstract factory. Each toy is a product. Pirate and space are two families.

That is the whole idea. In code, the "box" is an object with one method per kind of thing you need (ship(), flag(), captain()). Your program holds only that box, so it cannot accidentally create a pirate ship with a space flag.

Real software has the same shape all the time:

  • A payment gateway gives you a charger, a refunder and a webhook checker. A refund for a ZenPay charge must go to ZenPay, and its webhook must be checked with ZenPay's rules.
  • A UI theme gives you a button, a checkbox and a scrollbar that look alike.
  • A database driver gives you a connection, statements and result sets that all speak the same wire protocol.
  • A cloud provider gives you storage, queue and secrets clients that share one login and one region.
