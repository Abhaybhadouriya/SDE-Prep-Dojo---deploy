The story: the travel plug

Explain like I'm 5

You fly from India to London with your phone charger. The charger has round pins. The wall in the hotel has three flat slots. The charger works fine. The wall works fine. They just do not fit each other.

You do not rewire the hotel and you do not cut your charger open. You buy a small travel adapter: round holes on one side, flat pins on the other. The charger thinks it is plugged into an Indian socket. The wall thinks a British plug is in it. Everybody is happy and nobody changed.

Software has the same problem all the time. Your checkout code expects a PaymentGateway with a charge(orderId, amountPaise, currency) method. The bank you just signed with gives you an SDK whose method is makePayment(reference, rupees) and returns a number like 51 for "insufficient funds". The Adapter pattern is the travel plug in between.

From your world

The Linux compat layer is a giant adapter. A 32-bit program on a 64-bit kernel passes structs whose fields are 32 bits wide and laid out differently (long and pointers are 4 bytes in that world). The kernel's COMPAT_SYSCALL_DEFINEn() wrappers (functions named compat_sys_xyz()) read the 32-bit layout, convert it into the native struct, and call the same core code the 64-bit path uses. User space did not change, the core syscall did not change; the shim in between did all the translating. That is exactly what an adapter class does.
