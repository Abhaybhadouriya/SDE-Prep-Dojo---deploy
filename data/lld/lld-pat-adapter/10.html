In interviews

Adapter rarely appears as "implement the Adapter pattern". It appears as a requirement hidden in a case study: "we support three payment providers", "SMS goes through Twilio today but may change", "integrate with the legacy inventory system". When you hear an external system you do not control, say "I'll put it behind our own interface with one adapter per provider".

  • Digital wallet and shopping cart / checkout: payment gateway adapters (cards, UPI, net banking).
  • Food delivery and ride hailing: payment providers and map or ETA services.
  • Notification service: an adapter per SMS, email and push vendor behind a channel interface.
  • Logger: adapting to existing sinks (files, syslog, a third-party log library).
  • Stock exchange: market-data feeds in different formats normalised into one tick type.

In the interview reports we collected, candidates almost never wrote "Adapter" by name (a Razorpay logger round named Facade; Strategy and Factory dominate). The pattern is still the expected answer whenever an external provider appears, so name it yourself. See company patterns for what each company asks.

Strong 30-second answer: "Adapter converts an existing interface into the one my client expects. I prefer an object adapter because it works with any subclass and does not leak the adaptee's methods. It translates names, data and results, nothing else. Compared with Decorator and Proxy, which keep the same interface, an Adapter changes it."
