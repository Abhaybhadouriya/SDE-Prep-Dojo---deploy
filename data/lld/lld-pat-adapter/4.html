How it works: one charge, step by step

  1. Checkout calls the Target method with our types: an order id, an amount in paise (integer, no rounding errors) and a currency.
  2. The adapter checks what the adaptee can handle (INR only) and converts units: paise to rupees.
  3. It calls the adaptee's own method with the adaptee's own argument order and types.
  4. The adaptee answers in its own language: an integer status code.
  5. The adapter translates that answer back: 0 means SUCCESS, 51 means DECLINED, anything else is an ERROR.
  6. Checkout receives a PaymentResult. It never saw a rupee double or a bank code.

An adapter does three kinds of translation, and only these: names (charge to makePayment), data (units, types, argument order, request shapes) and results (codes and exceptions into your result type). Business decisions such as "retry twice, then try another provider" belong in the client or in a separate class, not in the adapter.
