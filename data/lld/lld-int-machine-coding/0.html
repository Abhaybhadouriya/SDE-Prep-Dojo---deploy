The story: the science-fair volcano

Explain like I'm 5

At the science fair you have two hours to build a volcano. At the end, the judge walks up and presses the button.

Kid A spends 90 minutes painting beautiful rocks. The button does nothing. Zero points.

Kid B makes a plain volcano that erupts in the first 40 minutes. Then she paints it, then she adds a second button for green lava. When the judge presses, it erupts. When he asks "Can it do blue lava?", she shows where the colour goes in.

Kid B wins. Not because her rocks were prettier, but because it worked every time someone pressed the button, and she could explain how to change it.

A machine-coding round is that science fair. The "button" is your driver program. The judge is the evaluator who runs it. This page teaches you to be Kid B.

From your world

It is board bring-up. On a new board you do not write every driver first. You get the serial console printing, then the kernel booting to a shell, and only then the NIC and the fancy offloads. At each step the board boots. If the demo is in an hour, a board that boots with two drivers beats one with ten half-written drivers that panics at init. Checkpoints here are your "it still boots" points; the driver script is your bring-up regression script.
