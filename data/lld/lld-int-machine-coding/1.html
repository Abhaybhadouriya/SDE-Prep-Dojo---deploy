Anatomy of a machine-coding problem statement

Every problem doc from these rounds has the same five parts. Read the Flipkart quick-commerce problem in post 7588244 (2026-02-18) or the BNPL (Buy Now Pay Later) one in post 8331479 (2026-06-13) and you will see them all. Here is what each part tells you to do.

Part of the docWhat it saysWhat you do with it
DescriptionThe business story (instant delivery, pay later, a Q&A site)Underline every noun (entities) and verb (service methods). Ignore the marketing words.
Core / P0 requirementsNumbered features that must workThis is the whole exam. Copy them as a checklist into a comment at the top of your main file.
BonusExtra features (ratings, dashboards, auto-cancel, notifications)Touch only after every P0 works and is demoed in your driver.
GuidelinesTime, language, in-memory only, no UI/HTTP, driver required, AI rule, evaluation criteriaThese are the grading rubric. Read them twice. The Flipkart 2026 block lists demoable correct code, readability, entity modelling, modularity, extensibility, separation of concerns, abstractions, exception handling and comments.
Sample test casesExample commands and outputs, often in a loose "i: / o:" formatThese are your API. Turn each line into a driver command. The doc usually says the exact format is not required.

Words that change the design. "Thread-safe" or "multiple users acting simultaneously" (a lock story is P0, not bonus). "Read from FILE/STDIN/DRIVER" (support a command script). "Make reasonable assumptions and convey them" (write them in a comment block). "Extensible" (an interface wherever there are several kinds of something).
