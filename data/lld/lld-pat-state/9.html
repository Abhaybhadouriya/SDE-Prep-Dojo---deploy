When to use it, when not to

Use it when
  • An object reacts to the same events differently depending on its lifecycle stage, and the same switch (state) appears in several methods.
  • States have real behaviour and guards ("has money but item empty"), not just labels.
  • You expect new states (maintenance, partially refunded) and want to add them without editing all the others.
Avoid it when
  • There are two or three states with trivial behaviour: an enum and an if are clearer.
  • The states are mostly labels and the interesting part is which moves are legal: use a transition table.
  • Many states share most of their processing (the TCP case): one switch plus shared code may be simpler.
  • The lifecycle is long-running, distributed and must survive crashes: use a workflow engine or a saga with persisted state, not in-memory objects alone.
