Key relationships and patterns (and why each)

Pattern / ideaWhereWhy it earns its place
ObserverEventBus, EventListenerThe order service publishes ORDER_SHIPPED and is done. Adding "also tell the courier" is one more subscription, not an edit to the order service. A failing listener is caught so the others still run.
StrategyChannelSender, one per ChannelEach channel's sending algorithm is swappable. A new channel is a new class plus one registerSender line.
DecoratorRetrying, Failover, RateLimiting sendersCross-cutting behaviour you can stack per channel in any order, without RetryingSmsSender, RetryingRateLimitedSmsSender subclass explosions.
FacadeNotificationServiceCallers see submit and historyOf; queue, worker, preferences and senders stay hidden.
Priority queue (time, priority, sequence)queue with a comparatorOne structure handles "now vs later", "HIGH before LOW" and "FIFO for ties". No separate scheduler thread.
Producer-consumersubmit enqueues, worker dequeues, Condition changedCallers never wait for a provider; the worker sleeps exactly until the next job is due or a new job arrives.
Idempotency keyseenKeysUpstream retries (the order service published twice) do not double-send. The key is chosen by the caller: "101/placed/customer".
Dependency injection of timeClock, SleeperScheduling, rate limits and backoff are tested without real sleeps. A Singleton clock would make that impossible.

The SMS sender, assembled

Do not put channel logic in a switch (channel) inside NotificationService, and do not make EmailNotification extends Notification with send() overridden. Both glue "what to say" to "how to send it", so a new channel edits old code. Keep the request (what) separate from the sender (how). See Open/Closed and composition over inheritance.
