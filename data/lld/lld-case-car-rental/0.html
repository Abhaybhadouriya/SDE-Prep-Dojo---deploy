The story: the toy-car shelf

Explain like I'm 5

Your class has a shelf of toy cars. Anyone can borrow a car, but only for a time they write in the notebook next to the shelf: "Red jeep, Asha, Monday 10 to Tuesday 10". Before you write your name, you read the notebook to check that nobody has the red jeep for any of your hours. If Ravi wants the red jeep on Tuesday from 10, that is fine, because Asha gives it back at exactly 10.

One day Asha and Ravi run to the notebook at the same moment, both see the jeep is free, and both write their names. Now two children think they have the same jeep. The fix: only one child may hold the pencil for the red jeep at a time. Read, check and write while you hold the pencil, then pass it on.

That notebook is our list of reservations per car, the time ranges are TimeSlot objects, and the pencil is a per-car lock. Everything else in the design (search filters, pricing rules, pick-up and return) is about keeping those pieces small and swappable.

From your world

"Is this car free from 10 to 14?" is the same question as "does this new mapping overlap an existing VMA?" in mmap. The kernel keeps intervals per address space and checks for overlap under the mm lock before inserting. We keep intervals per car and check under that car's lock. And per-car locks instead of one global lock is the same move as replacing the big kernel lock with per-inode or per-socket locks: unrelated cars never wait for each other.
