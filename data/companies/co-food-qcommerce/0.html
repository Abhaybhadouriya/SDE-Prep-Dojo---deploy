The story: ten minutes to your door

Explain like I'm 5

You press a button and, ten minutes later, milk arrives. To make that magic work, a small shop near you (a "dark store") must really have the milk, a rider must be close, and your phone must show the rider moving. If the app says "in stock" but the shelf is empty, you are sad. If the app tells you "order placed" three times, you are annoyed. So Zomato (and its Blinkit), Swiggy (and its Instamart) and Zepto interviewers ask: how do you keep stock correct, send each message once, and track every order live?

From your world

Dark-store stock shown in the app is a cache with a TTL, like an ARP or route cache: fast, but it can be stale, so the real decrement must happen at the source. Sending each notification exactly once is like duplicate-ACK suppression: dedupe by a message id. Order status events flowing through Kafka are a ring-buffer commit log that many consumers (tracking, notifications, analytics) read at their own pace.

Zomato's parent company is now called Eternal, so some posts say "Eternal" or "District" or "Nugget"; we group them under Zomato. Blinkit reports are counted under Zomato too.
