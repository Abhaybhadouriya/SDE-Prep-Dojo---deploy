Worked example: dark-store stock for Instamart

An SDE-3 candidate for Swiggy Instamart (2025-11) was rejected after a round that felt good. Useful for SDE-2 too, because it shows what "generic" looks like. Source: post 7377393.

  1. The prompt. Design an Instamart-like service: search and discovery over dark-store inventory, and order management.
  2. The candidate's design. Elasticsearch for search, an inventory cache with short TTLs invalidated when a dark store updates stock, and pessimistic or optimistic locking for contention at order time, following a public framework closely.
  3. The result. The interviewer seemed satisfied, but the feedback was negative.
  4. Lesson. Our reading, not the post's: the answer named the right tools but stayed generic. Make it specific to quick commerce: serviceability (which store serves this address), available-to-promise per store, reservations at checkout, and how stale search stock is allowed to be.

Full design on this site: Quick Commerce (d68).

Scenario: "In stock" but not on the shelf

Search shows milk as available, the order is accepted, and the picker finds the shelf empty. It happens in about 1 order in 200 during the evening peak.
Weak answer: Shorten the cache TTL to one second.
Strong answer: Reserve stock at checkout against available-to-promise (on hand minus reserved minus a safety buffer) in the store's own database, update search from stock events and watch consumer lag, and let pickers mark shortfalls so cycle counts fix drift.
