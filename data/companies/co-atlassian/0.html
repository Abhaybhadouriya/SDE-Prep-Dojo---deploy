The story

Explain like I'm 5

Imagine a huge school where every class keeps its own notebooks: one class has drawings, another has stories, another has to-do lists. The head teacher wants stickers - "math", "urgent", "sports" - that work on every notebook, so anyone can find "all urgent things" in one go.

That is Atlassian. Jira, Confluence and their other tools are the different notebooks. The most common Atlassian design question is literally that sticker system: a tagging service shared by all products. The second most common is "which pages are most popular this week?" - a leaderboard for documents.

So Atlassian design rounds feel less like "survive a billion requests" and more like "design a clean, long-lived platform": good APIs, a sensible database schema, the right indexes, and clear reasons for each technology. Scale still matters, but several reports say the interviewer spent most of the hour on APIs and schema.

From your world

A shared tagging service is like a VLAN tag on Ethernet frames: one small label, defined once, understood by every switch regardless of what the frame carries. The hard parts are the same too: a common format every "product" agrees on (the API contract), a lookup in both directions (which tags on this item, which items have this tag - like MAC table and reverse lookups), and keeping counts right when millions of frames, or tags, arrive.
