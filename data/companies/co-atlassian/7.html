Behavioral angle: the values interview

Atlassian lists five values and runs a dedicated interview on them. Its guide for design roles suggests the STAR method for that interview, and the engineering guide says to give precise examples from your own experience. Here is each value in plain words, with a story shape from a systems/networking career:

Value (official name)What it means, simplyStory from your world
Open company, no bullshitShare information by default; speak up with care.You published a candid post-mortem of a bug you shipped, including your own mistake, so other teams could avoid it.
Build with heart and balanceMove with urgency, but think options through first.Under release pressure you proposed a smaller, safe fix for a routing crash instead of a risky rewrite, and scheduled the rewrite later.
Don't #@!% the customerThink about the customer side first, for all customers.You pushed to fix a rare but customer-visible link-flap instead of a more fun performance task.
Play, as a teamPut the team first; don't take yourself too seriously.You took on unglamorous test-lab automation so the whole team stopped losing hours to manual setup.
Be the change you seekHave the courage to improve things yourself.You noticed packet captures were impossible to share, and built a small tool everyone now uses.

The values interviewer is often not an engineer. Explain technical stories in plain words ("the router was losing data when it got busy") - exactly the skill this site trains with its ELI5 boxes.

The manager interview asks for the business reason behind your past projects. Prepare one project told three ways: the technical challenge, the customer or business impact, and who you worked with.

Values round only: Atlassian's how to nail your design interview is written for product designers, not system design, but its STAR advice applies to the values round.
