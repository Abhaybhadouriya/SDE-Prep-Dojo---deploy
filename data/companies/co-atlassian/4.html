Worked example: the cross-product tagging service

A P40 candidate in India who received an offer (2025-08) got this exact prompt in the one-hour system design round. Source: post 7070747.

  1. The prompt. Let users tag content in several products (Jira issues, Confluence pages, Bitbucket pull requests), click a tag to see all content with it, and see a dashboard of popular tags. It must be product-agnostic, so a new product can join without much work.
  2. Entities. Tag(tenant_id, tag_id, name), EntityTag(tenant_id, entity_ref, tag_id), where entity_ref is (product, type, id) so no product table is joined; a counter per tag for the dashboard.
  3. What was probed. API specification, database schema and choice (SQL or NoSQL), communication protocols, SQS vs RabbitMQ vs Kafka, and rate limiting.
  4. The result. Hire, but with low confidence: the interviewer felt the design was a little rushed.
  5. Lesson. Atlassian grades the reasoning, not the number of boxes. Spend real time on the two indexes (by tag and by entity), the product-agnostic reference, and why each technology was picked.

Full design on this site: Tagging System (d62).

Scenario: A new product joins next week

Trello wants tags on its cards. Your design stores Jira issue ids in a column called issue_id and joins the Jira table to show titles.
Weak answer: Add a card_id column and a join to the Trello table.
Strong answer: Store a generic entity_ref (product, type, id) and keep tag data free of product tables. Products send display data (title, URL) in events or answer a small "resolve" API, so a new product only registers itself and starts calling the tag API.

  • How is the popular-tags dashboard kept up to date without counting on every read?
