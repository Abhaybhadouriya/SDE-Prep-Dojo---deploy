Worked example: the flash sale

An SDE-2 candidate with 4.3 YOE who was selected (2026-10) got this in the system design round, after a machine-coding round on BNPL. Source: post 8567797.

  1. The prompt. Design a flash sale that runs for about 15 minutes under massive traffic and avoids both overselling and underselling.
  2. Requirements we would write. Show stock, reserve on "buy", pay within a few minutes, never sell more than stock, and release unpaid reservations quickly so stock is not stuck (that is the underselling half).
  3. Entities. Sale(sale_id, starts_at, ends_at), SaleItem(sale_id, sku, stock), Reservation(res_id, sku, user_id, expires_at, status), Order.
  4. How we would drive the hard part. Atomic decrement of stock (Redis DECR or a conditional UPDATE) at reserve time, a queue or token gate in front to absorb the spike, and a sweeper that returns expired reservations to stock within seconds.
  5. Lesson. The post gives only the prompt. "Avoid underselling" is the half most candidates forget: say out loud how expired holds go back to stock.

Scenario: Sold out with stock left

The sale ends and the dashboard shows 3,000 units still in the warehouse, yet users saw "sold out" for the last 10 minutes.
Weak answer: Increase stock in the next sale.
Strong answer: Unpaid reservations held stock until the sale ended. Give holds a short TTL (for example 5 minutes), run a sweeper or use Redis key expiry to return them, and show "few left" instead of "sold out" while holds are pending.
  • How do you stop one user buying 50 units?
  • What if the payment succeeds after the hold expired?
