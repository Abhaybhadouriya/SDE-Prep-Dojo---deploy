The story

Explain like I'm 5

Imagine a toy-shop test with two parts. First, they hand you blocks and say: "Build a working toy car in two hours, and it must really roll." That is machine coding. Then a senior builder asks: "Now draw how you'd build a whole toy factory that makes a million cars on sale day." That is the design round.

The surprise is that the senior builder sometimes wants the drawing of the factory (HLD), sometimes the detailed plan of one machine (LLD: classes, tables, APIs), and often both in the same hour. Flipkart candidates who prepared only one kind of drawing are the ones who wrote "rejected" in their posts.

Flipkart is India's large e-commerce company, so its prompts are shopping-flavoured: flash sales (Big Billion Days), ticket booking, payments, carts, search. And because each round eliminates you, the design round is where SDE-2 is won, lost, or down-levelled to SDE-1.

From your world

The LLD-plus-HLD mix is like being asked to design both a router's control plane architecture (which daemons, how they talk, how they fail over) and the exact structs and state machine of one protocol daemon. You have done both kinds of work; the interview just asks you to switch between zoom levels on demand. Flash-sale stock counting is your classic shared-counter race, the same problem as a lock-free ring-buffer index.
