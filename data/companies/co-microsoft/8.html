Common mistakes and tips from the posts

  • Textbook answers. A URL-shortener interviewer refused the standard answer and drove into components and failures (post 7392060). Know why each box exists.
  • Struggling on proximity search. A senior-manager round on "nearest store" was a no-hire (post 7423217). Practise Proximity Service & Nearby Friends (Yelp) and Pattern: Geo & Proximity Queries.
  • Good theory, weak practice. One rejected SDE2 heard "good theory" but not enough depth when scaling a single-client service (post 8551527). Use real numbers and real tools.
  • Every obvious choice gets questioned. In a file upload design the interviewer wanted entities and triggers, not a Dropbox recital (post 7422741).
  • Tip: expect to code a piece of your design (proximity search, config service component).
  • Tip: concrete scale numbers may be given to you; use them in your estimates (post 8453723).
  • Tip: for AI-assisted LLD rounds, design first, prompt the AI, then critique its output out loud (post 8426189).

Scenario: "Design a distributed key-value store like Redis"

The interviewer spends most of the round on sharding, replication and consistency (post 7769548).

"I will use Redis Cluster." (Uses the product you were asked to design.)

Consistent hashing for partitions, leader-follower replication per shard, quorum reads and writes (R + W > N) when strong consistency is needed, hinted handoff for temporary failures, and a clear CAP choice per use case. See Distributed Key-Value Store (Dynamo).

Likely follow-ups
  • What happens during a network partition?
  • How do you add a node without moving all keys?
