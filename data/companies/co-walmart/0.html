The story

Explain like I'm 5

Walmart is the world's biggest shop. Picture thousands of stores and warehouses, each with shelves, and a giant notebook that must always say how many apples are on each shelf. When a hundred people grab the last apples at once, the notebook must not say "-3 apples".

So Walmart's design questions are about keeping counts and money correct while many people act at once: inventory, flash sales, ticket booking, ledgers. Their interviewers also like the classic social and game questions (feeds, chess leaderboards) to see if you know caching and scaling basics.

Walmart Global Tech in India (Bangalore, Chennai) runs many of Walmart's platforms. Walmart also owns Flipkart, and the two companies' prompts overlap: flash sale, booking, inventory. The big difference is format: Walmart often hires through one-day hiring drives where each round is a gate.

From your world

"Many people grabbing the last apple" is the classic shared-counter race you know from kernel code: two CPUs doing read-modify-write on the same variable. The answers are the same family too: an atomic compare-and-swap (optimistic locking with a version number), a lock (pessimistic locking, SELECT ... FOR UPDATE), or giving each CPU its own slice (sharded counters, like per-CPU variables).
