Worked example: inventory in 20 minutes

A Senior Software Engineer candidate (7 YOE, 2025-07) got only 20 minutes for this HLD. It is a senior report, but the prompt is common at SE-III too. Source: post 7008972.

  1. The prompt. Design an inventory service that handles high concurrency without hurting the user experience.
  2. What happened. The interviewer was not satisfied with the first approach; the candidate pivoted and discussed trade-offs, but the interviewer did not engage much.
  3. How we would use 20 minutes. Two minutes on scope (which operations, how hot is one SKU), then the core: stock per (sku, store) with a conditional decrement or a reservation with a TTL, a cache for reads, events to downstream services. Then ask which part they want to go deeper on.
  4. Lesson. In a short design slot, lead with the correctness core (no oversell under concurrency) before scaling boxes, and check in early so a silent interviewer tells you what they want.

Scenario: Two stores, one last unit

Online orders for a store and in-store checkouts both decrement the same SKU count. Twice a day, a customer pays for an item that is no longer on the shelf.
Weak answer: Lock the inventory table during every checkout.
Strong answer: Use a conditional update (UPDATE stock SET qty = qty - 1 WHERE sku = ? AND store = ? AND qty > 0) or a reservation with a TTL, so only one of two concurrent buyers wins. Keep a small safety buffer for in-store sales and reconcile with cycle counts.
  • How do you show availability on the website without hitting the database for every view?
  • How do you rebalance stock between stores?
