Interview questions with best answers

Level 5 - Interview-ready
Will I get a system design round at Google L3 or L4?

At L3, almost never. At L4, usually not as a separate round in 2026 reports we read, but design questions appear as follow-ups. Some India loops reported two design rounds (post 8571448), so ask your recruiter.

How to answer

Answer with data and a plan: confirm the format, then weight prep.

Likely follow-ups
  • What if the recruiter says 'maybe'?
Drop repeated device events within 10 seconds. Now there are a million devices. What changes?

Partition events by device ID so each worker owns a subset of keys and keeps its own last-seen map with time-based eviction. Memory per worker stays bounded. See Pattern: Counting, Ranking & Windowed Aggregation.

How to answer

Code the single-machine version first, then explain the partitioning in two minutes.

Likely follow-ups
  • What if events arrive out of order?
  • What if a worker crashes?
Design an LFU cache where eviction uses a custom score.

Hash map from key to node, plus buckets (or a heap) ordered by score with a tie-break on recency. Each get or put updates the score in O(1) or O(log n). See Distributed Cache (Redis-like).

How to answer

Clarify tie-breaks and what 'score' means before coding.

Likely follow-ups
  • How would you make it thread-safe?
  • How would it work across machines?
Generate unique short strings for a URL shortener (coding round).

A counter encoded in Base62 is simplest and collision-free; for many machines, give each a range or a machine prefix. Random strings need a collision check. See URL Shortener (TinyURL).

How to answer

Compare the two approaches in one sentence each.

Likely follow-ups
  • How long can the strings be?
  • How do you stop people guessing the next code?
If you do get a design round: design an image and video hosting service with no sign-up.

Upload to object storage via a presigned URL, store metadata in a key-value store, transcode in the background into several formats, serve through a CDN, rate limit anonymous uploads. See Video Upload & Transcoding (YouTube) and Pattern: Handling Large Blobs.

How to answer

Simple first, then the bottlenecks the interviewer points at.

Likely follow-ups
  • How do you handle a viral file?
  • How do you delete abusive content?
Tell me about a time you gave critical feedback.

Pick a specific case: a teammate's patch skipped a failover test. You raised it privately, showed the risk with data, offered to pair, and the fix shipped with the test.

How to answer

STAR, focus on how you kept the relationship and the outcome.

Likely follow-ups
  • How did they react?
  • What would you do differently?
How do you handle an unclear project?

Write down what you know and what you do not, find the decision-maker, make a small prototype to learn fast, and confirm scope in writing.

How to answer

Show a method, then a real example.

Likely follow-ups
  • What if the requirements changed later?
What happens after the interviews?

Interviewers submit feedback, a hiring committee reviews the packet, then you meet managers in team matching. Matching can take weeks.

How to answer

Ask the recruiter about timelines; keep practising lightly during matching.

Likely follow-ups
  • Can a mixed packet still pass?
