Interview questions with best answers

Level 5 - Interview-ready
How should I spend the 60 minutes of an Amazon SDE-2 HLD round?

Expect 15-30 minutes of LP questions first, so the design gets 30-45 minutes. Spend about 5 on requirements, 3 on numbers, 5 on entities and APIs, 10 on a happy-path diagram and the rest on two deep dives.

How to answer

Say the plan out loud at the start so the interviewer can redirect you. Leave time for their deep dive.

Likely follow-ups
  • What if the interviewer skips APIs and wants architecture?
  • What would you cut if you had only 25 minutes?
Two users try to book the last seat at the same moment. How do you stop double booking?

Use a short hold: a conditional update (UPDATE seat SET held_by=? WHERE id=? AND held_by IS NULL) or a version column for optimistic locking. The hold has a timeout; payment confirms it. A Redis lock alone is risky if it expires mid-payment. See Ticket Booking (BookMyShow) and Pattern: Dealing with Contention.

How to answer

Name both optimistic and pessimistic options, pick one for the traffic pattern, and explain the hold expiry.

Likely follow-ups
  • How do you handle a hot event with 1M users at release time?
  • What happens if payment succeeds after the hold expired?
Push or pull for a news feed with some users having millions of followers?

Hybrid. Push post IDs into followers' feed caches for normal users (fast reads). For celebrities, do not fan out; merge their recent posts at read time. Store the post first, then fan out through a queue. See News Feed (Instagram/Facebook).

How to answer

Start with the read/write ratio, then show why pure push breaks for celebrities and pure pull is slow.

Likely follow-ups
  • How do you rank the feed?
  • What happens when a user unfollows?
Design a service that moves a user's settings from an old device to a new one.

Device registry, settings metadata service, a transfer orchestrator, and a short-lived encrypted channel. Store only metadata in the cloud; pull the bulk from the old device during setup to save storage. Track progress so a failed transfer can resume. See Device Settings Sync & Backup/Restore (Alexa).

How to answer

Clarify which devices, how big the settings are, and whether the old device is online. That choice drives the design.

Likely follow-ups
  • What if the old device is lost?
  • How do you keep 10 devices in sync after setup?
The interviewer asks: why DynamoDB and not MongoDB or Postgres?

Tie the choice to the access pattern. Key-value lookups at large scale with predictable latency fit DynamoDB; joins and strong multi-row transactions fit Postgres. Mention partition key choice and hot partitions. See Choosing Storage: DB Types & Key Technologies.

How to answer

Never answer with a brand name alone. Say the access pattern, the consistency need and the scaling plan.

Likely follow-ups
  • What is your partition key?
  • How would you run a range query?
Design a distributed rate limiter. What changes if over-limit requests must be queued, not rejected?

Token bucket per key in Redis with an atomic script; the gateway checks it. For queue-instead-of-reject, put excess requests on a per-key queue and drain it at the allowed rate, with a maximum queue age. See Distributed Rate Limiter.

How to answer

Compare two algorithms in one sentence each, then pick one and explain where it lives.

Likely follow-ups
  • How do multiple services sharing one API key stay consistent?
  • What if Redis is down: fail open or closed?
Your log pipeline takes 1M writes per second and consumers fall behind. What do you do?

Kafka absorbs bursts; watch consumer lag per partition. Scale consumers up to the partition count, batch writes to storage, and sample or drop debug logs before error logs. Alerting reads from a separate fast path. See Log Aggregation (ELK at scale) and Kafka Track Overview.

How to answer

This was a 15-minute deep dive in one report. Talk about backpressure like TCP flow control.

Likely follow-ups
  • How do you avoid alert storms?
  • How long do you keep logs and where?
What is the Bar Raiser and how do I prepare for it?

An experienced interviewer from outside the hiring team who protects the hiring bar and can block an offer. Expect deep LP probing and any technical topic, sometimes HLD of your current project.

How to answer

Prepare a failure story and a disagreement story with numbers. Be ready to explain your project's architecture.

Likely follow-ups
  • What would you do differently in that project now?
  • What did your manager think?
Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager (Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit).

Example for a networking engineer: you argued, with test data, that a feature should not ship without a failover test. You lost the argument on schedule, committed, added monitoring, and when an issue came up you had data ready to fix it fast.

How to answer

Show respect, data, a clear decision and full commitment afterwards. Do not make the manager the villain.

Likely follow-ups
  • What if you were right in the end?
  • How did the relationship look afterwards?
My round is labelled LLD but the interviewer starts asking about architecture. What do I do?

Ask early: "Do you want classes and interfaces, or the system architecture?" Then follow their lead. Prepare both for every design slot. See HLD vs LLD: Handling Combined Design Rounds.

How to answer

Confirming the format is a good signal, not a weakness.

Likely follow-ups
  • Can you write the code for the booking service you just drew?
As an SDE-1 candidate, how much HLD should I prepare?

Basics, not depth: client-server, load balancer, cache, SQL vs NoSQL, queues, and one or two simple designs like a rate limiter. Most SDE-1 design time goes to LLD. See Fundamentals: Scale, Cache, DB, CAP.

How to answer

Be honest about what you do not know and reason from first principles.

Likely follow-ups
  • How would your design work across two servers?
