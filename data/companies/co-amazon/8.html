Common mistakes and tips from the posts

Scenario: "Design a URL shortener" and the interviewer doubts your estimate

You chose 6-character Base62 codes. The interviewer says that is not enough.

Change the answer to match the interviewer without numbers. (The candidate in post 6898756 did this and the feedback was "lacked depth for SDE-2".)

Show the maths: 62^6 is about 56.8 billion codes. At 100 million new links a month that lasts decades. Then offer 7 characters if they expect more, and explain the trade-off.

Likely follow-ups
  • How do two servers avoid generating the same code?
  • Jumping to FR/NFR without understanding the problem - the candidate in post 7638107 said this cost the round. Ask what the product is for first.
  • Writing to the feed before storing the post - persist first, then fan out with events (post 6891316).
  • Iterating from a broken first design - one interviewer wanted the right concurrency design up front (post 6855714).
  • Only happy path - an Alexa notification question pushed hard on offline devices and fallbacks (post 8457154).
  • Skipping ML-flavoured prep - recommendation prompts appear (post 7176045); know the precompute-and-serve shape in Recommendation System (Amazon / Netflix).
  • Weak LP stories - "expected more complex LP stories" is a common rejection reason in EU loops (post 6801242).
  • Tip: say the partition key out loud. One interviewer pushed for an explicit Kafka key instead of round-robin (post 7274661).
  • Tip: prepare HLD of your own current project; Bar Raisers and HMs ask "scale your current system 10x".
