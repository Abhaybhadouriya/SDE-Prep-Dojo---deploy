Behavioural angle: Leadership Principles as STAR stories

Amazon lists 16 LPs on its official page. Each interviewer is assigned a few and asks two or three questions, often digging with "what exactly did you do?" and "what was the number?". Answer in STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Many posts say LPs carry as much weight as the technical part (post 8426283, post 6801242).

From your world

A systems or networking career is full of great LP material: production outages, customer escalations, performance hunts, hard trade-offs between hardware cost and features. The trick is to translate it. Say "customers lost traffic for 20 minutes" instead of "the BFD session flapped". Then give the technical detail only when asked.

LPStory idea for a systems / networking engineerTypical question
Customer ObsessionYou talked to the team that used your driver or tool and changed the design for them.Tell me about a time you went beyond what the customer asked.
OwnershipYou stayed on a field escalation (router crash, link flaps) past your own module until it was fixed.A time you took on something outside your area.
Invent and SimplifyYou replaced a complex manual config process with a script or a simpler state machine.A time you simplified a process.
Are Right, A LotYou predicted a scaling limit (table size, buffer) from data before it hit customers.A time your judgement was proven right with incomplete data.
Learn and Be CuriousYour switch from kernel / networking to cloud: Docker, Kubernetes, Kafka, system design.What have you learned recently and how?
Hire and Develop the BestYou mentored a junior on debugging with packet captures or crash dumps.How have you helped someone grow?
Insist on the Highest StandardsYou blocked a release because a regression test for a failover path was missing.A time you refused to compromise on quality.
Think BigYou proposed a test framework or telemetry pipeline for the whole product line, not one feature.A time you proposed a bold idea.
Bias for ActionDuring an outage you rolled back first and root-caused later.A time you acted with incomplete information.
FrugalityYou reused existing hardware or open-source tooling instead of buying new.A time you did more with less.
Earn TrustYou admitted your own bug that caused a customer issue and fixed it publicly.A time you got tough feedback or admitted a mistake.
Dive DeepYou found a race condition or memory leak by reading traces and kernel logs.Walk me through the hardest bug you solved.
Have Backbone; Disagree and CommitYou argued with data against a design, lost, then fully supported the decision.A time you disagreed with your manager.
Deliver ResultsYou shipped a feature for a customer deadline despite a blocked dependency.A time you delivered under pressure.
Strive to be Earth's Best EmployerYou improved on-call load or documentation for your team.How did you make your team's work better?
Success and Scale Bring Broad ResponsibilityYou thought about the effect of a change on many customers' networks (safe rollout, rollback).A time you considered wider impact of your work.
  1. Build 8-10 stories, not 16. One good outage story can cover Ownership, Dive Deep, Bias for Action and Deliver Results.
  2. Two minutes each. Situation and Task in 20 seconds; most time on your Action; end with a number and what you learned.
  3. Prepare a failure story and a disagreement story. Bar Raisers ask both.
  4. Expect GenAI questions. Several 2026 posts report "how do you use AI tools in your work?" (post 8584003, post 8520348).
