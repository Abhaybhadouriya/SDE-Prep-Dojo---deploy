The story: a school with 16 house rules

Explain like I'm 5

Imagine a big school that hires new helpers. Every helper must be good at two things: fixing things (the puzzles and building tests) and following the 16 school rules (be kind to visitors, own your mess, keep learning, and so on). Every teacher who meets you checks both. One special teacher, the Bar Raiser, comes from a different classroom and can say "no" even if everyone else says "yes". So you practise your puzzles and you bring real stories that prove you follow the rules.

That is the Amazon loop. Each round is part technical (coding, low-level design or high-level design) and part behavioural, where you answer questions about your past using Leadership Principles (LPs, Amazon's 16 written values). This page shows what candidates reported in 105 high-level design (HLD) reports from 2025-04 to 2026-11, what each level is expected to do, and a two-week plan that uses the pages on this site.

From your world

Think of the loop like a change-review board for a core router upgrade. Each reviewer tests one area (data plane, control plane, rollback plan), and one independent reviewer from another team can veto the change. You pass by showing evidence, not by promising. In the LP part, your evidence is a real past story with numbers: "packet loss went from 2% to 0.1%".
