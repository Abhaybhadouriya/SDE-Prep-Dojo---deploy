Worked example: the Groww GTT order service

An SDE-2 candidate (1.5 YOE, 2026-09, not selected) got this one-hour HLD at Groww. Source: post 8539548.

  1. The prompt. An order service already places orders on the exchange and listens to updates, but exchange orders are only valid for the day. Design GTT (good till triggered) orders on top of it.
  2. The candidate's design. About 1M GTT orders a day from estimates; GTT order ids kept in Redis, removed when a trade update arrived, and an end-of-day cron that re-placed whatever was left. Kafka sat in front of the order service to absorb load.
  3. The pushback. The interviewer moved to order history and to what happens when each step fails. A Redis set plus a nightly cron gives no durable state and no answer for a crash in the middle.
  4. Lesson. For money and orders, start from a durable state machine (ACTIVE, TRIGGERING, TRIGGERED, FAILED) with conditional updates, an outbox for events and an idempotency key on the placed order; then explain each failure.

Full design on this site: Stock Broker App (d64), section 7.3.

Scenario: Square-off at 15:20

At 15:20 the system must close every open intraday position. Last month the job crashed halfway and 4,000 positions stayed open overnight.
Weak answer: Run the job earlier and add retries.
Strong answer: Shard positions by instrument or account, give each shard a claim (lease) and a state per position (OPEN, SQUARING, CLOSED), place exit orders with an idempotency key, and let a watchdog pick up shards whose lease expired. Alert if anything is still OPEN at 15:25.

  • How do you make the job safe to run twice?
