Worked example: stock price alerts

An L4 (SDE-2 backend) candidate who received an offer (2026-03) got this as the HLD round, which Uber used as the bar raiser. Source: post 7706639.

  1. The prompt. Users subscribe to stock tickers and get notified when a price moves by more than a chosen delta. The candidate had never seen it and described it as a mix of Top-K with sliding windows and a price tracker.
  2. Requirements we would write. Create, list and delete alerts (symbol, delta or threshold, window); fire each alert once per crossing; notify within seconds of the tick; millions of rules, thousands of symbols, bursts on crash days.
  3. Entities. Alert(alert_id, user_id, symbol, type, threshold or delta, window, status), Tick(symbol, price, ts), FiredAlert(alert_id, fired_at).
  4. The pushback. The post warns that the interviewer did not wait for a basic design before asking deep-dive questions, so a slow "requirements, then boxes" opening could backfire.
  5. Lesson. Have a 2-minute skeleton ready (feed, per-symbol evaluator, notifier) and then go straight to the hard part: index rules per symbol in sorted books so a tick touches only the rules it crosses.

Full design on this site: Stock Price Alerts (d63).

Scenario: Crash day: 15M alerts in ten minutes

The market drops 5% at the open. Most "down 5%" rules fire in the same few minutes. Your notifier queue is growing and users complain alerts arrive late.
Weak answer: Add more evaluator pods and a bigger database.
Strong answer: The evaluator is not the bottleneck; the fan-out is. Key alerts-fired by user, scale notifiers on consumer lag (KEDA) and pre-scale before the open, batch the fire-once claims (one UPDATE ... WHERE alert_id = ANY(:ids) AND status = 'ACTIVE' per 50-100 ms), and collapse several alerts for one user into one push.
