Behavioral angle: Collaboration & Leadership

Uber's guide names a 60-minute Collaboration & Leadership interview and tells you to prepare one or two past examples per competency in STAR form (Situation, Task, Action, Result), and to study Uber's values. The values page changes over time, so read the current wording just before your loop rather than memorising an old list.

As a systems/networking engineer moving to SDE, your stories are strong because they are about real production pain. Map them like this:

Theme they probeStory from a networking/kernel careerThe "R" to land
Ownership of an outageA line card dropped packets under burst load; you traced it to a ring-buffer overflow in the driver and shipped a fix plus a counter.Drops went to zero; the new counter caught the next regression early.
Working across teamsHardware, firmware and QA blamed each other for a link flap; you set up a shared capture and a daily 15-min sync.Root cause in days instead of weeks; the sync became a team habit.
Disagree and decideYou argued against shipping a release with a known memory leak; you proposed a smaller release with the fix.Customer escalations avoided; you explain how you disagreed respectfully.
Learning fastYou picked up Python/Go or Kubernetes to automate test labs.Lab setup time cut; shows you can grow into SDE work.
Customer focusA large carrier hit a routing bug in production; you joined the war room and wrote the workaround.Service restored; you turned the fix into a regression test.

Only claim numbers you can defend. "Cut lab setup from a day to an hour" is fine if true; an interviewer will ask how you measured it.
