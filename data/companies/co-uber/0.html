The story

Explain like I'm 5

Uber is a giant game of "who is closest?". A child waves for a ride, and a map full of moving cars must find the nearest one in a blink. Food apps ask the same question about restaurants. A stock app asks a cousin of it: "whose price just jumped the most, and who wants to know?"

So Uber interviewers love puzzles about things that move and change every second: cars on a map, prices on a ticker, orders in a kitchen. They want to see if you can keep a live picture of the world, find the right items fast, and tell the right people without shouting at everyone.

That is why the most repeated Uber prompts in our data are stock price alerts, nearby restaurants (Uber Eats home page) and real-time driver heat maps. All three are the same skeleton: a high-rate stream of events, a smart index (by price threshold, or by location), and a fan-out to the few users who care.

From your world

You already know this pattern from routers. A line card sees millions of packets per second, matches each one against a forwarding table (longest-prefix match), and sends it out of only the right port. A stock alert system is the same: each price tick is a "packet", the subscription thresholds are the "routing table", and the notification service is the "egress port". Uber's geo questions are a 2-D version of the same lookup problem: a geohash prefix is very much like an IP prefix.
