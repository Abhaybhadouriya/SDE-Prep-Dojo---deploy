When you hit it

Reads usually outnumber writes. Hello Interview's pattern guide puts typical read-to-write ratios at around 10:1 at the start, climbing to 100:1 or more for social products, and notes that a single feed load can fan out into a hundred-plus reads. Facebook's TAO graph cache was described by its authors as serving more than a billion reads and millions of writes every second. A database built to give strong guarantees on every write is the wrong place to serve that many reads.

Signals in the interview question

  • "Millions of users view, few create." URL shortener redirects, product pages, news feeds, video metadata, search suggestions.
  • A latency target on reads. "p99 under 100 ms" or "autocomplete must feel instant" means a database round trip on every request is too slow. (p99 is 99th-percentile latency: 99 of every 100 requests finish faster than this number. It measures the slow tail, not the average.)
  • Celebrity or viral content. One item gets a huge share of traffic (a cricket score during an IPL final, a flash-sale product). That is a hot key.
  • Global users. A user in Chennai should not wait for a round trip to a database in Virginia. That is a CDN or regional-replica problem.
  • The data tolerates a few seconds of staleness. Like counts, view counts, product descriptions, profile photos. If it does, caching is easy. If it does not (account balance, seat availability at checkout), you must design the freshness story carefully.

From your world

p99 versus average is worst-case interrupt latency versus average interrupt latency. A driver whose average is 5 us but whose worst case is 2 ms still drops packets; a service whose average is 20 ms but whose p99 is 2 s still loses users, because a page that makes many calls almost always hits the slow tail somewhere.

From your world

You already know this hierarchy: L1, L2, L3, DRAM, disk. A CPU does not make every load hit DRAM - it keeps hot lines close and accepts the cost of keeping them coherent. A web cache is the same trade at a bigger scale, except there is no hardware coherence protocol (no MESI). You must build invalidation yourself, which is why it is famously hard. A CDN is like a route cache pushed to the line cards: the fast path never asks the route processor.
