  • A 1-D index on lat or lng returns a strip; a spatial index gives nearby points nearby keys.
  • Every geo query is two steps: cheap loose candidates, then exact distance (or ETA) filter and rank.
  • Geohash: 5 bits per char, alternating lng/lat; always search the 8 neighbours.
  • Quadtree adapts to density (split at ~100 points); great for static points in memory, poor for constant moves.
  • S2 = sphere-correct, Hilbert-ordered int64 ranges (index and shard). H3 = equal-distance hexagons (aggregate and smooth).
  • R-tree (PostGIS GiST) for polygons: zones, geofences, serviceability.
  • Moving locations are ephemeral, latest-value-only, write-heavy: keep them in memory, expire stale ones, ingest via Kafka.
  • Shard by geography, balanced by load; border queries scatter-gather to a few shards.
