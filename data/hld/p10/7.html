How real companies do it

  • Uber dispatch (DISCO): S2 level-12 cells as shard keys, 4 s GPS pings, ringpop for sharding and forwarding, candidates ranked by road ETA (HighScalability, ringpop).
  • Uber H3: hexagons for surge and marketplace analysis; compaction cut a California set from 10,633 hexagons to 901 (H3 blog).
  • Tinder: S2 geoshards (levels 7 and 8, about 45 and 22.5 miles) balanced by load, 20 times more computation handled than the single index (Tinder Engineering: Geosharded Recommendations, Part 1; ByteByteGo summary); about 3 shards per 160 km search (System Design Newsletter). Elastic's customer story adds that partitioning users into geo-indexes cut CPU about 4x (Elastic).
  • Google S2 / MongoDB 2dsphere and Redis GEO: the two most common off-the-shelf answers (S2, Redis).
