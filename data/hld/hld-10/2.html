Two-phase commit (2PC)

Two-phase commit makes several databases commit or abort as one unit. A coordinator runs two rounds with the participants (the databases).

  1. Phase 1: prepare. The coordinator asks every participant "can you commit transaction 42?". Each writes the changes to its own log and takes the locks it needs.
  2. Vote. Each participant answers YES (and promises it can commit even after a crash) or NO. Any NO, or a timeout, means abort for everyone.
  3. Decision is logged. The coordinator durably records COMMIT. From this moment the transaction is decided.
  4. Phase 2: commit. The coordinator tells everyone to commit; they apply and release locks. Participants that were down learn the decision when they come back.
  5. The blocking problem. If the coordinator dies after the votes but before telling anyone, a participant that voted YES cannot decide alone - another participant might have voted NO, or the coordinator might have logged COMMIT. It must keep its locks and wait. Rows stay locked until the coordinator recovers.

Three-phase commit (3PC) adds a "pre-commit" round so each step can time out, but it assumes a network where you can tell a slow node from a dead one, so it is rarely used. The real-world fix is to replicate the coordinator and participants with consensus (Raft/Paxos, see hld-09), which is how Google Spanner and CockroachDB run 2PC across shards, and how Kafka's own transaction coordinator stores its state in a replicated internal topic.

2PC is good when
  • All participants are databases you control that speak XA (or one distributed DB does it for you)
  • You truly need atomic, isolated commits (money moving between two shards of one ledger)
  • Transactions are short
2PC hurts when
  • Participants are separate services or third-party APIs (they cannot "prepare")
  • Locks are held across network round trips; throughput drops and latency grows with the slowest participant
  • The coordinator becomes a blocking single point unless replicated
  • Kafka, most NoSQL stores and HTTP APIs cannot join
