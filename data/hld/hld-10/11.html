Phrases that score: "I won't dual-write; the event goes through an outbox in the same transaction." - "Delivery is at-least-once, so every consumer is idempotent on event id." - "The card charge is the pivot; before it we compensate, after it we retry." - "Kafka's exactly-once stops at Kafka's edge; the sink must be idempotent."

Related reading on this site: Replication & Consensus, API Design (idempotent APIs), Microservices & Resilience, Big Data & Streaming, Pattern: Multi-step Processes, Kafka Exactly-Once, Kafka Connect & Streams, Pipeline: Retries & DLQ.

The 60-second answer. "When one business action touches several services, I never dual-write and I avoid 2PC across services. Each service commits its change and an event row into an outbox in the same local transaction; Debezium or a poller relays the outbox to Kafka, keyed by the entity id so events stay in order. A saga orchestrator such as Temporal drives the steps and runs compensations in reverse if a step fails, with the payment as the pivot. Delivery is at-least-once, so every consumer records processed event ids in the same transaction as its effect, and every API that can be retried takes an idempotency key."
