The story

Explain like I'm 5

At school there is a wall of cubby holes with little doors. Some are small (for a pencil box), some are big (for a lunch bag). Your mum buys you a present and says: "It will be in a cubby at school." The postman comes, asks the wall "which cubby is free and big enough?", and the wall opens one door. He puts the present in and the door clicks shut. The wall sends your mum a secret number. After school you type the secret number, and only that one door opens. If you forget to come for three days, the teacher takes the present out so another child can use the cubby. And if the school's phone line is broken, the wall still remembers the secret numbers by itself, so you can still get your present.

That is the design. The wall of cubbies is a locker bank (Amazon calls a location a Locker), each cubby is a compartment with a size, the postman's question is compartment assignment, the secret number is a pickup code, the three-day rule is expiry, and "the wall remembers by itself" is edge-first design for an offline kiosk.

Why it is asked: our gap analysis found an Amazon SDE-2 report of exactly this HLD round, with the interviewer probing APIs, schema, concurrency, distributed locking and notifications. Amazon interviewers often pick "a problem similar to a real Amazon service". Walmart's version is buy online, pick up in store: the same reserve-then-hand-over problem, with store inventory and pickup slots instead of boxes. It is also a classic low-level design (LLD) question, so be ready to zoom from boxes-and-arrows down to a state machine (see HLD vs LLD).

Say this early: "Scale here is modest. The hard parts are correctness - never give one compartment to two parcels, never open a door for the wrong person - and the physical world: kiosks go offline, doors jam, people do not show up. I will design the kiosk as a small edge system that can work alone, with the cloud as the source of truth for reservations."

From your world

A locker kiosk is an embedded network device. It has a control plane in the cloud (intent: which reservations exist, which codes are valid), and a local agent that drives hardware (door solenoids, sensors) and reports operational state. That is the same split as intended config vs operational state on a router managed by a controller, and the sync problems are the same: the device may be offline, messages arrive late or out of order, and you reconcile by version numbers.

New words, once each: Idempotent means safe to retry: doing it twice has the same effect as once, usually by sending an idempotency key. Compare-and-set (CAS, a conditional write) means "write this only if the row still has the version I read", so two writers cannot both win. A geohash is a short string that names a square on the map, so "lockers near me" becomes a lookup by prefix. An outbox is a local table of events waiting to be sent, so nothing is lost while offline. TTL (time to live) is an automatic expiry. Edge means "on the device in the field", not in the data centre. Kafka is a durable, ordered event log that many services read. The rest are in the glossary.
