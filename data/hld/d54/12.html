What should checkout reserve for a locker order?

  1. A specific compartment, from checkout until delivery
  2. Nothing; assign at drop-off and hope
  3. Capacity for (locker, size, delivery day), with the compartment chosen at drop-off
Capacity reservation avoids wasting boxes while still preventing over-booking.

Two drop-off requests race for the same free box. What prevents a double assignment?

  1. An update that only succeeds if state is AVAILABLE and the version matches
  2. Reading the state twice before writing
  3. Asking the driver to wait 5 seconds
Compare-and-set makes exactly one writer win; the other retries with another box.

When should the pickup code be issued?

  1. When the door opens for the driver
  2. When the door closes and the occupancy sensor reports the box is occupied
  3. When the order is placed
Tie the code to physical confirmation; otherwise a box with no parcel gets a code.

With 40 active 6-digit codes at a kiosk, what is the chance a single random guess opens some box?

  1. 1 in 1,000,000
  2. 1 in 25,000
  3. 1 in 40
40 / 1,000,000 = 1 / 25,000. That is why escalating lockouts matter.

A parcel's pickup window has passed. When does its compartment become AVAILABLE?

  1. Immediately at the deadline
  2. At midnight
  3. Only after a driver removes the parcel and the sensor reports empty
Expired is not empty. The physical world decides.

How do pickups keep working when a kiosk is offline?

  1. The cloud pushes code hashes, compartments and deadlines to the kiosk in advance, so it validates locally
  2. The kiosk accepts any 6-digit code while offline
  3. They do not; customers wait
Edge-first design: intent is pushed down early, events are queued and replayed later.

Why is a plain atomic counter (held = held + 1) without a condition a bad reservation primitive?

  1. It is too slow
  2. It can exceed the total and is not safe to retry (a retry counts twice)
  3. DynamoDB does not support it
Use a conditional update (held < total) plus a unique order id for idempotency.
