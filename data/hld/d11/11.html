  • Available to promise = on hand - reserved - safety stock. Show available, not on hand.
  • Every stock change is an event; the count is a derived view (event sourcing + CQRS).
  • Key Kafka by item+location: per-key order and exactly one writer, no locks.
  • Browsing reads a stale-tolerant cache; reservations use an atomic conditional update.
  • Deltas vs absolute counts: ignore deltas older than the last physical count.
  • Pick partitions high up front; changing N reshuffles keys.
  • Watch per-partition lag: one hot partition means a hot key or a slow pod.

60-second answer: "Every source (tills, online orders, DC receipts, returns, counts) emits events into Kafka. A transformer makes them canonical and keys them by item+location, so each item-store's events are ordered and owned by one processor instance, which applies them to an on-hand position with no locks and persists it. Positions stream into a cached availability view that serves the website at hundreds of thousands of reads per second with a few seconds of lag. Reservations for online orders use an atomic conditional update on the same row, so the last unit can't be sold twice. Physical counts are absolute events that correct drift, and the event log lets us audit or replay."
