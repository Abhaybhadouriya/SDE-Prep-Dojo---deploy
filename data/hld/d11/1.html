Requirements

Functional

  1. Track stock per item per location (store, distribution centre, fulfilment centre).
  2. Ingest every change: till sales, online orders, returns, truck receipts, store-to-store transfers, damage write-offs, hand counts.
  3. Answer availability fast: "Is this in stock at my store?", "Can you ship it to my PIN/ZIP code by Friday?"
  4. Reserve stock for online orders (pickup or ship-from-store) so two buyers cannot get the last unit.
  5. Trigger replenishment: when stock is low, tell the ordering system to send more.
  6. Reconcile with physical counts and fix drift.

Non-functional

  • Freshness: a till sale reflected in availability within seconds (say p99 under 5 s).
  • No oversell on reservations: strong consistency for the reserve step.
  • Eventual consistency is fine for browsing ("in stock" badges), with a short lag.
  • Very read-heavy: far more availability checks than stock changes.
  • High availability: a store's tills must keep selling even if the central system is slow, so ingestion must be async and buffered.
  • Auditability: keep every event so we can explain any number.

The words of inventory (learn these first)

You might confuse: on hand (physical) vs available (what we may promise). Websites should show available, not on hand. And ATP (available to promise) is retail jargon for exactly that number.
