The story

Explain like I'm 5

Imagine a giant music library with every song in the world. You cannot carry the library home, so the library gives you a magic radio. You say "play the dinosaur song", and a helper looks it up in a big card catalogue, even if you said the name a bit wrong. Then the helper does not send you the whole record. It sends you the song in small pieces, the first piece very fast, so music starts almost at once while the next pieces are still on their way. The library also keeps copies of popular songs in little shops near every town, so the pieces do not have to travel far. And every time you listen, the helper writes it in a notebook, so next week it can say "you liked the dinosaur song, try this one".

That is the design. The card catalogue is the metadata store (songs, artists, albums) plus a search index that forgives spelling mistakes. The pieces are audio chunks of an encoded file kept in object storage (like Amazon S3). The little shops near every town are the CDN (content delivery network: many cache servers close to users). The notebook is the listening history, an event stream that feeds recommendations, play counts and royalties.

Say this early: "Music streaming is two systems. A small, read-heavy metadata system (songs, playlists, search) and a huge, dumb byte-delivery system (audio files on a CDN). The app servers never stream bytes themselves; they hand out signed CDN links. Listening history is a third, write-heavy event pipeline." That one sentence separates you from candidates who route audio through their API servers.

From your world

Think of the CDN as a tiered cache hierarchy, like L1/L2/L3 caches in front of DRAM, with S3 as main memory far away. A play start is a cache-miss latency problem. Chunked streaming with a playout buffer is the same idea as a jitter buffer in VoIP: fill a few hundred milliseconds of audio first, then keep the buffer ahead of the play head so a slow packet never causes silence.

New words, once each. Metadata is data about the songs (title, artist, duration), not the sound itself. A codec encodes sound into bytes (Ogg Vorbis, AAC). Bitrate is bytes per second of audio, in kilobits per second (kbps). Adaptive streaming means the player switches between bitrates as the network gets better or worse. An inverted index maps each word to the list of documents that contain it; it is how Elasticsearch searches. Eventual consistency means copies may disagree for a short while but converge. QPS is queries per second. The rest are in the glossary.
