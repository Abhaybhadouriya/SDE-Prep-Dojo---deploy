The story

Explain like I'm 5

A library has a million books and a very busy librarian. A child walks in and says "I want something good." The librarian cannot read every book's back cover in the two seconds the child will wait. So she uses a trick. First she grabs a few shelves she knows this child likes: dinosaur books, books her friends borrowed, books everyone is reading this week. That is a few hundred books in seconds. Then she quickly flips through those and keeps 50. Then she looks at those 50 carefully and picks the best 10, making sure they are not all about dinosaurs. At night, when the library is closed, she reorganises the shelves and writes notes about every child, so tomorrow's picking is even faster.

That is a real recommendation system. Grabbing shelves is candidate generation (also called retrieval). Flipping through is light ranking. Looking carefully is heavy ranking. Making sure they are not all the same is re-ranking for diversity and business rules. The night work is offline computation (training models, building lists). Notes that update as soon as the child returns a book are nearline updates.

This matters a lot to the business. Netflix wrote in 2012 that 75% of what people watch comes from some sort of recommendation. Amazon's item-to-item collaborative filtering paper (2003) was named by IEEE Internet Computing in 2017 as the paper from its 20 years that best withstood the test of time.

Who asks this? Our gap analysis found "design a recommendation system" in three independent curricula (system-design-primer, Grokking Advanced, checkcheckzz's hot-questions list) but no dated SDE-2 report naming it. The chips mark e-commerce and content companies whose core product is recommendations, where a variant ("recommended products", "recently viewed", "people also bought") is natural. At SDE-2 level you are tested on the systems (pipelines, stores, latency, freshness), not the ML maths.

Open with: "It is a funnel: cheap retrieval from millions to about a thousand, a light ranker to a few hundred, a heavy ranker to a few dozen, then re-ranking for diversity and rules. Expensive work goes offline; fresh signals come in nearline through a stream; only the last mile is online."

New words, once each: QPS is requests per second. p99 means "99 out of 100 requests are faster than this". An embedding is a short list of numbers (say 128) that describes a user or an item, trained so that similar things get similar numbers. ANN (approximate nearest neighbour) search, the core of a vector database, finds the vectors closest to a query vector without scanning all of them. HNSW is the most common ANN index. A feature store is a key-value store of precomputed model inputs ("features"). A data lake is cheap bulk storage (S3/HDFS files) of raw events. A distilled model is a small, fast model trained to copy a big model's answers; a multi-task model predicts several outcomes (click, buy, hide) at once. All are explained with a picture just before the estimation, and general terms are in the glossary.
