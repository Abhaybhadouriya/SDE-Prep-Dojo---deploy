How the real companies do it

  • Netflix: offline / nearline / online architecture on AWS; Hermes for publishing offline results, Manhattan for near-real-time event processing, results in Cassandra, EVCache and MySQL. 75% of viewing came from recommendations (2012).
  • Amazon: item-to-item collaborative filtering with offline-computed related-item lists, refreshed daily; differential-probability relatedness to avoid bestseller bias.
  • Instagram Explore (2019): ig2vec account embeddings plus FAISS nearest-neighbour retrieval over millions of accounts; tens of thousands of eligible candidates sampled to 500; ranking 500 -> 150 -> 50 -> 25 with distillation. (2023): billions of items, two-tower retrieval with daily item embeddings, multi-task ranking of the best 100, models fine-tuned hourly.
  • YouTube (Google, 2016): the classic two-stage deep network, candidate generation then ranking.
  • X / Twitter: open-sourced "the-algorithm": in-network candidates from the Earlybird search index (about half), out-of-network from graph and embedding services (SimClusters, TwHIN, GraphJet), light and heavy rankers, home-mixer, visibility filters.
  • Uber Michelangelo: the feature store reference (offline Hive, online Cassandra, Kafka + streaming for near-real-time features).
