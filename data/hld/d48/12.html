Quick quiz

Why does a recommender use a multi-stage funnel?

  1. ML models only accept small inputs
  2. Scoring the whole catalogue per request is far beyond the latency and compute budget
  3. To make A/B testing easier
17K QPS x 100M items is 1.7 trillion scores per second; the funnel makes it tractable.

Updating recommendations seconds after a user starts watching something is an example of...

  1. Offline computation
  2. Model training
  3. Nearline computation
Event-driven, stored for the next request, not served directly: nearline (Netflix's example).

What is the main limitation of a two-tower retrieval model?

  1. It cannot use user-item interaction features, because the towers are independent
  2. It cannot be cached
  3. It only works for text
Independence allows precomputed item embeddings and ANN search; cross features are left to the ranker.

100M items x 128 dims x 4 bytes is about...

  1. 5 GB
  2. 51 GB
  3. 500 GB
100M x 512 bytes = 51.2 GB of raw vectors; HNSW roughly doubles that.

Training-serving skew is best prevented by...

  1. Training more often
  2. Using a bigger model
  3. One feature definition for both paths and logging features as served
That is the core job of a feature store.

Amazon's item-to-item CF scores B as related to A when...

  1. A's buyers are more likely to buy B than the average customer
  2. A and B have the same price
  3. B is a bestseller
Differential probability avoids recommending bestsellers to everyone.

The ranking service times out. What should the API return?

  1. An error page
  2. A fallback such as the light ranker's order or a precomputed list
  3. Nothing
Recommendations must degrade gracefully; Netflix recommends always having a precomputed fallback.
