"Why SQL over NoSQL?" - an Amazon interviewer asked exactly this. The honest answer is both, for different data. The contact and agent-capacity records sit on the correctness path: assigning a contact must, in one transaction, check the agent still has a free slot, take the slot, and move the contact from QUEUED to OFFERED. Two routers racing for the same agent must not both win. A relational database gives you that with a single transaction and row locks or a version check. The volume is modest (tens of writes per second per queue), so SQL scale is not a problem. Messages are the opposite: high volume, append-only, never joined, read by one key in order - a natural fit for a partitioned NoSQL store.
