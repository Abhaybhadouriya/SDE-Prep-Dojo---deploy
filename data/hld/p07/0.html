The story

Explain like I'm 5

You take your dirty clothes to a laundry shop. The shop does not make you stand at the counter for two hours while the machine runs. The person at the counter takes your bag, gives you a little ticket with a number on it, and says "come back later, or we will text you". Behind the counter, the bag goes into a line of bags. Whichever washing machine is free takes the next bag. If a machine breaks in the middle, the bag goes back into the line and another machine washes it. If a bag is so strange that every machine fails on it (it is full of sand!), they put it on a special shelf for the manager to look at. When your clothes are clean, your ticket says "ready".

That laundry is the whole pattern. The counter is your API server: it answers fast. The ticket is a job ID. The line of bags is a queue (a list of waiting work). The washing machines are workers (programs that only do the slow work). The special shelf is a dead-letter queue (DLQ): a place for jobs that keep failing. The text message is a notification or webhook.

The idea is simple. The hard parts are the "what ifs": what if a machine dies halfway, what if two machines wash the same bag, what if a thousand bags arrive at once, and how does the customer know what is happening? This page climbs from the naive answer to the one interviewers want.
