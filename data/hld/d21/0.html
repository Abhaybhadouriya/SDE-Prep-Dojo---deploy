The story

Explain like I'm 5

The toy shop has a wall of big buttons, one for each toy. Every time a child presses a button, the shop must make a tally mark for that toy, because the toy makers pay the shop one coin per press. A helper stands next to the wall. Every minute she writes on a board: "Teddy: 12 presses, Robot: 40 presses". Two rules make her job hard. First, some children press the same button twice very fast by mistake - that should count once, or the toy maker pays too much. Second, some children's presses reach her a little late because they had to walk around the queue - she must still put those in the right minute. At night, a second helper counts every press again slowly from a recording, and if the numbers differ, the slow count wins.

That is the real design. A press is an ad click. The tally board is the aggregated count per ad per minute. "Count once" is deduplication using a unique click ID. Late presses are late events, handled with event time and watermarks. The recording is the raw event log in Kafka and object storage, and the night-time recount is batch reconciliation.

Money is involved, so this design is about correctness under failure: crashes, retries, duplicates, late data, and a few very popular ads (hot keys). It sits on top of everything in the Kafka track. If you understand this page, you understand stream processing in interviews.

From your world

Deduplication by click ID is exactly TCP sequence numbers: the receiver drops a retransmitted segment it has already seen. Per-ad partitioning in Kafka is ECMP flow hashing: all clicks for one ad go to one partition, so one worker owns that ad's counter without locks. And a watermark is like a reassembly timeout: "I have waited long enough for out-of-order fragments; close this buffer now." Where it breaks: TCP drops late fragments after the timeout; here we must not drop paid clicks, so late ones go to a correction path instead.

Reported in the wild: "Design an ad click aggregator" is commonly reported for Meta E4/E5 (E4/E5 = Meta's SDE-2 / senior levels) - Hello Interview's breakdown is built around it - and it is a chapter in Alex Xu's System Design Interview Vol. 2. The same skeleton answers "design YouTube view counts", "real-time analytics dashboard", and "clickstream pipeline".
