Requirements

Always agree scope first. Email is huge (calendar, contacts, chat). Cut it down to the mail core.

Functional (in scope)
  • Send email to anyone, internal or external (SMTP to other providers).
  • Receive email from the internet and from other users of our service.
  • List the inbox and folders or labels, newest first; open one email; mark read or unread; move, label, delete.
  • Attachments, up to about 25 MB per email (the Gmail personal limit; bigger files become a Drive link).
  • Full-text search over my own mail (from, subject, body, attachments names).
  • Conversations (threads) and new-mail notifications on all my devices.
  • Spam, phishing and virus filtering.
Out of scope (say so)
  • Calendar, contacts, chat.
  • Rich editor, smart compose, AI summaries.
  • Admin console, eDiscovery and legal hold (mention as a follow-up).
  • Bulk marketing platform (that is closer to notification system).

Non-functional

  • Durability: never lose an accepted email. This beats everything else. Losing a contract or a bank OTP is far worse than a 30-second delay.
  • Scale: about 1 billion users (Google said 1.5 billion monthly Gmail users back in 2019).
  • Latency: inbox list under ~200 ms at p99 (p99 = 99% of requests are faster than this; the slowest 1% are what users complain about - like tail latency through a switch buffer, not the average); an internal email visible to the receiver within a few seconds; search results under a second.
  • Availability: high, but per mailbox we prefer consistency. A user must never see their read/unread state flip back and forth.
  • Security and privacy: TLS on every hop, encryption at rest, a mailbox is read only by its owner.
  • Interoperability: must speak standard SMTP, and follow the rules other big providers enforce: SPF (which IPs may send for a domain), DKIM (a signature on the message that proves the domain sent it unchanged) and DMARC (the domain's policy when those fail: deliver, quarantine or reject). Deep dive 6 covers them in depth.

Say early: "Email is a write-heavy, never-lose, per-user-partitioned system. Every query is scoped to one user, so I will partition everything by user_id." That one sentence sets up the whole data model.
