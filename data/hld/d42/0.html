The story

Explain like I'm 5

You write a letter to Grandma. You drop it in the red post box near your house. The post office truck takes it to the big post office in your town. That post office looks at the town name on the envelope and sends it to Grandma's town. Grandma's post office puts it in her own little mailbox. Grandma opens her mailbox when she wants and reads it. If Grandma's town is closed for a holiday, your post office keeps the letter safe and tries again tomorrow. It never throws it away.

Email works the same way. The big words are only new names for the post office parts:

  • The app you type in (Gmail web, Outlook) is the MUA, the Mail User Agent. It is your pen and your house.
  • The servers that carry mail between companies are MTAs, Mail Transfer Agents. They are the trucks and the sorting offices. They talk a protocol called SMTP (Simple Mail Transfer Protocol).
  • The part that drops a letter into one person's mailbox is the MDA, the Mail Delivery Agent.
  • Your client reads its mailbox with IMAP or POP3, or, for web and mobile apps, a private HTTP API.

This picture comes straight from the ByteByteGo guide on email delivery. The rest of this page is about doing it for a billion mailboxes, where every letter must arrive exactly once and be findable in a second.

From your world

SMTP relay is store-and-forward, exactly like a router with a buffer: each hop takes full custody of the packet, queues it, and forwards it when the next hop is reachable. The MX lookup is a routing-table lookup keyed by domain instead of prefix. The big difference: a router may drop a packet and let TCP retransmit end to end. An MTA may never drop. Once it says "250 OK", the message is its responsibility.
