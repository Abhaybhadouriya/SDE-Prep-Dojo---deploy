Failure modes

  • Inbound flood (spam wave or a mailing-list storm). The edge sheds load early (temporary 4xx makes honest senders retry later, which is SMTP's built-in backpressure); the queue absorbs the rest; scorers autoscale on lag.
  • Mailbox shard down. Fail over to a replica (seconds). Deliveries for those users wait in the queue; nothing is lost. Reads may fail briefly: consistency over availability, as the ByteByteGo chapter says.
  • Remote provider down. Mail waits in that domain's lane; other domains are unaffected. Retry with backoff, bounce after days.
  • Bad spam model. Good mail lands in spam. Canary the model, monitor "not spam" clicks, roll back the image tag, and re-score from the Kafka log.
  • Search index drift. A bug leaves deleted mail searchable. Fix by replaying events or rebuilding one user's index from the mailbox (the mailbox is the source of truth).
  • Hot user. A shared support inbox gets 1M mails a day. Give it its own shard, and paginate with the time-ordered id.

From your world

SMTP 4xx "try again later" is flow control done by the receiver, like a TCP zero window: the sender keeps the data and slows down instead of dropping it. A 5xx is a hard reject, like an ICMP unreachable.
