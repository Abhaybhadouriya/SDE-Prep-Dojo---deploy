How real companies do it

  • Gmail (Google) (spam blog, sender rules). Layered ML + rules spam filtering, blocking over 99.9% of spam, phishing and malware; TensorFlow models added about 100 million blocks a day (2019, 1.5 billion monthly users). Since 2024 it enforces sender rules on everyone who mails it: SPF/DKIM, TLS, spam rate under 0.3%, and DMARC plus one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders.
  • Exchange Online / Outlook (Microsoft) (data resiliency, Preferred Architecture). Every mailbox database lives in a Database Availability Group with at least three copies across datacenters, including a lag copy with 7 days of restore points. Transport keeps a shadow copy before acking (Shadow Redundancy) and a post-delivery copy (Safety Net). ESE scans each database every 7 days and patches single corrupt pages from replicas. The older on-premises "Preferred Architecture" used 4 copies (3 HA + 1 lagged) on cheap JBOD disks, replicating in software instead of buying SANs.
  • Fastmail (storage architecture). Each 2U server holds up to 100 "slots", each its own Cyrus IMAP instance. A user's "store" is 3 slots (one master) spread over different machines, replicated by a log-based sync process, with failover usually under 10 seconds. Checksums on every record and file, plus weekly master-replica audits.
  • Facebook Messages (2010) (blog). Unified email, SMS and chat for 350 million users sending 15 billion messages a month. Chose HBase over MySQL (long tail performance) and Cassandra (consistency model), stored attachments in Haystack, and built for "small hot set, huge cold set".
