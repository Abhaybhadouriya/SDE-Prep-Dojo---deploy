Requirements

Functional
  • Detect the serving dark store for the user's address; show the ETA ("9 mins") or "not serviceable".
  • Search and browse (categories, text search, autocomplete) showing only items in stock at that store, with store-level prices and offers.
  • Cart, checkout with stock reservation, coupons, delivery fee.
  • Payment: UPI, card, wallet, cash on delivery (COD).
  • Order lifecycle: placed, picking, packed, rider assigned, out for delivery, delivered; cancel and partial refunds for missing items.
  • Store operations: picker app (pick list with bin locations), packer, rider app, inbound (GRN), damages, cycle counts.
  • Live order tracking for the customer.
Non-functional
  • Speed: search p99 under 200 ms; checkout under 1 s; the whole order delivered in about 10-15 minutes.
  • Inventory accuracy: never sell what is not on the shelf (an "out of stock after ordering" costs a refund and a customer). Search may be a few seconds stale; the reservation may not.
  • Peaks: evenings, weekends, festivals, cricket matches, rain. Several times the normal rate.
  • Store isolation: one dark store's problem (power, network, rush) must not slow the others.
  • High availability; payments exactly-once from the user's view.

Opening line that sets you apart: "Quick commerce is not 'Amazon but faster'. The unit of everything is the dark store: search, stock, price, ETA and capacity are all per store. So I will shard almost every piece of state by store_id, and the hard problems are keeping one store's stock honest and hitting a 10-minute clock."

Set your own requirements first. A Zepto SDE-2 report says the candidate was expected to define requirements, after which the interviewer added peak-time prioritisation and multiple payment methods (post). Leave room in your design for exactly those two.
