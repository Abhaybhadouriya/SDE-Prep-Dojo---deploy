Architecture, step by step

  1. Which store? The app sends lat/lon. Serviceability maps it to an H3 hexagon cell, looks up which store serves that cell right now (stores can shrink their area when overloaded or raining) and returns store_id and an ETA. The store_id rides in a signed session token on every later call.
  2. Search. Elasticsearch finds matching products (text, synonyms, ranking); a stock filter removes SKUs that are out of stock at this store, using a Redis set or bitmap per store refreshed from inventory events with a short TTL. Prices and offers are per store too.
  3. Reserve. At checkout, the order service asks inventory to reserve the whole cart in one transaction on the store's shard: for each line, reserved += qty only if on_hand - reserved - safety_buf >= qty. Any line short: return what is available and let the user adjust.
  4. Pay. Prepaid: UPI, card or wallet through a PSP, with a reservation timer (a few minutes). COD: skip payment and confirm at once, with a risk check (limits for new accounts).
  5. Publish. On payment success (signed webhook, idempotent), the order becomes PAID and an outbox row publishes order.paid to Kafka, keyed by store_id so each store's events stay in order.
  6. Pick. The store's scheduler places the order in a priority queue (deadline first) and gives it to a free picker, with a pick list sorted by bin location to shorten the walk. Big orders may be split between two pickers by zone (chilled, ambient).
  7. Stock moves. Each scan is a PICK movement: on_hand -= qty, reserved -= qty. If the bin is empty (the system was wrong), the picker marks it short: inventory corrects on_hand and the customer gets a substitute offer or a partial refund.
  8. Rider. Dispatch assigns a rider when the order is nearly packed (predicted ready time), choosing among riders at or returning to the store, and can batch two orders going the same way if both still make their promise.
  9. Track. Order status changes and rider GPS stream to the app over SSE or WebSocket until delivery. Delivery closes the order; any missing item triggers an automatic partial refund.
