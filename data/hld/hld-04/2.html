Modern cloud figures from Hello Interview's "numbers to know" are useful too: under 1 ms inside an availability zone, about 1-2 ms across zones in a region, and 50-150 ms across regions. Single machines are also far bigger than many old rules assume - cloud instances with hundreds of GB to many TB of RAM and 25-100 Gbps networking are common - so "one big box" goes further than you think.

From your world

You know cache lines, DRAM and PCIe costs. Zoom out one level: memory : SSD random read : data-centre round trip : cross-continent round trip is roughly 100 ns : 150 us : 500 us : 150 ms, or about 1 : 1,500 : 5,000 : 1,500,000. A network hop inside a data centre costs as much as thousands of memory reads. That is why designs put caches close to the app, batch network calls, and keep chatty services together. Where this breaks: on a box you can often hide memory latency with prefetching; across a network you hide latency mostly by not making the call (caching) or by doing calls in parallel.

From your world

You have spent years shaving nanoseconds off a per-packet budget of a few ns. On that scale, one same-DC service call (500 us) is the time to forward about 75,000 minimum-size packets at 100G line rate, and one cross-region round trip is about 22 million of them. That is why web designs count network hops, not instructions. Where this breaks: on a NIC the budget is fixed and predictable; in a data centre the median hop is 0.5 ms, but the p99 can be several ms because of queueing, retransmits and garbage-collection pauses.
