Common mistakes

MistakeFix
Doing exact arithmetic (86,400 x 37...)Round to one significant figure. 86,400 becomes 10^5. Interviewers want speed.
Forgetting the peakAlways give average and peak, and say your peak factor.
Forgetting replicationMultiply storage by the replica count (usually 3), or say erasure coding.
Mixing bits and bytesNetwork is in bits per second; storage is in bytes. Divide by 8.
Mixing reads and writesCompute each separately; the ratio drives cache and DB choices.
Numbers with no conclusionEvery number ends with "so...": "so it fits in memory", "so we need sharding", "so a CDN".
Treating planning numbers as factsSay "roughly" and "assuming". Offer to adjust.
Estimating for ten minutesThree minutes. Skip any number that changes nothing.
Using real company figures you are not sure ofUse your own clearly stated assumptions instead.

Tip: write units next to every number on the board (40 writes/s, 6 TB, 5 Tbps). Most estimation errors are unit errors, and units make your working easy for the interviewer to follow.
