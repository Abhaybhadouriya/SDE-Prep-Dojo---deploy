Quick quiz

Each of 100 servers has a 1% chance of a 1-second response. Roughly what share of requests that wait for all 100 are slow?

  1. 1%
  2. 63%
  3. 100%
1 - 0.99^100 is about 0.63, the example from The Tail at Scale.

Why do most search engines shard the index by document rather than by term?

  1. Intersections stay local and replies are small and even
  2. Queries touch fewer machines
  3. It needs no replicas
Term sharding touches fewer machines but ships long lists and creates hot shards.

When is PageRank computed?

  1. For each query
  2. When a user clicks
  3. Offline over the link graph, stored as a static score
It does not depend on the query.

What does a hedged request do?

  1. Sends every request to all replicas at once
  2. Sends a second copy to another replica only if the first is slower than about the 95th percentile
  3. Retries after a failure
This caps extra load at a few percent while cutting the tail.

Why store docID gaps instead of docIDs in posting lists?

  1. Gaps are small numbers that compress into 1-2 bytes
  2. It makes lists unsorted
  3. Gaps are needed for PageRank
Delta + variable-length encoding shrinks lists and memory bandwidth.

How do you make news searchable in minutes without rebuilding the whole index?

  1. Edit posting lists in place on every leaf
  2. Turn off the result cache
  3. Stream fresh pages into a small real-time tier merged at query time
Immutable main index + fresh tier; fold in later.

What protects thousands of leaves from a "query of death"?

  1. Hedged requests
  2. Canary requests to one or two leaves first
  3. Longer timeouts
Only fan out once the canary leaves answered successfully.
