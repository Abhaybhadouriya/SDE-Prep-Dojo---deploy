How real companies do it

  • Google, 1998 (Brin and Page). 24 million pages; URL server, Python crawlers (about 300 connections each, over 100 pages/s peak with four crawlers), barrels, sorter, lexicon; PageRank with d = 0.85; anchor text and proximity.
  • Google, 2003 ("Web Search for a Planet"). Over 15,000 commodity PCs; clusters of a few thousand machines; DNS picks the cluster; index shards of random documents with replica pools; docservers for snippets; reliability in software.
  • Google, 2010 (Percolator). Incremental indexing on a Bigtable-like store; 50% fresher results.
  • Google, 2013 ("The Tail at Scale"). Hedged and tied requests, micro-partitions, good-enough results, canary requests.
  • Google today. Hundreds of billions of pages, an index of well over 100 million GB, rendering with Chrome, canonical clusters, hundreds of ranking factors.
  • Twitter/X search (see Post Search): a real-time in-memory tier plus a larger SSD tier, built daily in batch.
