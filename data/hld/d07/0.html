The story

Explain like I'm 5

Imagine your town has many kitchens, many hungry families and many kids on bicycles. You phone a helper and say "one dosa, please". The helper tells the kitchen. The kitchen says "it will be ready in 15 minutes". Now the clever part: the helper does not send a bicycle kid right away, because the kid would just stand outside the kitchen and wait. And she does not send one too late, because then the dosa goes cold. She picks a kid who is about 15 minutes away, so the kid arrives just as the dosa comes off the pan.

While the kid rides to your house, the helper looks at a map with little moving dots and tells you "the dosa is 4 minutes away". If it suddenly rains and everyone orders at once, the helper does not panic. She tells new families "it will take a bit longer today", so nobody is promised something she cannot do.

That helper is the system we will design. It has three kinds of users, which is why people call it a three-sided marketplace: the customer who orders, the restaurant that cooks, and the delivery partner (we will just say rider) who carries the food. The hard parts are not "save an order in a database". The hard parts are where (which restaurants and riders are near you), when (send the rider at the right moment), and how long (an honest ETA, which means estimated time of arrival).

If you have read Uber ride hailing you will see many familiar parts: live locations, a geo index and matching. Food delivery adds a kitchen in the middle, and that changes the matching problem a lot.
