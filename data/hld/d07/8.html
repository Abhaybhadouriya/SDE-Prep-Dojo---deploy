Trade-offs, bottlenecks and failure modes

Say each row as: "I pick X because A; the cost is B; if C becomes a problem I switch to Y." Example for the first row: "I pick batched matching every few seconds because it lowers total wait and lets one rider carry two orders; the cost is a few seconds of delay and a harder algorithm; if a city is small and orders are sparse, I switch to greedy nearest-rider."

  • Dispatch for a zone crashes. Orders wait. The leader lease expires in seconds and another pod takes over. Because matching state is rebuilt from Kafka and Redis, nothing is lost. Alert on "orders unassigned for more than N minutes".
  • Redis rider index is lost. It refills within one ping interval (about 4 s), since every rider pings again. This is why we may keep it in memory at all.
  • Restaurant tablet is offline. The order sits in CONFIRMED. After a timeout, call the restaurant automatically or by an agent, then auto-cancel and refund. Never leave the customer waiting silently.
  • Payment callback is late. The order stays in PLACED (payment not yet confirmed). A reconciler polls the payment provider with the same reference, and never charges twice. See checkout.
  • Kafka consumer lag grows at peak. Notifications arrive late but orders are safe. Scale consumers up to the partition count, and keep tracking on its own topic so it is never stuck behind analytics.
  • Hot restaurant. A famous biryani place gets thousands of orders. Its tablet queue and its partition are hot. Cap its open orders (product rule), and it is still only a few events per second for Kafka.
