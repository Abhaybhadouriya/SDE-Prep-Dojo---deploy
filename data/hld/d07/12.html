Quick quiz

In our estimate, which load is the biggest write load?

  1. Placing orders
  2. Restaurant menu updates
  3. Rider location pings
250,000 riders pinging every 4 s is about 62,500 writes/s, roughly 200x the order rate. That is why locations live in memory, not in the order database.

Why not dispatch the nearest rider as soon as the order is placed?

  1. The nearest rider is always busy
  2. The rider may wait idle at the kitchen, and greedy choices steal riders from later orders
  3. Riders cannot see orders until they are cooked
Timing and global efficiency matter. Batched matching, with dispatch timed to the prep time, wastes less rider time and keeps food hot.

What does SET rider:17:offer o_1 NX EX 30 guarantee?

  1. Only one offer can exist for rider 17, and it expires after 30 s
  2. Rider 17 must accept within 30 s or is banned
  3. The order is saved in SQL
NX = set only if absent, EX = expiry in seconds. It is a fast efficiency lock, and the SQL conditional update is the real guard.

Why must a radius search on a geo grid also check neighbouring cells?

  1. To find riders in other cities
  2. A point very close to you can sit just across a cell boundary
  3. Redis requires it
Cells are boxes, and closeness does not respect box edges. It is the same as two adjacent IPs sitting in different /24 prefixes.

The order status changes and the service must publish an event. What is the safe way?

  1. Commit to the DB, then publish to Kafka in the same request
  2. Publish to Kafka first, then commit
  3. Write an outbox row in the same DB transaction and relay it to Kafka
The outbox makes the state change and the event atomic. A and B each lose or invent events when a crash lands between the two writes.

On a huge peak night, what usually runs out first?

  1. Delivery riders
  2. Database CPU
  3. Kafka disk
Zomato publicly said it ran out of delivery partner capacity on NYE 2020. Autoscaling fixes CPU, not riders.

A price changed between adding to the cart and placing the order. What should the order API do?

  1. Charge the old price silently
  2. Re-validate at order time and return a conflict with the new total
  3. Charge the new price silently
Display can be eventually consistent, but the moment of commitment must validate against the latest version and tell the user.
