Requirements

Always start an interview by agreeing on what you are building. Say it out loud, write it in one corner of the whiteboard, and ask the interviewer to confirm. Here is the list we will design against.

Functional (what it does)
  • Discover: show restaurants that can deliver to my address, with search and filters (cuisine, rating, "fast delivery").
  • Menu and cart: see the menu with live availability ("out of stock" items), add items to a cart.
  • Place order and pay: one tap places the order exactly once, even if I tap twice or the network flickers.
  • Restaurant side: a tablet app accepts or rejects the order and gives a prep time. A restaurant can switch itself off when the kitchen is full.
  • Rider assignment: pick a rider and offer them the job. The rider accepts or declines.
  • Live tracking and ETA: show the order status and the rider moving on the map.
  • Notifications: push messages at every stage. Ratings after delivery.
Non-functional (how well it does it)
  • Never lose or duplicate a paid order. Orders and payments need strong consistency.
  • Discovery is fast: a target of p99 under about 200 ms for the restaurant list (our target, not a published figure).
  • Assignment is quick: Swiggy's vendor case study describes city-scale assignment batches finishing inside a one-minute SLA.
  • Tracking is fresh: the rider dot moves every few seconds. A few seconds stale is fine.
  • Menus, ratings and search can be eventually consistent (a few seconds late is OK).
  • Survives peaks: dinner time every day, rain, cricket finals and New Year's Eve.
  • Availability: 99.99% for ordering. Discovery can degrade (show cached results) instead of failing.

Out of scope (say this out loud, it saves time): grocery and quick commerce, ads, restaurant onboarding, rider payroll, and the details of the payment system (that is its own design: payment system and wallet and ledger).

Open with the sentence: "The core loop is discover, order, cook, assign, track. Orders must be exactly-once and strongly consistent; everything around them (menus, search, tracking) can be eventually consistent and should degrade gracefully at peak." It shows the interviewer you already know where the hard parts are.
