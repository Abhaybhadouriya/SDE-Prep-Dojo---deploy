How real companies do it

  • Swiggy. The Swiggy Bytes posts on re-architecting logistics describe hundreds of services taking part in one order, and assignment as a batched optimisation over order-rider pairs within a geographic zone. Akka's customer story describes city-scale assignment inside a one-minute SLA and ML predictions at P99 71 ms.
  • Zomato. The CEO's New Year's Eve updates (4,254 orders per minute at the 2020 peak, as reported by The Week) and the admission that rider capacity ran out first are the best public lesson on peaks.
  • DoorDash. Its engineering blog has public posts on the dispatch system and ETA models. They are worth reading for how a marketplace tunes the cost function with experiments.
  • Uber. H3 (hexagonal cells, 16 resolutions) is open source and used for marketplace analytics and surge. Uber Eats shares the ride-hailing location stack.

Related designs: ride hailing (the same location stack), surge pricing and ETA, maps and routing, chat (WebSocket gateways), e-commerce checkout (idempotent order and saga), product catalogue search. Patterns: geo and proximity, real-time updates. Pipeline: ShopStream architecture.
