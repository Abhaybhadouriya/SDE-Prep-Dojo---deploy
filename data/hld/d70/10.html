How real systems do it

Seen in 6 recent reports. Every variant, with company, level, date and source post, is in Recently Asked HLD Questions #46 and #52. Company view: the Uber guide and the Amazon guide.

  • Uber H3: Uber's hexagonal grid. Events (requests, trip starts, food orders) are bucketed into hexagons; supply and demand per hexagon drive surge pricing and marketplace analysis. 16 resolutions, each about 1/7 the area of its parent, 64-bit cell ids.
  • Apache Pinot at Uber: city operations dashboards that mix real-time and historical data, fed from Kafka, with FlinkSQL for pre-aggregation and joins; some real-time tables target under 5 minutes freshness at p99.
  • AWS IoT Core: a managed MQTT broker with per-device certificates and rules that forward to Kinesis, Kafka (MSK) or Timestream; its limits (128 KB payload, 100 publishes per second per connection, 30-1,200 s keep-alive) shape device batching.
  • Redis pub/sub: at-most-once delivery, pattern subscriptions, and sharded pub/sub since 7.0 for cluster scale.
  • Flink windows: tumbling, sliding and session windows, watermarks, allowed lateness and side outputs for late data.
