The story

Explain like I'm 5

Imagine every house in your town has a little robot by the door. Every few minutes the robot shouts a number to the town hall: "my house used 2 cups of electricity!" Thousands of robots shout at once, so the town hall has a helper who writes every shout on a long paper roll, in order, so nothing is lost. Another helper reads the roll and colours a big town map: houses using a lot are red, houses using a little are blue. A third helper keeps a diary so you can ask "how much did my house use last July?". And a fourth helper watches for danger: if a fridge robot shouts "I am hot!" three times in a row, the helper rings a bell. When you say "I heard the bell, I am fixing it", the helper stops ringing. When the fridge is cold again, the helper writes "fixed" in the book.

That is the whole design. The shouting robots are devices sending telemetry (automatic measurements sent over a network). The paper roll is Kafka, a durable ordered log. The map painter is a stream processor such as Apache Flink that groups readings by place and time. The diary is a time-series database (TSDB): a store built for "value of X over time". The bell-ringer is the alert evaluator and its little state machine (OPEN, ACKNOWLEDGED, RESOLVED, IGNORED).

Interviewers dress this one problem in four costumes. Amazon asked an SDE2 candidate to design a smart-meter electricity monitor with a usage dashboard (2026-10), and asked a selected L5 candidate in Spain to design a heatmap of the latest temperature from sensors spread across a continent, with historical queries (2026-09). Microsoft asked an SDE 2 candidate for an alert-monitoring system for machine sensors, where users acknowledge, resolve or ignore alerts (2026-01). Uber asks for a real-time heatmap of driver density, and in one 2026-03 loop the candidate was offered SDE-2 instead of senior after struggling to explain exactly how Redis pub/sub would push updates to dashboard WebSockets. Links are in the interview section.

Say this early: "This is a write-heavy, append-only pipeline with three readers: a live view that wants the latest value per area, a history view that wants downsampled ranges, and an alerting path that wants per-device state. I will ingest once into Kafka and feed all three from it."

From your world

You have done this at small scale. SNMP polling and streaming telemetry (gNMI) from routers is exactly device telemetry: counters every few seconds from thousands of boxes. A NIC ring buffer that absorbs a burst before the kernel drains it is what Kafka does for a thundering herd of devices. Interface flap dampening (hold-down timers, so a link going up and down does not spam the routing table) is the same idea as alert hysteresis and debounce below. And RRDtool-style round-robin archives (keep 5-minute samples for a day, hourly for a month) are what we call downsampling and retention.

New words, once each: MQTT is a tiny publish/subscribe protocol made for small devices on bad networks; devices publish to topics on a broker. A geohash turns a latitude/longitude into a short string where nearby points share a prefix. H3 is Uber's open-source grid of hexagons covering the Earth. A window is a slice of time a stream processor aggregates over. A watermark is the stream processor's estimate of "all events older than this have arrived". Pub/sub lets a publisher send to a channel without knowing who listens. A WebSocket is a long-lived two-way connection between browser and server.
