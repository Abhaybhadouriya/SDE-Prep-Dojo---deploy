Quiz

10M meters report every 15 minutes. Why is the average rate (about 11K per second) misleading?

  1. Meters send more often at night
  2. Kafka cannot handle 11K per second
  3. If they all report on the quarter hour, millions arrive in seconds; you need jitter and a buffer
Synchronised clocks create a thundering herd. A random per-device offset spreads the load; Kafka absorbs what remains.

A meter was offline from 2 a.m. to 8 a.m. and uploads its readings at 8 a.m. Which time should bucket them?

  1. Event time (when measured)
  2. Processing time (when received)
  3. The broker's clock
Event time keeps usage in the right hour; watermarks and allowed lateness decide how long to wait.

Redis pub/sub is at-most-once. How does a dashboard avoid silently missing an update?

  1. It cannot; accept the loss
  2. Deltas carry a version; on a gap the client refetches the snapshot from the Redis hash
  3. Publish every message three times
State lives in the hash, the channel only signals. Versions detect gaps.

A driver pings every 4 s. The heatmap counts pings per cell over 20 minutes. What is wrong?

  1. Nothing
  2. Cells are too small
  3. One driver counts about 300 times; count distinct drivers (latest cell per driver, or HyperLogLog)
Density means drivers, not messages.

What stops a retried breach event from opening a second alert for the same sensor?

  1. A partial unique index on (sensor, rule) where state is OPEN or ACKNOWLEDGED
  2. A bigger Kafka retention
  3. Asking users to delete duplicates
The database enforces one active alert per incident, so retries are harmless.

Why store sum and count in rollups instead of only the average?

  1. Averages take more disk
  2. You can re-aggregate sums and counts into coarser buckets; you cannot average averages correctly
  3. TSDBs do not support averages
Daily average = sum of hourly sums / sum of hourly counts.

Why do Uber-style heatmaps use hexagons (H3)?

  1. They are easier to draw
  2. They compress better
  3. All six neighbours are the same distance away, which suits moving objects and smoothing
Squares have neighbours at two distances; hexagons limit error for things crossing cell edges.
