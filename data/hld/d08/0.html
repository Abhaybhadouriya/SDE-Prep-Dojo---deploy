The story

Explain like I'm 5

You are in a toy shop with a basket. You can put toys in and take them out as often as you like. Nobody minds, because nothing is final yet. That basket is your cart.

At the counter, the shopkeeper does three things in order. First she checks the toy is really still on the shelf and puts it behind the counter with a note: "for you, for 10 minutes". Then you pay. Then she writes you a receipt. If your money card does not work, she does not keep the toy behind the counter forever: she puts it back on the shelf for the next child. That "put it back" is called undo.

And if you were not sure she heard you and you hand over your money twice, a good shopkeeper looks at the ticket number on your basket and says "you already paid for this one". That ticket number is the most important idea on this page.

In real systems the basket is the cart, "behind the counter for 10 minutes" is an inventory reservation with a TTL (time to live), paying is a payment authorisation, the receipt is the order, the undo steps are compensations in a saga, and the ticket number is an idempotency key.

The cart and the checkout look like one feature, but they need opposite things. The cart must always accept writes, even when parts of the system are failing. The order must be exactly right, even if that means saying "please try again". Keep that split in mind, because the whole design follows from it.
