How real companies do it

  • Amazon: Dynamo and the cart. The 2007 Dynamo paper uses the shopping cart as its leading example: always writable, conflicts reconciled (merged) at read time. It is the origin story of "AP for the cart".
  • Amazon: idempotent APIs. The Builders' Library article "Making retries safe with idempotent APIs" explains client request tokens and returning the original response, which is the model for Place Order.
  • AWS Prime Day 2024. DynamoDB peaked at 146 million requests per second, and Aurora ran 376 billion transactions on 6,311 instances: key-value and relational stores side by side at the biggest scale.
  • Flipkart. For Big Billion Days 2023 it prepared for over 2,000 orders per second on its app, with 1.5 million requests per second when the sale opens. The second number is why the flash sale design exists.
  • Shopify. "Building resilient payment systems" covers timeouts, circuit breakers, idempotency keys and reconciliation, the toolkit for the payment step.
  • Microsoft Azure and microservices.io both document the saga pattern (orchestration vs choreography), and Chris Richardson's site documents the transactional outbox.

Related designs: flash sale (the hot-SKU version of this problem), payment system, wallet and ledger, hotel booking (holds with TTLs), food delivery, unique IDs. Concepts: distributed transactions and events, resilience patterns, multi-step processes (sagas).
