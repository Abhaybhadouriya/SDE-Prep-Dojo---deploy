Requirements

Functional
  • Cart: add, remove and change quantity. The cart persists across devices and for guests (a cart token).
  • Checkout page: choose an address and delivery speed. See item prices, promotions, tax, shipping fee and the promised delivery date.
  • Place order: one click creates one order, and charges the customer once.
  • Payment methods: card, UPI, wallet, cash on delivery.
  • Inventory: do not sell what we do not have (or only within an explicit backorder policy).
  • After the order: confirmation email/push, order history, cancel before shipping, refunds.
Non-functional
  • Cart: always available for writes. Amazon's Dynamo paper describes the shopping cart as the example service that must accept "add to cart" even during failures. Eventual consistency is acceptable.
  • Order and payment: strongly consistent, exactly-once in effect, auditable.
  • Latency: the checkout page loads in about 1 s, and Place Order answers in about 1-2 s (our targets).
  • Availability: 99.99% for Place Order (about 52 minutes of downtime a year).
  • Scale: sale days are 10x or more of a normal day (see the estimate).
  • Correct money: integer minor units (paise, cents), never floats. Prices shown equal prices charged.

New words, once each: ACID = a database transaction is all-or-nothing, and two transactions do not see each other's half-done work. CAP = when the network splits replicas apart, each side must choose between Consistency (refuse or wait, so nobody reads a wrong answer) and Availability (keep answering, maybe with stale data). AP = choose availability: always accept writes and merge later. CP = choose correctness: reject writes you cannot make safely. Saga = a chain of small local transactions, each with an undo step (deep dive 2). More on CAP: Fundamentals: Scale, Cache, DB, CAP.

Say this early: "I will split the problem in two. The cart is an availability problem, so AP, a key-value store, and merge conflicts kindly. Checkout is a correctness problem, so a saga of local ACID transactions with idempotency at every step." The interviewer now knows you understand CAP in practice, not just in theory.
