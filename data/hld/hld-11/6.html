Circuit breaker: stop calling a service that is down

Retries help with short blips. When a dependency is down for minutes, every call still waits for a timeout and burns resources. A circuit breaker is a small proxy (a library object or the sidecar) around the call. It watches recent failures and, past a threshold, stops making the call at all and fails fast, usually with a fallback. It is a three-state machine, named after the electrical breaker in your house. Click through it.

  1. Closed. Requests go through. The breaker keeps a rolling window of results (last N calls or last N seconds).
  2. Trip. Once the window has enough calls (a minimum volume) and the failure rate passes the threshold, the breaker opens. Slow calls can count as failures too.
  3. Open. Every call fails fast without touching the network. The caller returns a fallback. A timer starts.
  4. Half-open. When the timer ends, a small number of trial calls are allowed through to test the water.
  5. Still broken. Any trial failure sends it straight back to Open and restarts the timer (some designs grow the timer each time).
  6. Recovered. Enough trial successes close the breaker and reset the counters. Traffic flows normally.

Envoy (and so Istio) has a related tool called outlier detection. Instead of breaking the whole dependency, it ejects one bad host from the load-balancing pool. Defaults from Envoy's API: eject after 5 consecutive 5xx, for a base of 30 s (multiplied by how many times it was ejected), and never eject more than 10% of the cluster. That last cap matters. If the dependency is broken everywhere, ejecting every host would only make things worse.

Use one breaker per independent thing. Microsoft's Azure guide warns against a single breaker for a sharded store: one bad shard would trip the breaker and block the healthy shards too. Key breakers by host, shard or endpoint. Also, retries must understand the breaker. A "circuit open" error is not retryable.

From your world

A circuit breaker is like BFD plus route dampening. You detect a flapping neighbour, withdraw it so traffic stops being black-holed, and wait a penalty time before you trust it again. Half-open is the probe after the dampening timer runs out.
