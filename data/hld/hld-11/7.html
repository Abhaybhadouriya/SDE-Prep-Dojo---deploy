Bulkheads: one leak must not sink the ship

A ship's hull is split into sealed compartments called bulkheads. A hole floods one compartment, not the ship. In software, a bulkhead gives each dependency (or each customer class) its own limited pool of resources: threads, connections, semaphores, queues, even separate pods. If the recommendations service hangs, it can use up only the 10 threads in its own pool, not the 200 threads that also serve payments.

Netflix's Hystrix used exactly this: a thread pool per dependency. Its wiki says the Netflix API ran 40+ pools of 5-20 threads each, most set to 10. A cheaper variant is a semaphore bulkhead: a counter that limits concurrent calls without separate threads. It sheds load, but unlike a thread pool it cannot walk away from a call that hangs. At a bigger scale, bulkheads become separate pods per customer tier, separate Kafka consumer groups per priority, or whole cells (AWS calls this cell-based architecture: independent copies of the stack, each serving a slice of customers). In Kubernetes, CPU and memory requests/limits plus ResourceQuota per namespace are bulkheads too.

From your world

Bulkheads are per-queue buffer carving on a switch, or cgroups limits for processes. One flow cannot eat the shared packet buffer, and one container cannot eat all host memory. Same idea, applied to threads and connections.
