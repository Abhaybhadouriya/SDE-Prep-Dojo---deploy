How the real companies do it

  • Zoom: meeting servers are Multimedia Routers (MMRs) grouped into Meeting Zones, managed by Zone Controllers that report to a Global Cloud Controller. Zones are duplicated in each data centre; participants go to the nearest data centre and least-loaded server; 2-person meetings can go P2P; UDP first with TCP/TLS 443 fallback; usable to about 45% loss; 50% spare capacity kept. Third-party write-ups describe SVC layering and "route, don't transcode".
  • Discord (2018): 2.6M concurrent voice users, 220 Gbps and 120 Mpps egress, 850+ voice servers in 13 regions. Elixir signalling, homegrown C++ SFU, etcd for discovery, one voice server per guild, no ICE, Salsa20 encryption for native clients, and automatic failover by reassigning the guild.
  • Microsoft Teams (April 2020): 200M daily meeting participants and 4.1B meeting minutes a day. Scaled by adding regions per geography, smaller clusters, Protobuf + LZ4 for cache payloads (65% smaller), time-of-day load balancing, and planned degradation (typing indicator off, prefetch off).
  • Jitsi (open source): Jicofo signalling separate from jitsi-videobridge (the SFU); Octo cascading across six AWS regions; one modest server forwarded 1,000+ streams at about 20% CPU.
  • LiveKit (open source, Go on Pion): SFU with Redis mapping each room to a node; self-hosted rooms fit on one node, the cloud version meshes servers for bigger rooms.
