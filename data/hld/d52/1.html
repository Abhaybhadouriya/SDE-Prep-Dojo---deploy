Requirements

First, a few names you will see everywhere. WebRTC is the browser and mobile standard for real-time audio, video and data between endpoints. RTP (Real-time Transport Protocol) carries the media packets, and SRTP is its encrypted form. RTCP is the side channel that reports loss, jitter and bandwidth. SDP (Session Description Protocol) is a text description of codecs, streams and keys; MDN points out it is really a data format, not a protocol, and it describes the media without carrying it. Signalling is however you exchange SDP and network addresses; WebRTC deliberately leaves that to you, and most systems use a WebSocket.

Functional

  1. Create and join meetings A host schedules or starts a meeting and gets a link. Participants join with the link, optionally after a waiting room.
  2. Audio and video Everyone can publish a microphone and camera and see a speaker view or a gallery (for example 25 tiles).
  3. Screen sharing One participant shares a screen; text must be sharp even if the frame rate is low.
  4. Meeting controls Mute, unmute, mute-all by host, raise hand, in-meeting chat, active-speaker highlight.
  5. Recording The host can record to the cloud and download it after the meeting.

Out of scope (say so): live transcription and AI summaries (mention they read the same audio the recorder reads), webinars for 10,000 viewers (that becomes one-to-many streaming, see Video Streaming), dial-in phone gateways, breakout rooms.

Notice the unusual one: freshness beats completeness. A database must never lose a write. A video call should happily drop a late packet rather than wait for it. That single fact changes the transport, the retry policy and the buffering.
