Where Kafka, Kubernetes and Docker fit

ComponentFitNotes
API, meeting, placement, signalling servicesKubernetes Deployments in Docker imagesStateless or Redis-backed; scale with HPA on connections and CPU; pre-scale with scheduled scaling before :00 and :30. See Scheduling & Autoscaling.
Signalling WebSocket gatewaysKubernetes, with long terminationGracePeriodSecondsDrain connections slowly on deploy so clients reconnect gradually, not all at once.
SFU media serversOften bare metal or VMs; on Kubernetes use hostNetwork, one pod per node, a fixed UDP port rangeMedia needs public IPs, wide UDP port ranges and line-rate packet I/O; kube-proxy NAT and overlay networks add cost. Discord runs voice on physical servers; LiveKit documents running with host networking.
TURN servers (for example coturn)Same as SFU: host networking, public IPs, anycast or geo DNSSized by bandwidth, not requests.
Recorder bots and transcodersKubernetes Jobs or a worker Deployment, GPU node pool optionalClassic queue-driven batch work; autoscale on queue depth.
KafkaEvents, never mediaTopics: meeting-events (joined, left, ended: billing, audit, analytics), media-qos (per-participant loss/jitter every few seconds, at millions of messages per second), recording-ready. Media packets are too latency-sensitive and too disposable for a durable log. See Kafka Core Concepts.

Do not say "media goes through Kafka". Kafka adds milliseconds of batching, writes to disk and keeps data for days. Media wants microseconds, RAM only, and is useless after 300 ms.
