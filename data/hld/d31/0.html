The story

Explain like I'm 5

A big school gives every drawing a sticker number so nobody's drawing gets mixed up. At first one teacher has one roll of stickers: 1, 2, 3... But now the school has 100 classrooms and the line at the teacher's desk is too long. So the head teacher gives every classroom its own colour. The red room writes "red-1, red-2", the blue room writes "blue-1, blue-2". No two stickers can ever be the same, because no two rooms have the same colour. And if every sticker also starts with the time of day, you can line up all the drawings in the order they were made, just by reading the stickers.

That is the whole design. A unique ID is a number that names one thing (a tweet, an order, a chat message) and is never reused. On one database you get this for free with an AUTO_INCREMENT column. Once you have many databases (shards) or many app servers creating rows at the same time, a single counter becomes a bottleneck and a single point of failure. So we give every generator its own "colour" (a machine ID), put the time at the front, and add a small counter for things made in the same millisecond.

Twitter called its version Snowflake (no two snowflakes are alike). Instagram, Discord, Sony, Baidu and Meituan built close cousins. This page compares them all and shows which knob each one turned.

Where this shows up: almost every other design needs IDs. The URL shortener needs short keys, chat needs message IDs that sort by time, news feed needs post IDs you can paginate by. Interviewers also ask it on its own: a Microsoft SDE-2 report from March 2026 asked for "a distributed, highly available unique ID generator" (see our gap analysis).
