Snowflake pros
  • No network call per ID; scales by adding workers
  • Fits BIGINT; index-friendly (new keys append at the right edge)
  • Decodable: creation time and source are inside the ID
Snowflake cons
  • Depends on clocks; must handle rollback
  • Worker IDs are a finite pool to manage
  • Not strictly ordered across workers; leaks creation time and rough volume

Failure modes to name

  • Clock rollback: refuse or wait, alert; never issue.
  • Duplicate worker ID: lease with fencing; check at startup that no other live holder exists.
  • Coordinator down: running workers keep going; new ones start from a locally cached worker ID (Leaf does this) or wait.
  • Hot right edge: every insert goes to the last B-tree page. Usually fine, but on some range-partitioned stores (HBase, Bigtable) monotonic keys hit one region; salt or hash the key there.
  • Epoch exhaustion: put the epoch near your launch date and write down the end date. 41 bits from 2010 ends in 2080.
  • Security: sequential IDs let outsiders count your orders or guess URLs. For public IDs, add an opaque random token or encrypt the ID.
