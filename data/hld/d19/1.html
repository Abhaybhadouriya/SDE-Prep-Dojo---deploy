Requirements

Start every design by asking questions. Here are the ones that matter for logs, with the answers we will assume.

  1. Who writes logs? About 20,000 containers (pods) across a few thousand Kubernetes nodes, plus some virtual machines.
  2. Who reads them? Engineers debugging incidents, alert rules that look for error patterns, and security/audit teams.
  3. How fresh? A line should be searchable within about 10-30 seconds of being written.
  4. How long do we keep them? 7 days hot (fast search), 30 days warm, 1 year cold for audit.
  5. Can we lose logs? Debug logs: a tiny loss is acceptable under extreme overload. Audit logs: never.
Functional
  • Collect stdout/stderr from every container and system logs from every node.
  • Search by time range + labels (service, namespace, pod, level) + free text ("timeout", an order ID).
  • Live tail ("follow") a service's logs.
  • Alert on patterns ("more than 100 OutOfMemory lines per minute").
  • Redact secrets and personal data (PII) before storage.
Out of scope (say it out loud)
  • Metrics dashboards (that is Metrics & Monitoring).
  • Distributed tracing UI (we only keep the trace_id field so you can jump to traces).
  • Business analytics on events (that is a data lake; see Ad Click Aggregator).
