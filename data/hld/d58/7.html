Where Kafka, Kubernetes and Docker fit

PieceRole here
KafkaTopics raw-articles (key = feed_id), articles-parsed (key = article_id), clicks (key = user or anon id). Fetchers produce bursts; parsers and the clusterer consume at their own pace. Retention of a few days lets us replay after a parser or clustering bug. See Kafka core concepts and consumer groups.
KubernetesFetchers, parsers and the feed API are stateless Deployments. Scale the feed API with an HPA on CPU or requests per second; scale parsers on consumer lag (KEDA). The ranker can be a CronJob or a single leader-elected Deployment. See Deployments and autoscaling.
DockerEach stage ships as a small image; the parser image bundles language-detection and HTML-extraction libraries, which are heavy, so keep them in a multi-stage build. See Dockerfile.

Why key raw-articles by feed_id? Items from one feed stay in order on one partition, so the parser sees an "update" to an article after its first version. Clustering is keyed differently (by article), so we re-key into a second topic. Consumer lag on the clusterer is your "freshness" alarm: lag in seconds is roughly how late news reaches the board.
