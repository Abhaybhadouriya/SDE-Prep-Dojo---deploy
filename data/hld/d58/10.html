How real systems do it

  • Google News (WWW 2007 paper) - more than 4,500 sources; personalisation from MinHash/PLSI user clusters and co-visitation, clicks in Bigtable keyed by user, separate update and serving servers. What the paper teaches beyond the algorithms: (1) the heavy work (clustering users with MinHash and fitting PLSI) runs offline as MapReduce jobs, while co-visitation ("people who read story A in the last few hours also read B") is updated online, because news goes stale in hours and a model trained yesterday cannot know today's stories; (2) the three signals are blended with a simple weighted sum, which is easy to tune; (3) the team judged the system with live click-through tests against a "most popular stories" baseline rather than offline accuracy alone. That is the candidate-plus-signals shape of deep dive 5.
  • Google's crawler (WWW 2007 paper) - 64-bit SimHash with k = 3 over 8 billion pages in 64 GB.
  • Google News 2018 redesign - AI storylines; Full Coverage and headlines are the same for everyone; only the 5-story "For you" briefing is personal. That is the "shared feed, personal layer on top" choice this page recommends.
  • AltaVista (Broder 1997) - shingles and MinHash clustered over 30 million documents.
  • WebSub (W3C) - the push protocol many blogs and news sites support through a hub, which is how an aggregator can hear about a new article in seconds instead of on its next poll.
