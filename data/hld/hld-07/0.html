The story: the school library

Explain like I'm 5

Your school library has thousands of books. If they were piled on the floor, finding "Dinosaurs" would mean looking at every single book. So the librarian does three smart things.

First, she decides what questions kids ask ("Where are the dinosaur books?", "Which books did Riya borrow?") and puts books on shelves to answer those questions fast. That is data modeling.

Second, she makes a card box sorted A to Z that says "Dinosaurs - shelf 7". Looking in the card box is fast because it is sorted. That is an index.

Third, when two kids want the last copy of the same book at the same time, she has a rule: one kid gets it, the other waits or picks another. That is locking and isolation.

From your world

A table with a primary key is a lookup table like a routing table keyed by prefix. An index is a second, sorted lookup structure pointing into the same entries, like keeping both a MAC table and an ARP table over one set of hosts. The write-ahead log is a filesystem journal, and row locks are mutexes and seqlocks. The new part is scale and failure: the "table" lives on disk, is shared by thousands of concurrent writers, and must survive a crash halfway through.

Every system design answer has a database in the middle. Interviewers do not want a perfect textbook schema. They want to see that your tables, keys and indexes come straight from the questions the product asks, and that you know what happens when many users write at the same moment. This page builds that skill from the ground up.
