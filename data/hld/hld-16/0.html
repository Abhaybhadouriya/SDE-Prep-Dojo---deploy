The story

Explain like I'm 5

You want to build a new town. First a town planner draws a map: here is the school, here is the hospital, these roads join them, and the water tank sits on the hill so water flows down. That is the big picture. Nobody talks about doors yet.

Then a carpenter builds one house. Now the questions are small and exact. How many rooms? Which door opens into which room? Can we add a room later without breaking a wall? That is the small picture.

A good builder can do both, and knows when to zoom in and when to zoom out. Design interviews test exactly this. The planner round is HLD (high-level design). The carpenter round is LLD (low-level design).

Hello Interview puts it in one line: system design is the map, and low-level design is the blueprint for one building on that map. HLD asks "which services, which databases, how do they scale and fail?" LLD asks "which classes, which methods, who owns which data, and can I add a feature without a rewrite?" Indian product companies also run a third flavour, the machine-coding round: an LLD problem you must actually code, run and demo in about 90 minutes.

From your world

HLD is the network diagram you draw for a customer: sites, links, which box is the route reflector, what happens when a link fails. LLD is the code inside one box: the structs, state machines and locks of the BGP daemon. A machine-coding round is writing a small daemon live, with clean modules, and running it. Where this breaks: LLD rounds are graded on object-oriented design (interfaces, classes, patterns), so the C habit of one big file of structs and switch statements loses points even when it works.
