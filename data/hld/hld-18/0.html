The story: the bouncer at the cinema

Explain like I'm 5

A new superhero film opens, and everyone wants tickets. A bouncer stands at the cinema door. He does not ask every child to do a maths test; that would make the line very slow. Instead he watches. Most children walk in with their parents, look around, chat, and buy one or two tickets. But some "children" look exactly the same, wear the same coat, arrive at the same second, and each one tries to buy fifty tickets to sell outside for double the price. The bouncer lets the normal kids walk straight in. For anyone who looks a bit odd, he asks one easy question. And the fifty identical kids? He spots that they all came off the same bus, and stops the whole bus.

The bouncer is edge defense. Watching quietly and letting most people pass is silent risk scoring. The easy question is a challenge (a CAPTCHA or an invisible browser test). Noticing that many visitors share the same coat and bus is behavioural clustering: judging a group, not a single request. A rule book at the door ("no bags bigger than this", "no more than ten entries per minute per family") is a WAF rule engine with rate-based rules. And the crowd-control barriers down the street that stop a stampede before it reaches the door are DDoS shielding.

From your world

You have built all of these as packet features. A WAF is an ACL engine for HTTP: ordered rules, match conditions, actions, hit counters. A rate-based rule is a policer keyed on a flow tuple. Count mode is an ACL entry with log and no deny. TLS fingerprinting (JA3/JA4) is passive OS fingerprinting like p0f reading TCP SYN options, moved up to the TLS ClientHello. And the anycast edge that absorbs floods is BGP doing what you already know it does: announcing one prefix from hundreds of sites so attack traffic lands close to its source and is dropped there.

This page goes deeper than the DDoS and API-security parts of hld-14 Security & Identity. It is the layer that makes d02 Distributed Rate Limiter and d09 Flash Sale survive real traffic, where a big share of requests at the start of a sale are not human.
