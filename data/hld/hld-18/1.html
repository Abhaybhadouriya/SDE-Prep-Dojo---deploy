What bots actually do

Must know (SDE-1)

Not all bots are bad. Search engine crawlers, uptime monitors and link-preview fetchers are welcome, and good ones can be verified (for example by reverse DNS or signed requests). The problem is automation that abuses a business flow. OWASP keeps a catalogue of these called the Automated Threats to Web Applications. The ones interviewers ask about:

ThreatWhat the bot doesWhere it hurts
Credential stuffingTries username/password pairs leaked from other sitesLogin endpoint; account takeover
Scalping / inventory hoardingBuys or holds limited stock (tickets, sneakers, sale phones) faster than humansCheckout, add-to-cart; flash sales
ScrapingCopies prices, listings or content at high volumeSearch and product pages; cost and competitive harm
Fake account creationMass sign-ups for promo abuse, spam or fake reviewsSign-up, referral and coupon flows
CardingTests stolen card numbers with tiny paymentsPayment endpoint; chargebacks
SMS pumpingTriggers OTP SMS to premium numbers it profits fromOTP and "send code" endpoints
Application-layer DDoSFloods expensive endpoints (search, login) with valid-looking HTTPOrigin CPU and databases

Notice that most of these requests are syntactically perfect. A credential-stuffing request is a normal login POST. No signature catches it. That is why modern edge defense has two very different halves: signature and rule matching (the WAF, good at malformed or malicious payloads like SQL injection) and behavioural risk scoring (bot management, good at valid requests made by the wrong kind of client or at the wrong rate).
