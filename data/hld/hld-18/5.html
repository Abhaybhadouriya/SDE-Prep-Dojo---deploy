Behavioural clustering: judging the crowd, not the request

Good to know (SDE-2)

A careful bot can make each individual request look human: residential IP, perfect Chrome fingerprint, polite rate. What it cannot easily hide is that it is one of ten thousand clients doing the same unusual thing. Clustering looks for groups of sessions that share features no natural population shares.

Useful clustering keys:

  • Fingerprint + path: sessions with the same device/TLS fingerprint that hit the same sequence of URLs.
  • Timing regularity: humans are bursty and irregular; scripts are metronomic or uniformly random.
  • Shared artefacts: one token, cookie or device ID used across many IPs or countries; many accounts sharing one device, card or payout address.
  • Graph links: accounts created minutes apart, from the same subnet, with sequential email patterns, all redeeming the same coupon.

Managed services do this for you: AWS WAF Bot Control's machine-learning rules analyse traffic statistics every few minutes to detect "distributed, coordinated bot activity" across many IP addresses, and need up to 24 hours to build a baseline after you enable them. In your own platform, the usual design is a streaming pipeline: edge logs go to Kafka, a stream job computes per-fingerprint and per-cluster aggregates over sliding windows, and the result is pushed back to the edge as a deny or challenge list keyed by fingerprint, not just by IP.

From your world

This is NetFlow-based anomaly detection versus per-packet ACLs. A single SYN is fine; a hundred thousand SYNs with the same TTL and window size from scattered sources is a spoofed flood. The interesting signal lives in the aggregate over flows, which is why you sample at the edge and classify in a collector, then push back a filter: the classic control-plane / data-plane split.
