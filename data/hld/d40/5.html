Architecture: the booking flow

Click Next or Play. The read path (search, view) is on top, and the write path (hold, pay, confirm) is below.

  1. Search. The guest searches "Goa, 24-27 Dec, 2 adults". The search service queries an Elasticsearch index (geo + amenities + an approximate availability bitmap). The results may be seconds stale, and that is fine.
  2. View. The guest opens a hotel. The hotel and rate services serve content and nightly prices. Availability comes from the Redis cache, because reads outnumber writes by about 10x.
  3. Hold. The guest clicks "Reserve". The reservation service runs one transaction on the inventory shard: it checks and increments total_reserved for each night, and it writes a HELD reservation that expires in 10 minutes. If any night is full, it rolls back.
  4. Pay. The payment service creates a payment intent with the payment provider (PSP), using the reservation id as the idempotency key. Card details are tokenised in the browser and never touch our servers.
  5. Confirm. The PSP sends a webhook. In one transaction, an idempotent handler moves the reservation from HELD to CONFIRMED. If the payment fails or the hold expires, a sweeper releases the counters.
  6. Propagate. CDC from the inventory database (Debezium, or Airbnb's SpinalTap) publishes changes to Kafka. Consumers update Redis, the search index, emails and analytics. They are eventually consistent and never on the booking path.

From your world

A hold with a TTL is a DHCP lease. The server hands out an address for a fixed time. If the client never renews, the lease expires and the address returns to the pool. Nobody has to "remember to free it", because the clock frees it. The CDC stream is like netlink route notifications: the routing table (the database) is the truth, and listeners update their own copies (FIB, caches) from the change events.
