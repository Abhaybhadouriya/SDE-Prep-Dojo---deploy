Requirements

Always agree on scope first. Here is a scope that fits 45 minutes. It follows the ByteByteGo hotel chapter, with the Airbnb variant added.

Functional
  • Search for stays by city, dates and guests. We keep this light, because search has its own page (post search, geo indexes).
  • View a hotel or listing page with its price per night.
  • Reserve a room type, or a listing, for a date range.
  • Cancel a reservation, with a refund under the policy.
  • Hotel staff or hosts can change rooms, prices and the calendar.
  • Optional: allow overbooking, for example selling 10% more rooms than the hotel has. Hotels do this because some guests cancel.
Non-functional
  • No double booking. This is the one rule we never break. Booking needs strong consistency.
  • Searching and browsing must be fast and always available (about 100-300 ms at p99 - p99 = 99% of requests are faster than this; the slowest 1% are what users complain about). Search results may be slightly stale.
  • Booking may be slow. A few seconds is fine, because a payment is in the path.
  • High concurrency on hot items: a holiday weekend, a viral listing, or a flash sale.
  • Read-heavy: most visitors look and never book.

From your world

p99 is like tail latency through a switch buffer: the average packet sails through, but the 1 in 100 that waits behind a burst is the one the user feels. Design targets are set on the tail, not the average.

Say this early: "Search is an AP problem and booking is a CP problem." Search can show slightly old availability. Booking is checked against the source of truth, inside one transaction. That one sentence frames the rest of the design.
