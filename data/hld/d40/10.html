How real companies do it

  • Airbnb, payments (blog). Orpheus is an idempotency library that splits each request into pre-RPC, RPC and post-RPC phases. It uses a leased row lock per key, keeps idempotency tables on a sharded primary, and classifies errors as retryable or non-retryable.
  • Airbnb, data changes (blog). SpinalTap is a CDC service. It tails database changelogs and publishes ordered-per-row, at-least-once events. Availability, pricing and search indexing react to these events, and reservation workflows are the motivating example.
  • Airbnb, database scaling (blog). In 2015 they moved the message-inbox tables (nearly 1/3 of the writes on the main database) to their own database. They used MySQL replication and replica promotion, with under 10 minutes of planned downtime. They prefer vertical partitioning by function to horizontal sharding.
  • Airbnb, architecture (ByteByteGo summary). It grew from a Rails monolith ("Monorail") to SOA. It then moved to a micro + macroservice hybrid, because hundreds of services became hard to manage.
  • Agoda (OTA) (blog). PriceAggregator caches supplier prices per itinerary. It pre-fetches the most valuable itineraries within each supplier's requests-per-second budget, and it beat both TTL-only and LRU-based approaches in their A/B tests.
