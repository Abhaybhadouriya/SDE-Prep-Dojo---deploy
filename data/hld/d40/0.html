The story

Explain like I'm 5

Grandma runs a tiny guest house with 3 bedrooms. She keeps a big notebook. Each page is one night, and each page has 3 empty boxes. When a family books Friday and Saturday, she writes their name in a box on the Friday page and on the Saturday page. If a page has no empty box left, she says "sorry, full".

One day two families phone her at the same moment. Grandma answers one, puts the other on hold, and only writes in pen after the first family pays. While someone is "thinking about it", she writes their name in pencil. If they do not call back in 10 minutes, she rubs it out.

That notebook is the whole design. The pages are dates. The boxes are inventory, which means the rooms we can still sell. Writing in pencil is a hold, a temporary claim that expires after a set time (its TTL, time to live). Writing in pen is a confirmed reservation. Answering one phone at a time is concurrency control: rules that stop two writers from filling the same box.

The hard part is not the web pages. It is making the notebook correct when it is split over many servers and thousands of people use it at once. A hotel can be overbooked. It can also be underbooked, because holds that never expire block real guests. A good design avoids both.

Two flavours of the same question. Hotel (Marriott, Booking.com): a hotel has room types with a count, for example "40 Deluxe King rooms". Airbnb: each listing is usually one unit, so each night is either free or taken. Both use the same idea: one row per (thing, night). We cover both, because interviewers switch between them.
