S2 and H3: grids for the whole planet

Google S2: a cube and a Hilbert curve

S2 projects the sphere onto the 6 faces of a cube. Each face is split into 4 children, again and again, for levels 0 to 30. So level k has 6 x 4k cells, and a level-30 leaf cell is about 1 cm across (S2 developer guide). Cells are numbered along a Hilbert curve, a space-filling curve that never jumps far, so nearby IDs usually mean nearby cells. Each cell ID is a 64-bit integer, and you get the parent by clearing low bits. Cells are close to equal area everywhere (within a factor of about 1.7 on average, per the S2 docs), which fixes geohash's polar problem. MongoDB's 2dsphere index is built on S2.

A big S2 feature is the region coverer: give it any shape (a circle of 2 km, a city polygon) and it returns a small set of cells at mixed levels that covers it. Each cell is a contiguous range of 64-bit IDs, so "points inside this shape" becomes a handful of range scans on an ordinary sorted key-value store.

Uber H3: hexagons

H3 is Uber's open-source grid, written in C, with 64-bit cell IDs. Uber's blog explains the choice of hexagons: a hexagon's centre is the same distance from all 6 neighbours. A square has 4 edge neighbours and 4 corner neighbours at a longer distance, and a triangle has three different distances. Equal neighbours make "spread demand to nearby cells" and "rings around me" simple and fair.

H3 facts worth knowing, from Uber's blog and the H3 resolution table:

  • 16 resolutions, 0 (coarsest) to 15. Each finer level is about 1/7 the area of its parent.
  • The grid sits on a 20-face icosahedron with 122 base cells. Hexagons cannot tile a sphere alone, so 12 pentagons exist, placed in the ocean on purpose.
  • Average size: resolution 7 is about 5.16 km2 (edge about 1.4 km), resolution 8 is about 0.74 km2 (edge about 530 m), resolution 9 is about 0.105 km2 (edge about 200 m).
  • Unlike geohash and S2, close H3 IDs are not reliably close on the map. You do not prefix-scan H3. You compute the neighbour ring with gridDisk(cell, k) and look up those exact IDs: WHERE h3_cell IN (...).
  • Uber uses it to measure supply and demand per hexagon for surge pricing, and for dispatch decisions such as matching nearby pooled rides.
