The story

Explain like I'm 5

A big playground has a friendship game. Each child gets a pile of picture cards of other children who live close by. You look at one card at a time. Put it on the right if you want to be friends, on the left if not. A teacher watches the cards. If Asha puts Ben's card on the right and Ben puts Asha's card on the right, the teacher rings a bell: "You two are friends!" The teacher must never miss a bell, even if both children put their cards down at the very same moment. And nobody should see the same card twice - that would be boring.

That is the system. The pile of cards is the recommendation stack: nearby profiles that match your filters. Right and left are swipes - tiny writes, but billions of them. The bell is match detection, which needs a careful, atomic check. "Never the same card twice" is the seen-filter, where a Bloom filter shines.

New words, once each: QPS is requests per second. p99 means "99 out of 100 are faster than this". A Bloom filter is a compact bit array that answers "definitely not seen" or "probably seen". Redis is an in-memory key-value store, and a Lua script in Redis runs atomically inside the server, like a critical section: no other command on that shard runs in the middle. A geo index (geohash, quadtree or Google's S2 cells) turns "near me" into a lookup by cell. Geosharding means splitting the search index by area, so each shard holds the profiles of one region. Idempotent means safe to retry. TTL (time to live) is an automatic expiry. The rest are in the glossary.

Who asks this? Our gap analysis found Tinder in two curricula: Hello Interview's problem breakdowns and AlgoMaster's problem list. We found no self-reported target-company interview, so treat it as practice for two patterns that big companies test everywhere: geo-proximity search (like Yelp and Uber) and contention on a shared fact (like ticket booking).
