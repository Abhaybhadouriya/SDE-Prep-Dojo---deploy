Data model

DataShapeStore and why
Profileuser_id -> name, birthdate, gender, interested_in, age range, max distance, photo keysKV / document store (Tinder uses DynamoDB per the newsletter). Source of truth.
Searchable profileuser_id, age, gender, interested_in, geo point, S2 cell, activity and ranking scoresElasticsearch / OpenSearch, sharded by geography. Fed by change events (CDC / Kafka).
Swipepartition key = min(a,b):max(a,b); columns: a_to_b, b_to_a, timestampsRedis holds only right swipes from the last 14 days (hot, atomic check); Cassandra/DynamoDB holds every swipe (durable). Pair key puts both directions in one place.
Swipes by user(swiper_id, swiped_at) -> target, decisionWide-column, for history, rebuilding Bloom filters and analytics. Left swipes can be archived to S3.
Matchmatch_id = min:max, both ids, created_atKV store. A deterministic id makes "create match" idempotent.
Seen filterseen:{userId} -> Bloom filterRedis (RedisBloom). Rebuildable from swipe history.
Precomputed stackstack:{userId} -> list of candidate ids, TTL under 1 hourRedis.

The key trick is the pair key. If you store swipes by swiper (swiper_id as partition key), Asha's swipe on Ben and Ben's swipe on Asha land on different partitions, maybe different machines. Checking "did the other person already say yes?" then needs a cross-partition read, which races. Store both directions under one key, min(id):max(id), and the check becomes a single-partition, atomic operation.
