Item-item at scale: the offline table

Good to know (SDE-2)

The naive build compares every pair of items: with N items that is N squared pairs, impossible for tens of millions of items. The trick from the Amazon paper is to only visit pairs that co-occur: for each customer, emit every pair of items that customer bought. Because most customers buy few items, the real work is far below the worst case. Linden et al. note the worst case is O(N2M) but in practice it is closer to O(NM), because most customers have very few purchases.

  1. Start from the interaction log (Kafka topic landed in the lake): rows of (user, item, weight). Weight might be 1 for a view and 5 for a purchase.
  2. A Spark or MapReduce job groups events by user and emits every pair of items that user touched. A user with 10 items emits 45 pairs. Cap very heavy users (bots, resellers) or they explode the job.
  3. Sum the products per pair to get the dot product, divide by the two item norms (computed in a separate small pass) to get cosine. Apply shrinkage for small co-counts.
  4. For each item keep only its top-K neighbours. This turns a huge sparse matrix into a fixed-size list per item.
  5. Bulk-load the lists into a key-value store (Redis, DynamoDB, Cassandra), versioned, and flip a pointer when the load is complete.
  6. Online: the service reads the user's recent items, fetches their neighbour lists in one batched call, and merges them.
  7. Sum the scores of items reached from several of Eve's items, remove anything already bought or blocked, and hand the top candidates to ranking. Total online work: a few KV reads, no matrix maths.

Back-of-envelope

10 million items x top 100 neighbours x (8-byte item id + 4-byte score) = 10M x 100 x 12 bytes = 12 GB. That fits in a small Redis cluster with replicas. Compare with storing the full item-item matrix: 10M x 10M cells, which is not possible. Top-K truncation is what makes memory-based CF deployable. See Back-of-Envelope Estimation for the method.

Popularity leak. Raw co-occurrence counts make every item "similar" to the bestseller (everyone bought the phone charger). Cosine's division by the norm fixes most of this; some teams also divide by a power of the item's popularity. Always eyeball the neighbour list of a bestseller before shipping.
