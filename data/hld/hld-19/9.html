Gotchas and failure modes

Scenario: recommendations got worse after a "successful" retrain

The nightly ALS job finished, offline RMSE improved, but click-through on the home page dropped 8% the next morning.
Weak answer: "Roll back and retrain with more iterations."
Strong answer: Check version skew first: did the user-vector store and the item index flip to the new version together? Mixed versions produce random dot products. Then check the metric: RMSE on explicit ratings does not measure ranking on implicit traffic; evaluate recall@k on a time-based holdout. Then check for leakage (random split lets the model see the future) and for a popularity collapse (look at catalogue coverage of the top-10 lists). Add a canary: serve the new version to 1-5% and compare online metrics before the full flip.
Follow-ups: how would you make the two stores flip atomically? What offline metric correlates best with CTR for you?
  • Feedback loops. The model recommends popular items, they get more clicks, the next model recommends them even more. Add exploration slots and log what was shown (impressions) so you can tell "not clicked" from "not shown".
  • Bots and resellers. One user with 50,000 purchases emits about 1.25 billion pairs in the item-item job and links unrelated items. Cap per-user events and weight by 1/log(activity).
  • Redis LIMIT. FT.SEARCH returns 10 rows by default even if you ask for KNN 500. Add LIMIT 0 500.
  • Filtered ANN returns too few. If 99% of items are out of stock, a post-filter on KNN 500 may return 5 items. Redis switches between batch and brute-force filtering heuristically; other engines need a larger k or a pre-filtered partition.
  • Normalised vs raw vectors. Normalising item vectors turns MIPS into cosine and changes results. Decide once, and do it the same way at training, indexing and query time.
  • Already-seen items. CF loves recommending the thing you just bought. Keep a per-user "seen / bought" set and filter after retrieval (ask for extra candidates to make up for it).
