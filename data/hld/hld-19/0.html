The story

Explain like I'm 5

There is an ice-cream shop with a kind owner who remembers every kid. One day a new kid, Eve, comes in and says, "I loved the chocolate one." The owner thinks: "The kids who loved chocolate also loved the fudge one." So she gives Eve a taste of fudge. Eve loves it.

The owner did not need to know why chocolate kids like fudge. She only remembered who liked what. That is collaborative filtering: the crowd's taste does the thinking.

Later the shop gets huge: a million kids and ten thousand flavours. The owner cannot remember every pair. So she gives every kid and every flavour a tiny card with two numbers on it: "how sweet" and "how fruity". To guess if a kid will like a flavour, she just compares the two cards. Those cards are embeddings, and learning them is matrix factorization.

Finally, when a kid walks in, she must find the ten flavour cards closest to the kid's card, fast, while a queue waits. She keeps the cards sorted in clever piles so she only looks at a few piles. That is approximate nearest neighbour (ANN) search.

From your world

Think of a router that has never been told the topology but watches traffic. If flows from host A and host B keep going to the same destinations, it can guess that a new flow from A will likely go where B's flows went. Collaborative filtering is that: it learns from the traffic matrix (who talked to what), not from any description of the hosts. Matrix factorization compresses that huge, sparse traffic matrix into a few numbers per host, like describing every node by a short coordinate vector instead of a full adjacency row. ANN search is longest-prefix-match for "closeness": a coarse lookup narrows the search, then a fine lookup picks the winner.
