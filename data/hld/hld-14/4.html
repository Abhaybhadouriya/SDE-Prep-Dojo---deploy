OpenID Connect, SSO and SAML

OpenID Connect (OIDC) is a thin identity layer on OAuth 2.0. Add the openid scope and the authorization server (now called an OpenID Provider or identity provider, IdP) returns an extra ID token: a JWT whose audience is the client app, saying who logged in, when and how. It also standardizes a /userinfo endpoint, a discovery document at /.well-known/openid-configuration, and a JWKS URL where the IdP publishes its public signing keys.

Single sign-on

Single sign-on (SSO) means one login at a central IdP gives access to many apps (the service providers). The user's first app redirects to the IdP; the IdP sets its own session cookie; when the user opens a second app, that app also redirects to the IdP, which sees the existing session and sends the user straight back with a fresh assertion, no password prompt.

Karan Pratap Singh's book sums up SSO's two weaknesses: a single compromised SSO password opens every app, and every app now depends on the IdP. So the IdP needs MFA, its own high-availability design (see hld-13), and short sessions for sensitive apps. An identity broker sits between many apps and many IdPs (for example, letting a B2B SaaS accept each customer's own Okta or Entra ID) and translates between SAML and OIDC.
