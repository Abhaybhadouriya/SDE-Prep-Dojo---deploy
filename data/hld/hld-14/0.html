The story: the school fair

Explain like I'm 5

At the school fair, a teacher at the gate checks your face against your school photo. That is the teacher learning who you are. Then she puts a coloured wristband on you: green means you may ride the small rides, gold means you may also ride the big ones. Now every ride operator only looks at your wristband; they do not need to see your photo again. The wristband falls off at 6 pm, so a lost one cannot be used forever. When your mum lets your older cousin collect your prize, she does not give him her house key; she writes a note: "He may pick up one prize, today only."

Checking your face is authentication (proving who you are). The wristband colour is authorization (what you are allowed to do). The wristband itself is a token, and falling off at 6 pm is expiry. Mum's note is exactly what OAuth 2.0 does: it lets one app act for you with limited, time-boxed permission, without ever handing over your password. One gate that works for every ride is single sign-on.

From your world

You have deployed all of this on switch ports. Authentication is 802.1X: the port stays closed until RADIUS confirms who the device is. Authorization is the ACL or VLAN that RADIUS pushes back for that device: what it may reach after it is let in. A JWT is like a signed certificate the switch can check offline with the CA's public key, without calling RADIUS on every frame. Where this breaks: a switch checks once per port session, while a web service must re-check authorization on every request and for every object ("may user 42 read order 77?"), because one connection carries many users' requests.
