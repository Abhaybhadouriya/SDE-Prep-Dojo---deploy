Back-of-envelope estimation

A. UPI-scale traffic (real numbers)

  1. Monthly volume. UPI processed a record 21.63 billion transactions in December 2025, worth about Rs 27.97 lakh crore (Business Standard, from NPCI data).
  2. Average TPS. 21.63 billion / 31 days = ~698 million a day. Divided by 86,400 seconds, that is ~8,100 TPS on average.
  3. Peak day. The record day was 754 million transactions on 18 October 2025 (Business Today). That alone averages ~8,700 TPS over 24 hours.
  4. Peak second (our assumption). Traffic is not flat: evenings and festival sales spike. Assume the busiest minute runs at 3x the daily average, so ~26,000 TPS. Design for 30,000 TPS with headroom. This number is our assumption, not a published figure.
  5. Ledger writes. A P2P transfer is at least 2 postings, and a merchant payment with a fee is 3-4. At 30,000 TPS x 3 postings, that is ~90,000 posting rows per second.
  6. Storage. Assume ~200 bytes per posting with indexes. 754M x 3 x 200 B = ~450 GB a day, or ~165 TB a year before replication. Ledgers are append-only and kept for years (regulators), so plan tiered storage. Uber offloads old ledgers to cold storage in time-range batches.

B. ByteByteGo's 1M TPS wallet (node maths)

  1. Per-node capacity. Assume one relational node handles ~1,000 TPS.
  2. Operations per transfer. A transfer is a debit + a credit = 2 operations. So 1M transfers/s = 2M operations/s.
  3. Nodes. 2,000,000 / 1,000 = 2,000 database nodes. At 10,000 TPS per node it is 200 nodes, and at 100 TPS it is 20,000. The design goal is therefore to raise per-node throughput, which is what pushes ByteByteGo towards a local, append-only event log.
