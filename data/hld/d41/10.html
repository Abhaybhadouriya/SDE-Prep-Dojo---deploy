How real companies do it

  • Stripe: Ledger (blog). An immutable log of events. It models every system's work as money moving between accounts, and uses double-entry "clearing" to detect missing or wrong events. It handles 5 billion events a day, and 99.99% of dollar volume is verified within four days.
  • Square: Books (blog). A double-entry accounting database with three tables (books, journal_entries, book_entries) and a version counter per book. It is insert-only except for the cached balance. It is reported (per StackShare, not Square's own post) to run on Cloud Spanner and GKE. Payout = the pending book's balance.
  • Uber: LedgerStore (migration, indexes). An append-only, verifiably immutable ledger store. Uber moved more than a trillion entries off DynamoDB: 1.2 PB of compressed records and 0.5 PB of indexes. Strongly consistent indexes use 2PC intents, so a card hold is visible immediately and no duplicate charge happens. Other indexes are eventually consistent.
  • Airbnb: Orpheus (blog). An idempotency framework for payments: leases, primary-only reads, and retryable vs non-retryable errors.
  • NPCI UPI (flow explainer, volume). A national switch between banks. 21.63 billion transactions in December 2025.
