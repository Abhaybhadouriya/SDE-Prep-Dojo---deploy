Requirements

Functional
  • Top up the wallet from a bank or card, and withdraw to a bank. Card and bank calls go through a PSP (payment service provider, such as Razorpay or Stripe): a company that talks to card networks and banks for us.
  • Transfer balance from wallet A to wallet B (P2P) and pay merchants.
  • Show the balance and transaction history.
  • Refunds and reversals.
  • Optional, for UPI: pay by VPA (virtual payment address such as name@bank) through the NPCI switch. NPCI (National Payments Corporation of India) operates UPI, the central switch that routes each payment between banks. Collect requests and mandates.
Non-functional
  • Correctness above everything. No money is created or lost. Balances never go negative unless allowed.
  • Exactly-once effects under retries (idempotency).
  • Auditability and reproducibility. You can rebuild any balance at any past time. ByteByteGo puts this in its requirements.
  • Availability of 99.99% (ByteByteGo's target). This is an SLO (service level objective: the target we alert on), which allows about 52 minutes of downtime a year.
  • Throughput: ByteByteGo designs for 1 million transfers per second. UPI-scale numbers follow below.
  • Latency: a P2P transfer should finish in about a second or two, measured at p99 (p99 = 99% of requests are faster than this; the slowest 1% are what users complain about). Like tail latency through a switch buffer, the tail matters more than the average.

Open with the invariant: "For every journal entry, the debits equal the credits, and the ledger is append-only. Everything else (balances, statements, reports) is derived from it." Interviewers at fintech-heavy companies listen for exactly this.
