Requirements

Always spend the first 5 minutes here. Say the requirements out loud and write them on the board. This is where you show the interviewer you think about users first, not internals.

Functional (what it does)

  • Submit a job: run once at a time ("at 2026-12-01 09:00 IST"), after a delay ("in 10 minutes"), or on a recurring schedule (a cron expression such as 0 2 * * * = every day at 02:00).
  • Run the job on a worker: call an HTTP endpoint, put a message on a queue, or run a container image.
  • Retry failed runs with a back-off policy, then move them to a dead-letter queue (DLQ), a parking place for jobs that keep failing so a human can look.
  • Query and control: see status and history of each run; pause, resume, cancel, or "run now".
  • Priorities and tenants: a payment retry must not wait behind a marketing email batch from another team.
  • Out of scope unless asked: job dependencies (DAGs, "run B after A") - mention Airflow / Temporal as the extension.

Non-functional (how well it does it)

Scale100 million job runs per day (our assumption)Big multi-tenant company platform
Timeliness95% of runs start within 5 s of schedule; 99% within 30 sDropbox's ATF publishes a 95%-within-5-s target, a realistic number
DurabilityAn accepted job is never lostA lost "charge subscription" job is lost money
DeliveryAt-least-once; no two workers run the same run at the same timeExactly-once is not achievable for side effects; we make jobs idempotent instead
Availability99.9%+ for submit and for executionScheduler down = every team's crons silently stop
IsolationOne noisy tenant cannot starve othersShared platforms die from noisy neighbours

Say this sentence early: "I will give at-least-once execution with leases so two workers never run the same job concurrently, and I will ask job owners to make jobs idempotent. True exactly-once for side effects is not possible, so I won't promise it." This one line separates SDE-2 answers from SDE-1 answers.

You might confuse this with: a message queue (SQS, Kafka). A queue delivers work now. A scheduler holds work until later, and handles recurring schedules. Most schedulers are "a durable timer in front of a queue".
