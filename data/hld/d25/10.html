How real companies do it

  • Dropbox: ATF (Async Task Framework). A frontend writes tasks to their metadata store (Edgestore). A "store consumer" polls about every 2 s for due tasks and pushes them to SQS queues, one per (task type, priority). Workers claim a task explicitly before running it, send heartbeats, and an executor kills itself after three missed heartbeats. Built for 10,000 tasks/s, running about 9,000/s, with 95% of tasks starting within 5 s. Delivery is at-least-once with exponential back-off.
  • Slack: job queue. Over 1.4 billion jobs on busy days, peak about 33,000 jobs/s. After Redis hit its memory limit and dropped work, Slack put Kafka in front of Redis: a Go service (Kafkagate) writes jobs to Kafka, and JQRelay moves them to Redis, committing the Kafka offset only after the Redis write succeeds.
  • Netflix: Maestro. Netflix's workflow orchestrator for large-scale data and ML pipelines (a DAG layer above scheduling).
  • Airbnb: Airflow. Born at Airbnb, now Apache Airflow: scheduler, executor, metadata DB and DAGs written in Python.
  • Kubernetes CronJob. The tiny built-in version of everything on this page; read its docs for concurrency and missed-schedule semantics.

Related designs: notification system (scheduled sends), design a message queue, calendar and reminders, distributed lock service (leases and fencing), CI/CD platform (build jobs on K8s), rate limiter (tenant quotas), consensus and coordination, K8s scheduling and autoscaling.
