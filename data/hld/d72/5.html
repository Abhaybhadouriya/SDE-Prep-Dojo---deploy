Architecture: from "Bid" to every screen

Click Next or Play. Two halves: the read side (browse, search, live view) and the write side (bid, close, settle).

  1. List. A seller creates an auction through the Auction service, which validates the role, times and prices and writes the auction row (status SCHEDULED, then LIVE at starts_at) on the shard chosen by auction_id. Photos go to object storage via presigned URLs.
  2. Make it findable. CDC (Debezium) or an outbox publishes auction changes to Kafka keyed by auction_id; an indexer updates Elasticsearch (title, category, current price, ends_at). Search is eventually consistent: a price a second stale in results is fine; the item page reads the database or cache.
  3. Bid. The Bid service checks the user's role and the idempotency key, then runs one atomic conditional update on the auction row (see deep dive 1): accepted only if the auction is LIVE, now() < ends_at, and the amount beats the current price plus increment. In the same transaction it appends the bid row. The answer goes straight back to the bidder.
  4. Fan out the new price. The accepted bid becomes an event (outbox to Kafka). A small publisher forwards it to a Redis channel auction:{id}; SSE or WebSocket gateway pods subscribed to that channel push {version, price, endsAt} to every watcher. Versions let clients ignore stale or out-of-order messages.
  5. Notify. The same event triggers an "you have been outbid" push to the previous leader and updates watch lists.
  6. Close on time. The closer finds auctions whose ends_at has passed and closes each with a conditional update LIVE -> CLOSED. Bids are already rejected after ends_at by the bid check itself, so the closer's timing affects only how fast we announce, never who wins.
  7. Settle. Settlement checks the reserve, creates an order and a payment request for the winner with a deadline, notifies winner and seller, and on non-payment offers the item to the next-highest bidder (second chance).

The hot path, running: many bids queue up for one auction, one at a time wins the row, and every accepted bid is broadcast.
