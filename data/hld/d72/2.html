Back-of-envelope estimation

Method from Back-of-Envelope Estimation. The concurrent-auction figure is Hello Interview's; the rest are our assumptions.

10Mlive auctions
~1.7K/sbids (average)
~1K/sbids on one hot auction
~200K/sitem-page reads
  1. Auctions 10M live at any time; average length 7 days. New auctions: 10M / 7 days = about 1.4M a day = about 17 per second. Writes for listings are tiny.
  2. Bids Assume an average of 10 bids per auction. 10M x 10 / 7 days = 100M bids / 604,800 s = about 170 per second. Bids bunch near the end, and popular days peak, so plan for 10x: about 1.7K per second across the system. Any sharded database handles that.
  3. The real peak is one row A celebrity item can get 1,000 bids in its final minute, most in the final 10 seconds: up to about 1K bids per second on one auction. That is the contention problem; total throughput is not.
  4. Reads Assume 100M daily users x 50 item and search views = 5B a day = about 58K per second, peak about 200K per second. Serve from cache and search indexes.
  5. Watchers Assume 1% of live auctions have someone watching live, about 100K auctions, with up to 100K viewers on a hot one. Pushing every bid naively at the hot-auction rate is 1K bids/s x 100K viewers = 100M pushes per second for that one auction, which no gateway fleet should do. The fix is conflation: push only the latest price, at most every 200-500 ms per auction. Then the cost is 2-5 frames/s x 100K viewers = 200K-500K pushes per second, whatever the bid rate. Fan-out, not bid processing, is the scaling problem on the read side. The same trick drives live prices in the Stock Broker App (section 7.1).
  6. Storage A bid row is about 100 bytes: 100M bids a week x 100 B = 10 GB a week, 520 GB a year. Item listings with text about 2 KB each (photos in object storage): 1.4M a day x 2 KB = 2.8 GB a day.

What the numbers tell you: normal sharding solves the total load. The design is about one hot auction: serialising up to about a thousand bids a second on one item without losing any, and pushing each change to up to 100K screens.
