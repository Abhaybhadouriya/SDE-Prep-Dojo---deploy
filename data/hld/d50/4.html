Why hexagons, and why H3 specifically

Uber's H3 post explains the choice: postal codes and hand-drawn zones have odd shapes and change for unrelated reasons; square grids have two kinds of neighbours (edge and corner); hexagons "minimize the quantization error introduced when users move through a city". H3 projects the Earth onto an icosahedron with 122 base cells and only 12 pentagons, all placed in the ocean. A cell id is a 64-bit integer, so it makes a perfect Kafka key and Flink key.

From your world

An H3 cell id is like an IP prefix: a coarser resolution is a shorter prefix that covers all its children. Rolling up from res 9 to res 7 is route summarisation. Looking up "which cell am I in" is O(1) arithmetic, like a hash, not a tree walk.
