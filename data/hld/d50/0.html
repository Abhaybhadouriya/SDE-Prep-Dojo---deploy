The story

Explain like I'm 5

It is raining and school just ended. Fifty kids want a ride home, but only ten parents are waiting at the gate. A smart teacher draws the playground as a honeycomb of little hexagon tiles. In each tile she counts kids who want a ride and parents who are free. Where there are many kids and few parents, she rings a bell that says "rides here cost a bit more right now". Two things happen: some kids decide to wait for the rain to stop, and parents from the quiet tiles walk over because they earn more. Soon the numbers balance and the bell goes quiet. She also has a magic watch that tells each kid, "your parent will reach you in 4 minutes". To guess well, the watch looks at the map of paths, and then remembers how long it really took on rainy days before.

That is the whole system. The honeycomb is H3, Uber's open-source hexagon grid. The counting is a streaming job that reads every ride request and every driver's location from Kafka and aggregates per hexagon per time window, which Uber's SIGMOD 2021 paper says runs in Flink. The bell is the surge multiplier. The magic watch is the ETA service (estimated time of arrival): a routing engine on a road graph, plus a machine learning model (Uber's DeepETA) that corrects the routing engine's guess.

The surprising lesson: this system prefers freshness over correctness. Uber's paper says the surge pipeline "favors data freshness and availability over data consistency": late events are simply dropped. A price that is 99% accurate now beats a perfect price five minutes late.

New words, once each: QPS is requests per second. p99 means "99 out of 100 are faster than this". H3 is Uber's hexagon grid that gives every spot on Earth a cell id. Flink is a stream-processing engine: it reads events from Kafka and keeps running counts. A tumbling window is a fixed, non-overlapping time bucket (here 60 s). A watermark is the engine saying "I believe all events up to time T have arrived", so a bucket can be closed. Event time is when something happened on the phone, not when it reached the server. A residual is the error left after a first estimate (real time minus predicted time). All of these are in the glossary, and windows and watermarks get a picture in the architecture section.
