The story

Explain like I'm 5

Imagine the biggest toy shop in the world. It has so many toys that nobody can walk the aisles. So the shop keeps a giant card box: one card per toy, with its name, colour and price. Next to it is a magic finder book: look up the word "red" and it tells you which cards talk about red toys, so you do not read every card. When a toy sells out, a helper runs over and puts a sticker on the card that says "sold out", so you are not sad later. And on each card, other kids put gold stars to say if they liked the toy. Lots of kids read the stars; only a few write them.

That is the whole design. The card box is the catalog: the source of truth for every product. The finder book is the search index, an inverted index (a map from each word to the list of products that contain it). The helper with stickers is the change propagation pipeline that keeps the index fresh when price or stock changes. The stars are the ratings and reviews system, a classic read-heavy system.

Three things make it hard: every category has different attributes (a phone has RAM, a shirt has a size), the data changes all the time (Flipkart reports pricing, availability and offer updates peaking at about 10,000 per second per signal), and reads massively outnumber writes (Flipkart's reviews team reports around 100 writes for every 100,000 reads).

New words, once each: SKU (stock-keeping unit) is one sellable variant, such as "this shirt, size M, blue". QPS is queries per second. p99 means "99 out of 100 are faster than this". Elasticsearch and Solr are the two popular open-source search engines, both built on the Lucene library. A shard (see sharding) is one slice of the index on one machine. CDC (change data capture) streams every database change out as an event. A facet is a filter with counts, like "Brand: Nike (120)". CQRS means writing to one model and reading from another, precomputed one. More in the glossary.
