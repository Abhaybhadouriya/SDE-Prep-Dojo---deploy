Flink: stateful stream processing

Apache Flink runs a job as a graph of operators: sources (often Kafka), transformations, and sinks (a database, Kafka, files). The concepts that matter in interviews:

  • Keyed state: after keyBy(userId), each operator instance owns a key-value store for its keys (in memory or in RocksDB on local disk). Keys are grouped into key groups, the unit Flink moves when you change parallelism.
  • Checkpoints: Flink takes consistent snapshots of all operator state, using a method inspired by the Chandy-Lamport algorithm. Sources inject barriers into the stream. Barriers never overtake records. When an operator has seen the barrier on all its inputs, it snapshots its state (to S3/HDFS) and passes the barrier on. On failure, the job restarts from the last checkpoint and rewinds the Kafka offsets stored in it.
  • Exactly-once: aligned barriers give exactly-once state. For exactly-once output, the sink must take part too: Kafka transactions (two-phase commit, readers use read_committed) or idempotent upserts keyed by a deterministic ID.
  • Savepoints: manual checkpoints you keep for upgrades and migrations.

Real scale: Uber. Uber's 2021 paper "Real-time Data Infrastructure at Uber" reports trillions of Kafka messages and petabytes of data per day (as of October 2020), with Flink the de facto stream processor running several thousand jobs. Surge pricing is a Flink job that computes price multipliers per hexagon area over time windows from Kafka trip and driver events. It deliberately favours freshness and availability over consistency: late messages are simply not counted, and the pipeline runs active-active across regions. See Surge Pricing (d50).
