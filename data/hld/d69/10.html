How the real companies do it

Seen in 2 recent reports. Every variant, with company, level, date and source post, is in Recently Asked HLD Questions #56. Company view: the Microsoft guide.

Related: Device Settings Sync & Backup (d65) uses the same ideas - versioned documents, watch or long-poll, last-known-good - but for one user's handful of devices, with conflicts between devices and backups. This page pushes one config to a whole service fleet, so the hard parts are staged rollout and fan-out to hundreds of thousands of servers.

  • Facebook Configerator: Holistic Configuration Management at Facebook (SOSP 2015; linked here through The Morning Paper's summary, which loads reliably) describes configs as code in Git, compilers and validators, canary testing, and distribution to hundreds of thousands of servers, with thousands of changes a day.
  • Facebook LAD: Location-Aware Distribution replaced ZooKeeper-based distribution, supporting updates up to 100 MB (from 5 MB) and about 40,000 subscribers per distributor (from about 2,500).
  • AWS AppConfig: deployment strategies (growth, interval, bake time) and automatic rollback on CloudWatch alarms.
  • etcd and ZooKeeper: etcd API: revisions and watches; ZooKeeper programmer's guide (watches, znode limits).
  • Kubernetes: ConfigMaps, including how mounted ConfigMaps are updated eventually and environment variables are not.

Related on this site: Distributed Lock Service (consensus stores, leases), Key-Value Store, Distributed Cache (LRU, eviction), CI/CD Platform (staged rollouts), Notification System (fan-out), Raft, Pattern: Real-time Updates (long-poll, SSE, WebSocket).
