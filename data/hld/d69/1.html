Requirements

Functional
  • Create, update and delete configs, organised by namespace (service or team), environment (dev, staging, prod) and optionally region.
  • Every change creates an immutable version; view history and diffs; roll back to any version.
  • Validate before publish: JSON schema, type checks, custom validators ("timeout between 50 and 5,000 ms").
  • Deliver changes to subscribed service instances within seconds (push, watch or long-poll).
  • Staged rollout: canary %, waves by region, bake time, automatic rollback on alarms.
  • Feature flags with targeting rules (percent of users, tenant, country) evaluated in the client.
  • Access control per namespace, approvals for production, full audit log (who, what, when, why).
Non-functional
  • Reads must never become an outage: if the service is down, every client keeps running on its cached config. The config service is not on the request path.
  • Propagation: p99 under ~10 s from publish to all subscribed instances (for a full rollout step).
  • Consistency: strong at write (no lost updates, a single ordered version history per config); eventual at read (instances may briefly run different versions, which must be safe).
  • Scale: about a million client instances, thousands of changes a day.
  • Availability: higher than any service that depends on it, across zones and regions.
  • Secrets are encrypted and handled by a secrets manager, not as plain config.

Say this early: "The config service must never be in the request path. Services read config from local memory; the service only delivers changes. So its outage means 'no new changes', not 'everything is down'. And its most important feature is safe rollout, because a bad config spread instantly is the fastest way to break every service at once."
