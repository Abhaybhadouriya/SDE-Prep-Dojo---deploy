Back-of-envelope estimation

Assumptions for a large company. Real anchor: Facebook's Configerator paper (SOSP 2015) reports thousands of config changes a day distributed to hundreds of thousands of servers, and Facebook later wrote that its distribution layer (LAD) scaled to about 40,000 subscribers per distributor, up from about 2,500 with its earlier ZooKeeper-based design.

  1. Clients. 5,000 services x 200 instances on average = 1 million client instances, each subscribing to about 5 configs (its own, shared libraries, flags).
  2. Config data. 100,000 configs x 10 KB average = 1 GB for the current versions. Keep 100 versions each (most configs change rarely, so the real number is much smaller): at most 100 GB. Conclusion: storage is trivial; this is a delivery and safety problem.
  3. Writes. Assume 10,000 changes a day: about 0.1 per second, with bursts of tens per second during business hours. Conclusion: a single consensus group or one SQL primary is plenty for the write path.
  4. Steady-state connections. With long-poll at a 60 s timeout, 1M clients make about 1M / 60 = 17,000 requests per second even when nothing changes. Each distributor node holding 20,000-40,000 open connections (order of magnitude from the LAD post) means about 25-50 distributor nodes. Conclusion: clients must never talk to the store directly; a stateless fan-out tier in front absorbs them.
  5. A popular change. A shared config used by all 1M instances, 10 KB: 1M x 10 KB = 10 GB of transfer if everyone downloads it at once. Spread over a staged rollout (5%, 25%, 100% over 30 minutes) and served from distributor caches, that is a few GB per minute across 50 nodes: fine. Conclusion: ship small configs (or deltas), cache at distributors, and add jitter so 1M clients do not reconnect in the same second.
  6. Thundering herd after an outage. If the distributors restart, 1M clients reconnect: with random backoff over 60 s, about 17,000 reconnects per second, same as steady state. Without jitter, 1M in one second. Conclusion: jittered reconnects are a requirement, not a nice-to-have.
