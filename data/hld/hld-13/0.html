The story: two lemonade stands

Explain like I'm 5

Mia sells lemonade. She tells her friends: "My stand will be open almost every day." One day she gets sick and the stand is closed. Her friends are sad. So Mia makes a plan. Her brother runs a second stand on the next street. Every evening they write down how many cups they owe people in two notebooks, one for each stand. If one stand closes, friends walk to the other one, and the notebook there already knows who is owed a cup. Mia also counts how many days she was closed this month. If she was closed too often, she stops trying new recipes and fixes her stand first.

That small story holds the whole page. "Open almost every day" is an availability target. Counting closed days is a service level indicator. "If closed too often, stop new recipes" is an error budget policy. The second stand is redundancy, walking to it is failover, and the two notebooks are replication. How many cups you could forget when one stand burns down is the RPO. How long friends wait before the other stand serves them is the RTO.

From your world

You already build for this in networking gear. A redundant pair of supervisor cards with stateful switchover is active-passive failover; the state sync between them is replication; the frames lost during the switchover are your RPO, and the convergence time is your RTO. A carrier "five nines" requirement on a chassis is an availability target. Where this breaks: inside a chassis the two cards share a backplane and a clock, so the standby knows almost exactly what the active did; two cloud regions share nothing and talk over a slow WAN, so the standby is always a little behind.
