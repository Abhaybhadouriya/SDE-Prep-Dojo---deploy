Availability and the "nines"

Availability is the share of time a system does its job: uptime divided by (uptime + downtime). People count it in nines. 99% is "two nines", 99.9% is "three nines", and so on. Each extra nine cuts the allowed downtime by ten times, and usually costs a lot more money and effort.

Real anchors: Netflix stated an internal availability goal of 99.99% when it moved to multi-region active-active; Slack's 99.99% SLA allows under one hour of unavailability per year; Google's SRE book cites a published 99.95% target for Compute Engine.

Series and parallel: the two rules of availability math

A request usually passes through many parts: load balancer, app server, cache, database. If all must work, they are in series and you multiply: two parts at 99.9% give 0.999 x 0.999 = 99.8%. If any one of several copies is enough, they are in parallel: the system fails only when all fail, so availability = 1 - (1 - A)n. Two copies at 99.9% give 1 - 0.001 x 0.001 = 99.9999%.

Work one out loud: a page load that calls 5 internal services in series, each at 99.9%, lands at 0.9995 = about 99.5%. That is roughly 3.6 hours of downtime a month even though every team "hit three nines". This is why big designs add redundancy inside each tier and avoid long synchronous call chains.

From your world

You already know this from routers. Two line cards in a chassis with VRRP or HSRP between them are the "parallel" case: the virtual IP moves to the standby when hellos stop. A single upstream fibre is the "series" case. And the catch is the same: two "redundant" links in the same conduit fail together when a backhoe hits it. Independent failure domains matter more than the count of copies.

Words you will use

  • Single point of failure (SPOF): one part whose failure takes the whole system down.
  • Reliability vs availability: a reliable system does the right thing; an available one answers. A system can be "up" while returning wrong data, so a good SLI checks correctness too.
  • High availability vs fault tolerance: high availability means a short interruption during failover; fault tolerance means no interruption at all, and costs full duplicate hardware.
  • Failure domain: the set of things that break together: a host, a rack, an availability zone (AZ, one or more data centers with their own power and network), a region (a group of AZs in one geography).
