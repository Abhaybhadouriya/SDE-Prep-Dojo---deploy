Quick quiz

A request passes through three components in series, each 99.9% available. Roughly what is the end-to-end availability?

  1. 99.9%
  2. 99.7%
  3. 99.99%
  4. 97%
Series multiplies: 0.999^3 is about 0.997, so 99.7%.

A 99.9% SLO over 30 days gives an error budget of about...

  1. 4.3 minutes
  2. 8.8 hours
  3. 3.6 days
  4. 43.2 minutes
0.1% of 43,200 minutes is 43.2 minutes.

Which statement is true?

  1. An SLI is the target, an SLO is the measurement
  2. An SLA has an explicit consequence, such as credits, when missed
  3. The public SLA should be tighter than the internal SLO
SLI is the measurement, SLO the target, SLA the contract with consequences. Internal SLOs are kept tighter than public SLAs as a safety margin.

Your seat-booking service must never sell one seat twice across two regions. Which write strategy fits?

  1. Write local with last-writer-wins
  2. Write local with no conflict handling
  3. Write global (or one home region per show), read local
  4. Backup and restore only
An invariant needs a single authority. LWW silently drops one of two concurrent bookings.

What separates pilot light from warm standby?

  1. Pilot light keeps no data in the DR region
  2. Warm standby can serve traffic immediately at reduced capacity; pilot light must switch servers on first
  3. They are the same thing
Both replicate data. Only warm standby has the app running and able to serve right away.

Why are three in-region replicas not enough to protect against a bad migration that deletes a column?

  1. Replicas are too slow
  2. The bad change replicates to every replica; you need point-in-time backups
  3. Replicas only store indexes
Replication protects against hardware loss, not logical corruption.

With 8 workers and each customer assigned a random pair (shuffle sharding), one poison customer crashes its 2 workers. About what share of customers is fully affected (all their workers down)?

  1. 25%
  2. 50%
  3. All of it
  4. About 1/28, roughly 3.6%
C(8,2) = 28 distinct pairs, so only customers with the exact same pair (about 1/28) lose everything. Capacity loss is still 2 of 8 workers (25%); the win is that almost every other customer keeps one healthy worker, versus 25% of customers fully down with four fixed shards of two.

Which of these is one of the SRE "four golden signals"?

  1. Number of microservices
  2. Saturation
  3. Lines of code deployed
The four are latency, traffic, errors and saturation.
