Remember this

  • Each nine is 10x less downtime: 99.9% is about 43.8 min a month, 99.99% about 4.3 min.
  • Series components multiply availability down; parallel copies push it up only if they fail independently.
  • SLI = measurement, SLO = target, SLA = contract with consequences. Error budget = 1 - SLO.
  • Page on SLO burn rate (14.4x over 1 h and 5 min), not on CPU.
  • Active-passive = one writer, simpler, standby can rot. Active-active = faster recovery, needs conflict handling and spare capacity.
  • Multi-region writes: write global (invariants), write local (LWW/CRDT), write partitioned (home region).
  • RPO = data you lose, RTO = time you are down. Pick a DR tier per component: backup/restore, pilot light, warm standby, active-active.
  • Replication is not a backup: keep isolated, point-in-time, tested backups.
  • Cells and shuffle sharding limit blast radius from bad inputs and bad deploys. Shuffle sharding does not save capacity; it shrinks how many customers are fully down (about 1/28 with 8 workers in pairs).
  • Observe with metrics, logs and traces tied by a trace id; prove resilience with chaos experiments and game days.
