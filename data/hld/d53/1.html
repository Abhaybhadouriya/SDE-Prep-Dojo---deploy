Requirements

A few names first. A watch (also called a subscription or tracking) is one user's rule for one product, for example "tell me when this drops below Rs 2,999" or "tell me when it falls 20%". A price observation is one reading of a price at one moment. A crossing is the moment a price moves from above a user's target to at or below it. Politeness means fetching a website slowly enough that you never hurt it, and following its published rules.

Functional

  1. Price history A user (on the website or in the browser extension) opens a product and sees a chart of its price over 1 month, 1 year or all time, plus lowest, highest and average.
  2. Create a watch A user sets a target price or a percentage drop for a product. Hello Interview's version of the problem lists exactly these two features: view history, and subscribe to price-drop alerts with a threshold.
  3. Get notified When the price crosses the target, the user gets one email or push notification with the new price and a link.
  4. Manage watches List, edit, pause and delete watches.
  5. Stock variant (Uber shape) Users set "above" or "below" alerts, or "moved 5% today" alerts, on stock tickers fed by an exchange data feed.

Out of scope (say so): product search and discovery, comparing prices across many shops, reviews, and buying on the user's behalf. Search is covered by Product Catalog & Search.

Ask the interviewer one question that changes everything: "Do we get prices pushed to us (a feed), or do we have to go and fetch them (polling)?" Stocks: the exchange pushes a trade feed. Shop products: we usually poll, maybe helped by the browser extension. The rest of the design follows from that answer.
