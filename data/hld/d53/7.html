Where Kafka, Kubernetes and Docker fit

ToolRole hereConcrete setup
KafkaThe spine: observations, changes, alertsTopics price-observations (key product_id, 7-day retention for replay), price-changed (key product_id), alerts (key watch_id or user_id). A compacted topic product-current-price keyed by product doubles as a rebuildable cache. Replaying price-changed lets you re-run matching after a matcher bug.
KubernetesRuns the stateless fleets and scales themFetch workers, validator, matcher and API as Deployments. Scale fetch workers and matchers on Kafka consumer lag with KEDA rather than on CPU (see Scheduling & Autoscaling). A CronJob runs the nightly "percentage below 90-day average" batch and tier recalculation.
DockerPackages and isolates the fetchersIf a shop only offers a page that needs JavaScript, a headless-browser worker runs in its own container with tight CPU and memory limits (cgroups) and no access to internal networks. A parser crash or memory leak kills one container, not the pipeline. See Docker in production.

From your world

Consumer lag is queue depth. Scaling fetchers on lag is like adding worker threads when a ring buffer fills, and the per-shop token bucket in front of them is the shaper that stops the new threads from turning into a flood toward one destination.
