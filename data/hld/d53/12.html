Quiz

A product's price drops from 3,200 to 2,800. Which armed watches should fire?

  1. Every watch with target >= 2,800, armed or not
  2. Armed watches with target >= 2,800, disarmed in the same transaction
  3. Watches with target <= 2,800
The armed flag stops repeats, so the query does not need the old price (which may be wrong after a lost or reordered event). Option A re-alerts people already told; option C is the wrong direction.

Why are watches sharded by product id?

  1. Products have fewer rows than users
  2. It makes "list my watches" fast
  3. The hot query is "which watches care about this product's change", and it stays on one shard
Shard by the key of the hottest query. The user view is served by a secondary index.

Refreshing 500M products every hour would need about how many fetches a day?

  1. 12 billion
  2. 500 million
  3. 230 million
500M x 24 = 12B. That arithmetic is why we tier by interest (about 230M a day).

The extension reports a price of 1 for a 30,000 rupee TV. What should happen first?

  1. Alert all watchers immediately; the extension saw it
  2. Treat it as an untrusted hint: sanity-check it, and confirm with our own fetch or more reports before any alert
  3. Ban the user
Reports are hints. A validation gate protects users from both bad actors and bugs.

A price wobbles just above and below a user's target. What prevents ten alerts?

  1. A bigger Kafka cluster
  2. Caching the price in Redis
  3. Disarm after firing and re-arm only after the price rises a margin above target (hysteresis)
Hysteresis plus a per-watch rate limit keeps it to one alert per real crossing.

For stock-ticker alerts with thousands of symbols updating many times a second, where do the thresholds live during matching?

  1. In memory, per symbol, in sorted "above" and "below" structures on matcher processes sharded by symbol
  2. In a SQL table queried on every tick
  3. In the user's phone
The tick rate makes a database call per tick too slow. Persist alerts in a DB, but match in memory.

How do you keep a 2-year chart under 500 ms?

  1. Read every raw price point and draw them all
  2. Read daily rollups maintained in the background, and cache the result per product and range
  3. Recompute the chart in a nightly batch only
Downsample with continuous aggregates, then cache. About 730 rows instead of thousands.
