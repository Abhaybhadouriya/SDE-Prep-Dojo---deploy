How real systems do it

  • camelcamelcamel is the reference product: Amazon price history, threshold alerts and a Chrome extension. Hello Interview's breakdown frames it with 500M products, about 1M extension users, charts under 500 ms and alerts within an hour, and names extension trust and efficient change processing as the hard parts.
  • trivago aggregates about 70 billion hotel prices over a week to show "price context" (is this a good price?). Prices arrive on Kafka; they removed about 88% duplicates, rejected outliers after a log10 transform, computed percentiles in BigQuery, and show the 5th to 95th percentile as the expected range. That is the "is now a good time to buy" feature a price tracker grows into.
  • Stock alert systems (the Uber shape): an open design write-up sizes 3M users over about 5,250 stocks, stores a week of prices in a time-series DB, emits PRICE_CHANGE events when a move crosses 5% or 10%, and throttles because a stock can cross the same line many times a day.
  • Exchange feeds: Hello Interview's Robinhood breakdown describes the exchange trade feed as subscribe-by-symbol with a push per trade, which is why the stock variant is push-first and in-memory.
