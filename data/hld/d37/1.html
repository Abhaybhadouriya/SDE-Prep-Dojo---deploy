Requirements

Always say the scope out loud first. A good version for 45 minutes:

Functional

  • View the map: pan and zoom anywhere in the world.
  • Search a place / address and turn it into a point (geocoding).
  • Get directions from A to B for driving (walking/transit are follow-ups), with a route line and an ETA.
  • Turn-by-turn navigation with live traffic, and rerouting when a faster route appears or the driver leaves the route.

Out of scope (say so): Street View, reviews, ride hailing, multi-stop trip planning, offline maps (mention as follow-up).

Non-functional

  • Low latency: map tiles feel instant (target < 100 ms from a CDN); a route in < 1 s end to end, with the core graph search in milliseconds.
  • Accuracy: ETA close to reality (Google reports its ETAs were already accurate for over 97% of trips before 2020 work with DeepMind).
  • Freshness: traffic reflected within about a minute; road changes (a new road, a closure) within hours to a day.
  • High availability for reads (maps, routes). Slightly stale traffic is fine: availability over strict consistency.
  • Scale: billions of users, hundreds of thousands of GPS updates per second.
  • Privacy: location data is sensitive - aggregate it, strip identity, keep short retention.

Say early: "This system is read-heavy for maps and routes, but write-heavy for location pings. Those two paths scale very differently, so I will design them separately."
