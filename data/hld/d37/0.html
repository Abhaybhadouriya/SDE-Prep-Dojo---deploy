The story

Explain like I'm 5

Imagine a giant paper map of the whole world, so big it covers a football field. You cannot carry it. So we cut it into small square cards. When you look at your street, you only pick up the few cards for your street. When you zoom out, you pick up cards that show the whole city, with less detail on each card.

Now you want to go to Grandma's house. A wise helper has already walked every road and written down short cuts like "from the big bridge to the airport takes 20 minutes". So instead of trying every little lane, the helper jumps along those short cuts. And because thousands of cars are telling the helper "I am stuck here!", the helper knows which roads are slow right now and says: "You will arrive in 23 minutes."

That story already contains the three hard parts of this design:

  1. Drawing the map - cut the world into tiles (small square pictures or data files) at many zoom levels and serve them from a CDN (content delivery network, a cache close to the user).
  2. Finding the route - roads are a graph (junctions are nodes, road pieces are edges). Plain shortest-path search is too slow on a continent, so we precompute shortcuts.
  3. Knowing the traffic and the arrival time - phones send GPS points; we snap them to roads, turn them into speeds per road piece, and use history + live data + machine learning to predict the ETA (estimated time of arrival).

Google says Maps serves more than 2 billion users a month and gave about 1 trillion kilometres of directions in 2024 (Google, 2025). In the interview you will not build all of Google Maps. You will pick the core: tiles, routing, live traffic, ETA.
