How the real companies do it

  • Google: ETAs blend historical patterns for that time of week, live aggregate location data, authoritative data (speed limits, closures) and user reports; after 2020 it weighted the most recent 2-4 weeks more heavily (Google Maps 101). With DeepMind it added a graph neural network (GNN - an ML model that learns over the road graph itself, so a jam on one road informs its neighbours) over "Supersegments" (groups of adjacent road segments that share traffic), which cut inaccurate ETAs by up to 50% in several cities (DeepMind). Scale: 2B+ monthly users and about 100M map updates a day (20 years of Google Maps).
  • Microsoft / Bing Maps: Customizable Route Planning is the core of Bing Maps routing. It has three phases - slow metric-independent preprocessing, fast customization when weights change (about a second for Europe on 12 cores), and a millisecond query (CRP paper). The open-source OSRM engine offers both pipelines (CH and Multi-Level Dijkstra) if you want to try it.
  • Uber: the routing engine produces an ETA, and the DeepETA model predicts its residual (error) in a few milliseconds - Uber calls it its highest-QPS model (DeepETA). Uber also built H3 hexagons for geo aggregation. See Surge Pricing & ETA and Ride Hailing.
  • Lyft: map matching that also tracks cars when they leave the known map (an on/off-road filter), so the system notices missing or wrong roads (Lyft paper on arXiv).
