  1. 9
  2. 64
  3. 16
  4. 256
4z tiles: 43 = 64 (an 8 x 8 grid). 16 is zoom 2, 256 is zoom 4.

What does a contraction hierarchy add to the graph during preprocessing?

  1. A copy of the graph per region and per hour
  2. Extra junction nodes for every traffic light
  3. A grid of cells with live speeds per cell
  4. Shortcut edges that keep shortest distances
Contracting an unimportant node adds a shortcut between its neighbours when the path through it was the shortest one. Cells belong to CRP, not CH.

Why is CRP / Multi-Level Dijkstra preferred over plain CH when traffic changes every minute?

  1. Only cell-boundary shortcuts are redone, in seconds
  2. It needs no preprocessing step at all
  3. It stores one graph per traffic condition
  4. Its queries are always faster than CH queries
CRP keeps the slow topology step separate; customization for a new metric is fast (about 1 s on 12 cores for Europe in the paper). CH queries are a little faster, but re-contracting for new weights is slow.

What is "map matching"?

  1. Matching a rider to the nearest available driver
  2. Diffing two map versions to find changed tiles
  3. Deciding which roads a noisy GPS trace really used
  4. Snapping each point alone to its nearest road
Map matching snaps a sequence of noisy points to the most likely road path, commonly with an HMM and Viterbi. Snapping each point alone is the naive version that jumps between roads.

About how many location-batch requests per second do 2 million concurrent drivers produce if each sends a batch every 15 seconds?

  1. ~2 million
  2. ~30 million
  3. ~130-140 thousand
  4. ~15 thousand
2,000,000 / 15 is about 133,000 requests per second. Batching is what keeps it below 2 million.

In Uber's DeepETA design, what does the ML model predict?

  1. The whole route, replacing the routing engine
  2. The residual (error) of the routing engine's ETA
  3. Which map tiles the phone should prefetch next
  4. The speed of every road segment for the next hour
The routing engine produces an ETA; the model learns the correction, so it can be retrained without changing routing.

The traffic pipeline is down for 10 minutes. What should routing do?

  1. Queue route requests until traffic is fresh again
  2. Keep the last live speeds frozen until the feed comes back
  3. Return errors so users know the ETA may be wrong
  4. Keep routing with historical speeds for this time of week
Availability beats freshness: degrade ETA quality, never the ability to route. Frozen live speeds get worse every minute; history for this hour of the week is a better guess.
