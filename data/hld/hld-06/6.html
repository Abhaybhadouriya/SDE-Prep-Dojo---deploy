Webhooks: server-to-server push

A webhook is a reverse API call: when something happens (a payment succeeded, a repo got a push), the provider sends an HTTP POST to a URL you registered. It replaces "poll the provider every minute" with "the provider calls you". Payment gateways, GitHub, Slack and Shopify all use them.

GitHub's webhook best-practices page gives the rules in concrete form: respond with a 2xx within 10 seconds or the delivery counts as failed; put the work on a queue and process it in the background; verify the payload with your webhook secret; use the X-GitHub-Delivery ID (which stays the same on redelivery) to spot duplicates and replays; and redeliver missed events after an outage.

Use webhooks for server-to-server notification across companies. They do not work for browsers or phones (no public URL), which is what the next section is for. A payment flow in d10 typically uses the gateway's webhook plus a reconciliation job.
