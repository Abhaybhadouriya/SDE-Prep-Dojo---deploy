Requirements

Two flavours appear in interviews. Twitch (Zepto): many creators, each with a small-to-large audience. Live cricket (Oracle, JioHotstar): a few streams, gigantic synchronized audiences, paid access. Ask which one; the hard parts differ.

Functional

  • Broadcaster goes live with a stream key; stream shows on a channel page.
  • Viewers watch with adaptive quality on phone, web and TV.
  • Users subscribe to a match, tournament or channel; at play time they get a secure, expiring access link.
  • Show a live concurrent-viewer count; chat and reactions alongside (see Live Comments).
  • Record the stream for later playback (live-to-VOD); optional DVR (seek back a few minutes).

Non-functional

  • Latency (glass-to-glass): about 2-5 s for interactive streams; Twitch reports getting to about 3 s with low-latency HLS.
  • Scale: tens of millions concurrent for a final. The ICC reported a 59 million peak concurrent audience on Disney+ Hotstar for the 2023 World Cup final.
  • Spikes: audience can multiply within minutes at toss, match start or a big moment.
  • Availability: a live event cannot be re-run; aim for no single point of failure on the video path.
  • Smoothness over quality: low rebuffering beats a higher bitrate.
  • Security: only entitled users can play; links cannot be shared widely.

Say the split early: "There are two systems here - a video pipeline sized by streams, and a distribution + control plane sized by viewers. The CDN carries the video; my servers carry only small API calls." It frames every later answer.
