The story

Explain like I'm 5

Your friend is singing on a stage, and the whole town wants to watch at the same moment. She cannot sing into a million telephones. So she sings into one microphone. A helper cuts her song into tiny 2-second pieces, makes a big copy and a small copy of each piece (for big TVs and for small phones), and puts them on a shelf.

Every neighbourhood has a little shop. Each shop takes one copy of every piece from the shelf and hands it to all the families on its street. Families with a slow road get the small copy so it never stops; families with a fast road get the big one.

Only people with a ticket may watch the cricket match, and the ticket only works today. And there is a man counting heads at every shop and telling the town, "50 million people are watching!"

  1. Microphone = ingest. The broadcaster pushes one stream (RTMP, SRT or WebRTC) to the nearest ingest point.
  2. Cutting and copying = transcoding + packaging. In real time, the stream becomes an ABR ladder (1080p, 720p, 480p...) cut into short segments.
  3. Neighbourhood shops = CDN edges. Each edge fetches a segment once and serves it to thousands. See CDN.
  4. Big or small copy = adaptive bitrate. The player picks the rendition its network can sustain, every few seconds. See HLS / DASH.
  5. Ticket for today = signed, expiring playback link tied to a subscription.
  6. Counting heads = concurrent-viewer counter, a distributed counter problem (d39).

From your world

The CDN tree is multicast done in software at layer 7: one copy per branch, replicated at each hop, like PIM building a distribution tree - except edges pull segments over HTTP instead of routers forwarding packets. The player's buffer is a jitter buffer; ABR switching is like rate adaptation on a wireless link; the segment duration is your packetisation delay. Glass-to-glass latency is the sum of every buffer on the path, just like end-to-end delay in a router pipeline.

New words, once each:

  • CMAF (Common Media Application Format): one fragmented-MP4 segment format that both HLS and DASH players can read, so you package each stream once. Its small "chunks" or "parts" enable low latency (see HLS / DASH and LL-HLS).
  • PoP (point of presence): one CDN site, a rack of edge caches in a city, close to viewers (see CDN).
  • QoE (quality of experience): what the viewer feels - start-up time, rebuffering, bitrate, errors - measured by beacons from the player.
  • SRT (Secure Reliable Transport) and RTMP: protocols a broadcaster uses to push the live feed into ingest. SRT runs over UDP with retransmission, so it copes with lossy links. OBS is a popular free broadcasting app that sends RTMP or SRT.
  • Anycast: many servers announce the same IP address, and routing sends each viewer to the nearest one (see anycast).
