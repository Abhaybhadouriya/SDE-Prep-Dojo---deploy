Architecture: one frame's journey

Click Next or Play. Steps 1-4 are the video pipeline, 5-6 distribution and playback, 7-8 counting and recording.

  1. The broadcaster's encoder pushes RTMP or SRT to the nearest ingest PoP (anycast/DNS). The PoP terminates the connection and checks the stream key with one KV lookup.
  2. A routing service picks an origin data centre with spare transcoding capacity - the idea behind Twitch's Intelligest, which routes each stream using live views of origin compute and backbone capacity.
  3. The transcoder decodes once and encodes the ABR ladder in real time; the packager cuts aligned segments (and low-latency parts) and updates playlists for HLS and DASH.
  4. Segments land on the origin, fronted by an origin shield that collapses many edge misses for the same segment into one origin read.
  5. CDN edges serve viewers. Each edge fetches each new segment once and serves it to thousands; multi-CDN steering spreads 75 Tbps across providers.
  6. Before playing, the app calls the playback API: check subscription (cached), device limit and region, then issue a short-lived signed cookie/token. The edge validates the signature itself - no call to our servers per segment.
  7. Players send a heartbeat every 30 s; the counter aggregates per stream and region and publishes the number a few times a minute.
  8. The packager also writes segments to S3 for live-to-VOD; stream lifecycle and player quality (QoE) events go to Kafka for dashboards and alerting.
