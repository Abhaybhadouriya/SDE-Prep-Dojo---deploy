Back-of-envelope estimation

Our assumptions, said out loud. Two scenarios, because the bottlenecks differ.

A. Cricket final (few streams, huge audience)

  1. Viewers. Assume 50M concurrent at peak (the real 2023 record was 59M).
  2. Bitrate. Mostly mobile; assume an average of 1.5 Mbps across the ladder.
  3. Egress. 50M x 1.5 Mbps = 75 Tbps. No single CDN region does this alone: plan multi-CDN plus ISP-embedded caches.
  4. Segment requests. With 2 s segments each viewer fetches 1 segment every 2 s (plus playlist reloads): 50M / 2 = 25M requests/s at the edges. With low-latency parts (say 0.5 s) it is several times more - LL-HLS trades request rate for latency.
  5. Origin load. Edges collapse identical requests. Assume 2,000 edge caches x 6 renditions, one fetch per new segment every 2 s = 6K requests/s to the origin shield. That is the whole point of the CDN tree.
  6. Control plane. Playback-token requests: if 30M viewers join in the 10 minutes around the start, that is 50K/s to the entitlement/token API - the real backend spike.
  7. Viewer heartbeats. One per 30 s per viewer: 50M / 30 = ~1.7M heartbeats/s - more than the token API. Aggregate at the edge or sample.

B. Twitch-like (many streams)

  1. Streams. Assume 100K concurrent live channels (Twitch says hundreds of thousands are live at any time).
  2. Ingest. 100K x 6 Mbps source = 600 Gbps into ~100 ingest PoPs (Twitch runs nearly 100).
  3. Transcoding. Full ladder for all streams is very costly; Twitch once offered transcoding to only 2-3% of channels. Assume we transcode the top 10% (10K streams x 5 renditions); the rest get source-only.
  4. Recording. 100K x 6 Mbps = 600 Gbps = 75 GB/s = ~6.5 PB/day if all recorded at source quality. Keep only a short window for most; full VOD for partners.

The punchline: your servers never touch the 75 Tbps. The CDN does. Your scaling battles are (1) the control plane at match start (login, entitlement, tokens, home page), (2) the origin under cache misses, and (3) real-time transcoding capacity for many streams.
