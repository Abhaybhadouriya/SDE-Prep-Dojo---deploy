Why keep the count apart from the post row? Facebook's TAO paper (USENIX ATC 2013) says association counts are stored in a separate table so they never run SELECT COUNT(*). In TAO's production trace, 99.8% of requests were reads and assoc_count was 11.7% of those reads. 45% of count calls returned zero, while 1% returned more than 500,000. That spread - mostly tiny, a few gigantic - is exactly why one sharding plan does not fit every counter.

SQL or NoSQL? Both work for edges. The interesting choice is the counter itself. Watch out for Cassandra counter columns: the official docs say updates are not idempotent (a timed-out update may or may not have been applied, and replaying it can over-count), counters cannot have a TTL, and counter tables cannot hold normal columns. Fine for "approximate views", risky for anything billed.
