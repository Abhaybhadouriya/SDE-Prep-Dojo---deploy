Requirements

Say the scope first. "Counter" hides three different products, and the interviewer wants to see that you notice:

CounterExampleCan it go down?Must it be exact?Dedup needed?
Likes / votesInstagram like, Reddit upvoteYes (unlike)Close; user must see their own likeYes - one like per user per post
Views / playsYouTube views, Reddit post viewsNoNo - "1.2M views" is fineOften "unique viewers per window", and filter bots
Business countsAd clicks billed, stock left in a flash saleDependsYes - moneyYes - idempotent

Functional

  • like(post) / unlike(post) - at most one like per user per post.
  • recordView(video) - count views; optionally count unique viewers per day.
  • getCount(post) - shown next to every post, often for 20-50 posts on one screen.
  • hasLiked(user, post) - so the heart is filled in for you.

Out of scope (say so): who-liked-this list UI, ranking/trending (see Top-K and Trending Topics), ad billing (see Ad Click Aggregator).

Non-functional

  • Very high write throughput and, worse, skew: one viral post can take a large share of all writes.
  • Reads even higher than writes and low latency (tens of ms), because counts render with every feed item.
  • Eventually consistent total is fine (seconds of lag), but read-your-writes for the person who just liked.
  • No over-counting on retries (idempotent) and no lost likes (durable).
  • Highly available - a broken counter must never block posting or viewing.

Ask: "Is this a like counter or a view counter? Exact or approximate? Do counts ever go down?" The answers decide between a sharded exact counter, a stream-batched counter, and HyperLogLog. Interviewers reward you for asking before drawing.
