The story

Explain like I'm 5

Your class keeps one big jar of marbles. Every time someone does a good deed, they walk up and drop one marble in. That works for 20 kids. Now imagine the whole school, 2,000 kids, all running to the same jar at the same time. There is a long line, kids push, and some marbles fall on the floor.

So the teacher puts out 10 small jars. You can drop your marble into any jar - no line. When the teacher wants the total, she counts all 10 jars and adds them up. Even smarter: each class keeps a little paper tally and walks to the jars only once an hour with a handful of marbles. Fewer trips, same total.

And if the teacher wants to know how many different kids did good deeds (not how many deeds), she cannot just count marbles. She needs a clever trick that remembers names without writing every name down. That trick is called HyperLogLog.

The story already holds the whole design:

  1. One jar = one hot row. Every like on a viral post updates the same key. All writers queue behind one lock on one machine.
  2. Ten jars = sharded counter. Split one logical counter into N physical sub-counters. Writes spread out; reads add them up.
  3. Paper tally = batching. Aggregate many +1s in memory or in a stream, then write one +500.
  4. Counting different kids = unique counting. "Views" often means distinct viewers. Exact sets are huge; HyperLogLog gives about 1% error in 12 KB.

This problem shows up on its own (Grokking Advanced's "YouTube likes counter", Educative's "sharded counters" building block, systemdesign.one's case study) and, even more often, inside other designs: like counts in News Feed and Post Search, votes in Reddit / Quora, rating counts in Product Catalog & Reviews, reactions in Live Comments, and request counts in the Rate Limiter.
