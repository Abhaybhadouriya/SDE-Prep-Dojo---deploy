Requirements

First ask questions. Here are the ones that shape a CDC design, with the answers we will assume for an e-commerce company (think Flipkart, Walmart, Shopify scale, but these are our interview assumptions).

  1. Which databases? 64 MySQL shards (orders, payments, inventory) and 10 PostgreSQL databases (catalog, users). Each is a primary with replicas.
  2. Who consumes? (a) the analytics lakehouse, (b) the product search index, (c) cache invalidation, (d) other microservices that want "order changed" events.
  3. How fresh? Database commit to Kafka in under 10 seconds at p99; to the lake within ~5 minutes; to the search index within ~30 seconds.
  4. Can we lose a change? No. Duplicates are acceptable if consumers can handle them (they must).
  5. Can we slow the source database? Barely. Production traffic comes first.
Functional
  • Capture inserts, updates and deletes for chosen tables, with before and after values.
  • Initial full copy (snapshot) of existing rows, then continuous streaming.
  • Re-snapshot one table on demand (a new consumer, or a bug fix) without stopping the stream.
  • Handle schema changes (add column, rename) without breaking consumers.
  • Deliver to sinks: lakehouse tables with upserts, search index, cache deletes.
Out of scope (say it)
  • Writing back into the source databases (CDC is one-way).
  • Cross-table transactions as a single atomic event (we keep per-row order; see deep dives).
  • BI dashboards themselves (they read the lake through Trino/Spark).
