The story

Explain like I'm 5

The principal wants to know everything that changes in your classroom: who moved seats, who got a new pencil, who went home early. One way: every hour, a helper takes a photo of the whole class and compares it with the last photo. That is slow, it disturbs the class, and if Riya moved twice in one hour, the photo only shows where she ended up. A better way: the teacher already writes every change in a diary the moment it happens ("10:02 Riya moved to seat 4"). So the helper just reads the diary line by line and tells the principal. Nothing is missed, nothing is disturbed, and the news arrives seconds later instead of an hour later.

Change Data Capture (CDC) is the diary-reading way. Every real database already keeps a diary, because it needs one to survive crashes and to feed its own replicas: MySQL calls it the binlog (binary log), PostgreSQL calls it the WAL (write-ahead log). A CDC tool such as Debezium pretends to be a replica, reads that diary, and turns each row change into an event in Kafka. Everyone who cares - the data lake, the search index, the cache, other services - reads the events from Kafka.

The "photo" way also has a name: query-based extraction or batch ETL ("SELECT * FROM orders WHERE updated_at > :last_run" every hour). It is simple, but it misses deletes, misses intermediate states, needs an updated_at column everyone remembers to set, and loads the database. Shopify replaced exactly such a batch extractor because its data was at least an hour old (see real world).

From your world

The binlog/WAL is a journal, exactly like ext4's jbd2 journal: the database writes "what I am about to change" to a sequential log before touching the real data pages, so it can replay after a crash. CDC is a process that tails the journal and replays it somewhere else. Another way to see it: a routing daemon streaming RIB changes (add prefix, withdraw prefix) to the FIB on every line card, instead of re-dumping the whole table. Where the analogy breaks: a filesystem journal is recycled almost immediately; a database keeps old WAL/binlog only as long as someone (a replica or a CDC slot) still needs it - which becomes a real outage risk, as you will see in the deep dives.

Asked as "design a CDC pipeline from sharded MySQL to a search index and cache", "how would you keep Elasticsearch in sync with the DB?", "how do you build a data lake that is minutes fresh?", or as the fix for the dual-write problem in order/payment designs. It is commonly reported in SDE-2 loops at companies that run CDC at scale, such as Shopify, Netflix and Uber.
