Back-of-envelope estimation

Numbers below are assumptions to reason with, not any company's real figures. State them, then do the maths slowly. (Cheat sheet: 1 day is about 86,400 s, round to 105. See Back-of-envelope Estimation.)

  1. Payments per day. Assume a large e-commerce company: 50 million payment attempts per day.
  2. Average TPS. 50,000,000 / 100,000 s = 500 payments/s (exact: 50M / 86,400 = about 580).
  3. Peak TPS. Normal daily peak is about 3x, so 1,500/s. A festival sale (Big Billion Days, Prime Day) can be 10x: plan for about 6,000 payments/s. For a sense of scale, PhonePe has reported over 8,000 transactions/s at peak on its TStore system.
  4. Database writes per payment. 1 payment insert + about 3 status updates + 2-4 ledger postings + 2 outbox rows + 1 idempotency row: about 10 writes. At 6,000/s peak that is about 60,000 row writes/s. One well-tuned SQL primary handles a few thousand to tens of thousands of simple writes/s (rule of thumb), so we will need to shard.
  5. Storage per payment. Payment row about 1 KB, events 4 x 0.5 KB, postings 4 x 0.2 KB, idempotency record with saved response about 1 KB: about 5 KB.
  6. Storage per day and year. 50M x 5 KB = 250 GB/day, about 90 TB/year; with 3 replicas about 270 TB/year. Keep hot data (90 days) in the OLTP database and move older history to cheaper archive storage.
  7. Idempotency store. Keys are kept about 24 hours (Stripe's docs describe pruning keys after 24 hours): 50M x 1 KB = 50 GB. Small. It fits in the main SQL database or a replicated KV store.
  8. Kafka events. About 6 events per payment = 300M/day = 3,500/s average, maybe 36,000/s peak, at about 1 KB = 36 MB/s peak. That is light for a small Kafka cluster.
  9. Outbound calls. 1-3 PSP calls per payment, plus about 2 merchant webhooks: at peak about 30,000 outbound HTTPS calls/s. Those are slow (hundreds of ms to seconds), so workers must be async and connection-pooled.

What the numbers tell us: traffic is not the hard part (6,000/s is modest). The hard part is correctness under failure. Hello Interview's advice applies: do the maths only where it changes the design. Here it tells us to shard the write path and make outbound calls async. Then spend your time on idempotency and reconciliation.
