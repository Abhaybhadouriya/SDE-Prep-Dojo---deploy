Trade-offs, bottlenecks and failure modes

DecisionWe choseAlternativeWhy / when to switch
Main storeSharded SQLNoSQL (DynamoDB, Cassandra)ACID across payment + ledger + outbox. Switch if writes far outgrow sharded SQL, but then build idempotency and conditional writes yourself.
Cross-service consistencySaga + outbox + idempotent consumers2PC across services2PC blocks if the coordinator dies, and PSPs and banks will not join your 2PC anyway.
Calling the PSPAsync worker, short sync waitFully synchronous in the requestA sync call holds threads for seconds. Async scales better; the checkout page polls the status.
Result deliveryWebhooks + status APIPolling onlyWebhooks are faster and cheaper; polling is the backup when webhooks are lost.
Event busKafkaSQS / RabbitMQMany independent consumers and replay. SQS is fine for one worker queue with less ops work.
PSP strategyMulti-PSP routerSingle PSPResilience and cost, at the price of more reconciliation work.

"What if this box fails?"

FailureWhat the user seesWhat saves us
Payment service pod crashes mid-requestSpinner, then a retryClient retries with the same key; recovery point resumes the work.
DB primary failsA few seconds of errorsSynchronous replica promoted; clients retry with backoff and jitter.
PSP is slow or down"Confirming your payment"Circuit breaker, router sends new payments to another PSP, unknown state plus reconciliation.
Webhook from PSP lostNothing, if handledStatus sweeper polls stuck payments; daily recon.
Kafka broker diesNothingRF 3, acks=all; the outbox keeps rows until published.
Merchant endpoint downMerchant sees delayed updatesRetry topic with backoff, DLQ, merchant can re-fetch via GET.
Strengths of this design
  • No double charge even with retries at every hop
  • Every rupee traceable through events and ledger
  • Teams add consumers without touching the payment path
Costs
  • More moving parts (outbox relay, sweeper, recon)
  • "Unknown" states need UX and support processes
  • Sharded SQL needs care for cross-shard reports
