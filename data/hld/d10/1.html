Requirements

In the interview, spend about 5 minutes here. Say the scope out loud. A payment platform is huge (payins, payouts, subscriptions, disputes, FX, lending), so pick a slice and confirm it. The usual slice is: an e-commerce company (think Amazon or Flipkart) accepts money from shoppers through one or more PSPs.

Functional (what it does)

  1. Create a payment for an order: amount, currency, method (card, UPI, wallet, net banking).
  2. Get payment status at any time (the checkout page polls it, the order service reads it).
  3. Handle async results: banks and UPI often finish seconds or minutes later, through a PSP webhook.
  4. Refund a payment, fully or partly.
  5. Notify the merchant/order service (webhook or event) when the status changes.
  6. Record money movement in a ledger and reconcile it daily with PSP settlement reports.

Non-functional (how well)

  • Correctness over speed: never double charge, never lose a successful payment.
  • Strong consistency for payment state and money. Eventual is fine for analytics and dashboards.
  • High availability of the create-payment API, e.g. 99.99% (about 52 minutes of downtime a year).
  • Latency: our own overhead small (say under 200 ms p99); the bank part can take seconds and that is OK.
  • Durability and audit: keep every state change for years (regulators ask); append-only history.
  • Security and compliance: PCI-DSS for card data, encryption, least privilege, no CVV storage.

Words you might confuse. Payin = money coming in from a customer. Payout = money going out to a seller. Authorize = the bank puts a hold on the card. Capture = actually take the held money (often when the item ships). Settlement = the PSP later moves the real money to the merchant's bank account, usually in a daily batch. A payment can be "successful" today and settled in a day or two.

More new words, once each. 3-D Secure (3DS) = the extra step for cards where the bank shows its own page or asks for an OTP; the API signals it with next_action: redirect_3ds. PCI-DSS = the card industry's security standard; it applies to every system that stores, processes or even sees raw card numbers. NPCI = National Payments Corporation of India, the organisation that runs UPI. OLTP = online transaction processing: the live database that serves requests (as opposed to an analytics warehouse).

Say this early: "In payments I will trade latency and some availability for correctness. A slow payment is annoying; a double charge or a lost payment is a support ticket, a refund and a trust problem." It frames every later choice.
