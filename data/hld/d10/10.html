How real companies do it

  • Stripe describes client-generated Idempotency-Key headers, servers that save and replay results, and client retries with exponential backoff plus jitter (Designing robust and predictable APIs with idempotency). Brandur Leach's companion post shows a full Postgres implementation with atomic phases and recovery points (Implementing Stripe-like idempotency keys in Postgres).
  • Airbnb built Orpheus, an idempotency library that splits each call into pre-RPC, RPC and post-RPC phases, classifies errors as retryable or not, and always uses the primary database for idempotency records (Avoiding double payments in a distributed payments system; opens in a browser).
  • PhonePe's TStore reports over 8,000 transactions/s at peak. Writes first land in a Kafka write-ahead log and then in HBase as the source of truth; notifications fire only after both succeed; Elasticsearch powers transaction feeds (Demystifying TStore). A good story when an Indian interviewer asks "where would Kafka go in payments?"
  • Razorpay saves the first response for each idempotency key and returns it for repeats (payout idempotency docs), and documents signed webhooks (webhooks docs).
  • PayPal runs Kafka at payments scale: 1,500+ brokers and 85+ clusters, with a peak around 21M messages/s reported for Retail Friday 2022 (Scaling Kafka at PayPal).
  • Amazon explains client request tokens for safe retries in the Builders' Library (Making retries safe with idempotent APIs).
