Back-of-envelope estimation

You asked: "How do I estimate quickly without a calculator?" The trick is one number: a day has 86,400 seconds, round it to 100,000 (105). So "per day divided by 105" gives "per second". Rounding makes the answer about 14% too low, which is fine; we only want the order of magnitude. Full method: Back-of-Envelope Estimation.

Step 1: write QPS (queries per second)

  1. Writes. 100M new URLs/day ÷ 105 s/day ≈ ~1.2k writes/s (105 gives 1,000; the exact figure with 86,400 is 1,157/s, which we round to 1.2k from here on).
  2. Reads. 100 reads per write → 10 billion redirects/day ≈ 100,000 reads/s (exact: about 116,000/s).
  3. Peak. Rule of thumb: peak is 2-3x average. Use 3x: about 3,500 writes/s and 350,000 reads/s at peak.

Step 2: how long must the code be?

  1. Codes needed. Keep links 5 years: 100M × 365 × 5 ≈ 182.5 billion codes.
  2. Alphabet. Use base62: 0-9 (10) + a-z (26) + A-Z (26) = 62 characters, all safe in a URL.
  3. Try 6 characters. 626 ≈ 56.8 billion. Not enough for 182.5 billion.
  4. Try 7 characters. 627 ≈ 3.5 trillion. That is about 19x what we need. Pick 7.

Step 3: storage

  1. One row. code (7 B) + long URL (up to ~2 KB, average a few hundred bytes) + created time (8 B) + expiry (8 B) + owner ID (8 B) + small overhead. Call it ~500 bytes (assumption).
  2. Per day. 100M × 500 B = 50 GB/day.
  3. Per year. 50 GB × 365 ≈ 18 TB/year.
  4. 5 years. ≈ 91 TB, and with 3 replicas ≈ 275 TB. Too much for one machine, so we need sharding (splitting the table across many machines).

Step 4: bandwidth and cache

  1. Read bandwidth. A redirect response is tiny, just headers (~500 B): 116k/s × 500 B ≈ 58 MB/s. Easy for a handful of servers.
  2. Write bandwidth. 1,157/s × 500 B ≈ 0.6 MB/s. Tiny.
  3. Cache size. Rule of thumb: a small share of links gets most clicks. If we keep the hottest 20% of one day's new links: 20M × 500 B = 10 GB. That fits in the RAM of one cache node; we add a few more for throughput and replicas.
  4. Cache throughput. A Redis node handles roughly 100k+ simple operations per second (order of magnitude, from the research notes). At 350k reads/s peak, plan for ~6 shards with replicas, or put a CDN in front to absorb the most viral links.
