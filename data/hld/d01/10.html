How real companies do it

  • Hello Interview's Bitly breakdown walks the counter-based path: a Redis INCR counter handed out in batches, base62 encoding, a cache in front of the database, and a 302 redirect. It is the closest public write-up to how interviewers expect the answer to go. Read it.
  • ByteByteGo (Alex Xu) compares "hash plus collision resolution" against "base62 of a unique ID" and explains the 301 vs 302 choice. Chapter.
  • Twitter's Snowflake (archived on GitHub) is the classic way to make unique 64-bit IDs on many machines without a central counter: 41 bits of milliseconds, 10 bits of machine ID, 12 bits of sequence, so 4,096 IDs per millisecond per machine. Our unique ID generator page covers it in depth. Repo.
  • Instagram built its 64-bit IDs inside PostgreSQL: 41 bits time, 13 bits logical shard, 10 bits per-shard sequence ("Sharding & IDs at Instagram", on the Instagram engineering blog). The same idea of "put the shard inside the ID" works for short codes.
  • System Design Primer has a worked Pastebin/bit.ly style solution with estimates. Repo.

Bitly itself has not published a detailed architecture with numbers that we could verify, so this page does not quote any Bitly-internal figures. The numbers above are standard interview assumptions.
