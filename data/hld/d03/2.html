Back-of-envelope estimation

Same trick as always: per day ÷ 105 ≈ per second (estimation page). All inputs below are our assumptions; the method is what the interviewer grades.

Step 1: sends per second

  1. Daily volume. 100M users × 10 notifications = 1 billion notifications/day.
  2. Average rate. 1B ÷ 105 ≈ 10,000/s (exact: about 11,600/s).
  3. Normal peak. Rule of thumb 3x average: about 35,000/s (evenings, sale start).
  4. Campaign on top. 100M users in 1 hour = 100M ÷ 3,600 ≈ 28,000/s extra.
  5. Plan for about 60,000 sends/s at peak across all channels.

Step 3: storage

  1. Notification log row (IDs, user, channel, template ID, params, status, timestamps): about 500 B.
  2. Per day: 1B × 500 B = 500 GB/day.
  3. Keep 90 days for support and debugging: 500 GB × 90 = 45 TB; with 3 replicas ≈ 135 TB. A write-heavy, time-ordered log: a wide-column store (Cassandra, ScyllaDB, DynamoDB) fits well.
  4. Device tokens: 100M users × 2 devices × ~200 B ≈ 40 GB. Small; any key-value store.

Step 4: Kafka sizing

  1. Message size about 1 KB (IDs + rendered payload). 60k/s × 1 KB = 60 MB/s into Kafka at peak. A Kafka broker handles hundreds of MB/s of sequential writes (order of magnitude), so a small cluster of 3+ brokers is plenty, with replication factor 3.
  2. Consumers. The slow part is calling providers over the network. If load testing shows one push worker sends about 500/s (an assumption you would measure), 42,000/s needs about 85 workers. With classic consumer groups that means at least 85 partitions on the push topic (one partition feeds at most one consumer in a group).
  3. Retention. Keep 1-7 days so we can replay after a bug.
