Requirements

Spend the first five minutes agreeing what "feed" means. Is it Twitter-style (follow anyone, mostly text), Facebook-style (two-way friends, ranked), or Instagram-style (follow, photo-first, ranked)? The core design is the same; the numbers change.

Functional (what it does)

  • Create a post: text, plus optional photos or a video.
  • Follow / unfollow other users (one-way, like Instagram and X).
  • Home feed: posts from people I follow, newest or "most relevant" first.
  • Infinite scroll: load the next page smoothly.
  • Like and comment counts shown on each post.
  • Profile page: one user's own posts (this is the easy one).

Non-functional (how well)

  • Scale (assumption): 1B monthly users, 500M daily active users (DAU).
  • Read latency: first feed page in under ~200 ms p99 at the server (assumption; the phone adds network time).
  • Freshness: a new post reaches followers' feeds within a few seconds; up to a minute is acceptable (eventual consistency).
  • Availability over consistency: showing a slightly stale feed beats showing an error page.
  • Read-heavy: far more feed reads than posts. Twitter's 2012 numbers were about 300K QPS of timeline reads vs about 6K writes/s.
  • Read-your-own-writes: I must see my own new post immediately.

Out of scope unless asked: stories, reels/video transcoding (see Video Upload & Transcoding), search (see Post Search), ads insertion, direct messages (see Chat App), trending topics (see Trending Topics).

Say this early: "A feed is a read-heavy system where the hard choice is fan-out on write versus fan-out on read. I will use push for normal users and pull for celebrities, then show why with numbers." Interviewers at Meta and Amazon wait for exactly this sentence.
