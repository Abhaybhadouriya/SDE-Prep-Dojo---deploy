How real companies do it

  • Twitter (2012, "Timelines at Scale"): at about 150M users, roughly 300K QPS of timeline reads vs about 6K writes/s. Tweets were fanned out on write into Redis timelines of about 800 entries, each replicated 3 times; very large accounts were handled separately and merged at read time. (Raffi Krikorian's QCon talk; HighScalability summary is secondary.)
  • LinkedIn (FollowFeed, 2016): moved to a pull-based, indexed feed where ranking happens at read time, so ranking could use fresh signals. A good counterexample to "always push".
  • Facebook (TAO + memcache): the social graph lives in sharded MySQL behind TAO, a read-through, write-through graph cache with follower and leader tiers; the 2013 post says it serves over a billion reads per second. The memcache paper explains leases against thundering herds and stale sets.
  • Instagram: the "Scaling Instagram Infrastructure" talk covers running multiple regions with Cassandra, PostgreSQL and memcache for feeds and the graph.
  • X / Twitter ranking: the recommendation pipeline (candidate sources, a heavy ranker, filters and mixing) is open source as the-algorithm.

Related designs: Chat App, Notification System, Distributed Counter, Post Search, Trending Topics, Recommendation System, Live Comments. Patterns: Fan-out (Push vs Pull), Scaling Reads, Handling Large Blobs.
