Leaderless (Dynamo/Cassandra) pros
  • Any replica takes writes: highest write availability
  • No leader election pause
  • Tail latency hidden by waiting for the fastest W/R
Leaderless cons
  • Conflicts and siblings (or silent LWW loss)
  • Hard to give strong consistency or transactions
  • Repair is a constant background job

Failure modes to mention

  • Whole AZ/DC down: replicas must be placed across failure domains (Cassandra's NetworkTopologyStrategy, DynamoDB's AZ-spread groups). Use LOCAL_QUORUM for latency inside one region.
  • Background work hurting foreground latency: Dynamo had an admission controller that throttled repair and handoff when foreground p99 database read latency drifted above target (50 ms in their example).
  • Durability vs latency: Dynamo's optional in-memory write buffer cut p99.9 latency by 5x at peak, at the risk of losing buffered writes on a crash; they mitigated it by having one of the N replicas do a durable write.
  • GC pauses: JVM stores (Cassandra) can stall for seconds; Discord moved to ScyllaDB (C++) and went from 177 Cassandra nodes to 72 ScyllaDB nodes, with p99 message-insert latency falling from 5-70 ms to a steady 5 ms.
  • Clock skew with LWW: a node with a fast clock wins every conflict. Keep NTP tight or use vector clocks/CRDTs.
