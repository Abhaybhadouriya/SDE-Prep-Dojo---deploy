How real systems do it

  • Google Docs uses a central-server operational transformation model. The open Google Wave OT whitepaper (Apache Wave) describes the client-server protocol: the server orders operations, a client has one operation in flight and buffers the rest until it is acknowledged.
  • Figma - "How Figma's multiplayer technology works": decided OT was more complex than needed; CRDT-inspired, server-authoritative; one process per document; last-writer-wins per property. "Realtime editing of ordered sequences" explains fractional indexing.
  • Yjs and Automerge - open-source CRDT libraries used in production collaborative editors; their docs explain document updates, awareness (presence) and garbage collection.
  • Martin Kleppmann, "CRDTs: The Hard Parts" - interleaving anomalies, moving items, and why tombstones and metadata matter.
  • Ink & Switch, "Local-first software" - the essay that frames why offline-first apps choose CRDTs.

Related designs: Chat (WhatsApp) (WebSocket gateways, per-conversation ordering), Live Comments (fan-out over sockets), Online Chess (server-authoritative real-time state), File Sync (Dropbox) (conflicts when you cannot merge), and the Real-time Updates pattern. Consensus and leases are covered in Replication, Consensus & Coordination.
