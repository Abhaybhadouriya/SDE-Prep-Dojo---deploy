The story

Explain like I'm 5

Two kids, Alice and Bob, are writing a story in the same notebook, but each has a photocopy so they can both write at once. Alice adds a word at the start of a line. At the same moment Bob crosses out a word at the end of that line. Now their copies disagree. A kind teacher collects both changes and fixes them up: "Alice added one word at the front, so Bob's crossing-out is now one word further along." The teacher writes the fixed-up change on both copies, and the two notebooks match again. Nobody's word got squashed.

That teacher is the heart of Google Docs. Each keystroke is an operation ("insert X at position 5", "delete position 9"). People type at the same time on different copies of the document, so their operations are concurrent - neither knew about the other. The system must make every copy converge (end up identical) and also respect intention (Bob meant to delete that word, not whatever happens to be at position 9 now).

There are two famous families of solutions. Operational Transformation (OT) is the teacher: a central server orders the operations and rewrites ("transforms") each one against the others it did not know about. CRDTs (Conflict-free Replicated Data Types) give every letter a permanent, unique name, so "delete the letter named bob:7" means the same thing everywhere, in any order, with no teacher needed. Everything else in this design - WebSockets, one server owning each document, an operation log, snapshots, cursors - is plumbing around that core.

Positions are 0-based on this page, as in C arrays: in "abc", a = 0, b = 1, c = 2. So "insert X at 1" puts X between a and b, and "delete position 2" removes the "c".

Reported in the wild: "Design a live collaboration tool like Google Docs (OT vs CRDT)" in a Walmart staff loop (Dec 2024), and "Google-Docs-like collaboration" in Microsoft and Atlassian senior/staff reports. Hello Interview tags it hard. At SDE-2 you are expected to know why naive approaches lose edits and to pick OT or CRDT with a reason; at senior you also drive scaling of WebSockets and storage.
