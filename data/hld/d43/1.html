Requirements

In an interview, spend the first 5 minutes agreeing on scope. Say out loud what you will cover and what you will park.

Functional (what it does)

  1. Create, edit, delete events with title, time, time zone, location and description. Timed events and all-day events.
  2. Recurring events (daily, weekly, "second Monday of each month") with changes to a single occurrence, or to "this and all following" occurrences.
  3. Invites and RSVP. The organizer adds attendees. Each attendee sees the event and answers accepted, declined, tentative, or not yet answered.
  4. Views. Day, week and month views of one or more calendars, rendered in the viewer's own time zone.
  5. Reminders. Popup or email N minutes before each occurrence. Google's API allows up to 5 override reminders per event and 0 to 40,320 minutes (4 weeks) before the start (Events reference).
  6. Free/busy and "find a time". Show when colleagues are busy without showing what they are doing.
  7. Sharing. Owner, writer and reader roles on a calendar.
  8. Multi-device sync. A change on the laptop appears on the phone within seconds.

Usually out of scope: room booking optimisation, video-call links, natural-language event creation, and importing from other providers (mention iCalendar import in one line).

Non-functional (how well it does it)

PropertyTargetWhy
Correctness of event dataStrong consistency for writes and RSVPsA meeting at the wrong hour is worse than a slow page.
Read availability99.99%People open the calendar all day; Educative's design uses this target.
Read latency~100-200 ms for a week view, at p99Feels instant. Writes can take ~300-500 ms (Educative's numbers). p99 = 99% of requests are faster than this; the slowest 1% are what users complain about. Like tail latency through a switch buffer, not the average.
Reminder accuracyFires within a few seconds of the target minuteA reminder 3 minutes late is a missed meeting. Airbnb's Dynein scheduler aimed for most jobs within 10 seconds.
Reminder deliveryAt-least-once, de-duplicatedLosing a reminder is worse than a rare duplicate, but duplicates annoy users.
Views, free/busy, searchEventually consistent (seconds)These are derived views; a short lag is fine.

Say this early: "Event data must be strongly consistent, but almost everything else (views, free/busy, notifications) is a derived view that can lag a little and be rebuilt." It tells the interviewer you know where to spend consistency.
