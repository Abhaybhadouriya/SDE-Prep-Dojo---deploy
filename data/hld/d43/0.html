The story

Explain like I'm 5

Your class has a big wall chart. The teacher does not write "music class" in every Tuesday box for the whole year. She writes one note at the top: "Music every Tuesday." When you look at any week, you read the note and you know. If music is cancelled on one Tuesday, she sticks a small "no music today" sticker on that one box. And every Tuesday morning, the class helper rings a little bell ten minutes before music, so nobody forgets.

That is the whole calendar problem in one picture. The note at the top is a recurrence rule (a short formula that says when an event repeats). The sticker is an exception (a change to just one occurrence). The bell is a reminder (a notification that must fire at a precise time before the event).

A real calendar adds three twists. People live in different time zones, and some of those zones jump an hour twice a year for daylight saving time. Events have attendees who must each see the event on their own calendar and reply yes or no. And a user has a phone, a laptop and a watch that must all stay in sync.

This design is reported in real interviews. An Uber SDE-2 candidate reported being asked to design Google Calendar (LeetCode Discuss report). It is also a case study in Educative's Grokking Modern System Design and in DesignGurus' Grokking Advanced course, which covers both a calendar and a separate reminder service. Microsoft (Outlook) and Google (Calendar) build this product, so expect it there too.
