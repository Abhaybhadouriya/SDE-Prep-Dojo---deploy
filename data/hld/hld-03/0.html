The story: building a treehouse with a friend

Explain like I'm 5

Your friend says, "Let's build a treehouse." If you grab a hammer and start nailing, you might build a house for two kids when ten want to come, or forget the ladder. A good builder first asks: "How many kids? Do we need a roof? How much wood do we have?" Then draws a quick picture, shows it to the friend, and only then builds the tricky parts, like the rope bridge.

A system design interview is exactly that. The interviewer is your friend. Ask first, draw simple, agree, then go deep on the tricky part. Talk the whole time so your friend can help.

A system design round (also called HLD, high-level design) is a 45-60 minute conversation where you design a product such as a URL shortener, a chat app or a flash sale. There is no single right answer. The interviewer is grading how you think: do you clarify, do you size the problem, do you make sensible choices and explain their cost, do you notice what fails?

From your world

In networking and kernel work, a "design" question is usually a box: a forwarding pipeline, a driver, a memory allocator. You start from internals because the requirements are fixed by hardware. In an SDE loop the question is a product, and the requirements are vague on purpose. The interviewer wants you to start from the user and the API, then scale, and only then internals. Your instinct to dive into internals is a strength - just hold it for the deep-dive slot at minute 30. Where this breaks: on a line card, the spec tells you the packet rate; here, nobody tells you the numbers until you ask.
