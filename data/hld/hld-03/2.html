Minute by minute: the 45-minute clock

Most loops give 45-60 minutes; the first few go to introductions and the last few to your questions. That leaves about 38 minutes of design work, from minute 3 to minute 41. The clock below follows Alex Xu's and Hello Interview's suggested splits (both are guides, not laws). The dot shows where you should be.

MinutesStepWhat you doWhat you write on the board
0-3HelloShort intro. Repeat the prompt in your own words.The prompt
3-8Requirements3 functional requirements, 3-5 non-functional ones with numbers, 1-2 things out of scope. Ask; if the interviewer says "you decide", decide out loud.Two short lists: FR and NFR
8-11EstimatesDAU (daily active users), QPS (requests per second, average and peak), storage per year, read:write ratio (how many reads for every write). Round hard. Say what the number changes.4-5 numbers
11-15Entities, API, data modelNouns, 2-4 endpoints, the main table or key layout, the DB choice in one sentence.API list, 1-2 tables
15-25High-level designDraw client, gateway/LB, services, stores, queue. Walk each functional requirement through the boxes. Get a nod: "Does this cover what you had in mind?"The main diagram
25-37Deep divesPick 1-3 hard parts tied to the NFRs (hot key, consistency, fan-out, failure). Offer options, pick one, state the cost. At SDE-2 you choose them; at SDE-1 the interviewer may steer.Zoomed-in boxes
37-41Wrap-upBottlenecks, what fails and how you would notice (metrics, alerts), what you would do at 10x, how you would roll it out.A short "risks" list
41-45Your questionsAsk about the team's real systems. Some interviewers use this time for one more follow-up instead.

Careful: the most common way to fail is running out of time before a working design exists. If you are at minute 20 with no diagram, stop estimating and draw. A simple design that works beats a perfect half-design.

Click Next to see how the whiteboard should look at the end of each phase, for "Design a URL shortener".

  1. Minutes 3-8: requirements. Two short lists in a corner. Nothing else yet. This is your contract with the interviewer.
  2. Minutes 8-11: estimates. Three or four numbers. The key insight written down: reads are about 100x writes, so this is a read-heavy, cache-friendly system.
  3. Minutes 11-15: API and data model. Two endpoints and one table. A key-value store fits because every lookup is by code.
  4. Minutes 15-25: high-level design. Client, load balancer and stateless app servers, a cache for hot codes, the store. Walk "redirect" through it out loud (the moving dot).
  5. Minutes 25-37: deep dive. How codes stay unique across many servers: each server leases a block of IDs from a counter and base62-encodes them. Then: what if one link goes viral (cache plus CDN).
  6. Minutes 37-41: wrap-up. What fails first, what you watch, what changes at 10x. The full design is on URL Shortener.
