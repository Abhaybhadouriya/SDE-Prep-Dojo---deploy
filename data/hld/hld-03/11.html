Quick quiz

The interviewer finishes the prompt. What is the best first move?

  1. Draw a load balancer and three servers
  2. Ask scoping questions and list functional and non-functional requirements
  3. Start estimating storage for 10 years
  4. Pick a database
Requirements come first. They define what you will be graded against and stop you designing the wrong thing.

In a 45-minute round, roughly when should you have a complete high-level design on the board?

  1. Minute 5
  2. Minute 40
  3. Around minute 25
  4. Never - deep dives matter more
Aim to finish the first working design a little past the middle (minute ~25), leaving about 12 minutes for deep dives and 4 for wrap-up.

Which of these is the strongest trade-off statement?

  1. "I'd use cache-aside because reads dominate; the cost is brief staleness, bounded by a TTL; if staleness hurts, I'd write through."
  2. "Caching is always good."
  3. "It depends."
  4. "Redis is the industry standard."
It names the choice, the reason, the cost and the switch condition.

What most clearly separates an SDE-2 performance from an SDE-1 performance?

  1. Knowing more technology names
  2. Drawing more boxes
  3. Spending longer on estimates
  4. Driving the round, choosing deep dives and raising failure modes without prompting
SDE-2 is judged on ownership of the conversation and on trade-offs and failures, not on vocabulary.

In the notification example, why do OTPs get their own topic and workers?

  1. OTPs are bigger messages
  2. So a huge marketing campaign cannot delay time-critical OTPs
  3. Kafka requires one topic per channel
  4. To save money
Separating by priority isolates critical traffic from bulk traffic - a queue-level version of QoS.

You are asked about a technology you have never used. Best response?

  1. Pretend you have used it
  2. Change the subject
  3. Say so, state what you know, reason about whether it fits, and ask a question back
  4. Refuse to answer
Honest first-principles reasoning is graded well; bluffing is easy to detect.
