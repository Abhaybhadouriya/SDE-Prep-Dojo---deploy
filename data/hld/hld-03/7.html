Turning a pipeline or networking story into a STAR answer

Many loops (Amazon most of all) mix behavioural questions into design rounds: "Tell me about a time you handled a production failure." STAR means Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep each part to two or three sentences and put numbers in the Result. Here is one story shaped both ways, so the same experience answers a behavioural question and feeds a design deep dive.

  1. Situation "Our line cards were dropping control packets during route-flap storms. Neighbours timed out and sessions reset across the network."
  2. Task "I owned the punt path from the forwarding chip to the CPU and had to stop the drops without starving the data plane."
  3. Action "I measured queue depth on the punt ring, found bursts overflowing one shared queue, split it into priority queues so keepalives were never behind bulk updates, and added a policer so a storm could not take the whole CPU."
  4. Result "Session resets during storms stopped in our lab reproductions, and the design became the default for the next platform." (Use the real result you measured - never an invented number.)

When an interviewer asks "where have you seen this before?", tell this story in one breath and map each part. Where this breaks: a ring buffer drops when full; a Kafka topic keeps the backlog on disk, so the failure shows up as lag and latency, not as loss.
