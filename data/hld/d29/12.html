Quick quiz

"Good morning" is posted 12,000 times in 10 minutes; its baseline is 11,500. Is it trending?

  1. No, it is close to its normal rate
  2. Yes, it has the highest count
  3. Yes, any term above 10,000 trends
Trending measures surprise. 12,000 vs 11,500 expected gives a small score.

A count-min sketch estimate is...

  1. Never lower than the true count
  2. Never higher than the true count
  3. Always exact
Collisions only add to counters, and we take the minimum, so it can overestimate but never underestimate.

Why key term events by location|term?

  1. To compress them
  2. Because Kafka sorts by key
  3. So one worker owns each key's full count, with no locks or cross-worker sums
Same key → same partition → same worker. Local results per key are complete, so merging top-Ks is simple.

After a 20-minute outage, every term spikes when the job catches up. The root cause is...

  1. The sketch is too small
  2. Counting in processing time instead of event time
  3. Too few Kafka partitions
Backlog processed "now" lands in the current window. Event-time windows put old posts in their real minute.

One account posts a hashtag 10,000 times. What stops it trending?

  1. A bigger sketch
  2. A longer window
  3. Scoring on distinct authors (HyperLogLog) with trust weights
One author counts once, and low-trust accounts count less.

3 of 48 partitions have huge lag during a final. Best first fix?

  1. Add 100 consumers
  2. Pre-aggregate per second and salt the hot terms
  3. Restart Kafka
Skewed lag means hot keys. More consumers cannot split a single key; pre-aggregation and salting can.

Why is the read path cheap?

  1. Users rarely look at trends
  2. The API computes trends with a fast sort
  3. All lists together are about 1 MB, precomputed and cached
A tiny precomputed answer can be served from Redis and a CDN at any read rate.
