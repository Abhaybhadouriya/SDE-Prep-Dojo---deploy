The story

Explain like I'm 5

Imagine the whole school is talking in the playground. The principal wants to know: what is everyone suddenly excited about? She cannot listen to every child. So she gives each teacher a small tally sheet. Every time a teacher hears a word like "rainbow" or "ice-cream truck", she makes a tick. The sheet is small, so sometimes ticks for two words land in the same box - the count can be a little too high, but never too low. Every minute the teachers hand in their sheets. The principal notices something clever: children say "hello" all the time, so "hello" is never news. But "ice-cream truck" went from 2 ticks to 200 ticks in five minutes. That is trending - not the word said most, but the word that jumped the most compared to normal.

That is the whole design. The children's words are posts (tweets). The words we care about are terms: hashtags, names, phrases. The tally sheet is a count-min sketch, a small fixed-size table of counters that gives approximate counts. Teachers are stream processors running in parallel, each owning some of the terms. And the principal's clever rule is the trend score: compare the current rate with the term's usual rate (its baseline), not just the raw count.

This page builds on Top-K / Heavy Hitters, which covers "what is most popular". Trending is the harder cousin: "what is unusually popular right now, here, and by real people".

You might confuse this with...

  • Trending vs Top-K: Top-K = the items with the highest counts ("most played songs this week"); trending = the items whose count jumped most above their own normal, right now. Top-K alone would show "good morning" forever.
  • Trending vs search popularity: search popularity (autocomplete, "top searches") counts what people look for, usually over days, and powers suggestions; trending counts what people post about, over minutes, per place, by distinct users.
  • Count-min sketch vs HyperLogLog: the sketch answers "how many times was X said?"; HyperLogLog answers "how many different people said X?". Trending needs both.

From your world

You have done this on a router: sFlow/NetFlow-style flow counters hash flows into a fixed table of counters and accept collisions, and a DDoS detector does not alarm on "the busiest flow" (that is always your CDN) but on a flow whose rate jumps far above its usual rate. Trending topics is anomaly detection on word counts instead of packet counts. Where the analogy breaks: words have meaning - "#WorldCup", "#worldcup" and "World Cup" must be merged into one term, and people try to fake trends on purpose, so we count distinct real users, not just mentions.

Reported in interviews as "design trending topics / trending hashtags" (Razorpay reported), "top-K popular pages or feeds in a time window" (Atlassian P40/P50 reported), and the classic "top K heavy hitters" (Meta, Amazon). These come from candidate write-ups, so treat them as "commonly reported", not "always asked".
