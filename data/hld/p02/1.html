When you hit this problem

Signals in the interview question

  • A scarce thing: last seat, last room-night, last unit in stock, one driver, one meeting room, one locker slot.
  • "Must never" words: "no double booking", "never oversell", "balance can't go negative", "each job runs once".
  • A spike on one key: a flash sale on one phone model, a Taylor Swift on-sale, a celebrity post's like counter.
  • A hold while the user does something slow: "reserve the seat while they pay".

Two very different kinds of contention

Correctness contentionThroughput contention (hot key)
ExampleTwo users, one seat1 million users, 10,000 phones, one row
ProblemWrong answer (double booking)Right answer, but everyone waits on one lock
Main toolsAtomic conditional writes, locks, versionsQueues, sharded counters, waiting rooms, admission control

The real numbers are humbling. Ticketmaster's own statement on the 2022 Eras Tour presale reports 3.5 billion total system requests, 4x their previous peak, driven largely by bots and fans without codes, for about 2 million tickets. Shopify describes a February 2016 Kylie Cosmetics sale that took down not only her store but every store on the same database shard, because every checkout step rewrote the same MySQL record. Correctness tools alone do not survive numbers like that; you also need to control how many requests reach the contended row.
