The story

Explain like I'm 5

You want to send a giant teddy bear to your grandma. The post office clerk does not carry the bear across town herself. She gives you a special sticker that says "this box may go into warehouse shelf 42 until 5 o'clock". You take the bear straight to the warehouse, and the clerk only writes in her notebook: "teddy bear, shelf 42".

The bear is too big for one box, so you pack it in ten small boxes, numbered 1 to 10. If box 7 falls off the truck, you only resend box 7, not the whole bear. And when grandma wants to see it, she picks it up from the warehouse nearest her house, not from yours.

A blob (binary large object) is any big chunk of bytes your system stores but does not query inside: a video, an image, a PDF, a backup. A blob store (object storage: Amazon S3, Google Cloud Storage, Azure Blob Storage, MinIO) keeps them cheaply and durably. The sticker is a presigned URL: a short-lived, signed permission to put or get one specific object. The numbered boxes are a multipart upload. The notebook is your metadata database. The warehouse near grandma is a CDN.

The whole pattern fits in one sentence: keep the bytes and the facts about the bytes in different systems, and let the client talk to the byte store directly with a narrow, temporary permission.
