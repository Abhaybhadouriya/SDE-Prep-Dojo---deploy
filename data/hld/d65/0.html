The story

Explain like I'm 5

You get a new toy robot. Your old robot knows your name, your favourite song and that you like the lights dim at bedtime. You hold the two robots next to each other and the old one whispers everything to the new one. Five minutes later the new robot says "Good night, Ria" just like the old one did.

Now you have many robots in different rooms. You tell the kitchen robot "lights off", and every other robot must know the lights are off. So there is one notebook on the fridge. Every change is written on a new numbered line. Each robot remembers the last line number it read, and when it comes back from sleeping it reads only the new lines.

Every night, while you sleep, each robot also puts a copy of its memories in a safe at Grandma's house, and a second copy in another city, just in case.

The whisper is device-to-device transfer. The fridge notebook is a versioned change log; the numbered lines are versions. Telling all robots at once is push over persistent connections. The nightly copy is a scheduled incremental backup, and the second city is multi-region replication.

From your world

This is configuration management for routers. Old-to-new sync is like copying a running config from a failed line card to its replacement. Multi-device sync is like a routing table kept consistent with sequence-numbered updates (an OSPF LSA with a sequence number: a newer number wins, an older one is ignored). Incremental backup with chunk hashes is rsync. A device that comes back after a link flap asks "what changed since sequence N?" instead of a full table dump, unless N is too old, then it does a full resync.
