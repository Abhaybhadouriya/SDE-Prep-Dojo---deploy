Back-of-envelope estimation

Assumptions (state them; the interviewer will adjust): 20M users, 5 devices on average, so 100M devices; most smart speakers stay online all the time.

(a) Setup

  1. New devices per day: if devices are replaced every 3 years, 100M / (3 x 365) = about 90,000 setups/day, about 1/s average, with big peaks on sale days and holidays (say 50x: 50/s). Small.
  2. Settings size: a full settings document is maybe 50 KB (routines, preferences, smart-home links). 100M x 50 KB = 5 TB if we store a copy for every device. Store only differences from defaults (often 2-5 KB) and it falls to a few hundred GB. Pull it directly from the old device and the cloud stores almost nothing: that is the storage follow-up.
  3. Time budget: 50 KB over any network is milliseconds. The 1-5 minutes go to Wi-Fi join, account login, firmware check and app or skill downloads. So the design must parallelise those, not the settings copy.

(b) Multi-device state

  1. Persistent connections: 100M devices. At about 100,000 idle connections per gateway node (MQTT or WebSocket, mostly idle with heartbeats), that is 1,000 nodes; more with headroom across regions.
  2. Changes: 10 changes per user per day = 200M/day = about 2,300/s average, about 25,000/s at evening peaks.
  3. Fan-out: each change goes to up to 9 other devices: about 225,000 pushes/s at peak. Each push under 1 KB: about 225 MB/s across the fleet. Comfortable.
  4. Change log: keep 30 days per user for catch-up: 200M x 30 x 500 B = 3 TB. Older devices get a full snapshot instead.

(c) Backup

  1. Data: say 10M devices with files and media (phones and tablets; speakers back up only settings), 5 GB each first backup: 50 PB logical. After per-user dedup and compression, maybe 30-40 PB physical, times 2 regions.
  2. Daily incremental: 10 MB changed per device per day = 100 TB/day. Spread over 24 hours with jitter: 100 TB / 86,400 s = about 1.2 GB/s, about 10 Gbps. Without jitter, if everyone backs up at 02:00 for one hour, it would be 28 GB/s: so randomise.
  3. Chunk metadata: 4 MB average chunks: 40 PB / 4 MB = 10 billion chunk records x about 100 bytes = 1 TB of metadata. Needs a sharded key-value store keyed by chunk hash.

The best estimation insight here is negative: "settings are tiny, so the 1-5 minute target is not about bandwidth; it is about removing serial steps." It shows you looked for the real bottleneck.
