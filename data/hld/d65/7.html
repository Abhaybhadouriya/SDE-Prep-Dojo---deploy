Where Kafka, Kubernetes and Docker fit

Concrete settings. Partition key user_id gives per-user ordering, so a device never sees "theme = dark" after a later "theme = light". Consumer-group sizing: 32 partitions allow 32 push-service pods; each pod holds many users' partitions. Gateways scale on connection count; backup ingest and verifier workers scale on queue lag with KEDA and can run on spot nodes because every job resumes.

PieceToolWhy and settings
state-changesKafka, key = user_idPer-user ordering for fan-out; push service, audit and analytics are separate consumer groups. Retention a few days; the change log in the DB is the long-term catch-up source.
backup-eventsKafkaCOMMITTED, EXPIRED, SCRUB_FAILED events drive ref counting, GC, replication checks and alerts.
Connection gatewaysKubernetes DeploymentScale on connection count; raise file-descriptor limits; preStop drains connections slowly (devices reconnect with jitter) so a rollout does not reconnect 100k devices in one second.
Backup ingest and verifier workersKubernetes + KEDAScale on queue lag, not CPU. Spot or preemptible nodes are fine: every job is resumable.
Scrub and GCKubernetes CronJobFleet-level jobs (hourly scrub sample, nightly GC) are good CronJobs. Per-device schedules are not: 10M CronJobs is nonsense, so devices self-schedule.
EverythingDocker imagesA device simulator image to load-test 1M fake devices connecting and backing up.
