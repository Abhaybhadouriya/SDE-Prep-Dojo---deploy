How real companies do it

  • WhatsApp: Erlang on FreeBSD; in early 2012 one server held over 2 million TCP connections after kernel and BEAM tuning (WhatsApp blog "1 million is so 2011"; Rick Reed's Erlang Factory 2012 slides describe starting near 200K per server and tuning up).
  • Slack: stateful in-memory channel servers on a consistent hash ring (managed by a service they call CHARMs), gateway servers in many regions holding WebSocket subscriptions, presence servers that hash users, and roughly 500 ms global delivery, with load spikes at the top of each hour.
  • Discord: message store partitioned by (channel_id, bucket) with Snowflake ids; moved from 177 Cassandra nodes to 72 ScyllaDB nodes in 2022, with p99 history reads dropping from 40-125 ms to about 15 ms; Rust data services coalesce many reads of the same hot row into one query.
  • Facebook Messenger: moved mobile clients to MQTT, a light publish/subscribe protocol, to save battery and bandwidth on phones.

Related designs: Team Chat (Slack / Teams), Notification System, Live Comments, News Feed, Unique ID Generator. Patterns: Real-time Updates, Fan-out (Push vs Pull).
