The story

Explain like I'm 5

You draw a giant picture, as big as your bedroom floor, and you want every child in the world to see it. You cannot post the whole floor. So you cut the picture into small squares and post them one by one. If the postman loses square 7, you only send square 7 again, not the whole picture.

At the picture factory, many helpers work at the same time. Each helper takes one square and makes three copies of it: a big shiny one for children with big TVs, a medium one, and a tiny one for children with old phones. Then the copies go to small shops in every town, so each child picks up the picture from a shop near home instead of from the faraway factory.

That is the whole design. Cutting the picture into squares is a chunked, resumable upload: the file goes up in parts, and a lost part is resent alone. The factory helpers are transcoding workers. To transcode means to re-encode a video into another size, quality or format. The three copies are a bitrate ladder (also called renditions): the same video at, say, 240p, 480p, 720p and 1080p. The town shops are a CDN (content delivery network), a set of cache servers placed close to viewers.

One more trick: the player on your phone keeps checking your internet speed. When the train enters a tunnel it quietly switches to the tiny copy, and when the signal comes back it switches to the big one. This is adaptive bitrate streaming (ABR). It only works because we cut each copy into small time pieces called segments (for example 4-6 seconds each), so the player can change quality at every segment boundary.

From your world

A resumable upload is TCP's idea moved up to the application. TCP numbers every byte and a lost segment is retransmitted alone. Here we number every part of the file, and the server remembers which parts it has, like a SACK (selective acknowledgement) bitmap. Where the analogy breaks: TCP state dies with the connection. Upload state lives on the server for days, so the phone can resume after a reboot or a change from Wi-Fi to 4G.

New words, once each (you will meet them below):

  • Presigned URL = a URL that the storage service has signed for us, so whoever holds it can PUT exactly one object until it expires, with no other password. It is a bearer token: possession is permission, like a printed ticket that anyone can use.
  • ETag = a checksum-like tag the store returns for each uploaded part. We send the list of ETags back at the end to prove which parts make up the file.
  • DAG (directed acyclic graph) = a list of tasks with arrows meaning "depends on", and no loops. "Encode" waits for "split"; "package" waits for all encodes.
  • Keyframe (I-frame) = a frame that can be decoded on its own. GOP (group of pictures) = a keyframe plus the frames after it that depend on it, up to the next keyframe. A closed GOP never refers to frames outside itself, so you can cut the video there safely.
  • CDN = cache servers near viewers. Idempotent = doing it twice has the same effect as once. Shard = one slice of a database split by key. p99 = 99 of 100 requests are faster than this.

You might confuse:

  • Transcoding vs packaging. Transcoding re-encodes pixels (slow, CPU-heavy: 4K H.264 in, 720p H.264 out). Packaging only cuts encoded video into segments and writes playlists (fast, no re-encode).
  • Rendition vs segment vs manifest. A rendition is one full encoded version (720p at 2.5 Mbps). A segment is a few seconds of one rendition. A manifest (m3u8 / MPD) is the text menu listing renditions and their segment URLs.
  • Upload part vs encode segment. Parts are arbitrary 16 MB byte ranges of the raw file, for the network. Segments are cut at keyframes, for the encoder. They have nothing to do with each other.
