Requirements

Always fix the scope first. "Design YouTube" is huge (recommendations, comments, ads, live). In 45 minutes we pick upload, processing and playback, and say the rest out loud as out of scope.

Functional
  • A creator uploads a video file (up to a few hours long, many GB) with a title, description and visibility (public, unlisted, private).
  • The upload survives bad networks: pause, resume, switch networks.
  • The system converts the video into several resolutions and formats, makes thumbnails, and runs policy and copyright checks.
  • The creator sees the status: uploading, processing, ready, or failed.
  • Viewers stream the video on any device, and quality adapts to their network.
  • Out of scope (say it): search, recommendations, comments, live streaming, ads, monetisation.
Non-functional
  • Durability: once we say "uploaded", we never lose the original file.
  • Availability of playback is more important than freshness. A video may take minutes to become ready; that is fine.
  • Low start time for viewers: first frame in about 1-2 seconds, little rebuffering.
  • Scale: hundreds of hours of video uploaded every minute, and a read side far bigger than the write side.
  • Cost-aware: encoding and storage are the biggest bills. Most videos get few views.
  • Processing is eventually consistent: renditions appear over time.

Say this early: "This is two systems glued by storage. The upload pipeline is write-heavy, async and batch-like. The watch path is read-heavy and almost all CDN. I will design them separately." It shows you see the shape before the boxes.

Infra or product side? Your background is on the infra side (pipelines, throughput, hardware). This question is mostly infra-flavoured, which is good news for you, but interviewers still want the user, the API and the status flow first. Do not open with codec internals.
