Say the choice out loud (pattern: "I would pick X because A; the cost is B; if C becomes a problem I would switch to Y"):

  • "I would encode segment-parallel because a 2-hour video is then ready in minutes and a lost spot VM costs one segment; the cost is an orchestrator, fan-in logic and slight quality loss at chunk edges; if quality at the edges became a complaint, I would switch to longer chunks or whole-file two-pass encodes for premium content."
  • "I would send bytes straight to blob storage with presigned URLs because 240 Gbit/s through app servers is pure cost; the cost is less control over what arrives; if abuse grew, I would add a scan step before the file leaves the raw bucket."
  • "I would use a work queue (share group or SQS) for encode tasks because tasks take minutes and vary in length; the cost is losing Kafka's ordering and replay; if I needed replay for audits, I would keep lifecycle events on a normal Kafka topic beside it."

What breaks, and what we do

  • A worker dies mid-segment: the task's lease or Kafka offset is not committed, so it is redelivered; output keys are deterministic, so the rerun is harmless.
  • Orchestrator crashes: its state is in the DB or workflow engine; a new instance resumes from the last recorded step.
  • Kafka is down: uploads still complete (bytes are in the bucket); the outbox table holds the events and the relay publishes when Kafka is back. Videos are just ready later.
  • A region's storage is down: new uploads are routed to another region's bucket; reads come from the CDN and cross-region replicas.
  • Encoding backlog after a spike: prioritise. Paid or large creators first, low renditions first, 4K and AV1 later. Show "processing HD" to the creator instead of nothing.
  • Malicious files: crafted media files have exploited decoders before. Run ffmpeg as non-root, in a sandbox (gVisor or a tight seccomp profile), with CPU and time limits per task.
