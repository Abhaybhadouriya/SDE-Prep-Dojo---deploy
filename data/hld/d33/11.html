  • Places are static and read-heavy: precompute an index, cache, replicate. Friends are dynamic and write-heavy: in-memory with TTL, push over WebSocket.
  • A B-tree on lat and lng does not answer "near"; a spatial index turns 2-D into cells or a tree, then exact distance filters the candidates.
  • Geohash: each character = 5 bits = 32x smaller cell; length 5 is about 4.9 km, 6 about 1.2 x 0.6 km. Always query 9 cells.
  • Quadtree: split at ~100 points, ~2 GB for 200M businesses, minutes to build; rebuild + swap.
  • S2 for sharding by area (Hilbert order), H3 for aggregation (hexagons), R-tree/BKD for polygons.
  • Nearby friends: ~334k writes/s, ~13M deliveries/s; pub/sub is CPU-bound; at-most-once delivery is fine.
  • WebSocket servers are stateful: drain before removing.
