How the real companies do it

  • Twitter / X: started with inverted indexes in MySQL (20 TPS / 200 QPS in 2008, 12,000 QPS by 2010), replaced by Earlybird (Lucene-based, in-memory) and the Blender front-end (Twitter engineering, 2011); 2B queries/day at ~50 ms in 2012 (Earlybird paper, ICDE 2012); full archive of ~half a trillion tweets in 2014 (Building a complete Tweet index); ~1 s indexing latency in 2020 (Reducing search indexing latency to one second).
  • Meta: Unicorn, an in-memory, social-graph-aware index over trillions of edges serving billions of queries a day, became the backend for Facebook search (Unicorn, VLDB 2013).
  • Google: the 2003 "Web Search for a Planet" paper in IEEE Micro - document-sharded index, replica pools per shard, read-only serving (PDF).
  • Elastic: shard sizing guidance (10-50 GB) used by most companies running Elasticsearch/OpenSearch.
  • Interview write-ups: Hello Interview: FB Post Search (the numbers used on this page) and the system-design-primer Twitter solution (scatter-gather over a Lucene cluster).
