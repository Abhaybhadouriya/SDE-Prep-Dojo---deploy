Back-of-envelope estimation

These follow Hello Interview's FB Post Search assumptions (1B users, round a day to 100,000 seconds to make the maths easy). Say yours out loud.

  1. Posts. 1B users x 1 post/day = 1B posts/day. 1B / 100K s = 10K posts/s (peaks 3-4x during big events - Twitter saw 3-4x spikes in 2011).
  2. Likes. 10 likes per user per day = 10B/day = 100K likes/s. Likes, not posts, dominate writes.
  3. Searches. 1 search per user per day = 10K searches/s, bursty (10x on breaking news). Compare: Twitter served 18,000 QPS in 2011 and "hundreds of thousands" by 2020 (internal uses included).
  4. Raw posts stored. 1B/day x 365 x 10 years = 3.6 trillion posts. At ~1 KB each = 3.6 PB (lives in the post store, not in the index).
  5. Index size. Assume ~20 unique terms per post and 8 bytes per posting (a 64-bit post id): 1B x 20 x 8 B = 160 GB of postings per day, about 580 TB over 10 years before compression. Compression (delta + varint) cuts this several times, but it still does not fit in RAM. So: recent days in RAM, older years on SSD - exactly Twitter's split (real-time index of about a week in RAM; full index more than 100x larger on SSD).
  6. Index writes. 10K posts/s x 20 terms = 200K posting appends/s - spread over shards, easy for in-memory structures.
