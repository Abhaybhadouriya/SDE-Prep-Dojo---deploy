The story

Explain like I'm 5

Your class has a giant box of 10,000 drawings. Your friend asks: "Which drawings have a dinosaur?" Looking at every drawing takes all day. So the teacher keeps a special notebook. On the page called dinosaur she writes the numbers of every drawing with a dinosaur: 7, 52, 310... When a new drawing arrives, she looks at it once and adds its number to the right pages. Now any question takes one second: open the page, read the numbers.

The box got so big that one notebook is too heavy. So ten teachers each keep a notebook for their own pile of drawings. When you ask "dinosaur", all ten look at once, and you put their answers together.

The notebook is an inverted index: a map from each word (a term) to the list of posts that contain it (a posting list). The ten teachers are shards, and asking all of them at once then merging is scatter-gather. The rest of this page is about doing that for billions of posts a day, with likes changing all the time.

Two things make the real version hard. First, the box never stops filling: thousands of new drawings arrive every second, and a drawing should be findable within a minute, so the teachers must add to their notebooks while children are reading them. Second, children do not only ask "which drawings have a dinosaur?" - they ask "which dinosaur drawings are the newest?" or "which are the most liked?" Newest is easy if every teacher writes numbers in the order drawings arrive. Most liked is hard, because stickers (likes) are added to old drawings all day long, and rewriting every notebook page each time a sticker lands would keep the teachers busy forever.

So the design below has three jobs: an indexing path that adds each new post to the right shard within seconds, a query path that asks every shard in parallel and merges their answers, and a trick for like counts that updates the index rarely and fixes the order at query time.
