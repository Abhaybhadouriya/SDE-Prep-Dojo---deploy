Remember this

  • One row per (show, seat). Holding is one conditional UPDATE; check the row count; all-or-nothing for multiple seats.
  • The database is the judge. Seat maps and Redis are hints and accelerators.
  • Holds have a TTL; expired holds count as free inside the query (lazy), and a sweeper refreshes the map (active).
  • Hold, then charge, then confirm only if you still own the seat; otherwise refund automatically.
  • Webhooks and events are at-least-once: unique hold_id and booking_id make every step idempotent.
  • Hot on-sales are solved by shaping demand (waiting room, tokens, bot defence, best-available), not by autoscaling the database.
  • Kafka carries what happens after a booking (tickets, notifications, seat-map updates) via an outbox, keyed by show_id.
