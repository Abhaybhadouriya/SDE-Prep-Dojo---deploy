The story

Explain like I'm 5

You walk into a cinema and find a perfect seat. You want popcorn first, so you put your jacket on the seat. Everyone knows the jacket means "taken". If you come back with popcorn, you sit down: the seat is yours. If you forget and never come back, the usher removes the jacket after 10 minutes, and someone else can sit there.

Now imagine the most famous singer in the world plays one show, and the whole town runs to the door at the same second. The usher cannot let everyone in at once, or people get squashed and nobody finds a seat. So the usher gives everyone a number and lets in 100 people at a time. Everyone gets a fair turn, and the people inside can choose calmly.

The jacket is a seat hold: a temporary claim with a TTL (time to live), usually 5-10 minutes, long enough to pay. Sitting down is a confirmed booking. The usher who removes old jackets is a hold expiry process. The numbered line at the door is a virtual waiting room: a queue in front of the website that admits users at a rate the system can handle.

The hard part is not showing movie posters. It is one rule: a seat for a show is sold to at most one person, while thousands of people tap the same seat at the same time, payments fail and phones lose signal halfway.

Two flavours. BookMyShow (movies): thousands of shows a day, each with a few hundred seats, steady load with evening and weekend peaks. Ticketmaster (big concerts): a few huge on-sales where millions arrive at a known minute. Same data model, very different traffic. Interviewers often start with one and switch to the other. The sibling design Hotel / Airbnb Reservation uses the same hold idea for date ranges.

New words, once each (you will meet them below):

  • CP / AP = which side of CAP you pick when the network between copies breaks: CP refuses to answer rather than risk a wrong answer (seat ownership); AP keeps answering, maybe with slightly old data (browsing).
  • Webhook = an HTTP call the payment provider makes to our server when the payment finishes. It is an interrupt instead of polling: we do not keep asking "done yet?", they tell us.
  • PSP (payment service provider) = the company that actually moves the money (Razorpay, Stripe). UPI = India's instant bank-transfer system; the user approves the payment in a separate app, which is why holds must survive a few minutes away from our page.
  • SSE (server-sent events) = a one-way push stream from server to browser over plain HTTP. WebSocket = a two-way connection that stays open.
  • Lua script (in Redis) = a small program Redis runs as one atomic step, so "check 4 keys, then set 4 keys" cannot be interleaved with another client.
  • Idempotent = doing it twice has the same effect as once. TTL = time to live, after which a key or hold expires. Shard = one slice of a database split by key.

You might confuse:

  • Hold vs booking. A hold is a temporary claim on seats (10 minutes, no money yet). A booking is the paid, permanent result. Many holds never become bookings.
  • Optimistic vs pessimistic locking. Optimistic: do the work, and at write time check nothing changed (version number or WHERE status = ...); retry or fail if it did. Pessimistic: lock first (SELECT ... FOR UPDATE), then work, so others wait. Our conditional UPDATE is optimistic in spirit, with the row lock held only for one statement.
  • Waiting room vs rate limiter. A rate limiter rejects extra requests. A waiting room queues people in order and lets them in at a fixed rate, with a position number.
