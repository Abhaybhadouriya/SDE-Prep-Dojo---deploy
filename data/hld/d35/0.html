The story

Explain like I'm 5

The whole school votes for their favourite ice-cream flavour by dropping a slip into a box - thousands of slips every minute. The principal wants the top 3 flavours on the board at all times. One teacher counting every slip would be crushed. So each class counts its own slips on a tally sheet and sends up only its top flavours. A head teacher adds those small lists and writes the top 3 on the board. Sometimes the principal asks "top 3 this hour", so the teachers keep one tally sheet per hour and throw old sheets away. And when there are too many flavours to fit on a sheet, a clever teacher uses a tiny, magic sheet that is sometimes a little too high, but never too low.

That is the full design. The slips are events (a view, a sale, a click). The class tallies are partitioned counters. The "send up your top list" step is a local top-K merged into a global top-K. Tally sheets per hour are time windows. The magic sheet is a count-min sketch, a small probabilistic counter. A heavy hitter is simply an item that appears far more often than others.

This shows up under many names: "YouTube top K videos", "Amazon best sellers by category", "trending hashtags", "top-K popular Confluence pages", "most-hit API endpoints", even "which source IPs are flooding my router". The skeleton is always the same.

Reported in the wild: Amazon sales rank by category (system-design-primer's solved example), Atlassian P40/P50 "top-K popular Confluence pages/feeds" and "top-K excluding pages the user already viewed", Meta/Amazon "top K heavy hitters", and Razorpay "trending topics service". Grokking lists "top-N in a stream" as a very common question.
