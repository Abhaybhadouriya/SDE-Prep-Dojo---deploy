Remember this

You might confuse this with:

  • Geohash cell vs H3 cell: a geohash cell is a rectangle from cutting latitude and longitude in halves (sizes vary, 8 neighbours, some only touch at a corner); an H3 cell is a hexagon of near-equal area with 6 neighbours all at the same distance.
  • Distance vs ETA: distance is a straight line on the map; ETA is travel time on the road graph with traffic. Matching ranks by ETA.
  • Driver lock vs trip commit: the lock (CAS with TTL in Redis) is fast and temporary, only to stop two offers; the trip commit (conditional update in SQL) is the durable truth.

Remember this

  • Two systems: a hot, loss-tolerant location stream and a cold, strongly consistent trip store.
  • 1M drivers / 4 s = 250K writes/s, but only ~100 MB of live state: keep it in memory, shard by city.
  • Geo cells (geohash, H3, S2, quadtree); always search the neighbours.
  • Filter by distance, rank by road ETA.
  • No double booking: CAS AVAILABLE -> OFFERED with a TTL, then a conditional DB update + unique index as the fence.
  • Offers time out (about 15 s) and move to the next candidate.
  • Kafka: pings keyed by driver (acks=1), trip events keyed by trip (acks=all, outbox, idempotent consumers).
  • Hot cells at stadiums and airports: finer cells, batching, per-cell caches, surge, pre-scaling.
