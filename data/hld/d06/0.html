The story

Explain like I'm 5

Imagine a town drawn on a giant board full of little squares, like a chessboard. Every taxi has a walkie-talkie. Every few seconds each taxi says, "I am in square C4 and I am free!" A helper moves that taxi's toy car to square C4 on the board. When Asha wants a ride, she says, "I am in square D5." The helper looks at D5 and the squares touching it, finds the free toy cars there, and asks, "Which one can drive to Asha fastest on real roads?" The helper calls that taxi: "Do you want Asha's ride?" If the driver says yes, the helper quickly puts a red sticker on that toy car, so nobody else can give it a second ride. If the driver says no, or does not answer in 15 seconds, the helper tries the next car. Then Asha watches the toy car move towards her on the board.

In real words: the walkie-talkie calls are location updates (GPS pings) from the driver app, every few seconds. The board of squares is a geospatial index (a "geo index"): a data structure that splits the map into cells so we can find "who is near this point" without checking every driver. The helper is the matching (dispatch) service. The red sticker is an atomic lock on the driver's status, so two riders can never get the same car. "Watching the car move" is live tracking over a long-lived connection.

The hard parts are not the screens. They are (1) a firehose of hundreds of thousands of location writes per second, (2) fast "who is near me?" queries over moving points, (3) never double-booking a driver, and (4) handling crowds, like everyone leaving a stadium at the same moment.

From your world

Driver pings are periodic hellos with state, like OSPF hellos that also carry a tiny LSA: if a driver stops pinging for, say, 30 seconds, it is declared "dead" and removed, exactly like a dead interval expiring. The geo index is a FIB built from a fast-changing RIB: always in memory, rebuilt from the latest updates, never read from disk on the hot path. The "red sticker" is an atomic test-and-set (compare-and-swap) on one word of shared state, the same primitive you use for a spinlock. Where the analogy breaks: GPS is noisy (tens of metres), and phones on mobile networks drop out all the time, so "dead" must be judged softly.

New words, once each: QPS = requests per second. ETA = estimated time of arrival. Geohash, quadtree, S2, H3 = four ways to cut the map into cells (explained below). Idempotent = doing it twice has the same effect as once. All are in the glossary.
