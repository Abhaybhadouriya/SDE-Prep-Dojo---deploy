Requirements

There are two apps (rider and driver) and one marketplace in the middle. Agree the scope first; this question can grow forever (pooling, food delivery, scheduling).

Functional (what it does)

  • Fare estimate: rider enters pickup and drop, sees price and pickup ETA.
  • Request a ride and get matched to a nearby driver.
  • Driver accepts or declines an offer within a time limit.
  • Live tracking: rider sees the car approach and the trip progress.
  • Trip lifecycle: start, end, fare, payment, receipt.
  • Nearby cars on the map before requesting.
  • Ratings after the trip (low priority).

Non-functional (how well)

  • Scale (assumption): 1M drivers online at peak, 20M trips/day worldwide.
  • Matching latency: a driver offer within a few seconds of the request (goal: under ~5 s server side, assumption).
  • Location freshness: positions at most a few seconds old (drivers ping about every 4 s, as reported for Uber).
  • Strong consistency where money and assignment live: one driver, one trip; one trip, one charge.
  • Eventual consistency is fine for the cars-on-map view and analytics.
  • High availability: a dispatch outage strands people on the street, so design for 99.99% and regional failover.

Out of scope unless asked: surge pricing internals (see Surge Pricing & ETA), map routing itself (see Google Maps), shared/pool rides, food delivery (see Food Delivery), payments internals (see Payment System).

Say this early: "This is two systems glued together: a high-volume, loss-tolerant location stream kept in memory, and a low-volume, strongly consistent trip and assignment system. I will design them separately." That split is the key insight interviewers look for.
