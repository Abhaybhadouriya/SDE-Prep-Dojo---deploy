How real companies do it

  • Uber H3: a hexagonal grid with 16 resolutions where each finer level is about 1/7 the area; hexagons were chosen because all neighbours' centres are the same distance away. Uber uses it for surge pricing and marketplace analytics, and open-sourced it in 2018.
  • Uber DISCO and Ringpop (2015 talk, summarised by HighScalability, secondary): dispatch matched supply and demand using Google S2 cells, sharded by cell with Ringpop, a gossip (SWIM) membership plus consistent hash ring library. Ringpop's first user was the geospatial service holding live driver locations. Drivers sent GPS about every 4 seconds.
  • Uber Schemaless: a MySQL-based datastore Uber built for trip data; background reading on why "sharded SQL with a simple model" fit their trip store.
  • Uber and Kafka: Uber reports trillions of messages and multiple petabytes per day on Kafka, built a Consumer Proxy to push messages to many language runtimes, and published the retry-topic plus dead-letter-queue pattern for reliable reprocessing.
  • Uber real-time push platform: long-lived streaming connections from servers to apps, with priorities and TTLs on messages, for offers and trip updates.

Related designs: Surge Pricing & ETA, Google Maps, Food Delivery, Proximity Service, Payment System, Notification System, Chat App (same long-lived connection problem). Patterns: Geo & Proximity Queries, Dealing with Contention, Real-time Updates.
