Quick quiz

A metric has labels service (50 values), status (5) and pod (100). Max series?

  1. 155
  2. 5,000
  3. 25,000
  4. 250,000
Labels multiply: 50 x 5 x 100 = 25,000. 155 adds them instead of multiplying, the classic mistake. 5,000 forgets one label. 250,000 multiplies by an extra 10 that is not there.

What does up == 0 tell you in a pull system?

  1. The target served zero requests
  2. The last scrape of that target failed
  3. Alertmanager could not send a page
Prometheus sets up per target after each scrape: 1 for success, 0 for failure, a free liveness signal. Zero traffic would show in a request counter, not in up. Alertmanager health has its own metrics.

Your SLO is 99.9% over 30 days. What is the error budget?

  1. 0.1% of requests in 30 days
  2. 99.9% of requests in 30 days
  3. 0.1% of requests in every hour
  4. The time left before the SLA is breached
The budget is what you may fail: 0.1% of requests over the 30-day window. 99.9% is the target itself. The budget is measured over the whole window, not per hour, which is why burn rate exists. The SLA is a separate customer contract.

Why are histograms preferred over summaries for p99 across many pods?

  1. They use fewer bytes per sample
  2. Summaries cannot express a p99 at all
  3. Bucket counts add up across pods
  4. Grafana only plots histogram types
Bucket counters from every pod can be summed and then turned into one p99; percentiles from different pods cannot be averaged. Histograms are usually larger, not smaller. Summaries can report p99, just per pod. Grafana plots both.

Which should page someone at 3 AM?

  1. CPU at 85% on one node
  2. One pod restarted once
  3. Checkout SLO burning 14.4x
  4. Disk at 60% on a broker
Page on user-facing symptoms: a fast SLO burn means users are failing now. High CPU, a single restart or a 60% disk are causes or noise for dashboards and tickets, not for waking someone.

What makes delta-of-delta timestamp compression so effective?

  1. Scrapes are regular, so most values are 0
  2. Timestamps are stored as 16-bit integers
  3. Float values rarely change between scrapes
A regular interval makes the second difference 0, which costs about 1 bit. Timestamps are 64-bit milliseconds before encoding. Slowly changing values are what XOR compression exploits, which is a different trick for the values, not the timestamps.

The long-term store is down for 3 hours. What happens to remote write?

  1. Prometheus drops samples at once and moves on
  2. Prometheus stops scraping until the store is back
  3. Retries from the WAL; data past ~2 h may be lost
  4. Grafana queries fail on every Prometheus server
Remote write retries with backoff from the WAL, but the WAL only covers about 2 hours, so the oldest unsent samples can be lost for the long-term store. It does not drop at once. Scraping and local storage keep working. Local Grafana queries still work; only global ones are affected.

What is a dead-man's switch alert?

  1. An alert that fires when nobody acks a page
  2. An always-firing alert checked from outside
  3. A silence rule for maintenance windows
It fires all the time on purpose; an outside service pages if it stops arriving, which proves the alerting pipeline is alive. Escalating unacked pages is the paging tool's job. Silences are the opposite: they hide alerts.

10M series scraped every 15 s is about how many samples per second?

  1. ~67k
  2. ~670k
  3. ~6.7M
  4. ~150M
10,000,000 / 15 ≈ 667,000 samples per second. 67k and 6.7M are off by a factor of 10. 150M multiplies by 15 instead of dividing.
