The story

Explain like I'm 5

A big hotel has many doors: your room, the pool, the gym. At the front desk you show your passport once. The desk gives you a key card. Now every door just checks the card - nobody asks for your passport again. The card stops working after a few days. If you lose it, the desk can cancel it. And when you visit the hotel's sister hotel next door, their desk calls your desk, "Is this guest real?", and gives you a card too - without asking for your passport again.

The passport check is authentication (proving who you are: password, passkey, a code). The key card is a session or token. Doors checking the card are token validation on every request. "Which doors may this card open?" is authorisation. Cancelling a lost card is revocation. The sister hotel trusting your desk is single sign-on (SSO), done with OpenID Connect or SAML.

New words, once each: Authentication answers "who are you?"; authorisation answers "what may you do?". SSO (single sign-on) means one login works for many apps. An identity provider (IdP) is the service that does the login and vouches for the user. A token is a string the client shows on every request instead of the password. A JWT is a signed token anyone can check with a public key. OAuth 2.0 and OIDC are the standard protocols for handing out tokens (explained properly in the "Words you need" box at the start of the API section). MFA is multi-factor authentication: a second proof such as a code or a passkey. mTLS is TLS where both sides show certificates. TTL is an automatic expiry. The rest are in the glossary.

Who asks this? Our gap analysis found "design an authentication system" in AlgoMaster's problem list, and the concepts (OAuth 2.0, OIDC, SSO, TLS/mTLS) in Karan Pratap Singh's free system design book. We found no self-reported target-company interview of this exact title. But identity is a product at Microsoft (Entra ID), Google (Google accounts) and Amazon (Cognito, IAM), and "how does your design authenticate users?" is a follow-up in almost every design on this site. Expect it as a full round in security-heavy teams and as a 10-minute deep dive everywhere else.
