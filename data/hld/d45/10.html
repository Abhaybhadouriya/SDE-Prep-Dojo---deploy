How real companies do it

  • Reddit (2017 talk by Neil Williams): the r2 Python monolith (started 2008), Node.js frontends, new Python backend services over Thrift or HTTP, a CDN that routes to different stacks. Thing model in Postgres with async read replicas and memcached; Cassandra for newer, write-heavy features and the persisted listing index; job queues for votes and comment trees. Scale then: 320M MAU, 1.1M communities, 1M posts, 5M comments and 75M votes per day (slides, talk).
  • Reddit (2013 lessons): 2B page views/month on 240 servers in 2012, 2 TB in Postgres; moving databases to SSDs cut database servers from 12 to 1; RabbitMQ for votes, comments, thumbnails and precomputed queries; queue length as an early warning; avoid service-oriented architecture until the team is medium-sized (High Scalability).
  • Reddit (today, summarised by ByteByteGo): moving from the Python monolith to Go microservices, GraphQL Federation since 2021 with Go subgraphs from 2022, Postgres + memcached, Cassandra, Debezium CDC (change data capture: it tails the database's write-ahead log and publishes each committed row change to Kafka, like a journal replay in a filesystem), RabbitMQ and Kafka, on AWS and Kubernetes (ByteByteGo).
  • Quora: critical data (questions, answers, upvotes, comments) in MySQL; first vertical partitioning with the table-to-partition map in ZooKeeper, then in-house horizontal sharding of fewer than 10 big tables, range-based, with dark reads and writes before cut-over (systemdesign.one, Quastor).
