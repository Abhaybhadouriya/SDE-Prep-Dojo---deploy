Requirements

Functional

  • Users join communities (subreddits on Reddit, topics on Quora).
  • Create a post (text, link or image) in a community. On Quora: ask a question, write an answer.
  • Upvote or downvote a post or a comment. A user has at most one vote per item and can change or remove it.
  • View a community listing sorted by hot, new, top (hour, day, week, all time) and controversial.
  • View a post with its threaded comments sorted by best, top or new; "load more" for big threads.
  • A home feed: a merge of the communities you joined (Quora: topics and people you follow).
  • Out of scope unless asked: search (see Post Search), chat, ads, moderation tooling details, media transcoding.

Non-functional

  • Read heavy. Far more page views than votes, and far more votes than posts. Reddit's 2017 numbers: about 1M posts, 5M comments and 75M votes per day for 320M monthly users (QCon SF 2017 slides).
  • Listing reads fast: p99 under ~200 ms for a page of 25 posts (p99 = 99% of requests are faster than this; the slowest 1% are what users complain about). Our SLO (service level objective: the target we alert on) is that p99 over 5 minutes.
  • Votes feel instant to the voter (the arrow turns orange right away), but the global score and rank may lag by seconds. This is eventual consistency (all copies agree after a short delay), and it is fine here.
  • No double counting. Retries and double clicks must not add a second vote.
  • Highly available. Reading must work even if the vote pipeline is behind. Prefer AP (available and partition tolerant) for counts; keep money (ads, premium) in a strongly consistent relational store.
  • Durable posts and comments (never lost once acknowledged).

Say early: "Votes are not money. I will accept a few seconds of lag in scores to keep the write path cheap, but I will never count the same user twice." That one sentence frames every later decision.
