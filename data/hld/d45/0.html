The story

Explain like I'm 5

Your class has a big notice board. Anyone can pin a drawing on it. Every child gets one sticker per drawing: a happy sticker or a sad sticker. The teacher wants the best drawings at the top. But if she only counts stickers, a drawing from last month with lots of old stickers stays on top forever. So she makes a rule: new drawings get a little head start, and the head start grows every hour. Now fresh, liked drawings rise, and old ones slowly sink. Kids also write notes under drawings, and other kids reply to the notes, so the notes grow like the branches of a tree.

That is Reddit. The board is a subreddit (a community, such as r/india). A drawing is a post (Reddit calls it a link). Stickers are upvotes and downvotes. The teacher's rule is the hot ranking formula. The notes and replies are a comment tree. Quora is the same machine with different words: a question instead of a post, answers instead of top-level comments, topics instead of subreddits, and much more machine learning in the ranking.

Why do interviewers like it? It packs four classic problems into one product: idempotent counting (one vote per person), ranking with time decay, storing trees in a database, and caching a read-heavy site where every write invalidates many cached lists. Reddit's own loops are reported to include voting at scale, comment trees, home-feed generation and caching (techinterview.org Reddit guide), and "Design Reddit" and "Design Quora" are case studies in the Grokking courses (Design Gurus volume II, and Educative's modern course).
