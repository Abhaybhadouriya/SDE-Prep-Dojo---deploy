How real companies do it

  • LinkedIn (where Kafka was born). The 2011 paper "Kafka: a Distributed Messaging System for Log Processing" introduced the design. Jay Kreps's 2013 essay "The Log: What every software engineer should know about real-time data's unifying abstraction" explains why the log is the core idea. By 2019 LinkedIn reported 7 trillion messages a day on its own Kafka release branch.
  • Slack put Kafka in front of Redis for its job queue after Redis ran out of memory during an incident; their post reports 1.4 billion jobs a day and 33,000 per second at peak, with a Go HTTP-to-Kafka service and a relay that writes from Kafka to Redis.
  • Walmart described processing trillions of Kafka messages a day with 25,000+ consumers, and building a messaging proxy to stop rebalancing from causing lag (summarised by ByteByteGo).
  • Uber drove tiered storage (KIP-405) and published the retry-topic + DLQ pattern for reliable reprocessing.
  • AWS Builders' Library on queue backlogs: why a queue that falls far behind can take a very long time to recover, and how to shed or prioritise work.
