The story

Explain like I'm 5

A shop has a big board that shows the price of every sweet, and the prices change every second. Many children ask the shopkeeper: "Ring my bell when the lollipop gets 5 coins cheaper than yesterday". The silly way: every second, the shopkeeper reads every child's note from a big book in the back room. He is always running to the back room and never keeps up. The clever way: he keeps the notes for each sweet on a small board right next to that sweet's price, sorted from "smallest change" to "biggest change". When the lollipop price moves, he looks only at the lollipop board, rings the bells of the children whose number was reached, and throws those notes away so no bell rings twice. The big book in the back room is only for when his small boards get knocked over.

That is the design. The price board is the market data feed. The small sorted boards next to each price are in-memory threshold indexes per symbol, held by an evaluator service. The big book in the back room is the subscription database, used for durability and recovery, never on the hot path. Ringing the bell is the notification service. Throwing the note away is fire-once: the subscription is removed when it triggers.

Say this early: "The hot path is ticks times rules. I will never query a database per tick. Rules live in memory, partitioned by symbol, sorted by threshold, so each tick touches only the rules it fires. The database is the durable copy for rebuilds." In one 2026-05 Uber round, the interviewer's main catch was exactly the DB-per-tick flaw.

From your world

This is a packet classifier. Ticks are packets, subscriptions are rules (ACLs), and a router does not consult a disk for every packet: it compiles rules into a fast in-memory structure (TCAM, a trie) keyed by the fields that matter. Partitioning by symbol is like RSS hashing flows to CPU cores so each core owns its flows and needs no locks.

New words, once each. A symbol (ticker) names a stock, like AAPL or INFY. A tick is one price update. Closing price is the last price of the previous trading day. A rolling window ("within the last hour") always ends now and slides forward. A partition is one ordered slice of a Kafka topic. Consumer lag is how far a consumer is behind the newest message. Idempotent means safe to repeat. Hot partition means one key gets far more load than others. More in the glossary.
