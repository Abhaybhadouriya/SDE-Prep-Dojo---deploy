Where Kafka, Kubernetes and Docker fit

Concrete settings. Partition key symbol, with ticks and alert-changes co-partitioned (same key, same partition count) so one evaluator owns both streams for a symbol. Consumer-group sizing: 64 partitions allow up to 64 evaluator pods; hot symbols are spread by the hash, not by hand. Notifiers are a Deployment that KEDA scales on alerts-fired lag, pre-scaled before market open.

ToolRole hereConcrete setup
KafkaThe spineticks and alert-changes keyed by symbol, same partition count (co-partitioned) so one consumer owns both for a symbol; alerts-fired keyed by user. Consumer group for evaluators; static membership to avoid needless rebalances on restart. See Consumer Groups and Delivery Semantics.
KubernetesRuns feed handlers, evaluators, APIs, notifiersEvaluators as a StatefulSet (stable names, local disk for RocksDB checkpoints) or a Flink job on the Flink operator; notifiers as a Deployment scaled on alerts-fired lag with KEDA, pre-scaled before market open with a CronJob. See StatefulSets & Operators.
DockerPackagingOne image per service; local Compose stack with Kafka and a fake feed replaying a recorded trading day for tests. See Compose.

Build vs buy for the evaluator: a hand-written consumer with sorted books is short and fast. Flink or Kafka Streams give keyed state, checkpoints and windowing for free; Kafka Streams has built-in sliding and hopping windows with a grace period for late records. Either is a good answer if you can say what the framework does for you.
