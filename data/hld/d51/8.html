What breaks first, and what you do

  • Reconnect storm (region failover, office network blip, gateway rollout): millions reconnect at once. Jittered exponential backoff, edge caches for boot data, admission control on gateways, and "resume from event id" instead of full boot.
  • Channel server dies: ring manager reassigns in seconds; new owner rebuilds recent history from the store; gateways re-subscribe. Messages were persisted before ack, so none are lost.
  • Region outage: gateways are regional; drain users to the nearest healthy region (Slack describes such a draining mechanism). Channel servers and data are in a primary region with replicas; plan a DR failover (Multi-region & DR).
  • Downstream slow (search, notifications): Kafka absorbs it; lag grows; chat keeps working. Alert on lag in seconds.
  • Overload: shed in priority order - typing, presence, prefetch, then non-critical APIs. Teams rate-limited non-critical APIs and disabled some prefetching (calendar prefetch on mobile, 80% fewer requests) during the 2020 surge. Circuit breakers, bulkheads and timeouts everywhere (Resilience patterns).
Pros of the channel-server design
  • Single writer per channel gives simple, total order.
  • One copy per gateway keeps huge channels affordable.
  • Hot recent history lives in memory: most reads skip the DB.
  • Small blast radius per failed host.
Cons
  • Stateful tier: ring management, warm-up, split-brain fencing.
  • A single very hot channel is limited by one server.
  • Cross-region fan-out adds latency for far-away gateways.
  • More moving parts than a "Redis pub/sub + stateless servers" design.
