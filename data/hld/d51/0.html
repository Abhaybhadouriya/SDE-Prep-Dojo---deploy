The story

Explain like I'm 5

Think of a big school. The school has many rooms with notice boards: the "Art room" board, the "Football" board, the "Class 3B" board. You can only see boards for rooms you joined. When the teacher pins a note on the Football board, a helper in each building of the school runs to every child in that building who joined Football and says "new note!". There is one helper per building, not one per child, so the teacher only has to tell a few helpers. On the main door there is a big wall of name cards with a green sticker next to every child who is in school today. Children only look at the stickers of friends they can see on their screen, so nobody has to read the whole wall. And every child keeps a bookmark on each board: "I read up to note 41." If the board now has note 45, the child sees a little "4" bubble.

That is the whole design. The school is a workspace (a company or team, also called a tenant). The notice boards are channels; a direct message (DM) is just a tiny channel with two members. The helpers in each building are gateway servers: machines in each region that hold the users' long-lived WebSocket connections. The person who owns the Football board is a channel server: one machine responsible for that channel, found by hashing the channel id. The green stickers are presence. The bookmark is a read cursor, and the bubble is the unread count.

How is this different from WhatsApp? WhatsApp is mostly 1:1 and small groups, phone-first, and the server forgets messages once delivered. Team chat is the opposite: channels are large (thousands to hundreds of thousands of members), history is kept forever and must be searchable, the company (not the user) owns the data and needs retention and legal hold, and people sit on laptops all day so presence and typing traffic is huge.

New words, once each: QPS is requests per second. Fan-out is the number of copies one event turns into. p99 means "99 out of 100 are faster than this". Tenant is one paying customer (a company) whose data must be kept apart from others. All of these are in the glossary.
