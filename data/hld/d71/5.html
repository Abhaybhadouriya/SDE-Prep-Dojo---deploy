Architecture: from "every Monday" to a doorbell

Click Next or Play. The new pieces are the Subscription service and its Scheduler; everything else is an existing e-commerce or payments service we call.

  1. Subscribe. The app calls the Subscription service with the plan, start date, address and slot. We store the subscription as PENDING_AUTH, with the price and timezone copied from the plan.
  2. Authorise once. We ask the gateway to register a mandate (card e-mandate, UPI AutoPay or e-NACH). The customer is redirected, completes bank 2FA (OTP, 3-D Secure or UPI PIN), and comes back. The truth arrives by a signed webhook (mandate.activated), which flips the subscription to ACTIVE and sets next_cycle_at. This is the "redirect, bank 2FA, callback" flow in the Razorpay question. Webhooks can repeat; handling is idempotent by event id.
  3. Find what is due. The scheduler repeatedly claims due subscriptions: next_cycle_at <= now() on an indexed column (or a time-bucket table, see deep dive 2), using FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED so several scheduler instances share the work without collisions.
  4. Hand off to a queue. For each claimed subscription it inserts the cycle row (primary key = subscription and cycle number), advances next_cycle_at, and publishes cycle.due via an outbox, all in one transaction. Cycle workers consume at a controlled rate.
  5. Create the order. The worker re-reads the subscription (paused? skipped? cancelled?), applies any schedule changes, then calls the existing Order service with idempotency key = sub:cycle. Order reserves inventory and books the delivery slot as for any order.
  6. Charge. The worker asks the Payment service to charge the mandate with idempotency key sub:cycle:attempt. For Indian e-mandates the pre-debit notice went out at least 24 h earlier (the cycle's first step), so the charge is just the execution.
  7. Notify, retry, recover. Notifications send the pre-debit notice, "your order is on the way" and receipts. A failed charge moves the cycle to FAILED and the dunning component schedules retries; after the last one, the subscription goes to PAST_DUE, then suspends or cancels according to the merchant's setting.

The heartbeat of the system, running: every cycle goes round the same loop.
