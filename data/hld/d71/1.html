Requirements

First ask: "Are we creating physical orders (milk, groceries), digital charges (a plan on a payments platform), or both?" Auto-orders need inventory, delivery slots and a cut-off time; pure charges need mandates and invoices. The core (plans, schedule, cycles, idempotency, dunning) is shared.

Functional

  1. Plans A merchant defines plans: product or service, quantity, frequency (daily, weekly on chosen days, monthly on a day), price per cycle, optional trial, total cycles or end date. The same product can have several plans at different prices (Razorpay's weekly vs monthly).
  2. Subscribe A customer picks a plan, start date, delivery address and slot (for goods), and authorises payment once (mandate or card-on-file with bank 2FA).
  3. Automatic cycles On each due date the system creates the order and/or charge without the customer, sends a pre-debit or "your order is coming" notice before, and a receipt after.
  4. Manage Pause (from-to), resume, skip one cycle, change quantity, change plan (upgrade or downgrade), change address or payment method, cancel. Show upcoming cycles and history.
  5. Failures Payment failure: retry on a schedule, notify, then suspend or cancel. Stock failure: substitute, partially fulfil or skip that cycle, with a refund if charged.
  6. Merchant view Active subscribers, revenue per plan, failed payments, churn.

Non-functional (with numbers)

CorrectnessExactly one order and at most one successful charge per subscription per cycleA double charge is a support ticket and a regulatory problem; a missed milk order is an angry customer
TimelinessEvery due cycle processed before its cut-off (for example orders for Monday created by Sunday 23:00)Warehouses pick from the order list overnight
Compliance (India)Pre-debit notification at least 24 h before an auto-debit; AFA again for each debit above Rs 15,000RBI e-mandate rules, as summarised in Stripe's India recurring payments docs
DurabilityNo subscription or cycle state lost; full audit historyMoney and legal records
AvailabilitySubscription management 99.9%; the scheduler can lag minutes but must catch upCycles are batch-like, with slack before the cut-off
ConsistencyStrong within one subscription (state changes are serialised); eventual across servicesPause and the scheduler must not race and create an order anyway

Write down the cut-off rule early: "changes made before the cut-off (say 20:00 the day before) apply to the next cycle; after it, they apply to the one after." It removes half the race conditions the interviewer will throw at you.
