The story

Explain like I'm 5

The milkman has a notebook. On each page he writes one family's promise: "Sharma family, 2 milk packets, every Monday, until June." Every Sunday night he opens the notebook, finds every page that says Monday, and puts the bottles in his crate. He makes a tick next to this Monday on each page, so even if he opens the notebook twice he never packs the same family twice. Before he goes, he sends a little note: "Tomorrow I bring 2 packets, that is 60 rupees." If the family's money box is empty on Monday, he does not give up at once: he tries again on Tuesday and Thursday, and only then asks the family to fix it. If the family says "we are on holiday next week", he writes "skip" on that Monday. If they say "stop", he crosses out the page, but he keeps it so he can remember what they paid.

That is the whole design. The notebook page is a subscription; the promise itself (what, how often, at what price) comes from a plan. Sunday night is the scheduler finding due cycles. The tick is an idempotency key per cycle: one order or charge per (subscription, cycle), however many times the job runs. The note is the pre-debit notification that Indian rules require before an automatic charge. The money box tries are retries and dunning. "Skip" and "stop" are lifecycle changes.

This is a favourite HLD round because it looks easy and is full of edges. Amazon asked an SDE2 candidate to design recurring subscription ordering (the milk example) and the interviewer was strict about missing entity attributes (2026-08). Razorpay asked an SSE (selected) to build recurring subscriptions with weekly and monthly plans at different prices on top of its card flow, which includes a redirect, bank 2FA and a callback (2024-12). PhonePe asked an SDE-2 to add subscriptions to an existing e-commerce platform: which services to reuse, schema, API contracts and failure cases (2024-06). Walmart asked a senior candidate about grocery delivery with and without subscriptions, including why a message queue is needed (2025-05). Links are in the interview section.

Say this early: "A subscription is a schedule plus a payment authority. My job is to turn each due cycle into exactly one order and one charge, on time, and to handle the cycle when payment or stock fails. I will reuse the existing catalogue, order, payment and notification services and build only the subscription service and its scheduler."

From your world

The scheduler is cron plus a timer wheel: the kernel does not scan every timer each tick; it keeps timers in buckets by expiry time and only touches the bucket that is due. Our "time-bucketed due table" is the same thing on disk. The per-cycle idempotency key is like a TCP sequence number: a retransmitted segment with a number the receiver has already acknowledged is dropped, not applied twice.

New words, once each: a mandate is the customer's standing permission for a merchant to debit them repeatedly (a card e-mandate, UPI AutoPay, or a bank e-NACH mandate). Card-on-file or a token is a saved card stored by the payment network or gateway, not by us. AFA (additional factor of authentication) is the bank OTP or 3-D Secure step. Dunning is the process of retrying failed payments and reminding the customer. A webhook is an HTTP callback the payment gateway sends us when something happens. A saga is a multi-step process with compensating steps when one fails.
