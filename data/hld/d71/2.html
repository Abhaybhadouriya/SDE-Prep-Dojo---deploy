Back-of-envelope estimation

Method from Back-of-Envelope Estimation. Numbers are our assumptions; the shape matters more than the digits.

5Mactive subscriptions
~170/sorder creation in the Sunday-night window
~170/sorders in the nightly window
~5 GBcycle rows per year
  1. Subscriptions Assume 5M active: 3M grocery (weekly, often on Monday), 2M digital (monthly, mostly on the 1st).
  2. Peak cycle day Weekly milk is clustered: assume 40% of 3M on Monday = 1.2M orders for one day. Monthly plans billed on the 1st: assume 30% of 2M = 600K charges. These two loads do not land in the same window. Grocery orders are created on Sunday 20:00-22:00 (below). Card charges must be requested about 26 hours before the debit (the 24 h pre-debit notice plus a buffer, see below), so those 600K requests go out on the 30th, spread over the day: 600K / 86,400 s = about 7 per second, tens per second at most if bunched. The real collision is the scheduler's 26-hour look-ahead for card charges running at the same time as the grocery cut-off, so give the two job types separate queues and rate limits.
  3. Order creation window Monday orders are created between the cut-off (Sunday 20:00) and Sunday 22:00 for picking. 1.2M in 2 hours = 1.2M / 7,200 s = about 170 orders per second, each calling inventory and order services. Comfortable, as long as the scheduler does not dump 1.2M messages at once and melt the inventory service; rate-limit the dispatch.
  4. Charges with pre-debit For Indian cards, the pre-debit notice must go out 24 h before the debit; Stripe waits 26 h after the request before charging to leave a buffer. So the "1st of the month" charge is requested on the 30th. 600K notices over a few hours = tens per second. Easy, but it means the scheduler looks 26+ hours ahead, not at "now".
  5. Retries Assume 5-10% of recurring charges fail the first time (cards expired, insufficient funds; a typical industry range, ask). 600K x 8% = 48K failures entering dunning. Stripe's default Smart Retries policy is up to 8 retries within 2 weeks, so at most about 400K retry attempts spread over 2 weeks.
  6. Storage A cycle row is about 300 bytes (ids, dates, state, amounts, order and payment references, attempt count). 5M subscriptions x about 35 cycles a year (weekly and monthly mixed) = 175M rows = about 50 GB a year before indexes; partition by month and archive old partitions. Subscriptions themselves: 5M x 1 KB = 5 GB.
  7. Reads "My subscriptions" and "upcoming deliveries" pages: 5M users x 2 views a week = about 17 per second average. Read load is trivial; correctness is the problem.

What the numbers tell you: this is not a scale problem, it is a correctness and timing problem. One relational database (sharded by subscription_id if needed) handles it. Spend your time on idempotency, state machines and failure handling, which is what the interviewers in these posts pushed on.
