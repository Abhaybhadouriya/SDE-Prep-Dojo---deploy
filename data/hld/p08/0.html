The story

Explain like I'm 5

Your teacher has a notice about the school trip. She can do it in two ways. Way one: early in the morning she makes a photocopy for every child and puts one in each school bag. When you open your bag, the notice is already there - super quick for you, but a lot of copying for her. Way two: she pins one notice on the board. Every child walks to the board and reads it when they come in. No copying for her, but every child has to go and look, and if you have ten teachers, you have to visit ten boards.

Now imagine the head of the whole country's schools sends a notice to ten million children. Photocopying ten million notices takes forever! So for her, the board is better. For your class teacher with 30 children, the photocopies are fine. Smart schools do both: copies from your own teacher, plus a quick look at the big board for the famous people.

Putting copies in every bag is fan-out on write, also called push: when someone posts, you copy (a pointer to) the post into every follower's personal list right away. Pinning it on the board is fan-out on read, or pull: you store the post once, and each reader collects posts from everyone they follow at read time. "Fan-out" just means one input spreading out to many outputs, like the ribs of a hand fan. Doing both is the hybrid.
