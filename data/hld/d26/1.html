Requirements

Functional

  • Place an order: symbol, side (buy/sell), type (limit = "at this price or better"; market = "at any price now"), quantity, and time-in-force (DAY; IOC = immediate-or-cancel, fill what you can now and cancel the rest; FOK = fill-or-kill, all now or nothing).
  • Cancel or modify a resting order.
  • Match orders by price-time priority and produce trades (fills), full or partial.
  • Execution reports back to the member (broker) for every state change: accepted, partially filled, filled, cancelled, rejected.
  • Market data to everyone: every book change (level 2 data), every trade, and snapshots.
  • Pre-trade risk: reject orders a member cannot afford or that break limits (price bands, max quantity).
  • Downstream: trades go to clearing and settlement, surveillance and reporting.

Non-functional

PropertyTargetWhy
Fairness / determinismOne total order of events per symbol; same input always gives the same outputRegulators and members must be able to verify who traded first
LatencyMicroseconds inside the core is the goal of modern venues (rule of thumb); tight, predictable tail latencyMembers compete on speed; jitter is unfair
DurabilityNo acknowledged order or trade is ever lostA lost trade is a legal problem
AvailabilityFail over in seconds during market hoursDowntime halts a country's market
ThroughputHundreds of thousands of messages/s at the opening peak (our assumption, below)Bursts at open, close and news
AuditabilityReplay any moment of any dayDisputes and surveillance

Say early: "The core is a deterministic, single-threaded, in-memory state machine per symbol shard, fed by a sequencer that gives every input a number. Durability comes from the sequenced journal, not from a database on the hot path." That sentence tells the interviewer you know the real architecture (LMAX-style).
