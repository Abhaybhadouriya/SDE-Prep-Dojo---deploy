Architecture, step by step

Click through. The top half is the market-data path; the bottom half is the trading path. GTT and risk sit between them because they read prices and write orders.

  1. Ingest. Feed handlers sit on servers with leased lines to the exchange. They receive the A and B multicast feeds, drop duplicates by sequence number, request retransmits on gaps, and turn exchange formats into one internal tick format with integer prices.
  2. Bus and conflation. Normalised ticks go on a bus keyed by instrument (Kafka, or an in-memory pub/sub for the lowest latency). Conflators keep only the latest tick per instrument and publish a snapshot every 500 ms (or 250 ms to have margin). The whole conflated market is about 10,000 updates/s.
  3. Fan-out at the edge. Every WebSocket gateway receives the full conflated feed and keeps a map instrument -> set of local connections. Every 500 ms it builds one binary frame per connection with only that user's instruments. On subscribe, it first sends the snapshot from last_quote so the screen is never blank.
  4. Order in. The user taps Buy. The app sends client_order_id (a UUID made on the phone). The order service checks the schema, market hours, lot size and tick size, and rejects a repeated client_order_id by returning the earlier order.
  5. Pre-trade risk. The risk management system (RMS) holds every user's margin in memory. It blocks price x quantity (plus charges) for a buy, or checks holdings for a sell. The order row (status PENDING) and the hold are journaled durably before we go on. This step is where the 10 ms budget is mostly spent.
  6. Route to exchange. The exchange adapter for that exchange and segment keeps a long-lived session over a leased line and sends the order, tagged with our id. Adapters are partitioned by exchange+segment so NSE F&O trouble cannot slow BSE equity.
  7. Events back. The exchange returns ack, reject, partial fill and fill messages. The adapter writes them to order-events (Kafka, key = order_id). Consumers update order status, positions, holdings, release or consume margin, and push an update to the user's WebSocket.
  8. GTT and square-off. Two engines read the price stream (lightly conflated, for example 100 ms) and keep per-instrument sorted thresholds. When a GTT trigger or a risk threshold is crossed, they place a normal order through the same order service, with a deterministic idempotency key.
  9. Candles. A stream processor builds 1-minute OHLC candles per instrument. The forming candle lives in Redis for fast chart updates; closed candles are flushed in batches to SQL, and 5m, 1h, 1D candles are rolled up from them.
