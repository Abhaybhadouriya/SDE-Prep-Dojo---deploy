The story

Explain like I'm 5

There is a big fruit market far away (the exchange). Only a few shops are allowed to go inside and buy for you. Your shop is the broker.

The shop has a giant board on the wall that shows fruit prices. A runner stands at the market gate and shouts every price change, hundreds per second. The shop cannot copy every shout onto every child's little board, so every half second it writes just the newest price for each fruit. That is enough: nobody cares what the mango cost 0.3 seconds ago.

You can also leave a note: "When mangoes drop to 10, buy 5 for me." The market throws away all notes every evening, so the shop keeps your note in its own safe, watches the board, and when the price touches 10 it runs to the market with your order. The shop must never lose the note and never buy twice.

The runner is the exchange market-data feed. Writing only the newest price every half second is conflation. The little boards are the WebSocket connections to phones. The note in the safe is a GTT (Good Till Triggered) order: the exchange only keeps orders for one day, so the broker stores the trigger itself and places a real order when the price is hit. The shop owner who sells your fruit if you lose too much money is the risk engine doing auto square-off.

From your world

Conflation is exactly what a network switch does with a last-value register instead of a FIFO: when the consumer is slower than the producer, you keep only the newest value per key and drop the stale ones, so the queue can never grow without bound. It is backpressure solved by overwriting, like a status register that a slow CPU polls. The exchange feed itself is UDP multicast with sequence numbers and gap recovery, the same pattern as a routing protocol that detects lost updates by sequence number and asks for a resync.
