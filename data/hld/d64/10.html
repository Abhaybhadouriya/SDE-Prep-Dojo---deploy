How real companies do it

Seen in 6 recent reports. Every variant, with company, level, date and source post, is in Recently Asked HLD Questions #29. Company view: the Indian Fintech guide.

  • Zerodha (Kite): the "Scaling with common sense" blog says Kite serves orders, positions and portfolio from hot Redis caches filled by a separate async service; a Go ticker serves hundreds of thousands of WebSocket connections and millions of quotes per second; it has had multiple days of 8-9 million trades; and it adopted Kubernetes mostly for uniform deployments.
  • Kite Connect docs: the public WebSocket API allows up to 3,000 instruments per connection and uses binary packets of 8, 44 and 184 bytes for its three modes, a real reference for the frame sizes above. Its GTT API documents single and two-leg (OCO) triggers and statuses like active, triggered, disabled, expired, cancelled and rejected.
  • GTT validity: Zerodha's support pages say a GTT stays valid for one year and that a triggered GTT does not guarantee execution.
  • Auto square-off: Zerodha publishes its intraday square-off times and changed equity from 15:20 to 15:25 in December 2025, which shows this is a scheduled, per-segment rule.
  • Robinhood-style designs: Hello Interview's breakdown splits live prices from order placement and focuses its deep dives on scaling price updates, tracking order updates and keeping order state consistent: the same three themes as this page.
