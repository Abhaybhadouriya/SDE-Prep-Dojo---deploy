Requirements

Always say first: "We are the broker. The exchange does the matching (see Stock Exchange / Order Matching). We take orders from users, check risk, route them to the exchange, and show prices, positions and holdings."

Functional

  1. Live prices. A user opens a watchlist of about 20 instruments and sees last traded price (LTP), change and volume update live. Opening a stock page shows market depth (best 5 bids and asks).
  2. Place orders. Market, limit and stop-loss (SL) orders, buy and sell, for delivery (CNC), intraday (MIS) or, in futures and options, carry-forward (NRML: a position you can hold overnight until expiry). Modify and cancel open orders.
  3. Order updates, positions, holdings. The user sees an order move from open to filled in real time, then their positions (today's trades) and holdings (shares owned in demat).
  4. GTT orders. "If INFY falls to 1,400, buy 10 at 1,400." Valid for up to a year (Zerodha uses one year). Single trigger or OCO (one cancels other: target and stop-loss together).
  5. Auto square-off. Close intraday positions automatically when the loss crosses a risk threshold, and at a fixed time before market close.
  6. Charts. OHLC (open, high, low, close) candles at 1 minute, 5 minutes, 1 day, for years of history.
  7. Multiple exchanges and segments. NSE and BSE equity, F&O (futures and options), commodity (MCX), currency.

  • Correctness over speed for money. An order must never be placed twice, a GTT must never be lost, buying power must never go negative.
  • Availability during market hours (09:15 to 15:30 IST for NSE equity). A 5-minute outage at the open is front-page news. Off-hours maintenance windows are allowed.
  • Freshness is "good enough", not perfect for display: dropping intermediate ticks is fine; showing a price older than 500 ms is not.
  • Auditability. Regulators (SEBI in India) expect every order and its state changes to be traceable.

Split the problem out loud into two very different systems: market data (huge fan-out, lossy is OK, latest wins) and trading (small volume, every message matters, strict state machine). Robinhood-style guides make the same split: correctness where money moves, responsiveness where people only look.
