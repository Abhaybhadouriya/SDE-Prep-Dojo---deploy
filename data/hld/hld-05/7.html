CDN edges

A CDN is a fleet of caching reverse proxies placed in many cities (points of presence, or POPs), usually reached by anycast or GeoDNS. The origin is your own server or object store (like S3) that holds the master copy.

The primer and Karan Pratap Singh's book both describe this split. Netflix's Open Connect is the famous push-style example: Netflix gives Open Connect Appliances to ISPs at no charge, ISPs steer traffic to them with BGP sessions, and the content is filled nightly. Netflix's Open Connect site says it partners with over 1,000 ISPs on embedded deployments. If an embedded appliance fails, traffic falls back to other appliances or to Netflix's servers at internet exchange points. See Video streaming (Netflix) for the full design.

The CDN details interviewers ask about

  • Cache key: by default the URL. Add only the headers that really change the response (for example Accept-Encoding), or the hit rate collapses.
  • Invalidation: prefer versioned URLs (app.3f9a1c.js, /v/42/thumb.jpg) over purge calls. A new version is a new URL, so there is nothing stale to purge.
  • Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable for versioned assets; private or no-store for anything user-specific.
  • Origin shield: a middle cache tier so 300 POPs missing at once become one request to the origin. This is your defence against a thundering herd on a viral object (a thundering herd is thousands of requests hitting the origin at the same instant because the same item missed or expired everywhere at once).
  • Dynamic content: even uncacheable API calls benefit, because TLS terminates nearby and the edge reuses warm connections to the origin. Cloudflare's anycast post says it typically answers around 75% of requests from the edge without going to the origin.
  • Private content: signed URLs or signed cookies with an expiry let the CDN serve paid videos or private photos without asking your servers each time. See Handling large blobs.
