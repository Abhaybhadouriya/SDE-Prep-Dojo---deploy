Putting it together: a latency budget

Physical limits set your floor. Hello Interview's networking guide uses light in fibre at about 200,000 km/s, so New York to London (about 5,600 km) costs about 56 ms per round trip before any processing, while same-region hops are under 1 ms. Multiply by the round trips on this page and you can see why every layer exists.

~56 msNY-London RTT floor
< 1 mssame-region RTT
2-3 RTTTCP+TLS before first byte
1 RTTQUIC fresh handshake
  1. Hide distance: CDN and anycast bring the TLS handshake and cacheable data within about 10 ms of the user.
  2. Hide handshakes: keep-alive, HTTP/2 multiplexing and connection pools at the edge, LB and gateway mean the slow handshake happens once, not per request.
  3. Pick region by DNS, server by LB: latency-based DNS chooses the region; least-connections or P2C (power of two choices: pick two servers at random and send to the less loaded one, nearly as good as checking all servers and far cheaper) chooses the server.
  4. Fail fast at the edge: the gateway rejects bad tokens and over-limit callers before they consume backend capacity.
  5. Keep the inside fast: services talk HTTP/2 or gRPC with pooled connections inside one region, and only cross regions asynchronously. More in Reliability & multi-region.
