Architecture, step by step

Click Next or Play. Watch for the moment the design splits into a fast path (top) and slow paths (bottom).

  1. Request. At checkout the payment service calls the risk scoring service with a strict 100 ms timeout. Scoring pods are stateless, so they scale out behind a load balancer.
  2. Fetch features. In parallel, batched: profile and window features from the online store, plus the scorer itself updates and reads fast 60-second counters (so a burst of payments is visible instantly, even before the streaming job catches up).
  3. Score and apply rules. The feature vector goes to the model server (gradient-boosted trees or a neural network) for a probability, and to the rules engine for hard policies and lists. A small policy combines them: rules can force BLOCK or ALLOW; otherwise thresholds on the score pick ALLOW, CHALLENGE or BLOCK.
  4. Answer. The decision goes back to the payment service in well under 100 ms; CHALLENGE triggers an OTP / 3-D Secure step. The decision is idempotent per payment_id.
  5. Publish. The payment, its features and the decision are published to Kafka (asynchronously, never blocking the answer). Kafka is the source of truth for "what did we see and decide".
  6. Stream features. A Flink job keyed by card, device, IP and merchant computes sliding-window aggregates in event time and writes them to the online store within seconds. Flink runs on Kubernetes through the Flink operator and checkpoints its state.
  7. Collect labels. Events land in the data lake. Labels arrive later: chargebacks often come weeks after the payment, plus manual reviews and customer reports. They are joined to the original events by payment_id.
  8. Train and deploy. Training builds point-in-time-correct feature sets (only data known at payment time), evaluates on recent weeks, registers the model, runs it in shadow on live traffic, then promotes it.
  9. Degraded mode. If scoring fails or times out, the payment service applies a small local fallback policy (lists + simple limits) instead of blocking every payment or allowing everything.

Which process does what: the scorer decides; Flink is the only writer of streaming features; the batch jobs are the only writers of slow features; the training pipeline is the only writer of models into the registry; the payment service owns the fallback. Kafka connects them, so any one can fail without stopping the others.
