Requirements

Functional

  • Score every payment before it is authorised and return a decision: allow, challenge (step-up authentication) or block, plus a risk score and the top reasons.
  • Rules that fraud analysts can add or change in minutes without a code deploy ("block cards used from more than 3 countries in 1 hour").
  • ML models that use hundreds of features; deploy new models safely (shadow mode first).
  • Case management: challenged or suspicious payments go to a review queue for humans.
  • Feedback: chargebacks, manual reviews and customer reports become labels for training.
  • Lists: allow-lists and block-lists (known good merchants, stolen card numbers, bad devices).

Non-functional

PropertyTargetWhy
Latencyp99 < 100 ms for the whole decisionIt sits inside checkout; Stripe Radar's published target is the same order
AvailabilityHigher than payments itself (99.99%)If scoring is down, either payments stop or fraud flows
Scale100 M payments/day, peak ~10k/s (our assumption)Large PSP or marketplace at sale time
FreshnessVelocity features reflect events within a few secondsCard-testing attacks fire dozens of payments in seconds
QualityCatch most fraud while blocking very few good paymentsStripe reports blocking about 0.1% of legitimate payments as a reference point
Explainability and auditEvery decision logged with score, model version, rule hitsDisputes, regulators, debugging

Say early: "I will split the design into three paths: a synchronous scoring path under 100 ms, an asynchronous streaming path that keeps features fresh, and an offline path that trains models from delayed labels. Most of the interesting trade-offs are about how these three stay consistent."

You might confuse this with: anomaly detection on metrics (for example "payment success rate dropped"). That watches the whole system. Fraud detection judges each transaction and must answer before money moves.
