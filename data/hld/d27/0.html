The story

Explain like I'm 5

A guard stands at the school gate. Every child shows a badge, and the guard has one blink to decide: "come in", "wait, let me call your teacher", or "you cannot come in".

The guard is clever. She remembers things. "This badge already came in 10 times in the last minute. Strange!" "This child always comes at 8 a.m., but today it is midnight." "Three different children used the same lunch box today." She also has a list of rules from the principal, and every week the principal tells her which children she got wrong, so she gets better.

The badge is a payment (a card or UPI transaction). The guard is the scoring service, and "one blink" is a latency budget (Stripe says its Radar makes the decision in under 100 ms). The things she remembers are features: numbers that describe the payment and its history, like "transactions on this card in the last minute". The principal's list is the rules engine. Getting better each week from the principal's feedback is model training on labels (which payments turned out to be fraud). The three answers are allow, challenge (ask for an OTP or 3-D Secure, an extra check by the card's bank) and block.

Fraud is rare. Stripe has written that roughly 1 in 1,000 payments is fraudulent. That single number creates most of the difficulty: the model must find a needle in a haystack, every false alarm annoys a real customer, and you only learn the truth weeks later when a customer disputes a charge (a chargeback).

From your world

This is an inline IDS/IPS on a fast path. Each packet (payment) must be classified at line rate with a hard per-packet budget, using per-flow state (features keyed by card, device, IP, like connection-tracking entries keyed by 5-tuple), signature rules (the rules engine, like Snort rules) and anomaly scoring (the ML model). The slow path builds the state and new signatures off-line. Where this breaks: an IPS sees the packet stream directly; here the "flow state" is computed by other machines and arrives a few seconds late, so the freshest events may be missing exactly when an attack starts.
