The story

Explain like I'm 5

There is one giant library far away with every cartoon ever made. If every child in town drove there each evening to borrow a cartoon, the road would jam and everyone would be late.

So the library does something clever. Every night, while children sleep and the roads are empty, a truck brings copies of the cartoons children are likely to want tomorrow to a tiny library on every street corner. In the evening, each child just walks to the corner. The far-away library is still the boss: it keeps the list of what you like and gives you a ticket saying which corner library to visit. But the cartoons themselves come from around the corner.

And if a child's walkway is a bit slow today, the corner library hands over the cartoon in small pieces, and gives smaller, lighter pieces when the path is crowded, so the cartoon never stops.

That is Netflix's real design. The far-away library is the control plane running in AWS: sign-in, browsing, recommendations, deciding what to play and from where. The corner libraries are Open Connect Appliances (OCAs), Netflix's own cache servers, many of them placed inside internet service providers (ISPs). The night truck is the nightly fill. The small, lighter pieces are segments at different bitrates, chosen by adaptive bitrate streaming (ABR).

From your world

This is the control plane / data plane split you know from routers. The route processor runs protocols and decides; the line cards forward at line rate. Netflix's "route processor" is a set of cloud microservices; its "line cards" are OCAs that do one thing, push bytes very fast. Like a router, if the control plane hiccups, streams already playing keep flowing from the OCA (much like non-stop forwarding). Where it breaks: a router's data plane is in one chassis; here the "line cards" are thousands of servers spread across the world, owned partly by Netflix and placed in other companies' networks.

New words, once each (you will meet them below):

  • CDN (content delivery network) = many cache servers placed near viewers, so bytes travel a short distance instead of crossing the whole internet. Open Connect is Netflix's own CDN.
  • Microservice = a small, separately deployed service that owns one job (playback, steering, bookmarks) and talks to the others over the network. Think of one daemon per job instead of one giant binary.
  • IXP (internet exchange point) = a building where many ISPs and content networks plug into a shared switch fabric and peer with each other. An OCA "at an IXP" serves every ISP that peers there.
  • Wide-column store = a key-value store whose value is a sorted set of named columns, spread over many servers by key. Cassandra is the best-known one; Netflix uses it for viewing history.
  • EVCache = Netflix's replicated in-memory cache, built on memcached, sitting in front of those stores.
  • p99 = 99 of every 100 requests are faster than this number.
