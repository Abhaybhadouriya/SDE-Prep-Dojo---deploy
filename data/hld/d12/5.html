Architecture: what happens when you press Play

Whiteboard version (6 boxes): device, API gateway, playback service, steering, OCA, telemetry via Kafka, and offline, encoding pipeline to S3 to fill. Click Play.

  1. Request in. The device calls POST /playback/start. DNS and a cloud load balancer bring it to the API gateway (Netflix built Zuul for this), which authenticates, routes and sheds load.
  2. What can you play? The playback service checks your plan and the title's rights in your country, looks at your device's codecs, DRM and screen, and picks the renditions it may use. Metadata comes from in-memory caches (EVCache, Netflix's memcached-based replicated cache).
  3. From where? The steering service takes your IP address and finds OCAs that (a) serve your ISP's prefix, (b) are healthy and not overloaded, and (c) hold the files. It returns a ranked list, so the device has fallbacks without asking again.
  4. Keys. In parallel, the player gets a DRM licence for this device so it can decrypt the segments. The response also includes the resume position from your bookmarks.
  5. Bytes from next door. The player now talks only to OCA-7 inside its ISP: fetch the manifest, then segment after segment over HTTPS. No cloud servers are on this path.
  6. Adapt. The player's ABR logic watches download speed and buffer level. It starts at a modest bitrate for a fast start, then climbs while the buffer is healthy, and steps down before the buffer runs dry.
  7. Report. Every ~30 seconds, and on pause or stop, the player sends heartbeats (position, bitrate, stalls, which OCA). They flow through Kafka to update bookmarks (resume on another device), quality dashboards and recommendation data.
  8. Failure path. If OCA-7 stops answering mid-movie, the player moves to the next URL in its fallback list and keeps going from its buffer. The control plane is not needed for this.
