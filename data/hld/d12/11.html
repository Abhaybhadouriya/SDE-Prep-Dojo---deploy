  • Two planes: control plane in the cloud (what, who, from where), data plane at the edge (bytes).
  • Estimate egress first: concurrent streams x bitrate, tens of Tbps, so serve from inside ISPs.
  • Push-fill caches off-peak by predicted popularity; pull only for the long tail.
  • Video = ladder of renditions, cut into aligned segments, listed in a manifest; the player's ABR picks per segment.
  • Encoding is chunked, parallel and idempotent; publish only when every rendition is ready.
  • Steering returns a ranked list of OCAs, so the player can fail over alone.
  • Telemetry via Kafka drives bookmarks, quality dashboards and recommendations.

60-second answer: "I split it into a control plane and a data plane. The control plane runs as microservices in multiple cloud regions: auth, catalogue, recommendations, and a playback service that checks rights and device capabilities, picks renditions, gets a DRM licence and asks a steering service for the best edge caches for this client's IP. The data plane is a fleet of cache appliances inside ISPs and at exchange points, filled every night with what each region is likely to watch. The player downloads short segments from the nearest box and adapts bitrate to its buffer and throughput, falling back to other boxes if one fails. Content is encoded offline in parallel chunks into a per-title ladder. Heartbeats flow through Kafka for resume, quality monitoring and recommendations."
