Requirements

Functional

  1. Browse and search a catalogue, with personalised rows.
  2. Play a title on TVs, phones, laptops and consoles, with audio and subtitle choices.
  3. Resume where you stopped, on any device.
  4. Ingest new content from studios and prepare it for every device (encoding).
  5. Protect content with DRM (digital rights management: encrypted video plus licence keys).
  6. Collect playback telemetry (what played, bitrate, stalls) for quality and recommendations.

Usually out of scope: payments and sign-up, live sports, downloads for offline viewing, ads. Ask before skipping.

Non-functional

  • Fast start: video starts in about 1-2 seconds (a common target to state).
  • Smooth playback: very few rebuffers ("the spinner"), highest quality the network allows.
  • Huge egress at evening peak, spread worldwide.
  • High availability of play: browsing can degrade, play must work.
  • Eventual consistency is fine for most things (new titles, recommendations, resume position within seconds).
  • Cost efficiency: bandwidth is the biggest bill, so serving close to users matters.

Words you might confuse. Bitrate = how many bits per second the video uses (higher is sharper and bigger). Resolution = pixels (720p, 1080p, 4K); the same resolution can be encoded at different bitrates. Codec = the compression method (H.264/AVC, H.265/HEVC, VP9, AV1). Rendition = one encoded version (say AV1 at 1080p, 3 Mbps). Bitrate ladder = the set of renditions for one title. Segment = a few seconds of one rendition, the unit the player downloads. Manifest = the menu file that lists renditions and where their segments are (DASH MPD or HLS m3u8).
