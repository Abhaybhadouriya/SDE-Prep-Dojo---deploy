Strengths of a consensus lock service
  • One well-tested place for "who is leader" across many systems
  • Survives 2 of 5 replica failures
  • Watches give push notification of changes
  • Small config and name data sit right next to the lock
Weaknesses
  • Writes are serialised through one leader: not for 100k locks/s
  • Minority side of a partition cannot get locks (CP)
  • Leases still need fencing at the resource
  • Becomes a shared dependency: one bad client can overload it (Chubby added artificial delays to curb abusive clients)

Failure modes to name

  • Thundering herd of KeepAlives after a failover: every client reconnects at once. Fix: jittered reconnect, proxies, longer grace period.
  • Slow disk on the leader: fsync stalls delay heartbeats, followers time out, elections flap. Fix: dedicated SSD, separate WAL disk, alert on fsync latency.
  • Lock held forever by a live but stuck client (session alive, work stuck). Fix: lock TTL independent of session, watchdog in the client, revocation request (ZooKeeper's revocable lock recipe sets "unlock" on the node).
  • Using the service as a database or message bus: large values or high-rate pub/sub. Chubby's paper calls its event mechanism a poor fit for all but trivial pub/sub.
