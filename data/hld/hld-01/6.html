CDNs: a copy in every neighbourhood Must know

A CDN (content delivery network) is a network of cache servers spread around the world, called edge servers or PoPs (points of presence). Users fetch images, video segments, JavaScript and sometimes API responses from the PoP nearest to them, not from your origin data centre. That turns a 150 ms cross-continent trip into a 10-30 ms local one, and it removes most of the load from your servers.

Netflix is the extreme case. Its own CDN, Open Connect, places appliances inside ISP networks and pre-fills them with the shows people are predicted to watch, during off-peak hours at night. See Video Streaming (Netflix).

From your world

You guessed right: a CDN is partly anycast. Many CDNs announce the same IP prefix from every PoP over BGP, so the routing system itself delivers each user to a nearby PoP. Others use DNS to hand each user the IP of a close PoP. Then each PoP is a big caching proxy. Where this breaks: anycast picks the BGP-closest PoP, not always the fastest or least loaded one, so CDNs add their own steering on top.

Careful: never cache personal pages (a cart, an account page) on a shared CDN without making the user part of the cache key. Use Cache-Control: private or no-store for those. Cache keys and headers are a common source of real data leaks.
