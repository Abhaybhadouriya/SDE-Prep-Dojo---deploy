Latency numbers every engineer should know Must know

Latency is how long one operation takes. Throughput is how many operations you finish per second. A famous list by Jeff Dean and Peter Norvig gives rough costs. The numbers are from around 2012 and hardware has changed, but the ratios are what matter in an interview. The bars below use a log scale: each step right is 10x slower.

Four numbers are enough to carry into every design. The full list (with the "if L1 took 1 second" scale and a comparison with your NIC/PCIe numbers) lives on one page: Back-of-Envelope Estimation, latency section.

Operation (rule of thumb)TimeWhat it means for design
RAM read~100 nsIn-memory caches (Redis) are fast because they live here
SSD random read~150 usA DB index lookup on SSD is fine; thousands per request are not
Round trip in one data centre~0.5 msEvery service-to-service call costs this; avoid chains of 20 calls
Cross-continent round trip~150 msWhy CDNs and multi-region exist; you cannot beat the speed of light

Modern cloud numbers worth saying out loud (from Hello Interview's "numbers to know"): under 1 ms round trip inside one availability zone (AZ, one data centre or a group of them), about 1-2 ms across AZs in a region, and 50-150 ms between regions. Big cloud machines now have hundreds of GB to several TB of RAM, so a single well-sized database server goes further than old advice suggests.

From your world

You already know a cache miss to DRAM costs ~100 ns and a PCIe round trip costs around a microsecond. Scale the same instinct up: memory : SSD : same-DC network : cross-continent is roughly 100 ns : 150 us : 500 us : 150 ms, or 1 : 1,500 : 5,000 : 1,500,000. Network hops are the "DRAM misses" of distributed systems. Where this breaks: on a board latency is predictable; across a data centre the tail (the slowest 1%) can be 10x the median because of queueing, GC pauses and retransmits.

Word you will hear: p99. "p99 latency = 200 ms" means 99% of requests finish within 200 ms and 1% are slower. Interviewers prefer p99 over averages because averages hide the slow requests users actually notice. p50 is the median.
