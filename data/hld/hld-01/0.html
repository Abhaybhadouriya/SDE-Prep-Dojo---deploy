The story: one bakery, then a whole city

Explain like I'm 5

Priya bakes cookies in her kitchen. Her friends call her, she bakes, she hands them over. That is a client and a server: the friend asks, the kitchen answers.

Cookies get famous. One oven is too slow, so she buys a bigger oven (that is vertical scaling). Soon even the biggest oven is not enough, so she opens three kitchens and hires a helper at the door who sends each customer to the kitchen with the shortest line (that is horizontal scaling and a load balancer).

Everyone asks for chocolate chip, so she keeps a tray of them right by the door instead of baking each one fresh (a cache). She opens small shops in every neighbourhood that keep the most popular cookies (a CDN). Her recipe book is so important that she keeps photocopies in every kitchen (replication). The order book gets too fat, so she splits it: A-M in one book, N-Z in another (sharding).

One day the phone line between two kitchens breaks. A customer asks "Is the last cake still for sale?" The kitchen can say "I cannot check, please wait" (consistent) or "Yes!" and maybe be wrong (available). That choice is CAP. And when 500 orders arrive at once, she writes them on slips and puts them in a box so the bakers work through them at their own pace (a queue).

That is the whole page in one story. Every box in a system design diagram is one of these ideas. The rest of this page gives each one its real name, a picture, the numbers, the trade-offs and the interview answer.

From your world

Most of this page is ideas you already know at the chip or router level, scaled up to the data centre: cache = CPU L1/L2/DRAM hierarchy; load balancer = ECMP plus a NAT table; CDN = anycast plus caching at PoPs; replication = a redundancy pair; consistent hashing = resilient ECMP hashing; queue = a ring buffer between a producer and a consumer. Where this breaks: inside one box you have shared memory and one clock. Between servers you only have messages over a network that can lose, delay or reorder them. That is the source of every hard problem below.
