Replication: three ways to keep copies

Replication means keeping the same data on more than one machine, so you survive a crash and can spread reads. There are three main shapes, and the shape decides who is allowed to accept a write.

ShapeWho accepts writesGood atHard partReal examples
Single-leaderOne leader per shardSimple, no write conflicts, easy reasoningFailover (electing a new leader safely), leader is a write bottleneckMySQL/Postgres primary-replica, Kafka partition leader, etcd
Multi-leaderOne leader per region/datacenterLow write latency in every region, survives a region cutWrite conflicts: last-write-wins loses data, CRDTs (conflict-free replicated data types: counters, sets and text whose concurrent edits merge automatically) or app merges neededActive-active multi-region databases, offline-first apps
LeaderlessAny replica (via quorum)Very high availability, no failover stepStale reads, conflict detection (vector clocks), repairAmazon Dynamo, Cassandra, Riak

Sync, async and semi-sync

Separately from the shape, the leader decides when to tell the client "done":

  • Synchronous - wait until every follower has the write. Nothing is lost, but one slow follower stalls every write.
  • Asynchronous - reply once the leader has it, ship to followers later. Fast, but if the leader dies, the last few writes may vanish (replication lag becomes data loss).
  • Semi-synchronous / quorum - wait for some followers (often one, or a majority). This is the usual production choice. Meta ran MySQL with semi-sync to two nearby log-only replicas ("logtailers") for sub-millisecond commits, then moved the whole thing onto Raft (more on that below).

From your world

Think of a write-ahead log replicated like a journaling filesystem's journal (ext4's jbd2) being mirrored to another disk. Synchronous replication is a RAID-1 mirror that acks only when both disks have the block. Async is writing to one disk and letting a background thread copy blocks over: fast, but a crash loses the tail. Kafka's acks=all with min.insync.replicas=2 is semi-sync. See Kafka Replication & ISR.
