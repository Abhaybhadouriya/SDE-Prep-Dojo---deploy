Raft: replicating the log

Once there is a leader, every write becomes a log entry (index, term, command). The leader appends it locally, sends AppendEntries to followers, and once a majority has stored it, the entry is committed - it can never be lost. Only then does the leader apply it to its state machine and answer the client. Followers learn the new commit index from the next message and apply it too.

  1. Append locally. The client sends SET x=5 to the leader. The leader adds entry 4 (term 2) to its own log. Not committed yet.
  2. Replicate. The leader sends AppendEntries to each follower with the new entry [4, t2] plus the index and term of the entry just before it (prevIndex = 3, prevTerm = t2).
  3. Consistency check. A follower accepts only if its own log has that previous entry. F1 and F2 match and append. F3 is behind (no entry 3) and says no. F4 is down.
  4. Commit on majority. Leader + F1 + F2 = 3 of 5. Entry 4 is committed. The leader applies it and replies OK to the client. Two servers can be slow or dead and we are still safe.
  5. Repair the laggard. For F3 the leader steps back one index at a time and resends 3, then 4, until F3's log matches. Followers never decide what the log is - the leader's log is the truth.

The safety rules in one breath

Raft guarantees five properties: at most one leader per term (election safety); a leader never overwrites its own log (leader append-only); if two logs share an entry with the same index and term, everything before it matches too (log matching); a committed entry appears in every future leader's log (leader completeness, enforced by the "only vote for an up-to-date candidate" rule); and no two servers apply different commands at the same index (state machine safety). Raft tolerates crashes, not lies: it is not Byzantine fault tolerant.
