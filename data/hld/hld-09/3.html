Raft: electing a leader

Consensus means a group of servers agrees on a value, and once decided the decision is final. Raft was designed to be easier to understand than Paxos while giving the same fault tolerance. It builds a replicated state machine: every server has a log of commands and applies them in the same order, so every server reaches the same state. With 5 servers it keeps working with 2 failed, because 3 is still a majority.

Raft splits the problem into three parts: leader election, log replication and safety. Every server is a follower, a candidate or the leader. Time is cut into numbered terms; each term has at most one leader. The term number works like a generation counter: a message with an older term is ignored.

  1. Normal life. S1 is leader for term 1. It sends heartbeats (empty AppendEntries messages) so followers keep resetting their timers.
  2. Leader dies. Heartbeats stop. Each follower's election timer runs out at a random moment, typically somewhere in 150-300 ms. Randomness is the trick that makes split votes rare.
  3. First to time out runs. S2 times out first, bumps the term to 2, votes for itself and sends RequestVote to everyone with the index and term of its last log entry.
  4. Votes. Each server gives one vote per term, first come first served, and only to a candidate whose log is at least as up to date as its own. S3 and S4 agree. 3 of 5 is a majority.
  5. Leader. S2 becomes leader of term 2 and immediately heartbeats so nobody else starts an election.
  6. Old leader comes back. S1 wakes up believing it leads term 1. The first message it sees carries term 2, so it steps down to follower. Higher term always wins.

From your world

This is VRRP/HSRP with a brain. In VRRP the backup router takes over the virtual IP when advertisements stop for about 3 intervals. But VRRP has no majority vote: cut the link between two routers and both become master - split brain. Raft fixes this by requiring a majority of votes, so the side of a partition with only 2 of 5 nodes can never elect anyone. The election timer is exactly like OSPF's dead interval versus hello interval: the dead timer must be several hellos long, or one lost packet triggers a pointless reconvergence.

Timing rules that keep Raft stable

The Raft paper's rule is broadcastTime << electionTimeout << MTBF: a round trip must be much shorter than the election timeout, which must be much shorter than the mean time between failures. With broadcast times of 0.5-20 ms, election timeouts anywhere from about 10 to 500 ms work. etcd's tuning guide says the same thing in practice: set the heartbeat interval near the round-trip time between members and the election timeout to at least 10 times that round-trip time (defaults: 100 ms heartbeat, 1,000 ms election timeout). It also warns that a slow disk fsync on the leader can cost it leadership, because disk latency counts as liveness.

Real numbers (Meta, 2023): Meta moved MySQL replication for millions of shards onto Raft. With the old semi-sync plus external automation, failover took about 20-40 seconds. With Raft (a heartbeat every 500 ms, election after 3 missed heartbeats), failover typically finishes within 2 seconds and a planned promotion takes about 300 ms.
