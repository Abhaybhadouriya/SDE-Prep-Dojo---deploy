The story

Explain like I'm 5

A theme park opens a new ride with only 100 seats. At exactly 12 o'clock, 10,000 children run at the gate. If everyone pushes through at once, the gate breaks, children fall over, and nobody rides.

So the park does three clever things. First, a queue with timed tickets: you wait in a calm line, and the gatekeeper lets in a few children at a time, only as fast as the ride can take them. Second, a big board at the ride shows "seats left: 100, 99, 98...". Only one person is allowed to change the number, so it can never go below zero. Third, a child who gets a seat has 10 minutes to buckle in. If they wander off, the seat goes back on the board for the next child.

And when the board says 0, a helper runs back along the line shouting "all full!", so the children at the back can go home instead of waiting for nothing.

That is the whole design. The timed queue is a virtual waiting room. The board is an atomic stock counter. The 10 minutes is a reservation with a payment TTL. Shouting "all full" is a sold-out flag pushed to the edge. Everything else on this page is about making those four ideas work at millions of requests per second.

A flash sale is the extreme version of e-commerce checkout. Normal checkout spreads many buyers over many products. A flash sale points almost every buyer at one product in one second. The problem changes from "be correct" to "be correct while being hit by a wall".
