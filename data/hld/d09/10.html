How real companies do it

  • Flipkart Big Billion Days. About 1.5 million requests per second at sale open and capacity for over 2,000 orders per second (CPTO Q&A, 2023). Aerospike's write-up of a Flipkart talk describes 90 million queries per second across three data centres on Aerospike running on Kubernetes. The BBD 2015 memoir describes months of preparation and the user path vs order path split, after the first Big Billion Day in 2014 overwhelmed systems.
  • Shopify. The Kylie Cosmetics sale (February 2016) took down a shared shard. The fix was an edge throttle in Nginx/OpenResty Lua with a queue page and signed cookies (Part I), then a fair queue with signed arrival timestamps and a PID-controlled threshold (Part II).
  • Ticketmaster. The Eras Tour presale (November 2022): 3.5 billion system requests, 4x its previous peak, largely from bots and from fans without codes. Verified Fan pre-registration plus a queue is its answer.
  • Amazon. The Builders' Library explains load shedding and retries with jitter, the two habits that keep a spike from turning into a retry storm. Lightning deals are the same funnel on a smaller scale.
  • Managed waiting rooms (for example Cloudflare Waiting Room) productise the edge queue, if you would rather buy than build.

Related designs: e-commerce checkout (the normal path), ticket booking (seat holds with a TTL), rate limiter, payment system, hotel booking, top-k heavy hitters. Patterns: dealing with contention. Concepts: resilience patterns, reliability at scale.
