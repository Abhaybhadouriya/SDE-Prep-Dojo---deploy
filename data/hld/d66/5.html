Architecture, step by step

  1. Search request. The search service validates input, returns a search_id, and starts work. Results stream to the client as they arrive.
  2. Plan and read cache. The route planner expands the request into candidate itineraries: nonstop flights plus precomputed one-stop connections (valid layover times). For each (supplier, leg, date) it reads the quote cache first.
  3. Fan out to suppliers. For cache misses or stale entries, the aggregator calls suppliers in parallel with a per-supplier timeout, rate limit and circuit breaker. Each adapter converts the supplier's format (GDS, NDC XML, REST) to our model. Answers fill the cache and stream to the client; slow suppliers are cut off at the deadline.
  4. Re-price. When the user picks an itinerary, the pricing service asks that supplier for a live price and seat availability, applies markups and member discounts, and returns a signed price token valid for some minutes.
  5. Hold. The booking orchestrator creates the booking (status HOLDING) and asks the supplier to create a reservation for all segments with the chosen seats. The supplier, not us, locks the seats and returns a PNR with a ticketing time limit.
  6. Pay. Authorise the payment (not capture) with an idempotency key. If authorisation fails, cancel the holds.
  7. Ticket and confirm. Ask the supplier to issue tickets for every segment, then capture the payment and mark CONFIRMED. Events go to Kafka for e-tickets, notifications, accounting and analytics. If ticketing fails, compensate (void, release, refund).
