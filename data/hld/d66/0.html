The story

Explain like I'm 5

A bus company has one big seat chart in its office. Many ticket shops in town sell seats on that bus. If every shop kept its own chart, two shops could sell seat 12 to two different kids. So the shops do not own the chart: they phone the bus office and say "please hold seat 12 for me for 15 minutes". The office writes the shop's name on seat 12. If the kid pays in time, the office prints the ticket. If not, seat 12 becomes free again.

Phoning the bus office for every child who just wants to look at prices would jam the phone line, so each shop also keeps a notebook of prices it heard recently. Looking uses the notebook. Buying always phones the office.

The bus office is the airline's inventory system or a GDS (Global Distribution System such as Amadeus, Sabre or Travelport) that sells on its behalf. The shops are online travel agents and metasearch sites. "Hold for 15 minutes" is a reservation (PNR) with a ticketing time limit. The notebook is the search cache. Phoning only to buy is the look-to-book problem: most searches never become bookings.

From your world

The hold is a lease, like a DHCP lease on an IP address: the server that owns the pool grants it for a fixed time; if the client does not renew or confirm, the address returns to the pool. No client can "lock" an address by writing it into its own table. Search caching is like a route cache in front of a slow lookup: fast and usually right, but the forwarding decision that matters (the booking) is checked against the authoritative table.
