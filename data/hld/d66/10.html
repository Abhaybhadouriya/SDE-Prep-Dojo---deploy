How real companies do it

Seen in 6 recent reports. Every variant, with company, level, date and source post, is in Recently Asked HLD Questions #27. Company view: the Flipkart guide and the Indian Fintech guide.

  • Skyscanner: its engineering blog discusses a replicated cache for flight price search results; its partner docs describe serving cached quotes until they pass a configured age and then calling the partner live. Together they frame caching as a trade-off between quote accuracy and partner load.
  • Amadeus: its developer APIs split the flow into offers search, offers price (confirm availability and final price) and create orders (booking), the same three steps as this page. Amadeus also writes that look-to-book ratios can now easily reach 1,000:1.
  • IATA NDC: airlines increasingly sell through their own NDC APIs, so they now carry the search cost; IATA has published work on managing look-to-book volumes.
  • GDS ticketing time limits: airline guides (for example Finnair's) explain that unticketed reservations are cancelled when the time limit passes: the hold-as-lease model in practice.
  • Expedia Group: has written about using an in-memory data grid (Apache Ignite) to speed up flight searches.

Related designs: ticket booking (BookMyShow), hotel booking (Airbnb), payment system, e-commerce checkout, distributed lock service, price tracker alerts, distributed cache.
