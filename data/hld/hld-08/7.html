The decision flowchart

Use these questions in order. Each one either picks a store or passes you to the next question. Click through.

  1. Bytes or records? Anything bigger than a few hundred KB (DynamoDB's item limit is 400 KB) goes to object storage; keep only its key and metadata in a database.
  2. Correctness first. Money, bookings, inventory, anything with "must not double" goes in a relational database with transactions and constraints. This is also the default when nothing else applies.
  3. Write volume and shape. Billions of appends (messages, events, clicks) read by key and recent range: a wide-column or managed key-value store, modeled per query.
  4. Latency. Sub-millisecond reads of hot data that you can rebuild from the source of truth: Redis in front, never as the only copy of money.
  5. Search. Free text, typo tolerance, facets: an inverted index. "Similar meaning": a vector index. Both are derived copies fed from the source of truth.
  6. Analytics. Time-stamped metrics with tags: a TSDB. Ad-hoc aggregates over billions of rows: a columnar store like ClickHouse.
  7. Traversals. Only if the product walks many hops at query time do you add a graph database; one-hop edges fit fine in a sharded table.
  8. Polyglot is normal. Real systems combine stores: one source of truth, derived read models kept in sync by events.

Say it like this: "Orders need transactions, so Postgres is the source of truth. Product search needs full-text, so Elasticsearch fed by CDC, with a one-second staleness I am fine with. Product images go to S3 behind a CDN. Carts are hot and rebuildable, so Redis." One sentence per store, each with its reason.
