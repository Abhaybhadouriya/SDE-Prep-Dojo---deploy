Architecture: follow one comment

Click Next or Play. Ravi in Pune is watching the final. Asha in Delhi posts "What a six!".

  1. Connect. Ravi's app opens an SSE stream. The layer-7 load balancer hashes video_id so viewers of the same match tend to land on the same few gateways (co-location). Gateway G7 keeps the open connection.
  2. Subscribe. G7 adds Ravi's connection to its in-memory table under match42. If this is G7's first viewer of match42, it registers "G7 wants match42" in the endpoint store (or subscribes to the match42 pub/sub channel).
  3. Write. Asha posts. The comment service authenticates, rate-limits her (for example 1 comment per few seconds), runs a fast spam/profanity filter, assigns a time-sortable ID, and writes to Cassandra and the Redis recent buffer.
  4. Publish once. The comment goes to a dispatcher (or the video's pub/sub channel). It looks up which gateways want match42 and sends one copy to each, not one per viewer.
  5. Sample and push. Each gateway buffers incoming comments per video and every ~1 s sends a frame of at most ~10 comments, preferring friends, verified users and the viewer's own comment. That frame is written to each local connection.
  6. Reactions take a separate, lossy path. Taps are batched on the phone, posted as counts, written to Kafka, summed in 2-second windows by a stream job, and the totals are pushed on the same channel. Clients animate a swarm proportional to the totals.
  7. Failure. If G7 restarts, its 100k clients reconnect with random jitter, land on another gateway, and send Last-Event-ID. The new gateway replays what they missed from the recent buffer. Nothing durable was lost, because the gateway only held connections.

From your world

This is IGMP snooping in software. A switch forwards a multicast group only to ports that sent an IGMP join, instead of flooding every port. Here the "ports" are gateways, the "join" is the endpoint-store registration, and the dispatcher plays the switch. A gateway with no viewers of match42 never sees its traffic, just like a port with no group members.
