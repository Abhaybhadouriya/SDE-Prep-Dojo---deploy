Back-of-envelope estimation

We size for the hardest case, one mega-stream, then check the platform total. Numbers marked "assume" are ours. The method is in Back-of-Envelope Estimation.

  1. Viewers. Assume a final with 25 million concurrent viewers on one stream (the order of magnitude LinkedIn's talk quotes for a cricket semifinal).
  2. Comment write rate. Assume 1% of viewers comment once a minute: 250,000 per minute / 60 = ~4,200 comments/s. That matches Hello Interview's mega-stream example of 5,000 comments/s. A write path at 5,000/s is easy: one partitioned store handles it.
  3. Naive fan-out. If every viewer receives every comment: 4,200 x 25,000,000 = 1011 messages per second. That is impossible, and also useless: nobody can read 70 comments a second.
  4. Fan-out with a human cap. Cap what each viewer receives at ~10 comments/s and batch them into one frame per second. Now it is 25M frames/s, each ~10 x 150 bytes = 1.5 KB. Bandwidth: 25M x 1.5 KB = 37.5 GB/s = ~300 Gbit/s. Large but real; and compare it with the video itself: 25M viewers x 2 Mbit/s = 50 Tbit/s. Comments are under 1% of video bandwidth.
  5. Connections. LinkedIn reported about 100,000 persistent connections per frontend machine in practice. 25M / 100k = 250 gateway servers for this one stream; double it for headroom = ~500. LinkedIn did the same sum: 18 million viewers needs about 180 machines.
  6. Reactions. Assume 10% of viewers tap a heart every second during a big moment: 2.5 million taps/s. We never store or forward individual taps. Clients batch locally, servers count per (video, emoji, 2-second window). Hotstar reported around 5 billion emojis from 55.83 million users during the 2019 Cricket World Cup.
  7. Storage. Assume a platform-wide average of 50,000 comments/s: x 86,400 = 4.3 billion comments/day x ~300 bytes = ~1.3 TB/day, ~4 TB/day with 3 replicas. A write-friendly wide-column store (Cassandra/DynamoDB) is the natural fit.

Tip: step 3 is the turning point of the interview. Show the impossible number, then say "so we must sample and batch". The interviewer is waiting for that sentence.
