The story

Explain like I'm 5

Imagine a giant stadium. Every time someone shouts something funny, a helper writes it on a card. There are too many people for one helper to show every card to everyone, so each section of seats gets its own helper. The card-writer only sends cards to helpers whose section is watching that match. When the crowd goes wild and thousands shout at once, the helpers do not read every card aloud. They read a few good ones, and for the cheering they just hold up a big sign: "50,000 people cheered!"

That is the design. The sections are gateway servers (machines that hold open connections to viewers). The helper who only sends cards to the right sections is pub/sub fan-out (publish once, deliver to every subscriber of a topic). Reading only a few cards is sampling. The big "50,000 cheered" sign is aggregation of reactions.

The hard part is not storing comments. It is delivering them. One comment on a stream with 10 million viewers turns into 10 million network writes. This "write amplification" is why the problem is a favourite. Hello Interview and systemdesign.one both publish full breakdowns of it, and interviewing.io hosts a Facebook mock interview on exactly "design live comments" (interviewing.io mock). In India, use IPL or a World Cup match on Hotstar (JioHotstar) as your example. Interviewers like a scale story they recognise.
