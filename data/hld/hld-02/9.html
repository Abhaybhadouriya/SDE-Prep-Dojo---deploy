Leader election and Raft

Explain like I'm 5

The class needs one monitor who writes on the board. If the monitor is absent for a while, any kid can say "vote for me!". A kid who gets votes from more than half the class becomes the new monitor. Because you need more than half, two monitors can never be elected at the same time.

Many systems are simpler with one leader: it orders all writes, and followers copy it. The hard part is choosing the leader safely when machines crash and networks split. If two nodes both believe they are leader (split brain), they accept conflicting writes and you corrupt data.

Raft is the most popular consensus algorithm (a way for a group of machines to agree on one ordered log of commands). etcd uses it, so every Kubernetes cluster depends on it; Kafka's KRaft controllers use a Raft-based protocol too. Raft has three roles: follower, candidate and leader, and time is split into numbered terms. Click through an election.

  1. Silence. The old leader C crashed. Followers expect a heartbeat regularly. Each follower has a randomised election timeout (the Raft paper suggests 150-300 ms) so they do not all wake at once.
  2. Become a candidate. A's timer fires first. A increases the term to 5, votes for itself and sends RequestVote to everyone. A node only votes for a candidate whose log is at least as up to date as its own.
  3. Majority. B, D and E have not voted in term 5 yet, so they vote for A. With its own vote, A has 4 of 5 - more than the 3 it needs. Each node votes once per term, so no other candidate can also get a majority in term 5.
  4. Lead. A is leader and sends heartbeats (AppendEntries with no entries) to stop new elections. If C comes back, it sees term 5 > 4 and steps down to follower.
  5. Replicate. Clients send writes to the leader. The leader appends to its log, sends the entry to followers, and marks it committed once a majority has stored it. Only committed entries are applied. That is why a 5-node cluster survives 2 failures but not 3.

You can watch this live in the interactive visualisation on raft.github.io - kill the leader and see a new term start.

Try it: see who leads an etcd cluster

Kubernetes keeps all cluster state in etcd. On a 3-member etcd cluster you can ask each member for its status. Exactly one row says IS LEADER true, and all rows share the same RAFT TERM.

# on a kubeadm control-plane node: etcd only serves HTTPS with client certificates
ETCDCTL_API=3 etcdctl \
  --endpoints=https://10.0.0.1:2379,https://10.0.0.2:2379,https://10.0.0.3:2379 \
  --cacert=/etc/kubernetes/pki/etcd/ca.crt \
  --cert=/etc/kubernetes/pki/etcd/server.crt \
  --key=/etc/kubernetes/pki/etcd/server.key \
  endpoint status --write-out=table
+-----------------------+------------------+---------+---------+-----------+------------+-----------+------------+--------------------+--------+
|        ENDPOINT       |        ID        | VERSION | DB SIZE | IS LEADER | IS LEARNER | RAFT TERM | RAFT INDEX | RAFT APPLIED INDEX | ERRORS |
+-----------------------+------------------+---------+---------+-----------+------------+-----------+------------+--------------------+--------+
| https://10.0.0.1:2379 | 8e9e05c52164694d |  3.5.16 |   25 MB |      true |      false |         7 |      48211 |              48211 |        |
| https://10.0.0.2:2379 | 91bc3c398fb3c146 |  3.5.16 |   25 MB |     false |      false |         7 |      48211 |              48211 |        |

(IDs and sizes will differ on your cluster. etcd 3.6 prints a few more columns.) Stop the leader member and run it again: within about a second another member shows true and the term goes up by one.

Leases and fencing tokens: the trap with "I am still the leader"

Simpler systems elect a leader with a lease: "you are leader for 10 seconds, renew it before it runs out". The danger is a leader that pauses (a long garbage-collection pause, a VM freeze). Its lease expires, a new leader is chosen, and then the old one wakes up still believing it is in charge and writes. The fix is a fencing token: every new lease comes with a bigger number, and the storage rejects any write carrying a number smaller than one it has already seen.

From your world

Leader election is VRRP/HSRP choosing the active router, or OSPF electing a DR. The difference is safety under a partition: two VRRP routers that cannot hear each other both go active (split brain). Raft refuses to elect anyone without a strict majority, like an HA cluster that needs 2-of-3 votes before taking over. The term number is like a generation ID on a redundancy pair: a card that comes back with an older generation must step down. Where this breaks: VRRP elects in milliseconds on one LAN; Raft across regions pays a cross-region round trip for every committed write.

Careful: an even number of nodes does not help. Four nodes need 3 votes and survive 1 failure - the same as three nodes, with more traffic. Use 3 or 5 (sometimes 7).
