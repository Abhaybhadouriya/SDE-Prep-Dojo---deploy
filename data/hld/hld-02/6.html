Backpressure: when work arrives faster than you can do it

Explain like I'm 5

You pour juice into a cup faster than your friend can drink it. Soon it spills. Your friend says "wait!", and you pour slower. That "wait!" travelling back to the pourer is backpressure.

Backpressure is any way a slow part of the system tells faster parts upstream to slow down. Without it, the queue between them grows without limit: memory fills, latency climbs (a request waits behind millions of others), and finally something crashes. Rate limiting protects you from outside callers; backpressure protects the stages of your own system from each other.

There are only a few things a stage can do when it is overloaded. Name them in an interview:

  1. Block the producer Use a bounded queue; when full, the producer waits. TCP and gRPC streaming do this automatically. The Kafka producer does too: when its send buffer (buffer.memory) is full, send() blocks for up to max.block.ms.
  2. Reject quickly Return 429 or 503 at once so the caller can back off and retry later. Better than accepting work you will time out on.
  3. Shed load Drop low-priority work (analytics, recommendations) to protect critical work (checkout). Stripe's load shedder reserves capacity for critical requests this way.
  4. Buffer in a durable log Put Kafka between the stages. The producer writes at its speed, the consumer reads at its own, and the gap (consumer lag) is stored on disk instead of in memory. This absorbs spikes - but lag that keeps growing is still a problem you must fix.
  5. Scale the slow stage Add consumers (up to the number of partitions), or let Kubernetes autoscale on lag (KEDA, Kubernetes Event-Driven Autoscaling, can scale on Kafka lag; see Scheduling & Autoscaling).

Kafka consumers have backpressure built in by design: they pull. A slow consumer simply polls less often, and the broker never pushes more than it asks for (max.poll.records). The cost shows up as lag. Here is how lag looks when the consumer is losing the race:

$ kafka-consumer-groups.sh --bootstrap-server localhost:9092 --describe --group order-workers

order-workers  orders  0          1204331         1388012         183681  worker-1-6b0c...  /10.1.4.17   worker-1
order-workers  orders  1          1199870         1386544         186674  worker-2-91fa...  /10.1.4.18   worker-2
order-workers  orders  2          1201554         1390230         188676  worker-3-0d22...  /10.1.4.19   worker-3

LAG = log-end offset minus the consumer's committed offset. Lag that is large but flat means "behind but keeping up"; lag that rises every minute means drain < fill and you must act. More in Consumers & Consumer Groups and Observability & Failure Drills.

From your world

Backpressure is TCP flow control and Ethernet PFC pause frames. The receive window shrinks when the receiver's buffer fills, and the sender slows down. Consumer lag is ring occupancy; growing lag is a ring that fills faster than the driver drains it. Load shedding is tail-drop or WRED on a low-priority queue. Where this breaks: PFC can pause a whole link and cause head-of-line blocking across flows; a web system usually prefers to reject or shed per request type than to stall everything.

Danger: an unbounded in-memory queue is not a buffer, it is a delayed crash. Every queue needs a size limit and a decision about what happens when it is full.
