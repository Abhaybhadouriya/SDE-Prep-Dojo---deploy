Consistency models: what does the reader see?

When data lives on several machines, a write reaches them at different times. A consistency model is a promise about what a reader may see while that is happening. Think of it as a contract: "if you write X, then a reader will see ...".

Here is the ladder from strongest (easy for the programmer, expensive for the system) to weakest (cheap and fast, but surprising).

  1. Linearizable The system behaves as if there is one copy and every operation happens at one instant. Once any reader sees the new value, no later reader sees the old one. Used for locks, leader election, bank balances. etcd gives you this by default (reads go through Raft). ZooKeeper linearizes writes, but a plain read is served by whichever server you are connected to and can be stale unless the client calls sync() first. Spanner offers it too (Google calls it external consistency).
  2. Sequential Everyone sees operations in the same order, but that order may lag behind real time.
  3. Causal If B was caused by A (a reply to a comment), every reader sees A before B. Unrelated writes may appear in any order.
  4. Session guarantees Promises for one user: read-your-writes (I see my own post after I save it) and monotonic reads (I never see time go backwards when I refresh).
  5. Eventual If writes stop, all copies will agree in the end. No promise about when. Like counts, view counts and feeds live here happily.

What does this mean for a person staring at a screen? With eventual consistency, you post a photo, refresh, and it is not there yet. With read-your-writes, you always see your own photo, even if your friend in another city sees it a second later. That one guarantee removes most user complaints, and it is cheap.

Level 3 - Intermediate

Watch a stale read happen (and fix it)

The classic bug: a user writes to the leader (the main copy), then the next read goes to a follower (a read copy) that has not caught up. Click Next.

  1. Write. Asha changes her name. The app server sends the UPDATE to the leader database. The leader commits and replies "OK".
  2. Replicate later. The leader ships the change to the follower asynchronously (it does not wait). The follower is a few hundred milliseconds behind. This gap is called replication lag.
  3. Stale read. The app reads her profile from the follower (reads go to replicas to spread load). The follower still has the old name. To Asha the save "did not work".
  4. Fix. For a short time after a user writes, route that user's reads to the leader. Or remember the log position (LSN) of her write and only read from a follower that has reached it. That gives read-your-writes without making every read hit the leader.

From your world

Replication lag is routing convergence. After a link flap, some routers still have the old route for a moment and forward packets the old way (a stale read). OSPF floods LSAs until every LSDB matches (anti-entropy), and then the network is consistent again. Where this breaks: routing tolerates a few dropped packets during convergence; a bank balance cannot tolerate a "few wrong answers", so for money we pay for strong consistency.

Say it in one breath: "Payments, inventory decrement and seat booking need strong consistency. Likes, view counts and the feed can be eventual. For the user's own profile I give read-your-writes by reading from the leader just after a write."
